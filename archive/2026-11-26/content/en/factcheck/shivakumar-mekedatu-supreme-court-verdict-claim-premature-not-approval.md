## What we found

The claim is **false as stated**. On November 13, 2026, with Prime Minister Narendra Modi at a government event in Mysuru, Karnataka Chief Minister D K Shivakumar said the Supreme Court "has delivered a clear verdict" that Mekedatu "has to be built in Karnataka, and no one can stop it". The order that fits his description is the one of **November 13, 2025**, exactly a year earlier. In it the court **declined to entertain Tamil Nadu's challenge** to a Central Water Commission direction that Karnataka prepare a detailed project report, and called the challenge premature. That is a refusal to decide the question yet. It is not an approval of the dam, and it does not say it must be built.

## The evidence

### 1. What the court did

According to the account of the order, a bench led by Chief Justice B R Gavai heard *State of Karnataka vs State of Tamil Nadu* and found Tamil Nadu's plea **premature**. Senior advocate Mukul Rohatgi had argued for Tamil Nadu that a reservoir at a higher altitude could affect releases to the downstream state and harm farmers who depend on the Cauvery. The court did not rule on those arguments. It did not approve the project, and no report of the order says it directed construction.

### 2. What the direction says

The Central Water Commission direction that Tamil Nadu challenged only asks for a detailed project report. It also says **the prior approval of the Cauvery Water Management Authority and the Cauvery Water Regulation Committee** is a prerequisite for considering that report, and that the plan would be taken up only after Tamil Nadu's objections and the expert bodies' views are considered. So the next decisions sit with those two bodies, not with the court.

### 3. The Union government's position

The reports of the order record that the Union government had said in February 2025 that it had not accorded **any approval** for new dams on the Cauvery. Mr Shivakumar himself said on November 14 that the only outstanding requirement from the Centre is compensatory land for the forest that would be lost, and that Karnataka will prepare a revised project report. A revised report that has not yet been prepared has not yet been considered by anyone.

### 4. What else the project still needs

The project would use about **4.75 thousand million cubic feet** of water, mainly for drinking water in the Bengaluru region, and is meant to generate 400 MW. It lies inside the Cauvery Wildlife Sanctuary and could affect more than 5,000 hectares of riparian habitat, which means wildlife, forest and environmental clearances, in stages. Tamil Nadu's Law Minister has said Karnataka lacks forest and environmental clearance; the pending list in the November 2025 reports is consistent with that, though we did not find a newer status.

### 5. What Mr Shivakumar did say that holds

On November 14 he said the project would proceed "in accordance with the Supreme Court's directive". There is a court history here: in 2023 the court upheld a Cauvery authority order, with remarks on the authorities' expertise. Karnataka's right to ask for approval is not in dispute. What is in dispute is whether approval has been given, and on the record it has not.

## Why this claim spread

Mekedatu has been argued in terms of "the court" for years, and a premature-plea dismissal is easy to describe as a win. Both sides now talk about the same order differently: Tamil Nadu says the dam was never part of the tribunal's award or the court's judgment, and Karnataka says the verdict is clear. Our [report](/en/news/mekedatu-stalin-all-party-meeting-vijay-tamil-nadu-dam) of the exchange noted that none of the Saturday reports gave the date, case or terms of the order Mr Shivakumar cited.

## What would settle it

The text of the **November 13, 2025, order**, which should be placed beside the claim; Karnataka's revised project report and its compensatory land proposal; and any listing of Mekedatu on the agenda of the **Cauvery Water Management Authority**. Until the authority and its committee have approved a report, the supportable statement is that the court allowed the process to start and that the dam has not been approved.
