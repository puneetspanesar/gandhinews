## What we found

Three separate claims sit inside Tejashwi Yadav's video of November 20, and they do not stand or fall together. His point about money is **supported by the financing plan but incomplete**. His point that the bridge was already open is **true of one stretch, not of the whole structure**. His cost figure of more than ₹5,500 crore is **not backed by any document we could open**. Taken as a package, aimed at showing the Prime Minister was taking credit for a finished state project, we rate it **misleading**.

Mr Yadav, Leader of the Opposition in the Bihar Assembly, said Nitish Kumar had opened the bridge on June 23, 2025, that lakhs of vehicles had used it since, and, in his words, "There is no financial contribution by the Centre in the construction of this bridge." He put the cost at more than ₹5,500 crore. The Prime Minister dedicated the bridge on November 21.

## The evidence

### 1. Who paid: an ADB loan and Bihar's own money

The Asian Development Bank's Project Administration Manual for the Bihar New Ganga Bridge Project, dated June 2016, sets out a financing plan of **$715 million: $500 million from the ADB (70%) and $215 million from the Government of Bihar (30%)**. The plan has no separate row for the Government of India. The executing agency is the Government of Bihar acting through the Bihar State Road Development Corporation. The state's road construction minister was reported on June 22, 2025 as saying the same thing in rupees: an ADB loan of about ₹3,000 crore and about ₹2,000 crore from the state.

### 2. The catch: the Centre was the borrower

The same manual says "The Government of India has requested a loan of $500 million" from the ADB and "will make available the loan proceeds to the government of Bihar on a back-to-back basis." Its fund-flow chart lists the Ministry of Finance as the borrower. So there is no central grant in the plan, but "no central contribution" leaves out that the loan ran through New Delhi. That is how foreign-funded state projects are normally structured; it does not show that central money built the bridge. It does mean the statement is not the whole picture.

### 3. Opened in June 2025: one stretch

Reports from June 2025 say Mr Kumar opened the first phase, the stretch from Kacchi Dargah to Raghopur (4.57 km), and that the **Raghopur to Bidupur section was to follow**. A report of December 9, 2025 said the second and third phases were still to be finished, with about 85% of the overall work complete. The Prime Minister's Office describes what Mr Modi dedicated as a 9.7 km main bridge, with 10 km of approach roads, between NH-22 in Patna district and NH-322 in Vaishali district. Saying the whole bridge was open in June 2025 overstates what the reports support. The Prime Minister's address, as the Office published it, says nothing on who built or paid for the bridge and speaks only of the name given in honour of Nitish Kumar.

### 4. The cost: no document for ₹5,500 crore

The Prime Minister's Office release of November 20 puts the cost at about **₹4,280 crore**. The ADB's draft environmental assessment of November 2015 gave about ₹5,000 crore, and 2025 reports gave ₹4,988 crore. None of these is above ₹5,500 crore. The figures differ among themselves, possibly because they count different scope, and we could not reconcile them from the papers read.

## Why this claim spread

The video came the evening before the Prime Minister's first Bihar visit since May and a day before a ₹34,470 crore programme. It joined two live arguments: who deserves credit for the bridge, and whether a second inauguration was needed for something already in use. The BJP called the remarks irresponsible. Because the claim has a true core, namely that the state and the ADB, not a central grant, financed the bridge, it travelled with its weaker parts attached.

## What would change this rating

A Bihar government or BSRDCL document showing the final cost, or one showing the whole bridge open to traffic in June 2025, would change the second and third parts. A central grant or guarantee that Bihar is not repaying itself would change the first.
