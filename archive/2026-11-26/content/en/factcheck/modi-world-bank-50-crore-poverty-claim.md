## What we found

Prime Minister Narendra Modi's claim that **50 crore Indians have come out of poverty** is **mostly true, once you know which poverty line it uses**. Speaking at the FICCI annual meeting on November 19, 2026, he said the World Bank's latest report "says that in just 12 to 13 years, 50 crore Indians have defeated poverty". The World Bank's own database does show a fall of about 50 crore, but only on one of its two lines, and over a span that begins before his government.

The Bank publishes India's poverty figures on its Poverty and Inequality Platform. In the data version released in September 2026, **57.7% of Indians lived below $4.20 a day in 2011, and 16.1% in 2023**. With India's population at 126.99 crore in 2011 and 144.55 crore in 2023, that is 73.2 crore people falling to 23.3 crore, a drop of **about 49.9 crore**. The Prime Minister's number is right on that line.

## The evidence

### 1. What he said at FICCI

The Prime Minister's Office text of the address, released at 1:44 pm on November 19, has him saying that the World Bank's latest report "makes us proud" and that "in just 12 to 13 years, 50 crore Indians have defeated poverty". He then set it against the 1970s "Garibi Hatao" slogan, when the country's population was about 50 crore, and said the difference was policy and intent. He named no line and no year. The report he means is the World Bank's India Poverty and Equity Brief of October 2026, which he had also praised in a November 1 post, citing **49.6 crore people moving out of poverty between 2011 and 2023**. We could not open the brief; the figures here are worked out from the Bank's database, which the brief draws on.

### 2. It depends on the line

The Bank uses a $3 a day line for extreme poverty and $4.20 a day as the line for lower-middle-income countries, both at 2021 prices adjusted for what money buys. On the extreme line, the share of Indians fell from 27.1% in 2011 to **2.6% in 2023**, which is 34.4 crore people falling to 3.7 crore, a fall of **30.7 crore**. That is the figure behind the claim that extreme poverty has nearly ended. The 50 crore figure belongs to the higher line, where **23.3 crore Indians were still below $4.20 a day in 2023**. Saying 50 crore "defeated poverty" without the line suggests that poverty is nearly gone; the Bank's own numbers say 16 in 100 Indians are still below its middle-income line.

### 3. The clock starts before this government

The Prime Minister framed the number as the result of the last 12 to 13 years of policy. The Bank's series runs from 2011 to 2023. The Union government changed on May 26, 2014, so the first three years of the fall, **2011 to 2014**, fall under the previous government. The Bank has no survey year between 2011 and 2022, so the data cannot say how much of the fall came in which years. The Bank's database also records the 2011 survey and the 2022-23 consumption survey as different comparable spells, a sign that the two are not strictly like for like; the fall is large enough that this does not change the direction, but it is a reason to treat the exact number with care.

### 4. What the data support beyond poverty

Mr Modi also said India's Gini index, a measure of inequality, had fallen by over 20% to 23.0. The Bank's database gives a consumption Gini of 28.78 in 2011 and 23.0 in 2023, a fall of 20.1%, so that figure holds. The Gini here is built from consumption surveys, which measure spending and not income or wealth, and it says nothing about how the richest earn.

## Why this claim spread

The line suited a speech in which the Prime Minister was asking industry to pay and hire more, and it came with a World Bank name attached, which makes a number travel. It was shared as a bare headline, "50 crore out of poverty", with the line and the start year dropped. Those two words are what separate a number that is right from one that is complete.

## What would change this rating

If the Bank's brief used a different population series that moved the fall below 49 crore on the $4.20 line, the rounding in "50 crore" would be harder to defend. A fresh consumption survey for 2024 or 2025 would show whether the fall has continued. Until then the rating stays at mostly true.
