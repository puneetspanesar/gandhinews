## What we found

Shivraj Singh Chouhan's statement is true. The Union agriculture minister said at a farmers' gathering in Tarn Taran district on November 24, 2026, that **Punjab has not yet implemented** the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), and that he had written to the chief minister during the floods asking the state to bring it in.

The government's own answer in Parliament says exactly this. In a written reply on July 28, 2026, Minister of State Ramnath Thakur told the Lok Sabha that PMFBY is voluntary for states and that **the Government of Punjab has not opted for it**. The statement therefore holds. The context is what changes the picture: the Centre did not leave Punjab out, Punjab stayed out.

## The evidence

### 1. What the minister said

The ministry's press release on the Kisan Chaupal at Buh Haveliyan village, issued at 4:33 pm on November 24, says Chouhan **expressed regret** that Punjab has not so far implemented PMFBY. It adds that he had written to the chief minister during the floods so that farmers' losses in a natural calamity could be made good. The release is a summary of his remarks, not a verbatim record, so we treat the claim as the release states it.

### 2. What Parliament was told

The Lok Sabha answer to Unstarred Question No. 1451, put by Mahua Moitra and Malvinder Singh Kang, asked about PMFBY across states "including Punjab". The reply says the scheme "is voluntary for the States and Govt. of Punjab has not opted for implementation of the scheme". Further down it states plainly: "Punjab is not implementing the scheme. There is no proposal under consideration of the Government for any specific changes in the scheme for the State of Punjab."

### 3. The coverage table agrees

The annexure to the answer gives state-wise coverage and claims for 2023-24 to 2025-26, as on July 24, 2026. It lists **24 states and union territories**, with 4,351.44 lakh applications enrolled, ₹10,662.32 crore in premium collected from farmers, ₹46,002.08 crore in claims paid and ₹4,399.06 crore pending. Punjab does not appear in it, though its neighbour Haryana does, with 462.83 lakh applications and ₹1,594.54 crore in paid claims.

### 4. Why the wording matters

A reader could take "not implemented" to mean the Centre has withheld the scheme. The Lok Sabha record says otherwise. PMFBY is a demand-driven scheme: a state must opt in and pay its share of the premium subsidy, which is why the answer also lists late release of state subsidy among the causes of pending claims elsewhere. Chouhan's call on Punjab is a request to the state, not an announcement about a Central decision.

## What this does not settle

The check covers one point: whether Punjab is outside PMFBY. It does not say whether Punjab's farmers are better or worse off for it, or what Punjab offers in its place. The same press release carries other claims, including about crop residue funds and procurement, that rest on figures not given in the document; we have not rated them. Punjab's government has not, at the time of writing, replied in the sources we could open, and its reasons for staying out are not in the record we read.
