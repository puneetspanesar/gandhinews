## What has the RBI proposed?

**Draft rules that would hand customers the controls over their own digital payments, so that fraud can be stopped before the money leaves.** On Monday, November 23, 2026, the Reserve Bank of India put out draft amendment directions called "Preventive Safeguards for Electronic Banking Transactions". **Comments are invited until December 14, 2026**, through the "Connect 2 Regulate" section of the RBI website or by email.

It is a draft. The final text can change, and nothing binds a bank until the RBI notifies it. Our [news report](/en/news/rbi-stop-switch-trusted-person-draft-digital-fraud/) has the first day's detail.

## What is the "stop switch"?

**A single control that blocks every future outgoing debit from an account.** Once a customer triggers it, the bank must refuse customer-initiated debits, new payment mandates, new standing instructions and the registering of the account on new third-party UPI apps. Money can still come in.

Some things are left out. **Existing recurring mandates and standing instructions continue**, and so do bank-initiated debits to recover dues such as loan instalments or charges. To switch it off, the customer must authenticate at least as strongly as when it was switched on, and the bank may add a cooling-off period. The idea is for someone who suspects a scammer is on the line, or whose phone is lost, to freeze the account first and sort it out afterwards.

## What else could a customer switch off or limit?

**Each payment channel, one at a time.** Customers could turn individual channels off and on, and set a maximum per transaction, per beneficiary and per day for each channel, plus an overall daily cap across all of them. A customer could also fix a window of time in which a channel stays off. These limits would override any higher limit set by the bank or the payment system.

Cards would get separate switches for domestic and international use, and for ATM, point-of-sale, online and contactless transactions. Gift and transit prepaid cards are excluded.

## How would the trusted-person check work?

**The customer names an adult, and that person has one hour to approve any transfer above a limit the customer sets.** The check is voluntary and both sides must consent. The bank verifies the person with an officially valid ID, a mobile number different from the account holder's and an email address.

For a flagged transfer the trusted person is sent the amount, the beneficiary and any narration. **A rejection, or silence, means the transfer fails.** The trusted person is not a joint holder, nominee or signatory, cannot operate the account and cannot see balances, deposits, investments or insurance. Registering, changing or leaving the arrangement each carries a 24-hour cooling-off period, so a fraudster who has taken over the account cannot remove the safeguard at once. UPI transfers, merchant payments and recurring mandates are outside this check.

For older or less confident users, the same mechanism could let a son, daughter or friend act as a second pair of eyes. Banks may also ask more vulnerable customers to visit a branch or confirm by phone.

## What changes for new customers and new cards?

**Digital channels would be off by default** and switched on only with explicit consent and the required authentication. Once a channel is switched on, the bank must cap total transfers through it for the first 24 hours, at a level it sets from its own risk assessment. No report gives a rupee figure for that cap.

New physical and virtual cards would work only for contact-based ATM and point-of-sale use in India when issued. Forex prepaid cards could be used for contact and contactless transactions abroad.

## Who would have to follow it?

**Seven kinds of lender:** commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks, urban cooperative banks and rural cooperative banks. They would have to offer the controls through branches, ATMs, internet and mobile banking and phone banking, alert the customer at once to any change, and keep tamper-proof logs. One report says most directions would apply from July 1, 2027, and a wider definition of bank negligence from January 1, 2027. Treat those dates as unconfirmed until the final text.

## Why now?

**Because the rules so far mostly decide who pays after a fraud, not how to prevent it.** On June 24, 2026, the RBI strengthened limits on customer liability for unauthorised electronic transactions. The regulator says it now feels "a need to strengthen the same from a preventive perspective".

The Supreme Court has pushed the same way. In the "digital arrest" scam case it is hearing on its own motion, it told the RBI on November 10 that banks must repay at least the amount lost where due diligence failed. Reports say two ideas from the April discussion paper, a one-hour delay on some high-value transfers and an annual credit ceiling, have been dropped from the draft.

## What is still open?

**How big the limits will be, how banks will build it, and whether customers will use it.** The draft leaves the trusted-person threshold to the customer and the first-day cap to each bank, so no figure is fixed. Banks, payment apps and consumer groups can argue for or against default-off channels and the cooling-off periods before December 14. The RBI will then issue final directions, and customers will see the switches only once their own bank has built them. As of November 25, 2026, no bank has said when that would be.
