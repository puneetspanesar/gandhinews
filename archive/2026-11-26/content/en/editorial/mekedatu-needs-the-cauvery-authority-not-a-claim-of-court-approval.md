On Friday, with the Prime Minister beside him in Mysuru, Karnataka Chief Minister D K Shivakumar said the Supreme Court had delivered "a clear verdict" that Mekedatu "has to be built" and that "no one can stop it". On Saturday, Tamil Nadu's Law Minister said his government "will never allow" the dam, and DMK leader M K Stalin asked Chief Minister Vijay to call an all-party meeting at once.

Both statements are made in the name of the law. Neither describes it.

## What the court actually did

On November 13, 2025, a bench led by the Chief Justice declined to entertain Tamil Nadu's plea against a Central Water Commission direction to prepare a detailed project report for the dam. It called the challenge **premature**. It did not approve the reservoir. The direction it left standing says the **prior approval of the Cauvery Water Management Authority and the Cauvery Water Regulation Committee** is a prerequisite for considering the report at all. The Union government had said in February 2025 that it had given no approval for new dams on the river.

The project would use about 4.75 thousand million cubic feet of water, mainly for Bengaluru's drinking supply, and generate 400 MW. It sits inside the Cauvery Wildlife Sanctuary and could affect more than 5,000 hectares of riparian habitat, so forest, wildlife and environmental clearances are still needed. Mr Shivakumar himself says the Centre is yet to clear compensatory land and that Karnataka is preparing a revised report. A report not yet written cannot have been approved.

## The fair objections

Karnataka has a real need. Bengaluru's drinking water supply is stretched, and the state is entitled to ask for approval. The Supreme Court did not close that door, and Mr Shivakumar is right that Tamil Nadu cannot veto a process that is meant to weigh its objections.

Tamil Nadu has a real fear too. Delta farmers depend on timely releases for the Kuruvai crop, and a new reservoir upstream could hold back flows in a drought year. A promise to honour the state's 177 tmcft share is only as good as the rules for releasing it, and those rules are what the authority exists to write.

Where each side goes wrong is in the claim that the matter is already settled. If Karnataka's reading were right, there would be nothing left for the two Cauvery bodies to approve. If Tamil Nadu's were right, the court would not have let the report proceed.

## What should happen

First, Karnataka should **publish its revised project report** and its plan for compensatory forest land as soon as they exist, with the water-use and release assumptions, so that the other states and the public can read the same document the authority will.

Second, the Cauvery Water Management Authority should place Mekedatu on its agenda, hear Tamil Nadu, and **publish its minutes**. A decision made in a meeting room nobody can see will not end a dispute that has run for decades.

Third, the Union government should say whether it has given any approval since February 2025, and what clearances it needs before any are given. Mr Shivakumar asked the Prime Minister for help to move them. A reply in public would cost the Centre nothing.

Fourth, Mr Vijay should accept Mr Stalin's call for a meeting, but its aim should be a technical case before the authority: the worst-year flows, the Kuruvai schedule and the proposed release rules. A resolution that says "never" gives the authority nothing to weigh.

The court has left the door ajar, not open. The two states can keep shouting about who holds the key, or they can take the question to the body that is supposed to answer it.
