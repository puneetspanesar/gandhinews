Four people were shot dead in two cars on Friday afternoon at New Haven, in Manipur's Ukhrul district. Police say two men and two women, all Naga civilians, died on the spot and that the firing came from near a Kuki settlement. The Tangkhul Naga Long, the community's apex body, says this was **the fourth attack at the same spot**. A road that has been hit four times is no longer an unlucky stretch. It is a known risk, and the state has not secured it.

## What the state did

On Saturday Chief Minister Yumnam Khemchand Singh chaired a security review in Imphal. According to the government statement, combing operations will continue, posts are to tighten security in their areas, and measures for vulnerable areas were discussed. Deputy Chief Minister Losii Dikho called for dialogue and said violence cannot heal the losses.

These are the same words that followed the earlier ambushes. Our reports from October covered an attack in Kamjong, firing at Chassad and the transfer of a police chief after Naga women protested. Reports cited in coverage say **at least 50 people have died in Kuki-Naga clashes** since February. In each case the answer has been more forces and more patrols, and the next attack has still found a road that was open to it.

## The demands, and the fair reply

The Tangkhul Naga Long wants the case handed to the National Investigation Agency and **all central forces withdrawn**. It says it has lost faith in them, and it has given the Centre and the state three days, until around November 17, before an indefinite agitation. Families have said they will not claim the bodies until the demands are met.

There is a fair reply. Pulling the forces out of a district where two communities are shooting at each other could leave civilians more exposed, not less. Nor can an agency be given a case because a community has set a deadline. Police say the attack is suspected to be the work of Kuki militants; the state has named no group, and an investigation has to produce names before it can produce charges.

That reply does not excuse silence. The families are owed a reason, in public, for whatever the Centre decides on the NIA request, and for what it will do about forces that a whole community says it no longer trusts.


## What should happen now

First, **the Centre and the state should answer the NIA request** before the ultimatum runs out, yes or no, with reasons. If the state police are able to investigate, say what steps have been taken: the vehicles examined, the ground searched, the witnesses recorded.

Second, once the investigation names the attackers, the state should say so. The statement after Friday's ambush spoke of "unidentified armed miscreants". Nobody can hold a militant group to account if the government will not say who it is looking for.

Third, the Imphal-Ukhrul road needs a security plan that can be read and checked: where the points of past attacks are, which force holds which stretch, and when convoys move. The Meitei body COCOMI has asked that personnel on VIP protection be moved to vulnerable stretches. Its convenor, Y K Dhiren, said "protecting ordinary citizens must take precedence over non-essential security duties". Whatever one thinks of the rest of the arguments in this conflict, that is a request the state can act on this week.

Fourth, the Chief Minister should meet leaders of both the Naga and the Kuki groups, not only the forces. The woman shot in Kangpokpi on Thursday and the four killed on Friday belong to communities that each say the other side is armed and protected. A meeting at which both are in the room is the least that grief of this size deserves.

The state's own reports of its review do not say when the next one will be held. It should not wait for the next ambush.
