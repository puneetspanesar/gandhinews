Two people from Punjab's Gurdaspur district have been arrested over the **bomb blast that damaged a railway track** in Sri Ganganagar, Rajasthan, on November 3, and the state's anti-terror chief says a handler based in Pakistan supplied the device. Rajasthan Police ADGP Dinesh MN, who heads the Anti-Terrorism Squad (ATS), said on Sunday, November 15, that the accused had confessed and would be produced before a court.

The blast went off at about 9:45 pm on November 3 on the Anupgarh-Suratgarh line near Ramsinghpur village, in the 50 GB area of Sri Ganganagar district. It broke the sleepers, left a crater and damaged two of the cement pillars that support the track. **A passenger train had passed the same stretch about 75 minutes earlier.** No injuries were reported in the accounts available.

## Who has been arrested

The accused are **Mahakdeep Kaur, 21, and Davinder Singh, 22**, both residents of Gurdaspur. They were arrested on Saturday, November 14, in a joint operation by the Rajasthan ATS, Sri Ganganagar Police and Punjab Police. Police say Gurdaspur Police supplied the first tip-off and then traced the pair's movements and money transactions; Rajasthan teams sought inputs from central agencies and checked them by technical analysis.

Police recovered **two pistols, 46 live cartridges and ₹30,000 in cash**, which they allege was part of the payment. Three more people have been detained on suspicion of providing support, including a place to stay, a motorcycle and transport to the site.

## What police say happened

According to the ATS, Kaur was in touch with two Pakistan-based handlers who went by the names "Chacha" and "Shah". Dinesh MN said investigators had "recovered evidence confirming that a handler in Pakistan, known as Shah, provided them with the IED". Police say the handler also chose the site and told the pair what to do.

The account given by police is that the handlers asked Kaur to travel to Rajasthan and collect a package, and that the device was left at dead-drop spots near Ramsinghpur. They say she was paid **more than ₹2 lakh** and was romantically involved with a handler. Police say she had made contact after her husband was arrested in a drug case in Punjab; the marriage ended in divorce within six months, and her brother is reported to be in jail under the narcotics law. Neither of the accused had a known source of employment, police say.

## The earlier attempts

The first plan, on October 6, was abandoned because the handler **did not send the bomb**, police say. A timer-fitted device that Shah supplied was then planted on October 12 or 13 but did not explode because of a technical fault. The third attempt, on November 3, is the one that went off. Police say the pair made three visits in all to the stretch near Ramsinghpur, collecting the package from the dead-drop spots.

## Pakistan link under investigation

Rajasthan Police have alleged possible links between the blast and Pakistan's intelligence network. A senior ATS officer linked the approach to the ISI, but the police say the connection is still being investigated and no court has tested it. Dinesh MN said investigators are checking **whether the same network** that sent RDX and improvised explosive devices in an earlier case in the state was behind this one, and why Sri Ganganagar was chosen.

Police also say the same network tried to lure another woman in a similar case in Sri Ganganagar, using romantic overtures and money.

## Why it matters to travellers and border districts

The explosion hit a working line on the Anupgarh-Suratgarh section, and police say a passenger train had gone over the same stretch about 75 minutes before. The police account also has a timer bomb planted at the site on October 12 to 13 that failed to go off because of a technical fault, so **two attempts on the same stretch** preceded the successful one.

The case also shows the method police describe: handlers recruiting people with no steady income through money and romantic contact, then directing them by phone to dead-drop locations. Punjab Police sources have said the arrests followed a sustained investigation and close coordination with Rajasthan.

## What happens next

Kaur and Singh are to be produced before a court, after which the ATS can seek their custody. The ATS says it is examining why Sri Ganganagar was picked and whether the three detained men and other contacts are linked to the handlers. No formal charges have been made public, and the explosive used has not been named in the accounts available.
