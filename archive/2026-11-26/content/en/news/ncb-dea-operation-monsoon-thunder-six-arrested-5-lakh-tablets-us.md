Six people have been arrested in four Indian cities after the **Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB) and the US Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA)** broke the Indian end of a network that, according to investigators, posted prescription-type pills to buyers across the United States. The operation, code-named Operation Monsoon Thunder, was announced on Friday, November 20, 2026, and Union Home Minister Amit Shah congratulated both agencies on Saturday.

The NCB's records show **nearly 3,000 parcels carrying close to 5 lakh tablets** sent to American addresses between June 2022 and November 2026. That figure is the network's identified activity over four and a half years, not the quantity seized in the raids.

## What the agencies found

NCB teams raided premises in **Hyderabad, Delhi, Kolkata and Lucknow between November 4 and 13**. Six Indian nationals were arrested. By the account in published reports, four of them were held in Lucknow, one in Kolkata and one in Hyderabad. All of this is alleged; no court has yet tested the charges.

The tablets are psychotropic substances and opioid medicines of the kind sold only on prescription, among them alprazolam, lorazepam, tramadol, diazepam and zolpidem. Some have accepted medical uses, but they are regulated because they can be abused and addictive when taken without a doctor's advice.

Investigators describe a simple, hard-to-police chain. Operators in India are said to have **taken orders over Telegram, Skype and WhatsApp**, and tracked customers and consignments in shared online spreadsheets. Parcels went by post and other delivery services to a distribution set-up inside the United States, which handled the last stretch to buyers through drop-shippers.

## Money, data and the US end

Payment is where the case is still widening. Investigators have found money moving through **Venmo, bank accounts held in other people's names, UPI and cryptocurrency**, and say the Indian operators allegedly received their commissions through hawala channels. The NCB is tracing the proceeds to find who benefited and how much was earned.

The two agencies have also swapped data. The records list **more than 59,000 customers** in the United States, with transactions, delivery addresses, past consignments, postal tracking numbers and drug types. Appearing in such a list does not by itself show wrongdoing, and the agencies have not said any of those people face charges.

The NCB also identified **live consignments of more than 1,500 tablets** that were on their way to destinations in the United States. Acting on that intelligence, the DEA intercepted and seized two shipments. The US agency is following leads from the NCB, including details of the drop-shippers who received the parcels.

## What Shah and Washington said

Mr Shah wrote on X that he offered "heartiest congratulations to the NCB and the US DEA on the exemplary success of Operation Monsoon Thunder". He said the arrests and seizures in both countries showed the strength of the India-US partnership against drugs, and repeated the government's **zero-tolerance policy**: under Prime Minister Narendra Modi, he said, India is working to ensure that no narcotics enter the country or leave it for others.

The Home Ministry's own release, issued through PIB on November 21, carries the same message of congratulation to the two agencies.

## Why it matters

The NCB's own 2025 annual report, as cited in coverage of this case, shows pharmaceutical drug seizures by all enforcement agencies rose 77% from 2021 to **2,37,390 kg in 2025**. Online ordering, courier and postal services and digital payments have made it easier to move pills in small parcels, which is why this case turned on tracking messages, spreadsheets and money rather than on a single large haul.

For Indian readers the case is also a reminder that a prescription drug sent abroad without authority is a criminal matter at home. The Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act covers psychotropic tablets as well as street drugs, and the penalties depend on the quantity involved.

It follows a series of Home Ministry statements on drugs. On November 7, [Mr Shah set a goal of a drug-free India by 2029](/news/amit-shah-ahmedabad-gujarat-security-review-drug-free-india-2029) at a security review in Ahmedabad and asked Gujarat for an action plan against narcotics. On Saturday he was in Arunachal Pradesh, where he [presented the President's Police Colour to the state police](/news/amit-shah-arunachal-police-colour-banderdewa-siang-dam-protesters).

## What happens next

Only the Indian module has been hit so far. The NCB and the DEA are now working to identify the **suppliers, the sources of the tablets and everyone involved**, in India and abroad. Records suggest the drugs may have been sourced from several countries, and the hawala trail and the proceeds are still being followed. The accused will be produced before courts under the NDPS Act as the investigation goes on; the NCB has not announced further arrests or a date for the next stage.
