**Lakshya Sen has won the Kumamoto Masters Japan.** The eighth seed came from a game down to beat Japan's Koki Watanabe **13-21, 21-18, 21-14** in the men's singles final at the Kumamoto Prefectural Gymnasium on Sunday, November 15, in one hour and four minutes. According to reports, he is the first Indian man to win the event, and the title comes two weeks after his win at the Hylo Open in Saarbrücken.

## How the final turned

Watanabe, the home favourite, **led 3-0 early** and went on to take the first game 21-13. The Japanese player had reached the final after beating Kidambi Srikanth in the quarter-finals, as we [reported on Friday](/en/news/srikanth-watanabe-kumamoto-masters-quarter-final-exit-after-naraoka-win), and he started the final in the same sharp form.

Sen steadied in the second game. He led 13-10 and then **held off a late Watanabe fight to win it 21-18**, saving game points on the way according to reports from the venue. The decider belonged to Sen from the start: he led 11-7 at the interval and pulled clear to close it out 21-14.

## A second title in a fortnight

Sen arrived in Kumamoto as the **Hylo Open champion**. He beat Jonatan Christie 21-17, 21-10 in Saarbrücken on November 1, his first BWF World Tour title since the Australian Open a year earlier, as we [reported at the time](/en/news/lakshya-sen-hylo-open-title-indian-double-saarbrucken). Kumamoto is his second title in two weeks.

Reports on the final describe it as **his sixth World Tour title** and the first time he has won two World Tour titles in the same season. Against Watanabe he now leads the career head-to-head 4-2.

Asked about the week, Sen said he was happy to better his semi-final finish at Kumamoto last year and wanted to keep his form going.

## The route to the title

Sen's week began on Thursday, November 12, with a 21-8, 21-14 win over China's Dong Tian Yao in 35 minutes. On Friday he beat Japan's Yushi Tanaka 21-16, 21-15. On Saturday he **beat Indonesia's Alwi Farhan 21-12, 15-21, 21-11** in the semi-final, his first win over the world No. 10 after two defeats, as we [reported on Saturday](/en/news/lakshya-sen-alwi-farhan-kumamoto-masters-semifinal-final-sunday).

He was the last Indian left in the draw. Srikanth went out in the quarter-finals, and both Indian doubles pairs, Hariharan Amsakarunan and M R Arjun in the men's and Treesa Jolly and Gayatri Gopichand in the women's, had lost earlier in the week.

## What it means for the Finals

The title is worth **9,200 ranking points**. Before the final Sen was 12th in the BWF World Tour Finals race with 64,980 points. With the new points he should move to about sixth with 74,180 points when the standings update on Tuesday, which puts him inside the top eight that qualify for the season-ending Finals in Hangzhou, China. He is the world No. 13.

A place in the top eight is not yet confirmed. The standings are not final until the last qualifying event, and the picture can still change for players around the cut-off.

## What next

The **Hong Kong Open, from November 17 to 22**, is the last qualifying event before the Hangzhou Finals. Sen will want to protect his position there, and the updated race standings on Tuesday will show exactly where the Kumamoto title has put him.
