Wholesale price inflation in India rose to **12.29% in October**, from 11.39% in September, official data released on Monday showed, according to reports. It is the second straight month in which the wholesale price index (WPI) has set a new high for the current series, and the climb was driven almost entirely by fuel.

The all-commodities index stood at 113.3, against 112.5 a month earlier. Wholesale inflation measures how much more traders and manufacturers pay for goods than they did a year ago, before the cost reaches the shop shelf.

## Fuel pulls the index up

Fuel and power prices were **32.84% higher** than a year earlier, up from 25.85% in September. The fuel and power index rose to 115.7 from 110.5. Within the group, crude petroleum and natural gas were up 70.80% and mineral oils, the refined fuels, were up 45.57%. Electricity was 8.06% dearer, a sharp change from September, when it was still cheaper than a year ago.

The pressure tracks the West Asia war and the tanker strikes in and around the Strait of Hormuz that have kept oil dear since the summer. Fuel now carries the index, because every other major group either eased or moved only slightly.

## Factory goods and food ease

Manufactured products, which carry nearly two-thirds of the index weight, rose **9.54%**, a little below September's 9.76%, which had been the highest in the series. Costs are still high in the heavy industries: chemicals were up 17.53%, basic metals 17.21%, rubber and plastic products 13.90% and textiles 13.67%.

Primary articles, the raw produce and minerals that feed into other prices, rose **9.24%**, up from 8.67%. The food index, which has a weight of 24.99%, rose 8.58%, slightly below September's 8.66%, and stood at 120.2.

The reports did not mention any revision to the September figure. The final August reading was unchanged at 110.8 on the index, with inflation at 9.92%.

## How we got here

Wholesale inflation has been building since the summer. It was 9.92% in August and then [jumped to 11.39% in September](/en/news/wpi-inflation-september-2026), the highest since the index moved to its 2022-23 base, when crude and gas prices at the wholesale level were 57% higher than a year before. The October reading is a further 0.9 percentage points above that.

Our [preview of the release](/en/news/wpi-inflation-october-data-due-monday-rbi-december) noted that crude had eased from its October peak, but the figures show that the pass-through to wholesale fuel prices continued.

## Why it matters

Wholesale costs tend to reach consumers with a lag. Higher prices for fuel, chemicals, metals and plastics feed into transport, packaging, appliances and vehicles over the following months, and into the price of food that has to be moved and processed. Retail inflation was already [6.09% in October](/en/news/retail-inflation-october-2026-6-09-two-year-high), above the Reserve Bank of India's 6% upper limit.

That puts pressure on the central bank. The Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% on October 7, its first increase since February 2023, and economists have said a second increase is possible in December. A wholesale number that keeps rising strengthens that case, although the slight easing in factory goods and food offers some relief.

For households, a higher repo rate would raise the cost of floating-rate home and car loans, while costlier fuel and raw materials would show up gradually in shop prices.

## What happens next

The Monetary Policy Committee meets from December 2 to 4, and it will have both the October retail and wholesale readings in hand. The November retail inflation figure is due on December 14.

The Office of the Economic Adviser's release could not be opened when this report was written, and the figures here rest on independent reports of the data. No official comment on the drivers or economist reactions had been reported by midday on Monday.
