The CPI(M) on Sunday demanded a **judicial inquiry** into the purchase of 200 MW of electricity by the Keralam government, alleging the power was bought without tenders and without the approval of the State Electricity Regulatory Commission. State secretary M V Govindan, speaking in Thiruvananthapuram, said the Chief Minister, V D Satheesan, was involved in the decision. The government has not responded to the Sunday allegations. Satheesan has said that no purchase agreement was ever signed.

Govindan said the 200 MW was procured in October **at higher rates for three to four years**, under emergency provisions and without proper consultation. "This is a serious and dangerous development," he said, and asked the government to make all procurement files public. He also pointed to the open differences between Electricity Minister Sunny Joseph and the Chief Minister over the issue. He did not name the supplier or give a cost in the remarks as reported.

## What the opposition says

The demand is not new. On Tuesday, November 10, Govindan said an **artificial power shortage** had been created to push through an agreement with GNA, a power trading platform, and that "the principle of collective responsibility of the Cabinet has completely broken down". He alleged the Chief Minister was working through a particular group and that the Electricity Minister had been left uninformed.

The same day, Leader of the Opposition Pinarayi Vijayan, himself a CPI(M) leader, asked for an inquiry by a High Court judge. He described the episode as "a major loot that was nearly pulled off by the state government" and said it would have placed "a huge financial burden on the state in the name of a fake power crisis". Vijayan said the contract for 200 MW, from a private company called VEN Global at ₹11 per unit, was routed through the Chief Minister's Office without a tender or Regulatory Commission approval. He also said the Chief Minister had not answered his earlier demand to release all the related files.

## What the Chief Minister says

Satheesan's reply came at a press conference after the Cabinet meeting on Wednesday, November 11. "There was no contract, so where is the question of withdrawing from it," he said, adding that the government had signed nothing with GNA and had decided against buying power for now. Both he and Joseph, he said, learned of the withdrawal from news reports.

He said an Electricity Department file had offered two options: power from the Himachal Pradesh government at about ₹14 per unit, or from GNA at about ₹8. The file noted that Himachal had already sold its power to Rajasthan by October 18, which left GNA as the only option. The Himachal route, he said, would have added **₹2,232 crore** to the burden.

The Chief Minister blamed the previous LDF government for relying for a decade on short-term purchases without a long-term agreement or battery storage. "Purchase of power from outside had become a culture," he said. He also said Vijayan had first argued for buying from Himachal and then said no purchase was needed.

## How the offer unfolded

According to reports, the Chief Minister announced after the Cabinet on **November 4** that the state would buy from GNA at ₹8.63 per unit, about 1,812 million units in all up to March 31, 2027. The Himachal offer stood at ₹14.50 per unit for about 884 million units. The former chairman of the Kerala State Electricity Board (KSEB), M G Rajamanickam, had recommended Himachal.

About a week later the GNA plan was dropped. Additional Chief Secretary (Power) Puneet Kumar and KSEB chairman N Prasanth were reported to have told the Chief Minister that there was no immediate crisis that called for costly short-term buying. Daily consumption has fallen to about 90 million units in October and November, from 110 to 118 million units in summer. Hydel generation has halved to 12 to 13 million units a day. Satheesan said the KSEB had earlier written to him warning of prolonged power cuts from November. He appeared to hold the transferred KSEB managing director responsible for the push to buy through GNA.

## Why it matters to consumers

The price gap, ₹8.63 against ₹14.50 a unit, is what the two sides are arguing over, and the state's cost of buying power is at stake. Satheesan said NTPC has promised power from its Sinnar plant in Maharashtra at **₹5.18 per unit** from April. KSEB sources have put that supply at 270 MW in December and January, rising to 540 MW once a second unit is commissioned in February. The same sources say a shortfall of about 600 MW a day would remain, and officials have warned of outages if demand picks up.

## What happens next

Satheesan has promised that the UDF government will present a **permanent solution by the end of November**, with more renewable generation (the state meets about 15% of its need from renewables and buys the rest), battery storage for daytime solar power, a long-term purchase agreement and buying at the cheapest available rates. Whether the government orders any inquiry is not known. Asked whether he would order a probe into the October note that warned of power cuts, the Chief Minister said "Let's see".
