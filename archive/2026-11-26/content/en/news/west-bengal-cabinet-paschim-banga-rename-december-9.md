The West Bengal cabinet on Monday cleared a proposal to **rename the state Paschim Banga**, and Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari said it will be put before the Assembly on December 9. If the House passes it, the proposal will be sent to the Centre for approval.

Adhikari, who chaired the cabinet meeting in Kolkata, told reporters: "The proposal to rename West Bengal as 'Paschim Banga' will be tabled in the House on December 9." He added that once the Assembly passes it, it "will be sent to the Centre for its approval". Some reports on the day spell the name Poschim Banga.

## What was decided

The decision is a **cabinet clearance of a proposal**, not a change of name. Nothing alters until the Assembly votes and Parliament acts. The Assembly's winter session runs from **December 4 to 14**, and the government has listed eight bills for it, among them one that would let shopkeepers keep their shops open round the clock if they choose.

No text of the proposal had been released by the evening. It is not yet clear whether it asks for Paschim Banga in all languages, as Bengal BJP president Samik Bhattacharya had said a bill would, or only in Bengali.

## Why the state wants it

Officials told reporters the idea of a new name for administrative purposes "has been around for a long time". The reason they gave is practical: at meetings the Union government holds with states, representatives speak in the English alphabetical order, so **West Bengal's representatives always spoke last**.

Bhattacharya, a Rajya Sabha member, raised the matter in Parliament on August 12 during the debate on the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, calling the present name a "colonial legacy". Minister of State for Home Affairs Nityanand Rai replied that the Centre could consider such a proposal if it reflected the state's heritage and the wishes of its people. Last month Bhattacharya said a bill to rename the state in all languages would soon be introduced.

## A name that has failed before

The state has tried to change its name before, and each time the effort ended without Central approval.

* **1999:** the Left Front government under Chief Minister Jyoti Basu first moved to rename the state Bangla. It stalled for lack of political consensus.
* **2011:** after the Trinamool Congress under Mamata Banerjee came to power, it proposed Bangla in Bengali, Bengal in English and Bangal in Hindi.
* **2018:** the Assembly **unanimously passed a resolution** accepting Bangla in all languages. The Union government did not approve it.

This time the proposal comes from a state government led by the same party that runs the Centre. No reaction from the Trinamool Congress, the Congress or the CPI(M) had been reported by the evening. Trinamool, which backed Bangla in 2018, is now in opposition.

## The route to a new name

Under **Article 3** of the Constitution, Parliament alters the name of a state by law. The Bill needs the President's recommendation, and the President first refers it to the state legislature for its views. The Kerala route shows the steps: the Assembly passed a resolution in June 2024, Parliament passed the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Act in August 2026 and the Home Ministry brought it into force on August 25. Read about [how Kerala became Keralam](/en/news/kerala-becomes-keralam-what-changes).

Bengal's Assembly vote on December 9 is therefore only the first step of a longer process.

## What happens next

The proposal is due in the Assembly on **December 9**, within a session that closes on December 14. If it passes, the state government will forward it to the Centre. For the new name to take legal effect, Parliament must then pass a law under Article 3, as it did for Kerala.

The government has already made one language decision this autumn: in October it made Bengali compulsory for all government work, as [we reported](/en/news/west-bengal-makes-bengali-compulsory-government-work).
