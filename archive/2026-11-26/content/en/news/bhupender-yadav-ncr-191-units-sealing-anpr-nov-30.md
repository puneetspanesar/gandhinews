Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav on Saturday, November 14, **ordered the Central Pollution Control Board to seal 191 industrial units** in the National Capital Region that have not even ordered air pollution control devices, and gave the four NCR states until November 30 to install number-plate cameras. The review covered Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan as the capital's air worsened this month. According to reports, the Air Quality Index crossed 400, the "severe" mark, on Friday.

Yadav told officials to **move beyond planning to "rigorous, time-bound and coordinated enforcement on the ground"**, according to the account of the meeting. He said air pollution is a regional problem that no state border contains. He added that more than 30 review meetings with NCR governments and municipal bodies have been held over the past year.

## What the minister ordered

The 191 units are industries that the CPCB identified as having failed to place orders for air pollution control devices (APCDs), **despite repeated directions**. The Board was told to seal them immediately. One report of the meeting gave the figure as 91; two others, including the one carrying the fullest account, say 191.

On vehicles, states must install **Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) cameras by November 30**. If they do not, the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) will begin criminal prosecution of Transport Department officials in the states that miss the date. The cameras at fuel stations are to be linked to the VAHAN vehicle database and the pollution-under-control certificate database, so that old and polluting vehicles can be flagged. The Municipal Corporation of Delhi must finish multi-lane free-flow tolling at 154 Delhi border entry points by December 31.

Pre-BS-VI trucks and buses are to be replaced with BS-VI, CNG or electric vehicles by 2028 under the PARIVARTAN scheme, and states were told to speed up registrations of owners who qualify. Immediate action was ordered at **233 congestion points** across the NCR, where slow traffic adds to emissions.

## Who answers for what

Yadav asked the states to name secretary-level officers as nodal officers for 15 cities: **seven in Haryana, four in Uttar Pradesh and four in Rajasthan**. They are to carry out field inspections and send weekly progress reports to CAQM, which will issue notices to officers who do not perform. The state environment ministers who joined the meeting virtually included Manjinder Singh Sirsa of Delhi, Rao Narbir Singh of Haryana and Arun Kumar Saxena of Uttar Pradesh.

States were also asked to adopt Delhi's hotspot-based winter action plan, with plans for markets, schools, hospitals, railway stations and bus terminals, and to apply the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) strictly. Real-time monitoring will run through Integrated Command and Control Centres and CAQM's PAVAN platform.

## Industry, roads and waste

Of 2,345 industries targeted for Online Continuous Emission Monitoring Systems (OCEMS), **2,298, or 98%, are now connected**. One report says the remaining 47 have been closed or sealed. Yadav insisted that the particulate matter limit of 50 mg per normal cubic metre be enforced, with closure, power disconnection or sealing for units that exceed it. State pollution control boards were asked to help small units get funding for control devices under the scheme for the NCR.

Road dust remains a drag on the numbers. Only **3,221 km of the 5,480 km** of roads targeted for redevelopment have been completed, and agencies were told to repair potholes, pave road shoulders and central verges, run mechanical sweepers in double shifts with digital tracking, and step up deep-cleaning drives. More than 3,000 active construction and demolition sites need physical inspection, according to the account of the meeting.

On waste, the states must close a **processing gap of 6,747 tonnes a day**, stop open dumping and burning, and clear legacy waste at Okhla, Ghazipur, Bhalswa and Bandhwari.

## How we got here

The Centre has been tightening the NCR enforcement timetable through the year. At a review on December 3, 2025, industries that had not installed real-time emission monitors by December 31 were warned of closure, and the CPCB chairperson said then that strict action would follow. On May 27, 2026, Yadav met Delhi Chief Minister Rekha Gupta and asked for road redevelopment to be finished by October 2026 and ANPR cameras to be in place at all Delhi border entry points by September 2026.

This month the capital's air has deteriorated again. Our earlier reports on [the GRAP stage three order](/en/news/delhi-grap-stage-three-aqi-382-caqm) and on [Yadav's talks with start-ups on pollution solutions](/en/news/bhupender-yadav-startups-delhi-air-pollution-solutions) trace the run-up.

## Why it matters to residents

The orders are aimed at the sources that residents of Delhi, Gurugram, Noida, Ghaziabad and Faridabad cannot control themselves: factory chimneys, old diesel trucks and buses, dust from broken roads and burning waste. The prosecution threat against Transport Department officials puts personal liability on those who run the vehicle databases that the cameras will feed. If the cameras work as described, a vehicle without a valid pollution certificate can be flagged at a fuel station.

## What happens next

The first test is **November 30**, the date by which the states must have the cameras installed and after which CAQM can start prosecutions. The nodal officers' weekly reports begin now, and the Delhi toll systems at the border points are due on December 31. Yadav and the state environment ministers have said they will conduct regular field inspections.
