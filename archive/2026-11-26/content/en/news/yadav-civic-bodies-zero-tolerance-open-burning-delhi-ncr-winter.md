Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav on Thursday told the civic bodies of Delhi, Ghaziabad, Noida, Gurugram and Faridabad to adopt **zero tolerance for open burning** this winter and to hold officers accountable when enforcement fails. He reviewed the readiness of their Winter Action Plans for Delhi-NCR pollution control, joining the meeting virtually from COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye.

Minister of State Kirti Vardhan Singh, MoEFCC Secretary Narendra Bhooshan, Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) Chairperson Rajesh Verma and Central Pollution Control Board Chairperson Amandeep Garg were present. So were D Thara, Secretary in the Department of Capital Development at the housing and urban affairs ministry, the mayors and commissioners of the municipal corporations, the CEO of the Noida Authority and senior state officials.

## What the minister asked for

CAQM Member Secretary Tarun Pithode presented progress reports from the NCR cities, and Verma reviewed what had been done on the ground and what was still pending. Yadav's directions followed.

On burning, municipal corporations are to **penalise violators promptly** for setting fire to municipal waste, dry leaves and biomass. Every ward is to have a quick response team that resolves complaints within hours. Officials are to watch grievances in real time and **close them only after physical verification**, not on a message from the field. Each civic body is to open WhatsApp groups with named nodal officers for complaints, and mayors are to hold regular ward-level reviews.

On road dust, Yadav asked for faster repairs and a quick expansion of mechanical road-sweeping capacity. The machines are to **run in double shifts** through winter, tracked by GPS and supervised from the Intelligent Command and Control Centres. Dust, silt and construction debris are to be cleared from roadsides every day, and Gurugram was asked to green open spaces to bring down PM10.

At construction sites, civic bodies are to enforce dust norms strictly, with working wheel-washing facilities, frequent inspections and closure of sites that do not comply. The target set out was "No Visible Dust" around building work.

On waste, the minister asked for better daily processing and faster remediation of the legacy dumps at Bhalswa and Okhla. Civic bodies were asked to identify bulk waste generators under the Solid Waste Management Rules, as Delhi has done.

## Hotspots and GRAP

Each civic body is to appoint a dedicated nodal officer for pollution hotspots and prepare a hotspot-specific plan that mayors and officers review regularly. Yadav said every measure under the current stage of the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) must be carried out **without compromise**. GRAP is the set of graded emergency steps that apply as air quality worsens; Delhi has been under stage II curbs, as in our report on [Jahangirpuri's 499 reading](/news/delhi-aqi-331-jahangirpuri-499-grap-stage-two).

District collectors are to review municipal performance regularly and order corrective action where needed. The secretaries of the environment and housing ministries, together with the CAQM and CPCB chairpersons, are to review municipal performance across NCR.

Yadav asked that governance be citizen-centric and facilitative, without causing unnecessary public alarm, and said the season needs consistent, visible action in every ward. Civic bodies were also asked to run intensive awareness campaigns with all stakeholders.

## Models to copy

The meeting held up local initiatives as examples for the others: the Malba App of the Municipal Corporation of Delhi for construction waste, Faridabad's Pothole Ambulance, Gurugram's survey-based identification of sanitation workers, and a shareable construction and demolition waste portal run by the Ghaziabad civic body.

## How we got here

Five days earlier, on November 14, Yadav chaired a review with the Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan governments. According to the PIB release, he asked them to move from planning to **time-bound enforcement**, set a November 30 deadline for number-plate cameras at fuel stations, and told CPCB to seal 191 industries that had not ordered pollution control devices. Only 3,221 km of a target of 5,480 km of roads had been redeveloped, and 233 congestion points were flagged. States also had a gap of 6,747 tonnes a day in municipal waste processing to close.

Thursday's meeting took that pressure to the bodies that actually sweep roads and clear waste. In October the minister had asked start-ups for [cheap, scalable fixes](/news/bhupender-yadav-startups-delhi-air-pollution-solutions), and the air has stayed poor to severe on many mornings this month.

## What it means for residents

For a Delhi-NCR resident, the practical change is in complaints. A report about a burning pile of leaves or a dusty site is meant to reach a ward team and be resolved within hours, and it should not be marked closed until someone has visited. The reports of the meeting carry no penalty amounts or AQI targets, so the strength of the promise will show in how quickly civic bodies publish closure figures.

## What happens next

The directions put the secretaries of two ministries and the heads of CAQM and CPCB in charge of regular reviews of municipal performance. The next hard date on the record is **November 30**, the deadline set on November 14 for ANPR cameras, after which CAQM has said it may begin prosecuting transport officials. No date was reported for a follow-up meeting with the civic bodies.
