The US Trade Representative on November 25, 2026, **proposed an additional tariff of up to 25% on all goods of India**, after finding that Indian industrial policies are unreasonable and burden American commerce. India is one of 14 economies named in the same set of findings under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, which cover what Washington calls "structural excess capacity" in manufacturing.

At 10:48 pm that night, India's Department of Commerce rejected the premise. "**India does not have structural excess capacity in any sector**," its statement said. Nothing has taken effect: the US notices only open a comment period, and no start date for any tariff has been given.

## What the US notice proposes

For India the US proposes a tariff of **up to 25%**, applied "net of existing Section 301 duties" but not net of ordinary customs duties. The rate would come down to **17% if India reaches a trade agreement** that responds to the findings. Unlike for some other economies, trade lawyers who read the notices note that India has no higher alternative rate, so 25% is a ceiling.

USTR lists five grounds against India: **industrial plans to expand manufacturing**, inadequate labour protections, financial repression, market access barriers, and a failure to address global excess capacity. It also raises economic security concerns about reliance on inputs from non-market economies. It names **pharmaceuticals and medical devices, steel, solar and information and communications technology** as sectors where the effects are especially strong.

Some goods would be left out. Articles already under Section 232 duties, which include steel, aluminium and copper products, passenger vehicles and parts, wood products, patented pharmaceuticals and semiconductors, would stay under those duties and not face the new one. Informational materials, donations, accompanied baggage and products on an annex list are also excluded. The India notice further asks whether a textile mechanism should let a fixed volume of apparel and textile imports enter at a reduced rate.

## How the 25% would work with the existing duty

Indian goods already pay a **10% Section 301 duty**, imposed from July 24, 2026, after a separate forced-labour investigation. The notices do not spell out how the "net of" calculation works. If it follows the example law firms give for Bangladesh, a 10% duty already paid plus an extra 8% to reach an 18% ceiling, then a product paying the July duty could face roughly **15 more percentage points**. That is a reading of the notices, not a figure USTR has stated.

The finding follows an investigation launched on March 11, 2026, covering 16 economies. USTR heard nearly 140 witnesses and received about 1,070 comments. It made findings against 14: India, China, the European Union, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland, Taiwan, Singapore, Vietnam, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand. Mexico and Norway have no determination yet. Proposed rates for the others range from 15% for the EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan up to 35% for Vietnam.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer has said that several trading partners pursued industrial strategies built on manufacturing targets, government support and wage suppression, and that talks in the G7, the G20 and the World Trade Organization had not addressed it, which led the administration to consider trade action.

## What India said

The Commerce Department said it had engaged constructively in the investigation and that the notice is **under examination**. It said trade concerns should be settled inside the bilateral trade agreement talks that India and the US announced on February 2, 2026, and backed with a joint statement on February 7, and that India wants them concluded early. It noted that USTR has sought comments before any final decision.

India's position is a legal and political one: it does not accept the finding, and it wants the matter handled at the negotiating table rather than through tariffs. Our [fact check](/en/factcheck/commerce-ministry-no-structural-excess-capacity-any-sector-claim) of the "no excess capacity in any sector" line found that Ministry of Steel data fit it for steel but that solar module capacity of about 172 GW is nearly four times the 44.61 GW installed in 2025-26.

## How we got here

India and the US announced a trade framework in February 2026 that would have cut the US reciprocal tariff on India to 18%. It never took effect: on February 20 the US Supreme Court struck down the emergency-powers tariffs, and Washington used a temporary 10% duty under Section 122 from February 24. The forced-labour Section 301 duty followed on July 24. On October 5 Sitharaman said the talks had reached a plateau, as we reported in [our October 11 story](/en/news/india-us-trade-talks-plateau-russia-oil-tariff-law). On November 14, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar said the deal was in its [last stretch](/en/news/jaishankar-htls-india-us-trade-deal-last-stretch-national-interest).

## Why it matters

The United States is India's largest export market. Indian goods exports to the US were $42.79 billion between April and August 2026, up from $40.39 billion a year earlier, according to figures cited in our earlier report. A further duty on all goods, not on a short list, would reach every sector, including the pharmaceuticals and medical devices, steel, solar and ICT that USTR names, and the textiles it asks about. Exporters will also watch the 17% rate, which links the size of any tariff to whether a deal is signed.

## What happens next

Written comments and requests to appear at the hearing are due on **January 5, 2027**. Each economy has its own comment docket, and USTR asks which products should stay or go, whether rates should be adjusted for existing Section 301 and Section 232 duties, and whether any tariff line would cause serious dislocation. The public hearing opens at 10 am on **January 25, 2027** at the US International Trade Commission in Washington and may run on later days.

USTR has to hear interested parties before it decides, so no tariff can take effect before the hearings. Trade lawyers expect final action in early 2027 and not before late January. Any final notice is expected to include rules for goods already at sea. Meanwhile, the answer to whether India signs a trade agreement first will decide whether its rate is 25% or 17%.
