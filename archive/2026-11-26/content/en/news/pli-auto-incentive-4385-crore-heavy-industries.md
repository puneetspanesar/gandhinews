The Ministry of Heavy Industries said on Wednesday that **₹4,385.28 crore has been paid out in 2026-27** under the production linked incentive scheme for the automobile and auto component industry, counted up to November 25, 2026. Heavy Industries and Steel Minister H D Kumaraswamy reviewed the scheme that day with Additional Secretary Dr Haneef Qureshi, according to the release issued through the Press Information Bureau at 3:56 pm.

The release adds a running total that earlier ministry statements make easy to check: **₹6,771.64 crore has been paid since the scheme began**. That is about 26% of the scheme's ₹25,938 crore outlay, with 2026-27 accounting for roughly 65% of everything paid so far.

## What the release says

The scheme, known as PLI Auto, pays approved makers for vehicles and parts that count as advanced automotive technology products. To qualify, a product must carry at least 50% domestic value addition. By November 25, domestic value addition certificates had been issued to **9 "champion" vehicle makers for 139 products or variants**, and to 10 component champions for 53 products or variants.

The ministry's other figures, all reported by approved applicants and counted to September 30, 2026:

Investment is about 24% above its target. The release names no company and does not say how many firms were paid this year.

On payouts, the December 2025 statement put the total at ₹2,321.94 crore. Subtracting this year's ₹4,385.28 crore from today's ₹6,771.64 crore leaves **₹2,386.36 crore paid before 2026-27**, so this year's payout is already about 1.8 times everything paid in the earlier years put together.

## Who has been paid

The scheme has **82 approved applicants**, according to the ministry's statement of December 9, 2025, among them Tata Motors, Mahindra and Mahindra, Bajaj Auto, Hero MotoCorp, Hyundai, Kia, Maruti Suzuki, Ashok Leyland, Bosch and Bharat Forge. Only a few had been paid by then. The December 31 statement lists five makers paid for 2025-26 sales: Bajaj Auto (₹625.65 crore), Tata Motors (₹403.18 crore), Ola Electric (₹366.78 crore), TVS Motor (₹320.51 crore) and Mahindra and Mahindra (₹283.82 crore). Those incentives covered more than 13.6 lakh electric vehicles, including 10.42 lakh two-wheelers, 2.38 lakh three-wheelers, 79,540 four-wheelers and 1,391 buses.

The November 25 release does not say whether the same five, or more firms, account for this year's ₹4,385.28 crore. Electric two-wheelers made up three-quarters of those vehicles; the market is covered in our report on [October registrations](/en/news/electric-two-wheeler-registrations-october-2026-tvs-leads-48-per-cent).

## What happens next

The scheme's performance period runs for five years, from 2023-24 to **2027-28**, and the ministry's sales target is ₹2.315 lakh crore over the base year by March 2028. Reported sales of ₹82,993 crore are about 36% of that, so the final year has a long way to go. Payments follow verification of each year's claims: sales for 2023-24 were paid in 2024-25, for instance, so claims for the last performance year, 2027-28, would be settled after it ends.

The release gives no date for the next payment round. Whether the remaining ₹19,166 crore of the outlay is used depends on those claims and on the DVA certificates still to be issued.

The same ministry's other big push is the ₹11,000 crore scheme for heavy construction equipment that the Cabinet cleared the same day, which we report [here](/en/news/cabinet-cie-manufacturing-scheme-11000-crore-heavy-industries).
