The **49th ASEAN Summit opened in Manila** on Sunday, November 15, with Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. telling regional leaders that the bloc must act together on geopolitical tension, economic uncertainty and security risks. He welcomed delegates at the Philippine International Convention Center and gave the inaugural address that opened the plenary and the Leaders' Meeting.

The three-day meeting runs to November 17. It is the Philippines' last big moment as ASEAN chair this year, because the chairmanship passes to **Singapore** when the summit closes. India joins on Tuesday: Prime Minister Narendra Modi flies in on Monday for a dinner hosted by Mr Marcos, and attends the 23rd ASEAN-India Summit and the 21st East Asia Summit on November 17.

## Marcos's call for unity

Mr Marcos asked member states to strengthen cooperation to protect peace, stability and prosperity in Southeast Asia, and said the bloc must work together on emerging regional and global challenges. The agenda covers regional security, economic cooperation, maritime affairs and connectivity.

Foreign Affairs Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro was candid about how the year had gone. The chairship, she said, faced **"unprecedented headwinds"**, in particular the Middle East crisis and natural calamities. "We remain steadfast, pivoting our course as rough tides demand," she added.

## Leaders on the move

Manila has filled up since Thursday. Brazil's President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva arrived on Friday and signed agreements with Mr Marcos on livestock, agriculture and competition law. On Saturday came Canada's Prime Minister Mark Carney, who with Mr Marcos endorsed a **joint framework towards a Philippines-Canada free trade agreement by 2027**, along with Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, whose visit aims to deepen defence ties.

Also in the city are UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres, Indonesia's President Prabowo Subianto, Malaysia's Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, Thailand's Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul, Singapore's Prime Minister Lawrence Wong, Brunei's Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah, Vietnam's Prime Minister Le Minh Hung and Timor-Leste's Prime Minister Xanana Gusmao. Myanmar, kept away from leaders' meetings since the civil war began in 2021, is represented by its foreign minister, Han Win Aung, who arrived on November 12.

## Washington, Moscow and Beijing's orbit

The second half of the week brings the big powers. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov and US President Donald Trump are due on Tuesday, with Mr Trump scheduled for the ASEAN-US special summit before he goes on to China. The Philippine foreign ministry says Mr Marcos will hold bilateral meetings with Australia, Brazil, Japan and South Korea. Talks with India and Laos, and a meeting with Mr Guterres, were still being finalised. Mr Marcos then attends APEC in China on November 18 and 19.

## What India wants from Manila

India has framed the trip around the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership with ASEAN, which the government reviews through the **ASEAN-India Plan of Action 2026 to 2030**. This is the ASEAN-India Year of Maritime Cooperation, so maritime security leads, with trade and investment next. India's trade with the bloc is about $122 billion, and the long-running review of the 2011 goods agreement is still open, as we explained in [our preview of the trip](/en/news/modi-asean-india-summit-manila-november-16-18).

There is also the question of whether a Quad leaders' meeting is added, which US officials say they are working on and New Delhi has not confirmed. We cover that in [our report on the Quad track](/en/news/modi-manila-quad-leaders-summit-dylan-johnson-modi-leaves).

## What to watch

Tuesday is the day that matters. The handover to Singapore will close the Philippine year, and the outcome statements of the ASEAN-India and East Asia Summits will show how far leaders went on the Middle East crisis and on maritime security. Mr Marcos's own bilateral with Mr Modi, if it is confirmed would follow Mr Marcos's state visit to India in August 2025, when the two leaders held talks in New Delhi.
