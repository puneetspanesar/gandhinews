The Supreme Court on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, **asked the Election Commission to reply** to the INDIA bloc's application for leave to put 48 written questions to it on how the nationwide Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls was decided. It did not allow or refuse the application. A bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana said it would hear the application together with the SIR petitions on **December 9**.

Senior advocate Kapil Sibal, appearing for Rahul Gandhi and the other opposition petitioners, told the court the application had been filed the day before. He argued that the Commission's October 26 counter-affidavit relies on claims of unanimous decisions and approvals **without giving dates, file notings or records**. Senior advocate Harish Salve, for the Commission, said it had "serious reservations" about the application and told the bench: "We have something to say about that. There is very serious prejudice."

The Chief Justice allowed the Commission to answer in writing. "Whatever it is, you may file your reply. You can raise your objections," he said, and added: "We will examine the interrogatories issue and then take a call." Sibal had asked that, in the meantime, the Commission respond to the application.

## What the court has and has not decided

The order is procedural. The court has **not ruled on whether the questions can be served**, and it has expressed no view on the merits of the petition. The Commission now has until the December 9 hearing to file its objections; the bench will first hear the petitioners on the interrogatories and then pass orders. If it allows them, the Commission would have to answer on oath through its commissioners and its Director General (IT). If it refuses, the petitioners are left with the original prayer for the Commission's files and minutes.

## What the application asks

The application was filed through advocate-on-record Prasanna S under **Order V Rule 1, Order XXXVIII Rule 11(9) and Order LV Rule 6** of the Supreme Court Rules, 2013, and leans on the Constitution Bench ruling in the Puttaswamy case. It asks Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi to say on oath whether they approved the Commission's affidavit, and asks the Director General (IT) to answer technical questions about the Commission's digital systems. According to reports, the 48 questions fall into about ten groups. Among them:

- **How the affidavit was approved.** Whether the three commissioners approved the counter-affidavit filed by Deputy Election Commissioner Bhanu Prakash Yeturu, how, and on what dates.
- **Objections.** How many written objections, dissents or reservations the two other commissioners recorded between February 19, 2025, and October 26, 2026, on SIR, ECINET, Form 6 and appeals.
- **The orders themselves.** File numbers, dates and manner of approval for the SIR orders of **June 24, 2025, October 27, 2025, and May 14, 2026**, which the Commission says were unanimous.
- **Form 6.** Who introduced a new declaration on the online Form 6 page, and how many applications were rejected, kept pending or left unprocessed because of it.
- **ECINET and ERONET.** Whether anyone could change an electoral-roll entry other than on an Electoral Registration Officer's order, and, in the petitioners' words, "whether private contractors or vendors had write access to the electoral-roll database".
- **Approvals by phone.** "Whether urgent ECI approvals were obtained via WhatsApp or personal email."

The petitioners also point to an apparent conflict between the Commission's affidavit and an order of its First Appellate Authority under the RTI Act, which reportedly said no decision to begin a nationwide SIR had been taken, and ask whether a decision file existed when its public information officer said on August 27, 2025, that no such information was available.

## What the Commission has said

In its counter-affidavit of October 26, a 248-page response, the Commission said its three SIR orders were approved unanimously by all three members, that the Chief Election Commissioner did not act alone, and that the filing itself was "approved for filing before this Hon'ble Court by a unanimous decision of the three members of the Election Commission". It said the extra declaration used during SIR is a separate document, not an amendment to Form 6, and called the attempt to cast aspersions on the Commission "unfortunate". A supplementary affidavit followed on November 14 (see our report on [how excluded voters can return](/en/news/ec-supplementary-affidavit-sc-excluded-voters-restore-sir)).

## How we got here

The opposition's case grew out of a September 25 newspaper report that Sandhu and Joshi had recorded objections at least 14 times in 10 months to SIR-related decisions. The Commission replied that written notes and differing views are routine and its decisions were unanimous; we reported it in [the dissent report and the push to remove the CEC](/en/news/election-commission-dissent-report-move-to-remove-cec).

After a march towards the court on October 12, Gandhi, Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge, Akhilesh Yadav, Tejashwi Yadav, Saugata Roy, Sanjay Raut, M A Baby, D Raja, Supriya Sule and other leaders filed the petition. It asks for the Commission's files and minutes, an audit of deletions by a committee headed by a retired Supreme Court judge, and booth-wise publication of the voters deleted, with reasons. On October 15 the Chief Justice's bench issued notice to the Commission and all three commissioners, as we reported in [the court taking up the petition](/en/news/supreme-court-notice-cec-rahul-gandhi-sir-plea-conduct). VCK president Thol Thirumavalavan and Sajjat Ahmad Kichloo later joined as petitioners.

## Why it matters

The dispute is over whether the Commission's claim of unanimity can be tested against dated files. The SIR has already struck names off the rolls in several states, and the questions on Form 6 and deletions concern what a voter faces: whether a dropped name can be restored, and on whose order it was removed. By seeking the Commission's reply first, the bench has kept both routes open: sworn answers from the commissioners, or the disclosure of records the petition already seeks.

## What happens next

The Commission files its objections before **December 9**, when the court will hear the SIR petitions together with the application. The bench has said it will hear the petitioners on the interrogatories and then decide.
