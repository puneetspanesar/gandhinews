Uttar Pradesh Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath on Sunday **launched the distribution of 25 lakh free tablets** to students and young people from Lucknow, the next phase of the state's Swami Vivekananda Yuva Sashaktikaran Yojana. The first handover took place at Rashtra Prerna Sthal in the capital.

In a post on X, Adityanath said more than 60 lakh young people have already received smartphones or tablets under the scheme and that the new round would reach more. He said the aim is that a lack of resources should not stand in the way of young people's talent, and described the programme as advancing the vision of Digital India.

## Who gets a tablet

About **50,000 students from around 300 institutions** in Lucknow, Hardoi, Sitapur and Unnao were expected at the opening event. The rest of the tablets are to reach students across the state during 2026-27, according to the state's plan. The scheme is run by the Infrastructure and Industrial Development Department, and Akanksha Rana, Special Secretary in the IT and Electronics Department and managing director of the state electronics development corporation UPDESCO, said distribution would begin on November 15. The state cabinet cleared the purchase in August 2026, and devices have been moving to districts since.

There is **no income or age limit**. Students at institutions affiliated to Uttar Pradesh universities, boards, societies or councils qualify, as do students at central universities in the state. The list covers diploma, undergraduate, postgraduate, technical and vocational courses, including medical, engineering, management, nursing, pharmacy and paramedical programmes, and ITI trainees. Persons with disabilities, skilled workers registered on the Seva Mitra portal and trainees under MSME schemes such as the Vishwakarma Shram Samman Yojana and One District One Product are also eligible.

The tablets come with three learning platforms installed through mobile device management software: Infosys Springboard, Adhyayan and Yuva Sathi. Together they give access to about **18,000 Infosys courses**, study material, digital libraries and job-related information.

## How we got here

The scheme began on December 25, 2021, a few weeks before the 2022 Assembly election, when the state started handing out smartphones and tablets to students. Adityanath said on Sunday that the total has now crossed 60 lakh young people.

A study by the Indian Institute of Management Lucknow, covering **4,414 student beneficiaries across 51 districts** and 11 departments, put the count at about 59.83 lakh devices as of April 28, 2026: 37.19 lakh smartphones and 22.64 lakh tablets. Tablet recipients scored significantly higher than smartphone recipients on learning, aspirations, satisfaction and leadership, and the researchers recommended a larger share of tablets, which is the shape of the new phase.

The same study sounded a note of caution. Higher data use was not strongly linked to enrolment in online courses. The researchers called for curriculum-aligned content, mentoring by teachers and modules on digital well-being, so that a device in the hand turns into study time.

## What the Chief Minister said

Adityanath tied the programme to the Centre as well as the state. He credited **Prime Minister Narendra Modi's leadership** and what he called the double-engine government for speeding up digital empowerment. He also said that earlier governments in Uttar Pradesh had kept young people entangled in the politics of caste, region and family, and had not given them the technology to build their futures. He did not name any party.

He wished the beneficiaries well and said he was confident that access to technology would help them create their own opportunities.

## Why it matters

For a student from a family that cannot easily spend several thousand rupees on a device, a tablet is the difference between attending an online class or a coaching video and missing it. The state has said the devices are meant for competitive-exam preparation and online courses, and the preloaded content makes that the default use rather than an afterthought.

The timing is also political. Uttar Pradesh goes to the polls in 2027, and the scheme has been a standing part of the BJP government's pitch to young voters since its launch. No cost figure for the 25 lakh tablets, and no device specification, was given in the reports available on Sunday evening. The opposition had not responded by 6 pm.

## What happens next

Distribution will continue through 2026-27 after the Lucknow launch. The state has not said how many tablets each district will get or by when the full 25 lakh will have been handed out, and those dates are the next thing to watch.
