India defended a modest **209 all out** to beat New Zealand by 22 runs in the fifth and final one-day international at Bay Oval, Mount Maunganui, on Sunday, November 15, 2026, and won the five-match series 3-1. Left-arm seamer **Arshdeep Singh took 3 for 30** and was named Player of the Match, two days after India were bowled out for 89 in a 244-run defeat.

The win, according to reports, is India's first ODI series victory in New Zealand since 2019, and the first overseas ODI series win of Gautam Gambhir's time as head coach.

## A shaky start with the bat

Mitchell Santner won the toss for New Zealand and chose to bowl. The decision looked right early: India lost **Rohit Sharma for 7, Virat Kohli for 5 and Ruturaj Gaikwad for 18** to be 34 for 3 in the powerplay. Matthew Fisher took both Rohit and Kohli, according to reports, so the top order that had gone for a combined collapse on Friday failed again.

Captain Shreyas Iyer made 22 and put on 52 with Axar Patel, who scored 46. Ishan Kishan, who kept wicket, also made **46 off 58 balls**, and Naman Dhir added 22 off 31. Santner and Jacob Duffy took three wickets each for New Zealand, and Fisher two.

The tail then made the difference. Arshdeep, batting at No. 11, hit **14 off four balls including two sixes**, and with Mohammed Siraj, who finished unbeaten on 14, he added more than 25 runs for the last wicket in 10 balls. India were all out in 42.1 overs, which gave the bowlers 209 to defend.

## How the chase fell away

Siraj bowled Finn Allen for 1 early and India held the initiative from there, though Kohli dropped a chance at second slip off Arshdeep in the opening overs. Arshdeep then removed **Will Young, Nick Kelly and Kristian Clarke**. Gaikwad took a diving catch in the deep to get rid of Young, who made 22 and had been New Zealand's most dangerous batter in the fourth ODI.

Yash Thakur, on his ODI debut, bowled Dean Foxcroft, the home side's top scorer with 31 off 42 balls. Dhir had Santner caught for 24 and Auqib Nabi also took a wicket. Axar had Duffy caught by Kohli at long-on, and then Kishan stumped Blair Tickner off Axar to end the innings.

New Zealand were bowled out for **187 in 48.1 overs**. No batter passed 31, and Kelly, Santner and Young were all between 22 and 24. Arshdeep's 10 overs included two maidens, according to one report.

## What Arshdeep said

Speaking after the match, Arshdeep said the plan with Siraj at the end was to push past 200 so that the bowlers had something to bowl at. "We were disciplined in our bowling even when we weren't getting wickets," he said. On batting at No. 11, he joked: "Love batting at No. 11."

## Why the result matters

India went into the match 2-1 up, so a New Zealand win would have levelled the series at 2-2. The collapse in the fourth ODI, India's second-biggest defeat by runs in the format, had raised questions about Iyer's first stint as ODI captain. Our earlier report on [the 89 all out and the finale](/en/news/india-nz-fifth-odi-henry-released-india-89-all-out-series-finale) set out the stakes.

For New Zealand, the loss ends a long run of home ODI series without defeat, according to one report. Matt Henry, whose 6 for 27 won the fourth ODI, had already left for the Test squad and missed the decider.

## What happens next

The two teams now turn to Tests. India's squad, with Rishabh Pant fit again and [Gurnoor Brar added](/en/news/india-test-squad-new-zealand-pant-joins-kuldeep-released-brar-added), is in Wellington for the first Test at the Basin Reserve, which begins on November 19, 2026. Several players from both ODI sides will leave to join their Test camps over the next days.
