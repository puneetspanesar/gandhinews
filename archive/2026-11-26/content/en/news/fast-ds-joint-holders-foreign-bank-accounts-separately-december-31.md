The window for the Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers Disclosure Scheme, FAST-DS 2026, closes on **December 31, 2026**, 40 days from today, and the Income Tax Department's FAQs say plainly that **no declaration can be filed after that date**. The one-time scheme lets taxpayers regularise small foreign assets and foreign income they never reported, at a fixed price and with protection from prosecution under the Black Money Act.

The price depends on the category. In **Category A**, for assets bought with undisclosed income or foreign income on which tax was not paid, the declarant pays tax of 30% of the asset value plus an additional amount equal to that tax, so 60% in all. It is open only where the total value of the assets does not exceed ₹1 crore. On ₹80 lakh of assets, for instance, the bill would be ₹48 lakh.

In **Category B**, for assets bought out of income already taxed in India but left out of the return schedule, or acquired while the person was a non-resident, the fee is a flat ₹1 lakh, provided the total value of the assets does not exceed ₹5 crore. Above those limits the scheme is not available.

## What a declaration buys

FAST-DS is a voluntary scheme created by Chapter IV of the Finance Act, 2026. A valid declaration with payment gives protection from further tax, penalty and prosecution under the Black Money Act, 2015, for the income or asset declared. The scheme does not cover proceeds of crime where proceedings under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act are pending or have begun. **Immunity covers only what is declared**, so an asset left out stays exposed.

Valuation is fixed as on March 31, 2026. For foreign bank accounts the department's FAQs say the value is "the sum of all deposits made into the account from the date it was opened up to the valuation date," not the balance on a given day, and deposits that came from withdrawals of the same account are left out. Foreign currency is converted at the RBI reference rate on the valuation date, or through US dollars for currencies without one.

## How we got here

The scheme was announced in Budget 2026-27 and written into the Finance Act, 2026. The Central Board of Direct Taxes notified the rules, and **the scheme opened on August 16, 2026**. The FAQs set the sequence after filing: Form 2, the order fixing the amount payable, is issued within a month of the end of the month of declaration; payment is due within two months of the end of the month in which it is received; and a further delay of up to two months is allowed with simple interest of 1% a month. Beyond four months from the end of the month of the Form 2 order the benefit is lost, and a declaration on which payment is not made in time is treated as void.

## Joint accounts, according to reports

According to reports of fresh clarifications from the department on November 20, **every joint holder of a foreign bank account must disclose it separately**, even if only one of them put the money in, with the value split by share or equally where shares are not stated. The same reports say a declarant can revise Form 1 only until Form 2 is issued, that foreign currency is to be converted at ₹94.6543 per US dollar, and that a variance of up to 20% is allowed for some assets, such as immovable property, with a valuer's report, but not for bank accounts. We could not find the text of these supplementary answers on the department's pages, so treat them as reported.

Amit Agarwal, senior partner at Nangia & Co LLP, said the clarifications bring "much-needed certainty" on joint ownership, valuation and procedure. In his reading, foreign bank accounts whose combined balances did not exceed ₹20 lakh at any time in the relevant year, and whose funds came from disclosed sources, remain protected and may not need declaring. That is an adviser's interpretation, not a statement of the department. He added: "A complete reconciliation of overseas holdings is essential before filing."

## Why it matters

For families that hold an account abroad in joint names, such as spouses or parents and children, the practical effect, if the reports are right, is that each name needs its own declaration and its own share of the value. Because the payment timelines run from the month of declaration, a taxpayer who files in the last week leaves little room if Form 2 or payment goes wrong.

## What happens next

The deadline is fixed: **December 31, 2026**. The department's FAQ pages and the e-filing portal carry the forms and the full set of answers.
