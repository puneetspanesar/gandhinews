At least **16 people have died** and more than 370 have been admitted to hospitals in Imphal after drinking suspected spurious liquor in Manipur's Chandel and Kakching districts, officials said on Saturday, November 21, 2026. The deaths began on November 15 and the number of patients is still rising. Doctors suspect methanol poisoning, but the cause has not been medically confirmed.

An official of the Chandel district health office said the toll had **risen to 16 as of 1 pm** on Saturday. The Anal Naga Union (ANU), a community body, put it at 14 on Saturday, up from 13 on Friday. The count of deaths between November 15 and 20, as listed by officials, is nine in Chandel district and seven in Sugnu, Kakching district.

## Where the deaths happened

In Chandel, the dead come from villages around Chakpikarong, including Nungsai, Khambathel, Julbangching, Dangkhu and Nungpam. **Three more deaths were recorded on November 20**. The first reported victim, a 50-year-old man from Nungpan village, died on Wednesday night, according to officials. Some died at home, some at the Community Health Centre in Chakpikarong and some on the way to a bigger hospital.

In Kakching district, seven people died in the Sugnu area between November 15 and 19. One was brought dead to the Sugnu health centre; six died there or while being referred.

## Hospitals fill up

Patients are being treated at Shija Hospitals & Research Institute, Babina Multispecialty Hospital in Khabeisoi, JNIMS, RIMS and Jeevan Hospital in Imphal. Shija Hospitals said it had attended **over 300 patients**, several of them critical and in the intensive care unit. About 60 were at Babina, where a late-evening bulletin on Friday said all were stable and under observation. Some patients are on haemodialysis, Shija's joint managing director Chetan K said.

Officials gave a figure of 346 admitted as of Friday night; later reports put it above 370. The ANU said more than 600 people had been admitted by Friday and that medical findings shared with it pointed to **methanol poisoning in 70 to 80 per cent** of cases. The union's number is higher than the official one, and the two have not been reconciled. Patients report vomiting, stomach pain, headache, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain and difficulty breathing.

## Where the liquor came from

Chandel Superintendent of Police Dharmendra Kumar told the Deputy Commissioner, Mamoni Doley, that the liquor consumed by some of the dead was **bought at Sugnu** in Kakching district and was not brewed in Chandel. Police have ruled out known local brewing of traditional liquor in Chandel, and cases have been registered at Chakpikarong and Chandel police stations to trace the supply and distribution chain. Five of the first eight deaths in Chandel fell under Sugnu police station's jurisdiction, according to the Chandel police.

A joint drive by Kakching Police and Food Safety officials seized locally brewed liquor from seven places, and samples were sent for laboratory testing. No arrests had been reported when this story was written. "The exact cause of the deaths is still under medical and scientific investigation," the district administration said.

## What the government said

Chief Minister Y Khemchand Singh visited Shija and Babina hospitals on Friday, reviewed treatment and asked the hospitals to give the affected **the best possible treatment and assistance**. He said the toll had touched 11 in the previous 48 hours, a figure since overtaken. "The government will conduct an enquiry, and the culprits will be punished," he said.

The Chandel district administration formed a team for coordinated monitoring, deployed four rapid response teams with medicines and oxygen, and sent a medical team led by a general medicine specialist. The administrations of Bishnupur, Senapati, Kakching and Thoubal issued advisories urging people not to drink locally brewed liquor or drinks of doubtful origin. Chandel's advisory also asks anyone who drank from the suspected supply to **get a medical check even without symptoms**.

Chandel MLA SS Olish said party workers were arranging vehicles to bring patients to Imphal.

## What the ANU wants

The ANU has demanded a **special investigation team** headed by an officer not below the rank of Inspector General of Police, ex gratia for the families of the dead, and urgent improvements to health services in Chandel, which it says lacks specialist doctors, nurses and equipment and forces patients to travel to Imphal. Its senior vice-president PS Tonila said so on Saturday. The union was expected to meet the Chief Minister on Saturday evening. No ex gratia amount had been announced when this story was written.

## Why it matters

Methanol, an industrial alcohol, can blind and kill in small amounts, and symptoms such as blurred vision and breathlessness can appear hours after drinking. For families in the Chandel hills, the nearest equipped hospitals are in Imphal, a long drive over mountain roads, which is why the ANU is pressing for local specialists. Anyone who drank liquor bought from the Sugnu area since November 15 is being asked to go to a hospital at once.

## What happens next

Laboratory results on the seized samples will show whether methanol was present. Police are tracing the sellers and the supply chain, and arrests are the next test of the Chief Minister's promise. The ANU's meeting with the Chief Minister on Saturday evening will show whether an SIT and ex gratia are agreed. The toll may rise further while patients remain in intensive care.
