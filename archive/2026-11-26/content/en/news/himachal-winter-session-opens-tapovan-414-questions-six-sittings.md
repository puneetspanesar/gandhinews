The Himachal Pradesh Legislative Assembly began its **six-day winter session on Monday, November 16**, at Tapovan near Dharamshala, the state's second capital. It is the 13th session of the 14th Assembly and runs six sittings to Saturday, November 21. The first sitting was set to open with obituary references, including a tribute to **former MLA Nand Lal**, a four-time legislator who was chairman of a state commission when he died, and to a former Kangra district MLA who represented the House about five times.

Speaker Kuldeep Singh Pathania briefed the media on Sunday after reviewing the arrangements. No bill has been named in the reports of the briefing, though the government may bring amendment bills during the week.

## What is on the list

The Assembly Secretariat has received **414 questions**: 291 starred, which ministers answer orally on the floor, and 123 unstarred, which get written replies. All were filed online through the National e-Vidhan Application. The list for the first day is ready, and lists for later days will follow as the government sends in its replies.

Members have also filed notices for debate. About **13 notices have come in under Rule 62** and three under Rule 63, one for a short-duration discussion and two for two-hour discussions. Nine notices ask for discussions of two to three hours on state issues. The Business Advisory Committee, which fixes how much time each item gets, will finalise the agenda after the ballot for proposals.

Thursday, **November 19, is Private Members' Day**. Three notices have been received under Rule 101 for it, and members can move resolutions on matters of public interest. The Secretariat will keep accepting notices through the session. "Any issue of public interest that a member considers serious and important can be brought to the notice of the House," Pathania said.

## A new rule on the media

Pathania has also laid down a rule for the complex itself. **Only sitting MLAs** may hold press conferences, give interviews or record bites inside the premises. Former legislators and other individuals may not address the media there. Accredited journalists can still enter, and reporters may speak to anyone outside the complex.

The Speaker said the rule follows an incident in Shimla in which a former MLA held a press conference inside the Assembly building, a matter that was later raised in the House and on which the chair gave a ruling. He said sitting members enjoy privileges and full protection inside the House and its premises, and that he can take action against anyone who breaks the rules.

Security at Tapovan includes **CCTV cameras and drone surveillance**, restrictions on parking, and an ambulance with medical staff on site. The Secretariat plans to use the complex through the year and has proposed a convention centre and a hotel on about seven acres nearby.

## What the House is likely to take up

The Sukhu government comes to Dharamshala with two big decisions fresh on the record. On Friday, November 13, the cabinet approved the [Apna Parivar Sukhi Parivar Yojana](/en/news/himachal-cabinet-1500-women-300-units-free-power-sukhu), under which eligible women aged 18 to 59 get **₹1,500 a month** and eligible families 300 units of free power. About 3.5 lakh women have been identified. The cabinet did not say when payments begin, and the BJP has been asking what happened to the earlier version of the scheme announced on March 4, 2024.

On November 6, the Supreme Court settled the state's decades-old claim on the Bhakra Beas Management Board's projects by ordering Punjab and Haryana to give Himachal **11,973 million units of electricity over 20 years**, as our [report on the verdict](/en/news/himachal-bbmb-verdict-11973-million-units-sukhu-rattan) records. Chief Minister Sukhvinder Singh Sukhu said afterwards that "our fight is not over yet", naming a water cess on hydropower and the return of the Shanan project among the next steps. The reports of the Speaker's briefing do not say which of these subjects members have chosen for the Rule 62 and Rule 63 notices.

## How the House stands

The Congress holds **40 of the 68 seats**, the BJP won 25 in 2022 and independents took the other three. The Congress government survived a revolt in February 2024, when six of its MLAs cross-voted in the Rajya Sabha election and were disqualified, and it regained a strength of 40 after the by-elections that followed. Himachal has not re-elected a sitting government since 1985, and the next Assembly election is due around the end of 2027, as we set out in our [explainer on the 2027 polls](/en/explainer/gujarat-himachal-pradesh-elections-2027-what-to-know).

## What happens next

The Business Advisory Committee is due to fix the time for each item, which will show **how much of the week goes to the opposition's notices** and how much to government business. The first day's questions are on the list, replies to the later days' questions will be tabled as the government sends them, and Private Members' Day on Thursday brings the first resolutions. The session is scheduled to end on Saturday, November 21.
