The Centre told the Supreme Court on Wednesday, November 18, that the investigation into the June 2025 Air India crash at Ahmedabad is nearly done, and asked for **two to three more weeks** before the court takes the matter up again. Appearing before a bench led by Chief Justice Surya Kant, Solicitor General Tushar Mehta said, "We are at the fag end of the investigation." The bench agreed to the adjournment.

The petitioners, who want an independent inquiry into the crash that killed 260 people, pressed for speed. Advocate Prashant Bhushan told the court that **the final report has still not been made public** and asked for an early hearing.

## What the Centre told the court

The bench also included Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana. Mehta, who appeared for the Union government and the aviation regulator, the DGCA, said the extra time was needed so that investigators could **follow international protocols**. He rejected any suggestion of delay by design: "There is no reason why we should delay deliberately."

The Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB), which is running the probe, has also taken a position on the cockpit recordings the petitioners want shared. It says there is an **"absolute statutory prohibition"** on giving cockpit voice recordings and airborne image recordings to any external committee or to the public. It rests that on Rules 17(1) and 17(5), read with Schedule C, of the Aircraft (Investigation of Accidents and Incidents) Rules, 2025.

The process is slow in part because passengers of several nationalities were on board, which brought foreign experts into the probe, according to the submissions reported from court. Some aircraft components had to be sent to manufacturers abroad for examination.

## How we got here

Air India flight AI171, a Boeing 787-8 bound for London Gatwick, crashed into a medical college hostel seconds after take-off from Ahmedabad on **June 12, 2025**. Of the 242 people on board, 241 died, along with 19 people on the ground. Former Gujarat chief minister Vijay Rupani was among the dead. The pilot in command was Captain Sumeet Sabharwal and the co-pilot Captain Clive Kunder.

The AAIB released a 15-page **preliminary report on July 12, 2025**. It said both engine fuel control switches moved from RUN to CUTOFF within seconds of lift-off, about a second apart, and that the switches were later returned to RUN, but the deceleration of one engine could not be stopped. On the cockpit voice recording, one pilot asks the other why he cut off the fuel, and the other says he did not. The report recommended no action for operators of the aircraft or its engines at that stage.

The litigation was started by Pushkar Raj Sabharwal, the 91-year-old father of Captain Sabharwal, and the NGO Safety Matters Foundation. A pilots' body is also among the petitioners. They ask for a court of inquiry or an independent, transparent investigation that follows the international rules India has signed.

In July 2026 the bench **put the demand for a separate inquiry on hold** until the AAIB's final report is available. It said the AAIB probe had "substantially progressed" and that what happens to the case would depend on whether the report is well merited and accepted. On July 28 the court was told the report would come in the autumn, in a sealed cover. That target has passed without a published report.

## The petitioners' objections

Bhushan has told the court the investigation has run for **nearly a year and a half**. He has also raised what he calls a conflict of interest, saying three of the five members of the investigation team are from the DGCA, while the AAIB rules, he argued, call for a permanent body without DGCA members. The Centre's position, as stated on Wednesday, is that the delay is not deliberate and follows from the protocols on foreign participation.

The two sides differ on the physical evidence as well. The preliminary report says airport CCTV showed the ram air turbine deploying during the initial climb. The petitioners dispute that account and have cited simulations that, they say, show deployment while the jet was still on the ground.

## Why it matters

The preliminary report made no recommendations to operators, so the final report is the first place any would appear. The families of the 260 dead have waited for it for more than 17 months, and the court has said the future of the petitions depends on it.

Publication may come some time after the draft is finished. A draft final report is first shared with the participating states, which here include the United States and the United Kingdom, for comment.

## What happens next

The bench has not been reported to have fixed a date. An adjournment of two to three weeks puts the next hearing in early December. By then the Centre will have to show the court either the final report or a firm date for it. The petitioners want the report made public, and the question of an independent inquiry, parked since July, depends on what it says.
