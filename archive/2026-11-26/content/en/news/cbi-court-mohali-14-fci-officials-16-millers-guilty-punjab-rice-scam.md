A special CBI court in Mohali has convicted **14 officials of the Food Corporation of India and 16 private rice millers**, all of them for criminal conspiracy and cheating, in a case that began with the passing of substandard rice as Grade-A in Punjab's Sangrur district in the 2004-05 kharif season. The order was passed on Friday, November 13, according to the CBI, and the sentences run from one to three years of rigorous imprisonment.

The 14 FCI officials, who were also convicted under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, got **two years each and a fine of ₹15,000**. The longest terms, three years, went to six millers. Reports of the verdict name no judge and put no figure on the loss to the corporation.

## What the court found

The prosecution's case was that FCI staff who tested and accepted rice at three procurement centres, Malerkotla, Sunam and Tapa, worked with the millers who delivered it. Rice that did not meet the prescribed quality specifications was accepted as Grade-A, the millers were paid at the higher rate, and the corporation lost money. The CBI described it as a **conspiracy between officials and millers**.

The evidence began in September 2005, when joint surprise checks by the CBI found irregularities at the centres. Lab tests on samples of the rice confirmed that the consignments failed the specifications. The agency registered its case on January 7, 2006, and filed **three chargesheets on November 28, 2008**, one for each centre. The three trials were registered as CC 59, 60 and 66 of 2017.

The court convicted 30 people across the three cases:

- **Malerkotla:** 10 convicted, five FCI staff and five millers.
- **Sunam:** six convicted, three FCI staff and three millers.
- **Tapa:** 14 convicted, six FCI staff and eight private persons.

## Who got what

At Malerkotla, the five FCI men are Varinder Rajput, Girdari Lal and KP Singh, then technical assistants, and Raunaq Ali and RN Sharma, then assistant managers in quality control. Each got two years and ₹15,000. Among the millers, partners Harish Kumar and Rajnish Kumar of Shiv Shakti Rice Agro Products and Rakesh Goyal of RK Agro Tech **got three years** and ₹25,000 each. Ramesh Singla of Shiv Shakti and Narinder Kumar of Satkartar Rice and Agro Mills got two years and ₹20,000.

At Sunam, RC Verma, an assistant manager in quality control, and technical assistants Som Pal Singh and KL Gulati each got two years and ₹15,000. Of the millers, Kewal Krishan of Mahavir Rice Mills got **the lightest sentence in the case**, one year and ₹10,000, while Hari Ram and Jiwan Lal of KC Rice Mills got two years and ₹20,000 each.

At Tapa, the six officials are Gurmail Singh, a deputy manager in quality control, RS Sirohi, an assistant manager, and technical assistants Ramesh Kumar, Harinder Singh, Harish Kumar and Satnam Singh. Each got two years and ₹15,000. Budh Ram of Bansal Rice Mills, Pardeep Sharma of Sukhanand Rice Udyog Samiti and Bhushan Kumar of Ganga Rice Mills got three years and ₹20,000. Vinod Kumar of Jai Jwala Rice Mills, Bharat Bhushan of Dhaliwal Rice Udyog Samiti, Ashok Kumar and Surinder Kumar of Luxmi Rice Mills, and Ramesh Kumar of BID Rice Mills got two years and ₹20,000.

The fines come to ₹5.35 lakh in all, a small figure against a case that took more than two decades. No acquittals have been reported.

## How we got here

The case rests on the quality check that FCI staff carry out before the corporation pays a miller for rice delivered at a centre. In the prosecution's account that check was set aside at all three centres, the CBI's surprise checks in September 2005 exposed it, and the laboratory results on the samples are what the case was built on.

From the surprise checks to the order, **21 years and two months** have passed. The 14 convicted officials have been identified by the posts they held at the time; the reports do not say which of them are still in service or have retired.

## Reactions

Reports of the verdict carry no statement from the convicted officials, the millers or their lawyers, and none from the FCI. The CBI's description of the case as a conspiracy is the only account of the offence on the record beyond the sentences themselves. Whether any of the 30 has applied for bail pending an appeal, which is the usual next step after a sentence of this length, has not been reported.

## Why it matters

The FCI buys paddy, and the rice milled from it, from Punjab's millers every season, and the corporation's stocks feed the public distribution system. In this case the corporation paid the higher Grade-A rate for rice that failed its own specifications, and the court has held both the people who tested it and the people who supplied it responsible.

Only the 14 officials were convicted under the anti-corruption law as well as the Indian Penal Code, yet the six millers who got three years were sentenced to a year more than any official.

## What happens next

Reports so far do not say whether any of the 30 convicts has appealed to the Punjab and Haryana High Court or sought bail, and no date for any such hearing has been given. The court's full judgment, once available, will show the loss it found and how it weighed the lab evidence; neither is in the reports on the verdict.
