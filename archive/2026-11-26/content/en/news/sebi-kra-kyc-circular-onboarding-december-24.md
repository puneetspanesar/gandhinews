The Securities and Exchange Board of India has changed how investors' identity records are kept and shared across the market. In a circular dated **November 25, 2026**, the regulator told brokers, depositories, exchanges and clearing corporations to push common client details to the KYC registration agencies, or KRAs, and to tell those agencies when an account is closed or never opened. The rules **take effect on December 24, 2026**.

A KRA is the central store where an investor's know-your-customer record sits after the first broker, fund house or depository checks it. Every later intermediary can download it. The circular, numbered HO/38/14/(25)2026-MIRSD-POD3 and signed by Sebi Deputy General Manager Divya Hamirbasia, amends the KYC master circular of October 12, 2023. It is issued under Section 11(1) of the Sebi Act, 1992 and Regulation 17 of the KYC Registration Agency Regulations, 2011.

## What the circular requires

The central change is that **common information is held once, at the KRA**, instead of being collected again by every intermediary. Within three working days of finishing a client's KYC, the intermediary must upload the account opening form details, income range, FATCA and CRS information, politically exposed person status and occupation, along with the client's CKYC ID, DIGIPIN where available and the expiry date of the identity document used. The KRA must tag any item it has verified itself.

Intermediaries must update their own records from what the KRA and the central CKYC registry hold, and give the client an option to confirm the update. Where the KRA already shows a mobile number as verified and Aadhaar-seeded, **the intermediary need not verify it again**.

## Closed and dormant accounts

A second set of rules deals with records that outlive the relationship. Intermediaries other than mutual funds must inform the KRA **within five working days of an account closing**, and the KRA must update or delink the record within two working days. Existing closed accounts must be reported within three months.

For mutual funds, the trigger is a folio with zero balance and no transactions for six months. The fund or its registrar must then tell the KRA within five working days, and the KRA stops sending that client's updates. If a client downloads a KYC record and no account is opened within 21 working days, the intermediary reports it within five working days and the KRA delinks the record within two. Until now, a KRA could keep sending updates to firms that no longer had any relationship with the investor.

## Easier paperwork for some clients

- **Name change:** if PAN and Aadhaar already carry the new name, copies of both are enough. Otherwise a marriage certificate from a state government, a passport showing the husband's name or a gazette notification is accepted for marriage, and a gazette notification for other changes. Karnataka and Punjab, where private individuals cannot get gazette notifications, need extra papers including a notarised affidavit and newspaper notices.
- **Overseas address:** resident Overseas Citizen of India cardholders and resident foreign nationals can skip overseas address proof if they give a verified Indian address.
- **Portability:** a record whose address is verified against sources such as the Income Tax PAN database, Aadhaar XML, DigiLocker or mAadhaar is tagged "validated" and can be reused across intermediaries, with the KRA sharing the status attribute by attribute.

The circular also extends most of these provisions to individual non-resident clients from FATF-compliant countries, as set out in a separate Sebi circular of November 4, 2026.

## How we got here

Sebi floated the changes on **January 16, 2026** in a consultation paper, with comments invited until February 6. It said investors kept giving the same details to each intermediary, that records went stale, as when a minor turned 18 or an identity document expired, and that agencies went on sending updates after an account was closed. A working group of market participants, market infrastructure institutions, KRAs and industry bodies then reviewed the framework; the circular says its recommendations and the public feedback shaped the final text.

The final text differs from the draft in places. The paper proposed that closures be reported within three working days; the circular allows five. The circular also names the exact lookups, such as DigiLocker and Aadhaar XML, that make a record "validated".

## Why it matters

For an investor, the practical gain is **fewer repeat forms** when opening an account with a second broker or a new fund house, and fewer firms holding data on someone who has left. For intermediaries it means new upload and reporting duties on fixed clocks, and a clean-up of old records within three months. The circular puts no penalty on the face of it, and no industry body or broker association had put out a public response by Thursday evening.

## What happens next

KRAs must issue their standard operating procedure within 30 days of the circular. The circular sets the effective date at December 24, 2026, which it describes as 30 days from issue. Intermediaries then have three months to report existing closed and non-associated records. All other provisions of the 2023 master circular stay as they are.
