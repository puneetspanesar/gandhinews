The Supreme Court on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, **issued a show-cause notice for criminal contempt to advocate Pravin Singhal**, two days after he told Chief Justice of India Surya Kant in open court that he did not deserve to sit in the chair. A bench of Justices Dipankar Datta, Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana acted on a petition by the Supreme Court Bar Association (SCBA), which says Mr Singhal is not one of its members.

## What the court ordered

The bench recorded that it was **prima facie satisfied** that the SCBA's motion to initiate contempt proceedings "deserves to be taken forward". It directed Mr Singhal to file a reply affidavit by **January 8** and to remain personally present on the returnable date. The matter is titled Supreme Court Bar Association v. Pravin Singhal (Contempt Petition (Criminal) No. 3 of 2026). Reports give the returnable date as January 20, 2027.

Attorney General R. Venkataramani had given his consent for the action, which the Contempt of Courts Act requires before a private body can move the court. In his letter he described Mr Singhal's conduct as "extremely scandalous and unpardonable" and said it called for action under Section 15(1)(b) of the Act. The bench took the consent into account in recording its prima facie view.


## What happened on November 16

On Monday, Mr Singhal appeared before the bench of the Chief Justice, Justice Bagchi and Justice Mohana and asked for an urgent hearing. He said he had submitted a matter to the CJI's office on November 2 and had heard nothing for 15 days. Justice Mohana told him he could not speak in that way, and he offered an apology. When the Chief Justice asked him to wait, he refused and said: "You can't sit in that chair, you don't deserve. You don't have the integrity for that!"

The audio-video recording of the proceedings was switched off and security personnel escorted him out of the courtroom. Lawyers present told the court that the Chief Justice recalled that Mr Singhal had also made abusive comments against him during the anti-CEC agitations, which had led to contempt proceedings. SCBA secretary Praghya Baghel and joint secretary Meenesh Dubey mentioned the matter for action the same day.

## What was argued on Wednesday

SCBA president Pradeep Rai, a senior advocate, told the bench that the conduct had to be **dealt with firmly**. He said Mr Singhal had also posted an Instagram reel targeting the Chief Justice, and that several people who present themselves as Supreme Court advocates earn money from social media content aimed at judges.

Solicitor General Tushar Mehta, appearing virtually, said he **fully supported the petition** and that the dignity of the Court must be maintained.


## How we got here

The Chief Justice's court has seen a run of contempt matters this autumn. On October 16 a bench issued notices to Shiv Sena (UBT) MP Sanjay Raut and journalist Nikhil Wagle on the SCBA's petition over their remarks about the Chief Justice, as [our report that day](/en/news/supreme-court-contempt-notice-sanjay-raut-nikhil-wagle) set out. That case is still running: on Tuesday a different bench pressed Mr Raut on an apology and his later posts, as we reported in [our latest story on it](/en/news/sanjay-raut-contempt-supreme-court-affidavit-wagle-december-8), and the next hearing is reported for December 8. A separate notice was issued over a Kerala Congress post on the Chief Justice, covered in [our earlier report](/en/news/supreme-court-contempt-notice-kerala-congress-x-post-cji).

## Why it matters

Criminal contempt can lead to a fine, imprisonment or both, which is why the court has asked Mr Singhal to be present in person rather than answer on paper alone. Mr Rai told the court that Mr Singhal is not an SCBA member, so the association's petition rests on the court's own power to protect its dignity rather than on any membership rules. The bench's reasoning on Mr Singhal's conduct will be set out in the order, which was not available when this report was written.

## What happens next

Mr Singhal must file his reply by January 8, 2027, and appear on the returnable date, which the court has listed in January. We were unable to find any response from him to the notice.
