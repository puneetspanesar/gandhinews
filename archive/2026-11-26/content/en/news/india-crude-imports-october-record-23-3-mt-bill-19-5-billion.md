India's refiners brought in **a record 23.3 million tonnes of crude oil in October**, about 5.58 million barrels a day, and paid almost twice what they did a year ago for it, according to provisional data from the Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell (PPAC), the oil ministry's data arm, as reported on November 19 and 20, 2026.

The crude import bill came to **$19.5 billion**, up 87.5% from $10.4 billion in October 2025. Volumes rose far less. At 23.31 million tonnes, October's imports were **4.7% above September's** 22.26 million tonnes and about 11% above the 21 million tonnes of October last year. Almost all of the jump in the bill therefore came from price, with a smaller share from the extra barrels.

## What the data show

The Indian basket of crude, the average price paid by the country's refiners, averaged **$119.91 a barrel** in October, against $116.09 in September, a figure we reported on October 17 when the April-September bill was put at $90.6 billion. Reports of the PPAC release also quote a Brent average above $120 for the month, a level that does not match the daily prices we recorded through October, so we do not rely on it here.

After subtracting exports of petroleum products, the **net oil and gas import bill was about $16 billion**, up about 60% from $10 billion a year earlier. Product exports earned $6.3 billion, up about 91%, on 5.4 million tonnes.

Imports of finished products went the other way. India took in 2.44 million tonnes of petroleum products, down 2.8% from September and **about 41% below** October 2025. Cooking gas (LPG) imports fell to 1.30 million tonnes from 1.92 million tonnes a year earlier, and fuel oil imports fell 87%. Domestic fuel consumption was 20.49 million tonnes, nearly 10% above September and the highest since March.

## Who sold the oil

Russia stayed India's biggest supplier, at about **1.85 million barrels a day, roughly a third** of purchases. Saudi Arabia sent about 790,000 barrels a day, an eight-month high, and **overtook the United Arab Emirates**, whose 612,000 barrels a day was a seven-month low, to become the second-largest source. Reliance Industries raised its own Russian intake by 9.6% from September to 393,500 barrels a day, according to trade data.

The reports do not give October volumes for the United States or Iraq, so the full ranking below the top three cannot be set out. Our earlier reporting showed American cargoes had been losing ground: shipments of US crude, LNG and LPG to India fell 43% in September, and in November a state refiner was still paying a premium of about $18 a barrel over dated Brent to bring in a US cargo for Mumbai. Gulf supplies, meanwhile, passed Russian purchases on ship-tracking data in a report we carried on November 12, even as the Strait of Hormuz stayed the most dangerous stretch of water on India's supply map.

## How we got here

The price shock goes back to the West Asia war. The six-month bill to September was already **$90.6 billion**, up 46.3% on a year earlier on almost unchanged volumes. September alone cost $15.4 billion. October's $19.5 billion is well above September's figure, and this time volumes rose as well. On November 12 Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed two supertankers carrying more than four million barrels had exploded in the Strait, a claim that has not been independently confirmed.


## What it means for the bill, the rupee and pump prices

India imports about 90% of the crude it uses, so every dollar on the barrel reaches the trade deficit. Analysts quoted in reports estimate that **a 10% rise in oil adds roughly $12 billion** a year to the import bill, about 0.3% of GDP, and that a $10 rise widens the current account deficit by about 0.5 percentage points of GDP.

That is the pressure behind the rupee. It was near 97 to the dollar in mid-October, when the Reserve Bank of India agreed to sell dollars to Indian Oil, Hindustan Petroleum and Bharat Petroleum to cover their daily needs. A bigger bill means more dollars leaving the country each month, which is why the currency tracks crude.

For motorists the effect shows up in who pays rather than in the pump price. Official assurances on prices have been cautious: Union minister Suresh Gopi said on November 16 that petrol and diesel prices could not be cut immediately because oil companies had run up **₹42,000 crore of losses** during the conflict. A record month of purchases at near-record prices adds to that gap, which companies will want closed before any cut.

## What happens next

PPAC's full October reckoner, with the country-wise breakdown and April-October totals, is the next document to watch. Brent, which has eased from its October highs (January futures were near $98 on November 6), will decide whether November's bill is smaller. Goldman Sachs has forecast Brent averaging about $88 in 2027, while S&P Global Energy sees the fourth quarter of 2026 slightly above $100. Private refiners share their numbers with the data cell at their own discretion, so the October figures are preliminary and may be revised.
