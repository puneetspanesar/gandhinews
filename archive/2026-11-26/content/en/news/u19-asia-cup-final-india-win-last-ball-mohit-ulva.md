India's under-19 side **beat Pakistan by one wicket off the last ball** in the final of the Men's Under-19 Asia Cup at Al Amerat, Oman, on Saturday, November 14, chasing 338 and finishing on 339 for 9. Mohit Ulva, batting at No. 10, hit the winning four off the final delivery of the 50th over to give India a **record ninth title** and avenge their defeat in the group match on November 8.

The finish came from a chase that had looked gone. India were **252 for 8 in the 42nd over**, still 86 short, after a middle-order slide. They won with the highest aggregate in an India-Pakistan under-19 one-day match, according to reports from the ground.

## Pakistan's 337

Pakistan won the toss and batted. Usman Khan made **107, his second hundred of the tournament**, and Abdul Qadir hit 80 off 54 balls with eight sixes. Captain Farhan Yousaf added 63 off 60. Pakistan were 204 for 2 after 30 overs and looked set for well over 350.

Instead they **scored only 63 in the last 10 overs and lost six wickets**, ending on 337 for 9. Kavya Patel took four for 68, and Chigurupati Venkata, who replaced the injured Ishan Sood, took three for 40. The total is one more than the 336 we reported at the end of the innings, which came from live updates; the final score is 337 for 9. Our [report from the first innings](/en/news/u19-asia-cup-final-pakistan-336-india-chase-kavya-patel) has the build-up.

## How India chased

Aaryavir Sehwag and captain Lakshya Raichandani **put on 95 for the first wicket**, which ended when Niqab Shafiq had Sehwag caught by Usman Khan for 58 off 61 balls. Raichandani made **80 off 101 balls** with five fours. Rizwanullah, Pakistan's best bowler, finished with three for 53 in 10 overs, and Shafiq took two.

Wickets kept falling in the middle overs. Anvay Dravid was run out for 19 off 12 balls, VK Vineeth made 24 and Arjun Rajput 16, and at 252 for 8 Pakistan were clear favourites. What followed turned the match.

## Ulva and Venkata

Manal Chauhan, the left-hand allrounder, hit **47 off 32 balls with three sixes** and shared a stand with Ulva before Shafiq got him with the score at 302. India needed 36 more with one wicket left, and Venkata, the No. 11, walked in.

Ulva, an 18-year-old right-arm medium pacer from Jamnagar who first played tennis-ball cricket in his village, then **hit 49 not out off 26 balls**, five fours and three sixes at a strike rate of 188. He and Venkata, who was not out on 11 off eight balls, added **37 for the last wicket** and brought the target down to eight in the final over, bowled by leg-spinner Ahmed Hussain.

Five runs came off the first five balls. India needed three off the last ball to win. Ulva hit it wide of long-off for **four**.

## What the result means

India's ninth title is a record, and it takes the trophy back from Pakistan, who beat them by 191 runs in last year's final and by three wickets in the group game on November 8, when Hamza Zahoor's unbeaten 100 won it (our [report](/en/news/india-u19-pakistan-asia-cup-result-zahoor-century)). Pakistan had not lost a match in this tournament until the last ball of the final.

For Pakistan, a hundred from Usman Khan and a fast 80 from Qadir were not enough, and the defeat turned on the last 10 overs, when they managed 63 and lost six wickets. For India, the opening stand of 95 kept the chase alive and the lower order, Chauhan, Ulva and Venkata, scored the last 87 runs from 252 for 8.

## What happens next

The Asia Cup is over. The official scorecard and the player of the match award are to come from the Asian Cricket Council, and this report will be updated if they change any figure here.
