Delhi's government has readied **1,482 Chhath ghats at 996 locations** for the final day of the festival on Monday, November 16, when devotees stand in water at dawn to offer Arghya to the rising sun. Government offices, autonomous bodies and public sector undertakings in the capital are closed for the day, and more than 5,000 police personnel are on duty after Sunday evening's Sandhya Arghya brought large crowds to the ghats.

Chhath is observed from November 13 to 16 this year. Sunday's evening offering to the setting sun was the busiest moment so far, and Monday's morning offering ends the four-day observance.

## What the government has set up

According to the Chief Minister's Office, the arrangements include lighting, drinking water, toilets, changing rooms for women and sanitation at every ghat. The government's count is **81 permanent ghats, 1,372 temporary ones and 29 along the Yamuna**, which together make 1,482. The Municipal Corporation of Delhi and the New Delhi Municipal Council are responsible for cleaning, with teams on duty round the clock and a requirement to file waste-disposal compliance reports with photographs. The Delhi Jal Board is supplying tankers and drinking water, and the electricity department is lighting the ghats and their approach roads.

Mobile toilets, tents, bamboo barricades and safety railings have been put up. Some designated "model" ghats carry extra security, with CCTV cameras linked to a central control room, and the release says particular attention is being paid to women and children. Senior officials have been told to be present at the major ghats to fix problems on the spot. In the Chief Minister's Office's words, "The government's objective is to ensure a safe environment for the devotees."

## The new Munak Canal ghat

The showpiece is an **11 km ghat along the Munak Canal** in North Delhi, from Shalimar Bagh towards Inderlok. Before work began, about 1,400 truckloads of garbage, debris and silt were removed and encroachments were cleared, according to a report published on Sunday. The project cost about **₹115.29 crore** and has permanent steps on both banks, paved roads, changing rooms, toilets, lighting, CCTV, benches and green spaces. It can hold more than 30,000 people at a time and is meant to serve residents of five Assembly constituencies. The Delhi Jal Board is feeding it from the Haiderpur Water Treatment Plant. The canal ghat was inaugurated by BJP national president Nitin Nabin and Chief Minister Rekha Gupta.

At the heavily polluted stretches of the Yamuna itself, the government has set up artificial and filtered-water ghats, with Vasudev Ghat among them, so that devotees do not have to enter the river water directly.

## Sunday evening and Monday morning

Chief Minister Rekha Gupta offered Arghya at **Sonia Vihar on Sunday evening**, reaching worshippers by boat, accompanied by Delhi minister Kapil Mishra and BJP MP Manoj Tiwari, who sang Chhath songs with the devotees. She is due at **Hathi Ghat near ITO on Monday morning** to offer Arghya to the rising sun and worship Chhathi Maiya and the Sun God. Reports of Sunday's turnout at Hathi Ghat put the crowd at about one lakh, an estimate that has not been independently confirmed.

For commuters, traffic restrictions ran from **2 pm on Sunday to 8 am on Monday** around Ram, Soor, Bajrangi, Vasudev and Hathi Ghats, and on Bundh Road, Wazirabad Road, Vikas Marg and near IP Estate. Police focus extended to the East, North East and Shahdara districts.

## The public holiday

The holiday was announced on November 10 through a **notification from the General Administration Department**, issued in the name of the Lieutenant Governor, for Pratihar Shashthi or Surya Shashthi, the day of the morning Arghya. It applies to government offices, autonomous bodies and PSUs. The reports of the notification do not mention schools, so parents should check with their schools.

## Questions about the arrangements

The Chief Minister has said the festival reflects India's cultural heritage and family values, and her government says it inspected the ghats with ministers including Parvesh Sahib Singh before the festival. BJP leaders contrast the arrangements with the record of the previous Aam Aadmi Party government, which they accuse of neglecting the ghats and the river.

The Opposition sees gaps. Delhi Congress president **Devender Yadav** visited Soor Ghat in Wazirabad on Sunday and alleged that arrangements were inadequate, in particular that there were no proper changing facilities, and he raised concern about a drain discharging into the river near the ghat. An official inspection report could not verify these allegations, according to a report on Sunday evening. No statement from the AAP on the arrangements was found.

## The Yamuna question

Water quality remains the question that follows every Chhath in Delhi. Rekha Gupta says about **26 km of the Yamuna has been cleaned** in the past 20 months, while Manoj Tiwari claims nearly 30 km. Neither figure comes with published water-quality measurements, and reports from the ghats say stretches that receive untreated or inadequately treated sewage still worry residents. In October the Centre launched Yamuna-cleaning projects worth about ₹8,941 crore, including sewage treatment plants and sewer networks, as our [report on the project](/en/news/amit-shah-8941-crore-yamuna-sewage-projects-najafgarh) records.

## Why it matters

The festival is observed on riverbanks, canals and temporary ponds, so the state of the ghat decides how safe it is for fasting devotees who stand in water before sunrise. A temporary ghat without proper lighting, changing space or railings is a particular risk for women, children and the elderly. The same calendar is shaping official plans in other states: our reports on [Patna's ghats](/en/news/patna-chhath-ghats-19-crore-231-ghats-sita-sahu) and on [Jharkhand's moved foundation day event](/en/news/jharkhand-26th-foundation-day-main-event-moved-nov-28-chhath-khelgaon) show it.

## What happens next

The morning Arghya on **Monday, November 16**, closes the festival. The civic agencies' waste-disposal compliance reports and the sanitation drives that continue after the festival follow, and any published water-quality readings for the Yamuna will test the cleaning claims made by both the government and the Opposition.
