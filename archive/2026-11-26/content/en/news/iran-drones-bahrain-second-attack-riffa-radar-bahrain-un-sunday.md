Iran's Revolutionary Guards said early on Sunday, November 15, that they **fired six drones at a US air-defence radar** at Bahrain's Al Riffa helicopter base, the second attack on the Gulf kingdom in three days. Bahrain's armed forces said their air defences intercepted and destroyed the aircraft before they reached their targets, and no casualties were reported. Later in the day Manama asked the UN Security Council to condemn Iran and **force it to comply with Resolution 2817**.

The strike widens a confrontation that had seemed to be cooling. Only on Thursday President Donald Trump said five Gulf states had asked him to hold off further heavy strikes on Iran, and the Guards' first Bahrain claim came the next day.

## What happened before dawn

A journalist in Manama heard explosions at about 4 am local time, and residents reported a second series of blasts around 6:30 am. Sirens sounded and the Interior Ministry sent two alerts within about three hours, the first near 4:30 am. It told residents to **"remain calm"** and to go to the nearest safe place. These were the first such warnings in Bahrain since September, and the first nationwide alert in months.

The Guards said the drones went after a US air-defence radar at the Riffa base, which they described as a response to hostile American actions. In the same statement they declared that **"the Strait of Hormuz is closed"** and warned that any further US aggression would meet a harsher reply. The US military had not commented on Sunday's claim when the reports were filed.

The pattern echoes Friday. Then too the Guards claimed they had destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, after an air strike on a telecom tower at Sirik in southern Iran. US Central Command rejected that claim, saying two Iranian drones were shot down with no injuries or damage. We reported that exchange in [our story on the Sirik strike](/en/news/iran-sirik-strike-irgc-bahrain-radar-claim-centcom-denies-two-drones).

## Bahrain's complaint to the UN

Bahrain's Foreign Ministry said its air defences destroyed drones on Sunday and two more on Friday, and that the Guards had claimed responsibility for both attacks. It called them "a blatant aggression against Bahrain's sovereignty and territorial integrity".

The ministry cited **UN Security Council Resolution 2817**, which condemned Iranian attacks on countries in the region and demanded they stop. It asked the Council to condemn the latest attacks and take firm action to bring Iran into line. It also reserved Bahrain's right to self-defence under Article 51 of the UN Charter and held Iran fully responsible for the consequences. Manama rejected the use of its territory "as an arena for settling scores".

On the ground the mood was anxious. Hayat Ibrahim, a 65-year-old resident, said the attacks were what she had hoped would not happen, and that they brought "worry and uncertainty". A shopkeeper in Manama, Mohammed Abdallah, said he had hoped the period of attacks was over.

## A war with no settlement

The war began on February 28 with US and Israeli strikes that killed Iran's supreme leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. Over more than eight months Iran has restricted shipping through Hormuz and hit airports, ports, energy sites and US assets across the Gulf. A Pentagon inspector general's report in September found that Iranian attacks damaged or destroyed buildings at US installations in **eight countries, Bahrain among them**, in the first four months.

Diplomacy has not caught up. Iran's chief negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, says Tehran has set **seven conditions for a deal**, among them an end to the US naval blockade and the lifting of sanctions, and is waiting for a US reply through a Qatari mediator. On Saturday the Guards' navy said two oil tankers hit sea mines after using an "unauthorised" route with tracking systems switched off, while two others turned back after warnings. Iran claims full control of the strait; Washington rejects that. US Central Command says it has helped Gulf states export 1.5 billion barrels of oil through Hormuz since the summer.

Analyst Saeid Golkar warns that **"the danger lies in miscalculation"**, since limited exchanges can widen into a larger war. Trump, whose party took heavy losses in the midterms that some blame partly on the war, is due to discuss the conflict this week with the Chinese and Russian presidents.

## Why it matters to India

Bahrain is home to a large Indian community, and the Gulf remains India's main source of crude oil and a major route for Indian shipping. Each fresh incident around Hormuz keeps oil prices and marine insurance costs under watch, as we noted in [our report on the tanker mine strikes](/en/news/hormuz-three-tankers-mines-predator-qeshm-brent-india). Indian airlines have already suspended some Gulf flights this month.

## What to watch

The first test is whether the Security Council takes up Bahrain's request, and whether Washington answers the Riffa claim. A US reply to Ghalibaf's seven conditions via Qatar, and any new Guards statement on Hormuz, will show whether Sunday was a one-off or the start of a longer round.
