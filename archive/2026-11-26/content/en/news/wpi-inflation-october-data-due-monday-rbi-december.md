India's wholesale price inflation for October is due on **Monday, November 16**, and economists will be looking for a sign of whether the record run that took the index to 11.39% in September has peaked. The release comes from the commerce ministry and follows retail inflation of 6.09% for October, which breached the Reserve Bank of India's 6% upper limit on Thursday.

The two readings are measured differently, but they have been moving the same way since August. Wholesale prices track what traders and factories pay, so they usually reach shop shelves with a lag. A high October reading would add to the case that retail inflation has more room to run.

## What September showed

The wholesale price index (WPI) rose **11.39% in September**, up from 9.92% in August and 9.78% in July, according to the data released on October 14. It was the highest since the index moved to its 2022-23 base year, and it came in well above what economists had pencilled in. Our [report on the September numbers](/en/news/wpi-inflation-september-2026) has the full breakdown.

Fuel and power prices were **25.85% higher** than a year earlier, led by crude petroleum and natural gas, which were up 57.32%. Manufactured products, which carry 63.1% of the index weight, rose 9.76%, also a series high, with basic metals up 16.96% and chemicals up 16.74%. Food articles rose 8.14%, a 21-month high.

## Oil, the rupee and October

The biggest driver of the September number was crude, and October was no calmer. Brent traded at about $106 a barrel on October 14, and by October 25 analysts were describing crude as nearing $120 as risks in West Asia collided with the US Federal Reserve's rate decision. The same week, average oil prices paid by India were reported to have touched a 15-year high, a squeeze on oil marketing companies and on the Centre.

The Centre has absorbed part of that shock at the pump. Prime Minister Narendra Modi said on November 13 that when crude touched $120 the pressure was for a ₹50 a litre rise in diesel, that the government raised the price by ₹7 and absorbed the other ₹43. Diesel is a cost for farmers and for the trucks that move food and factory goods, but the WPI measures wholesale prices, so a retail price cap does not fully hold the index down.

The rupee has added to the import bill. It closed at 95.17 against the dollar on October 14 and was at 94.78 in early trade on October 23. As of Sunday it was trading above 94, after briefly dipping below 93.50, according to a weekly market outlook. Imported crude, metals and chemicals cost more in rupee terms when the currency is weak.

## Crude has eased since

Crude has since come off. Brent fell to about **$97.5 a barrel on Monday, November 9**, after a 17% drop in a week, when US President Donald Trump said renewed strikes on Iran were highly unlikely if Tehran signed a strong deal, as we reported at the time. That relief arrived in November, so it is unlikely to show up in October's wholesale figures, which are measured against prices through the month.

## What it means for the RBI

The Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% on October 7, its first increase since February 2023, and projected inflation of 6% for October to December. October's retail inflation has now matched that projection, and economists quoted after Thursday's release expect a second increase in December. Madan Sabnavis of Bank of Baroda said he expects the RBI to consider either 25 or 50 basis points, and ICRA's Aditi Nayar expects retail inflation of about 6.1% in November.

A wholesale number that holds near or above 11% would reinforce that reading. A clear fall would give the committee something to point to. Either way, the RBI will have October's retail and wholesale figures, and the November retail reading due on December 14, before its meeting.

## What to watch on Monday

Three lines in the release will matter most. The first is fuel and power, which stood at 25.85% in September. The second is manufactured products, where costlier metals and chemicals are feeding into factory-gate prices. The third is food articles, where onions and other vegetables have pushed retail food inflation to 8.94%.

For households, the link is indirect but real. Higher wholesale costs for steel, chemicals and plastics reach prices of appliances, vehicles and packaged goods over the next few months. A higher repo rate, if it comes in December, would raise the cost of floating-rate home and car loans.

## What comes next

The commerce ministry releases the October figures on Monday. The RBI also publishes its weekly foreign exchange reserves data on Friday, November 20, and the Monetary Policy Committee decides on December 2 to 4.
