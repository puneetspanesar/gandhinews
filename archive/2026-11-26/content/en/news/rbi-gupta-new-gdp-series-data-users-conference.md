Reserve Bank of India Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta said on Monday, November 23, that the **new GDP series brings demand-side and supply-side estimates of the economy much closer together**, a change she expects to make the central bank's reading of growth, and its rate decisions, steadier. She was speaking in Mumbai at a data users' conference on the new GDP, consumer price and industrial production series, organised by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation.

"In the earlier series, GDP estimates from the demand and supply sides did not always align," Gupta said. The gap between the two, reported as a "discrepancy", could be large and volatile. "The new series brings the two sides together through the supply-use table framework," she said. In nominal terms the two sides now agree closely, and she said a small gap may remain in real terms because each side is adjusted for price changes differently.

## What changed in the numbers

The new national accounts, released by MoSPI on February 27, 2026, **moved the base year from 2011-12 to 2022-23**. The ministry's release said the series adds a supply-use table framework to cut the gap between production-side and spending-side GDP, switches to double deflation for manufacturing and agriculture, and draws on GST data, corporate filings, e-VAHAN vehicle records and the Periodic Labour Force Survey. On the new basis real GDP grew 7.2% in 2023-24 and 7.1% in 2024-25, with 7.6% estimated for 2025-26 at that time.

Gupta said the series brings in wider surveys and administrative data, which should capture the informal economy better. Private consumption now uses shares from the latest Household Consumption Expenditure Survey, and investment is mapped to the sector that makes it. "The new series reflects an economy that has become larger, more diversified and more complex," she said.

## Why the RBI cares about revisions

For a central bank that sets interest rates on a quarterly read of growth, the practical gain is **smaller revisions between releases**. Gupta said the new series should produce estimates that are "more stable across successive vintages", and that this supports "better-calibrated and more stable policy, particularly monetary policy".

The point has a recent test. The Monetary Policy Committee voted 6-0 on October 7 to [raise the repo rate to 5.50%](/en/news/rbi-raises-repo-rate-to-5-50), the first increase since February 2023. Gupta herself backed the hike in the [minutes released on October 21](/en/news/rbi-mpc-minutes-rate-cuts-off-table-two-members-oppose-tightening). The committee meets again from December 2 to 4, with retail inflation at [6.09% in October](/en/news/retail-inflation-october-2026-6-09-two-year-high), above the 6% ceiling for the first time in two years.

## Inflation and industry

On consumer prices Gupta said the revised index, built on the 2023-24 Household Consumption Expenditure Survey in place of the 2011-12 survey, should make headline inflation less volatile. It follows the COICOP 2018 classification and, for the first time, **collects prices from 12 online markets**. House rent now covers rural areas, while free foodgrain under public distribution and employer-provided or government housing are left out. According to reports of the speech, the weight of food and beverages in the basket falls to 36.8% from 45.9%.

"These revisions should narrow the disconnect between measured and perceived inflation," she said. "As the share of online consumption grows, this will matter for how we read price signals."

The revised index of industrial production gives higher weights to motor vehicles, pharmaceuticals and electronics, and lower weights to refined petroleum products, basic metals and mining.

## What happens next

The first big test of the new GDP series for the RBI is close. MoSPI is due to release the July-September 2026 estimate on **November 30**, two days before the Monetary Policy Committee meets on December 2. The RBI had projected 7.2% growth for the quarter in October. The [April-June figure was 7.8%](/en/news/gdp-grows-7-8-in-april-june-quarter), the first quarterly reading released on the new base; our [explainer on how GDP is measured](/en/explainer/how-gdp-is-measured-new-base-year) sets out the method.
