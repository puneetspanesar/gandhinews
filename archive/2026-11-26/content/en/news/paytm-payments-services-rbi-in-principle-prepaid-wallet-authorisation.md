The Reserve Bank of India on **November 13, 2026** granted Paytm Payments Services Limited an in-principle authorisation to set up and run a payment system for issuing prepaid payment instruments, the category that includes mobile wallets. The company, a wholly owned subsidiary of One 97 Communications, the parent of Paytm, disclosed the letter to the stock exchanges the same day.

The nod is not a licence to start. The subsidiary can issue wallets only after the central bank grants it a **Certificate of Authorisation**, and the approval is conditional on an audit that has to be completed within six months.

## What the letter says

The letter came from the RBI's Department of Payment and Settlement Systems under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. According to reports of the company's filing, it responds to an application the subsidiary made on December 31, 2025.

The approval is **subject to the Master Directions on Prepaid Payment Instruments of August 27, 2021**, as updated from time to time. The company said that once the final certificate arrives, the subsidiary "can offer pre-paid instruments such as wallets" to customers.

The conditions are specific. The subsidiary must carry out a **system audit that includes a cyber security audit**, conducted by an auditor empanelled with CERT-In, a certified information systems auditor or a diploma holder in information system audit. The audit has to follow the RBI's consolidated cyber resilience and digital payment security directions for non-bank payment operators, which were reported to be dated November 5, 2026.

The report must reach the RBI **within six months of November 13**, that is by May 13, 2027. If it does not, the in-principle authorisation lapses automatically.

## How the wallet business got here

Paytm launched its wallet in 2014. In 2017 the business was moved into Paytm Payments Bank, a separate entity, to meet regulatory requirements. In 2022 the RBI barred that bank from onboarding new customers.

In January 2024 the central bank went further and barred Paytm Payments Bank from accepting fresh deposits and credits, **including wallet top-ups**, citing persistent non-compliance and supervisory concerns. That froze the wallet at the centre of the group's consumer business.

On April 24, 2026, the RBI **cancelled the bank's banking licence** under Section 22(4) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, saying its affairs were being conducted in a manner detrimental to the interest of the bank and its depositors. The regulator said it would seek the winding up of the bank through the High Court.

The new approval has nothing to do with that bank. Paytm Payments Services is a separate subsidiary, and the permission covers it alone. One 97 had said in its first-quarter results release for 2026-27 that the subsidiary had applied for a wallet licence.

## The market and the company

One 97 Communications shares touched a record high of ₹1,960 on November 13, and closed at ₹1,939.90 on the NSE at 3:30 pm, up 0.77% on the previous close, according to market data cited by reports. The stock is up nearly 50% so far this year.

The company's second-quarter results for 2026-27 showed revenue up 28% from a year earlier to ₹2,648 crore, with net profit of about ₹225 crore, according to reports on the results. Brokerages remain split on the stock: targets quoted after the results range from ₹1,340 to ₹2,200.

## Why it matters to users and merchants

A prepaid instrument is money loaded in advance on an app or card and spent later. For Paytm users, a wallet issued by the group's own subsidiary would let them top up and pay inside the app without relying on a bank partner. Payment aggregation for merchants is a separate business that the RBI regulates under its own payment aggregator directions, and the new approval does not touch it.

Nothing changes for customers yet. Until the Certificate of Authorisation is issued, the subsidiary cannot issue wallets, and the RBI has not said how long the review after the audit will take. The central bank has also not commented publicly on the approval beyond the letter.

## What happens next

The next step is on the company's side: commissioning the audit and filing its report with the RBI before May 13, 2027. Only after the central bank examines it can the final certificate be issued. The company has given no launch timeline, and an authorisation lapse is the stated consequence of missing the deadline.

Investors will watch the filing for any update on the audit, and the company's next quarterly results, due in early 2027, for how it describes the wallet plan.
