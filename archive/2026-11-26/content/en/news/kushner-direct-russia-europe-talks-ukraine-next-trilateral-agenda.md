The United States is shifting its Ukraine diplomacy towards **direct contact between Moscow and Europe**, its special envoy Jared Kushner said on Saturday, November 14, in a statement published after a week of talks in Abu Dhabi, Moscow and Ireland. Writing on X, Kushner said Washington would now focus on "facilitating direct engagement between Moscow and European partners" and on preparing the agenda for the next round of three-way talks with Russia and Ukraine. No date or venue for that round has been announced.

## What Kushner said

Kushner said that he and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff had stayed in close consultation with Russia, Ukraine and European partners since the trilateral meetings and their later discussions with Presidents Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelenskyy. He said **several new ideas** were being explored, some of which "may lead to breakthroughs directly" while others are meant to bridge remaining gaps.

He also set out what the US sees as the shape of a settlement. "A lasting peace requires credible security guarantees for Ukraine," he wrote, adding that NATO remains a critical pillar of European security. He said both sides privately accept that **practical compromises will be needed** to end the war, whatever their public positions, and that any final deal would need the consent of the parties concerned.

## How we got here

The first US-brokered Russia-Ukraine talks since February opened in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, November 10, and ended on Wednesday, November 11 with **a large prisoner exchange agreed** but no broader deal, as we reported [when the talks closed](/en/news/abu-dhabi-talks-end-prisoner-exchange-agreed-kushner-medinsky). On Thursday, November 12, Putin received Witkoff and Kushner in Moscow, where the Kremlin called the talks constructive and agreed to plan a humanitarian mission in Kostiantynivka; [our report on that meeting](/en/news/putin-witkoff-kushner-moscow-talks-kostiantynivka-humanitarian-trilateral) records Putin's complaint that Europe "ceased the dialogue with Russia". The envoys then met Zelenskyy in Ireland.

On Friday, November 13, Zelenskyy described what the Americans had raised with him. He said Washington put to him, for the first time, the question: "What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?" Kyiv, he said, will not surrender territory. He also called a possible new US-floated security framework for Europe **"dangerous"**, warning that Moscow could use it to weaken NATO and redraw borders, and he rejected neutrality, which he said would require a change to Ukraine's constitution.

## Where each side stands

Zelenskyy has said the right sequence is **a ceasefire first**, then an end to the war, then security guarantees. He said Ukraine is negotiating a package with partners that would include NATO and EU guarantees, military and financial support for a fixed period and a reconstruction plan, and that he would put any deal to Ukrainian voters only if the US and NATO partners confirm it. He has also said he expects Putin to raise Finland, Sweden, Hungary and the presence of US troops in the Baltic states.

Moscow's line, set out by Putin and Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov this week, is that Russia is open to talks with Europe but that Europe has offered few proposals and has yet to name a negotiator. Putin has repeated that the presence of any foreign troops on Ukrainian territory after a peace deal is "absolutely unacceptable". European governments say decisions on the continent's security must be taken by Europeans and not dictated by Russia. Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland have agreed to send representatives to future talks and have signalled readiness to contribute troops to a "coalition of the willing" once a ceasefire holds.

## Why it matters

Until now the war's diplomacy has run through Washington: the US carried messages between Kyiv and Moscow while Europe argued over who should speak for it. Kushner's statement signals that the US wants Moscow and European capitals to **talk to each other**, a channel Moscow says it is open to and European leaders say they want, provided they sit at the table on equal terms. The sticking points are unchanged: security guarantees for Ukraine, territory in Donetsk and Luhansk, and the future security arrangement for the continent.

For India, the effect is indirect but real. A prolonged war keeps oil, fertiliser and food-shipping prices exposed to the Black Sea and to strikes on energy plants, and India has consistently urged dialogue.

## What happens next

No date has been set for the next trilateral round. The agreed prisoner exchange and the proposed Kostiantynivka humanitarian mission are the nearest concrete steps. One report says Trump is due to meet Putin next week at a summit that also includes China's Xi Jinping, and that Ukraine is not expected to be on the agenda, although Moscow has said the two leaders may take up Ukraine there. Kushner's statement itself names no timetable, only that the US will work on the agenda and that **practical compromises** will be needed before any deal.
