A special court in Ghaziabad on Thursday allowed the Enforcement Directorate **four days' custody of Azam Khan**, giving the agency the Samajwadi Party leader from 4.30 pm on November 12 until 4.30 pm on November 16 to question him in a money laundering case linked to Mohammad Ali Jauhar University in Rampur.

The order moves Khan, who has been in judicial custody since early November, from jail into the agency's hands. The ED told the court that it needed to interrogate him in person to trace the money trail through **multiple entities and shell structures**. The court noted the alleged diversion of public funds and assets worth **₹426.46 crore** and allowed the application under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act.

## What the court allowed

The custody runs for exactly four days, ending at the hour it began. According to reports of the hearing, the court also directed that Khan undergo **medical checks** and be allowed to meet his lawyer daily. Reports differ on where he will be questioned, with some saying the agency's headquarters in Delhi and others saying Lucknow, and the court's order was not available to us in full.

Khan's lawyer argued that the arrest was meant to **harass him**. The agency's case is that **public money was diverted** to the private university, and it says the trail cannot be followed without questioning him.

## How the case reached this point

The money laundering case rests on the dozens of FIRs that Uttar Pradesh Police registered against Khan and his associates, including complaints by farmers who say land was forcibly taken for the campus. The ED also alleges that government money meant for public works was diverted to the private university while Khan was the state's urban development minister.

On October 28 the agency said it had **attached university land and buildings worth ₹1,170 crore**. Days later, on October 30, the Allahabad High Court granted Khan and his son Abdullah Azam Khan bail in the dual PAN card case, though it did not suspend their convictions, and noted that Khan faces 92 criminal cases. Within hours of his release from Rampur district jail, the ED took him into custody. On November 1, a Ghaziabad special court sent him to **14 days of judicial custody**, as we reported in our account of his [plea to the Supreme Court](/en/news/azam-khan-supreme-court-ed-arrest-plea-cji-this-week).

On November 5 the Supreme Court declined to hear his challenge to the arrest itself. A bench led by Chief Justice Surya Kant sent the petition to the Allahabad High Court and asked it to **decide interim relief on November 16**, as we reported in [our story on the transfer](/en/news/supreme-court-sends-azam-khan-ed-arrest-plea-allahabad-hc-november-16). Senior advocate Kapil Sibal had argued before the bench that the money laundering law was being wrongly invoked against his client.

## Why it matters

Khan, 78, is a former Uttar Pradesh cabinet minister and one of the Samajwadi Party's senior-most leaders, and the ED case is the latest in a long run of proceedings against him. Custodial questioning is the stage at which the agency typically seeks to put documents, bank trails and company links in front of the accused, and what Khan says in these four days is likely to shape the agency's arguments when the High Court meets.

For Khan the timing is tight. The custody ends at 4.30 pm on Monday, November 16, the same day the Allahabad High Court is due to take up his plea. Whether the court grants interim relief, or whether the ED seeks further custody or he returns to judicial custody, will decide where he spends the coming weeks.

## What happens next

The Allahabad High Court takes up Khan's plea on **November 16**. The ED must produce him before the special court when the custody ends.
