A minibus carrying, by one official's estimate, about 45 people crossed into the opposite lane on National Highway 715 at Rangajan in Golaghat district on Friday and hit a container truck head-on. **Twenty-six people were killed** and about 20 were injured, among them college students going home. The road runs along the edge of Kaziranga National Park, where speed is meant to be held down because animals cross it.

Everything now written about the cause is preliminary. Police say the driver lost control. Dashcam video from the truck, as described in reports, shows the minibus moving into the truck's lane at high speed. The district administration has ordered an inquiry into whether either vehicle broke speed or passenger-capacity rules, and a forensic team went back to the site on Saturday.

## The warning that was already on file

The most troubling fact is not from the crash. It is from the vehicle's record. The Golaghat District Transport Office found earlier fines against the minibus, including for overspeeding. It was fined ₹4,000 in Jorhat on March 3 for allegedly driving at 102 km an hour, and it carried several overspeeding fines from the Kaziranga stretch itself, some reportedly unpaid. An earlier report put the count at 12 challans worth ₹38,500.

A bus that had been caught speeding again and again, on the very road where speed is restricted, was still carrying passengers. The system recorded the offence each time and then let the vehicle go on. **A fine that can sit unpaid** while the bus runs is not a deterrent. It is a receipt.

## The case for patience, and its limit

The government's likely reply is fair as far as it goes. The inquiry is only days old. A speeding minibus on a long highway is hard to stop before it does harm, and no one should name a cause before the forensic work is done. The Prime Minister has announced ₹2 lakh from the PM National Relief Fund for each person who died and ₹50,000 for each injured person, and the Chief Minister has said he would visit the families.

Relief is right, and it is not accountability. Compensation answers a loss after it has happened. The question for officials is why a vehicle with this record was on the road, and who was responsible for taking it off.

## What should happen

First, the transport department should publish, within 30 days, the full history of this minibus: every challan, every payment, its permit and fitness status, and how many passengers it was licensed to carry.

Second, Assam should make unpaid challans for speeding a bar to renewing a fitness certificate or permit, so that a vehicle with repeated unpaid offences cannot keep earning from passengers. This is the desk's recommendation, and it needs a rule, not a promise.

Third, the Kaziranga stretch needs speed detection that issues a notice at once and flags repeat offenders to the district transport office and the police, so they can be stopped at the next check.

Fourth, the inquiry should state whether the bus was overloaded, and whether the 45 on board were above the permitted number. If they were, the owner and anyone who allowed it should face the law.

Fifth, the findings should be made public, in full, with the names of the officials responsible for each failure.

Twenty-six families do not need another list of condolences. They need to know why a bus the system had already marked as dangerous was allowed to carry their children.
