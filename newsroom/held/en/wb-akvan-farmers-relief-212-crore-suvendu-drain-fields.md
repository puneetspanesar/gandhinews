West Bengal Chief Minister **Suvendu Adhikari on Sunday announced a preliminary ₹212.3 crore package** for more than 5.73 lakh farmers whose crops and horticultural produce were damaged by the rain that followed Cyclone Akvan. He told reporters in Kolkata that about ₹205 crore has already been released from the State Disaster Response Fund for immediate relief, and that a **48-hour drive to pump water out of flooded fields begins on Monday**.

"Ours is a pro-farmer government, and we are very sensitive about these matters. We are standing by the farmers," Adhikari said.

## What the package covers

The state's first estimate puts the damage at **nearly ₹700 crore**, with paddy the largest share. About 1.6 lakh hectares are affected across eight districts: Hooghly, Howrah, Bankura, Purba Bardhaman, South 24 Parganas, North 24 Parganas, Purulia and Purba Medinipur. The crops hit include Aman paddy, mustard, lentil, vegetables, flowers, banana, papaya and betel vines.

Compensation is paid per hectare at three rates: **₹17,000 for irrigated land, ₹8,500 for rain-fed land and ₹22,500 for perennial crops**. The ₹212.3 crore total also covers seed assistance and other components, so it is not the same as the ₹205 crore already released from the disaster fund.

Two smaller lines sit inside the package. About 9,240 betel vine growers, cultivating 385 hectares in Purba Medinipur and in the Shyampur and Udaynarayanpur areas of rural Howrah, have **₹3 crore earmarked** for them. Another ₹5 crore is set aside for free seeds and summer vegetable seed kits, so farmers can restart rabi and summer cultivation once the fields dry.

Separately, about 19,000 farmers in the Gajole, Habibpur and Bamangola blocks of Malda will receive ₹8.12 crore for losses from a hailstorm and squall on November 11. That money is for a different weather event and is not part of the Akvan figure.

## The 48-hour drive

The most immediate part of the plan is the drainage drive. From Monday, November 23, the state will run a **48-hour campaign to drain waterlogged fields**, so that mature Aman paddy can be harvested before it rots in standing water. Pumpsets and labour, including workers under the rural employment scheme, will be deployed **at no cost to farmers**.

The Irrigation Department will monitor the work, with officials of the Disaster Management and Minor Irrigation departments. The Principal Secretary of the Disaster Management Department was to issue written instructions to the district administrations by Sunday night.

## How we got here

Akvan crossed the Odisha coast near Gopalpur in Ganjam district on the morning of Friday, November 20, and weakened as it moved inland. Our [landfall report](/en/news/akvan-deep-depression-monday-imd-landfall-night-nov-19) has the crossing and the evacuation. Its rain spread over south Bengal, and the India Meteorological Department's bulletin of that day asked farmers in Gangetic Bengal, Odisha and Jharkhand to drain fields and hold off rabi sowing, as we reported in our [farm advisory story](/en/news/akvan-rain-bengal-jharkhand-rabi-sowing-fog-imd). Odisha began its own damage assessment on Sunday, covered in our [report on the Odisha assessment](/en/news/odisha-akvan-damage-assessment-100-year-flood-plan).

## What farmers should expect

Loss assessment is still to be done. **Special camps under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana** will carry out detailed crop-loss assessments, with the Agriculture and Horticulture departments overseeing assessment and disbursal and district magistrates monitoring. The package is described as preliminary, so the final sum depends on what those assessments find.

For a farmer with irrigated Aman paddy, the rates mean ₹17,000 for every hectare counted as lost. For a grower of perennial crops such as banana or papaya, it is ₹22,500. These are the state's stated rates; how many hectares each farmer is credited with will come out of the camps.

No reaction from opposition parties or farmers' groups was available at the time of writing.

## What happens next

The drainage drive runs through Tuesday, November 24. After that, the camps and the district-level assessments decide how much of the ₹212.3 crore reaches which farmers, and the state has not yet given a date for disbursal beyond the ₹205 crore already released from the disaster fund.

Adhikari also announced ₹3 crore for projects in Nabadwip in Nadia district on the same day, including ₹2 crore to complete a research centre on Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, but that is a separate announcement from the farm relief.
