New Zealand's first election-night numbers show the race **too close to separate**. With 1.1% of the vote counted at 7:09 pm NZDT (about 11:39 am IST), Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's National Party had 30.79% of the party vote and Labour, led by Chris Hipkins, had 29.88%.

Polls closed at 7 pm local time, 11:30 am IST. The count is at a very early stage and the order has already changed between batches. An earlier snapshot, with 0.4% counted, had National on 32.61% and Labour on 25.27%, and another showed Labour narrowly ahead at 30.6% to 29.9%.

## The smaller parties

The Greens were on 12.32%, ACT on 11.05% and NZ First on 9.3% in the 1.1% count. Te Pāti Māori was on 0.29%, which means little this early, because the Māori electorates report later.

The Opportunity Party, led by Qiulae Wong, stood at 5.72% and, with 6.5% counted at 7:23 pm, was reported **over the 5% threshold for the first time in its history**. A party needs 5% of the party vote, or an electorate seat, to enter Parliament. Opportunity deputy leader Daniel Eb told supporters at 7:15 pm: "You have earned this, and tonight, let's make history."

## What happens next

The Electoral Commission aims to publish results from **about half of the voting places by 10 pm** and about 95% of the preliminary party vote by 11:30 pm NZDT, which is about 4 pm IST. Those are targets, not guarantees.

Special votes, including those cast overseas and outside a voter's own electorate, are not in that count. They will be added later and the official result is due on **November 27**. In 2023 they shifted a handful of seats.

The final polls had neither bloc with a clear path to a majority, with the right-wing bloc of National, ACT and NZ First and the left bloc of Labour, the Greens and Te Pāti Māori both short. Economic weakness and the cost of living dominated the campaign, and unemployment in the September quarter reached its highest level in 15 years. Coalition talks took about four weeks in 2020 and around 40 days in 2023.
