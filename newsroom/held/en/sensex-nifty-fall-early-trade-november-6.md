Dalal Street's six-day rally ran into a wall of selling from Asia on Friday morning. By 9:35 am the **Nifty 50 was down 148.55 points, or 0.60%, at 24,388.05**, its low of the day so far, and the Sensex was down nearly 300 points, or 0.37%, at 79,016.05.

The start had been calm. The Sensex opened 61 points higher at 79,371 and the Nifty only 22 points lower at 24,514, but selling spread within minutes. Smaller companies were hit first, with the **Nifty Smallcap 100 down 1.69%** and the Midcap 100 down 1.14% by 9:25 am.

## Asia takes the overnight blow

The trouble began on Wall Street, where the Nasdaq fell 5.52% on Thursday as chip and data centre stocks crashed. Asia followed on Friday. South Korea's **Kospi fell more than 8% at one point**, setting off a circuit breaker, and Japan's Nikkei 225 lost about 6%. Hong Kong's Hang Seng was down about 1.25%. Brent crude rose about 2% to around $100 a barrel, a worry for India, which imports most of its oil.


## Metals fall, IT holds up

At home, **Nifty Metal fell 2%** and Nifty Realty 1.56%. Hindalco dropped about 5% after its US arm Novelis reported results that raised worries over cash outflows and debt. Thermax sank about 14% after its September-quarter profit fell 5% to ₹113 crore, and Schneider Electric lost 8.6%. Groww's parent fell more than 5% after a block deal of about ₹3,708 crore.

Information technology went the other way. **Nifty IT rose about 2%**, with Infosys and TCS among the top Sensex gainers, as investors moved into exporters that earn in dollars.

Foreign investors have been steady buyers all week. They bought a net ₹14,974 crore of shares on Thursday, their fifth straight day of buying, while domestic institutions sold ₹10,444 crore. The rupee opened 2 paise weaker at 94.49 to the dollar. The US October jobs report, due later on Friday, is the next big test for global markets.
