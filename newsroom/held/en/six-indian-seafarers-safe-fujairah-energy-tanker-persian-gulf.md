The Union government says **all six Indian seafarers aboard the tanker MT Fujairah Energy are safe** after a projectile hit the vessel in the Persian Gulf on Tuesday, November 10. One of the six has minor injuries and has been taken ashore for medical examination.

Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal announced the news on Wednesday, November 11, in a post on X. The Liberia-flagged very large crude carrier (VLCC) was **sailing from Al Ruwais to Dubai** when it was struck, he said.

## What the minister said

Four crew members were injured in the attack, Sonowal said, and one of them is an Indian national. The rest of the crew, including the other five Indians, are "safe and accounted for", according to the post.

The ministry is "continuously monitoring the situation" and is "in close contact with the ship management company" to ensure "the ongoing security, medical care and wellbeing of our seafarers", the minister wrote. The post does not name the injured Indian, his rank or the hospital he was taken to, and does not say how badly the other three injured crew members were hurt or what their nationalities are.

It also leaves open who fired the projectile, what time the ship was hit and how much damage it suffered. No claim of responsibility had been reported by the time of writing.

## A month of attacks on shipping

The strike comes as attacks on merchant ships in and around the Strait of Hormuz continue. Maritime reports put the number of vessels hit in October at **37**, the highest monthly total since the conflict began on February 28, with ten more recorded in November so far. The International Maritime Organization has verified at least 130 attacks on commercial vessels in the region and says 25 seafarers have died in and around the Strait.

Indian crews have borne a share of it. On October 12 we reported that [two Indian seafarers were missing](/en/news/two-indian-seafarers-missing-after-tanker-attacks-off-oman) after drone strikes on tankers off Oman. On November 2, a ship carrying 14 Indian crew was attacked: **one Indian national died** and Omani authorities rescued 13 others. The tanker Velos Sapphire was hit on November 5, and on November 8 our report on [tankers hit by mines and fire in Hormuz](/en/news/hormuz-two-tankers-mines-fire-riyadh-blasts-houthi-missiles-november-8) described the latest escalation.

## What India has said

At a briefing the previous week, External Affairs Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said that **23 Indian nationals have died in the West Asia conflict**, including 15 seafarers. Five Indian nationals have also died in attacks on commercial shipping in the Black Sea region.

On November 10, a day before Sonowal's post, Jaiswal called for free and safe navigation through the Strait of Hormuz. He said India was in contact with all parties concerned to prevent attacks on civilian shipping and seafarers, including Indian-flagged vessels. India has consistently asked for the conflict, which began after US and Israeli attacks on Iran, to be settled through dialogue and diplomacy.

Earlier, the Directorate General of Shipping circular 47 of 2026 advised Indian seafarers on precautions in view of the heightened security situation in the Gulf, and Indian seafarers were asked not to take Hormuz voyages.

## Why it matters

The minister's post is the official confirmation that all six Indians on board are alive. It comes against a toll that shows what is at stake for Indian crews: **15 Indian seafarers have died** in the conflict since February, according to the External Affairs Ministry. This time all six Indians on the ship survived.

## What happens next

The ministry has not said whether the ship will continue to Dubai or whether the injured seafarer will be flown home. Sonowal's post says the monitoring and contact with the ship manager continue. The cause of the strike and the condition of the three other injured crew members have not been announced.
