Shares of ICICI Bank **rose to a record high on Friday**, climbing 2% to ₹1,526.35 on the BSE around 12:30 pm and taking the private lender's market value close to ₹11 lakh crore.

The move capped a strong week for the stock. It has **risen 10% over four sessions**, helped by several factors, among them softening bond yields. Lower yields raise the value of the government bonds that banks hold.

ICICI Bank was among the top gainers on the Sensex from the late morning, and private banks became **the strongest group on the market** as the day went on. At 1 pm the Sensex was up 646.73 points at 79,956.74, even as markets in Japan and South Korea fell sharply after a sell-off in US chip stocks.

## Behind the run

The rally follows a strong set of results. On October 17 the bank reported that its standalone net profit for the July-September quarter **rose 20.1% to ₹14,843 crore**, ahead of forecasts. Its loans grew 30% from a year earlier to ₹18.32 lakh crore, and gross bad loans fell to 1.20% from 1.58% a year ago, though its net interest margin narrowed to 4.18%.

Foreign money has also returned to Indian banks. Foreign institutions **bought ₹14,975 crore of Indian shares on Thursday**, according to provisional exchange data, their biggest day of buying this month so far, while domestic funds sold.

Earlier in the morning ICICI Bank had been up 0.67% and HDFC Bank 0.92%, while the wider market wavered. By the afternoon **ICICI Bank had stretched its lead** to 2%.
