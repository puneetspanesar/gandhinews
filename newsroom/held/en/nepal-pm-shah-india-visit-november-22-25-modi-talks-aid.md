Nepal's Prime Minister **Balendra Shah is expected in New Delhi from November 22 to 25**, his first bilateral visit to India since he took office on March 27, and Nepal has put a request for about $2 billion in flood reconstruction aid at the centre of the agenda. India has not announced dates, but the Ministry of External Affairs confirmed on Tuesday that an invitation has gone out.

## What Delhi has said

At the weekly briefing on November 10, MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said: "We have extended an invitation to the Prime Minister of Nepal to visit India." He added that discussions on the visit are continuing, and that "the entire range of bilateral relations that we have with Nepal will be discussed". He described the two countries as sharing an "expansive and special partnership".

Nepali officials quoted in Kathmandu put the programme at four days from November 22, with **delegation-level talks with Prime Minister Narendra Modi on November 23** and the signing of several agreements. Shah wants a small delegation of fewer than 15 people. India has suggested a stop beyond Delhi, possibly Ahmedabad in Gujarat, which Kathmandu has not confirmed.

## How the visit was prepared

The trip has been planned since early November. Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri travelled to Kathmandu on November 4 and 5, a visit that had been put off since May over protocol issues. We reported the [announcement of Misri's trip](/en/news/vikram-misri-kathmandu-visit-november-4-5-balendra-shah) on November 2. In Kathmandu he met Shah, Foreign Minister Shisir Khanal, Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle and Energy Minister Biraj Bhakta Shrestha, and conveyed Modi's invitation.

The two sides agreed to speed up cooperation on energy, roads, bridges, railways, pipelines, transmission lines and digital connectivity. A Nepali Foreign Ministry statement recorded Nepal's thanks for India's relief after the August 26 floods in the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi corridor and mutual satisfaction with cooperation in trade, hydropower, border management and energy.


## What Nepal wants

**Reconstruction aid is the biggest ask.** A senior Nepali Foreign Ministry official said: "The formal proposal has been tabled by our side. It is now up to India to take a call on it." The request is for about $2 billion, made up of $1 billion in concessional credit lines, $500 million in grants and $500 million for reconstruction and rehabilitation, according to Nepali officials. Misri said in Kathmandu that India is prepared to consider reconstruction proposals.

Other items on Kathmandu's list:

- **Air routes.** Nepal has sought additional air-entry routes from India since 2014 for its Bhairahawa (Gautam Buddha) and Pokhara airports, which struggle to draw international airlines without them. Civil aviation officials are to meet within about 10 days, and both sides have agreed to technical talks on Bhairahawa. No final understanding has been reached.
- **Pancheshwar.** The multipurpose project, conceived under the 1996 Mahakali Treaty, remains largely unbuilt. Cost-sharing talks have narrowed to a 25:75 or 30:70 split between Nepal and India. Water sharing is the main sticking point, and another round of technical talks is planned before the visit.
- **Trade and transit.** Nepal's trade deficit with India keeps growing, and the floods disrupted its main land trade route with China, which has sharpened Kathmandu's interest in more options through India.
- **Boundary.** Kalapani, Limpiyadhura and Susta remain unresolved. They did not come up in Misri's meetings, though Nepal hopes to add them to the visit agenda.

## Projects in the pipeline

Nepali officials expect four projects to be inaugurated: the Nepal Police Academy at Panauti, a bridge in Darchula, the integrated check post at Bhairahawa and a motorable bridge over the Mahakali River. Three more are expected to be launched jointly: an integrated check post at Dodhara Chandani, the Lower Arun hydropower project and a petroleum pipeline from Siliguri to Charali in Jhapa.

India says preparations are complete for an Ayodhya-Janakpur train service, and a trial run was held last Thursday.


## Why it matters

India and Nepal share an open border and more than 40 bilateral mechanisms covering border management, trade, security, water and hydropower, and officials on both sides say the harder task is putting agreements into practice. For Indian readers, the visit bears on **cross-border power and pipeline projects**, the Ayodhya-Janakpur rail link and the open frontier, where the Armed Police Force and the Sashastra Seema Bal agreed late last month to share information on cross-border crime.

For Nepal, observers in Kathmandu see the trip as an early test of whether Shah's government can turn political engagement into money and results on questions earlier governments left open. We reported earlier that [Nepal had acknowledged border encroachment without giving details](/en/news/nepal-acknowledges-india-border-encroachment-no-details).

Asked at the same briefing about the extradition of former Bangladesh Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina, a separate matter, Jaiswal said: "This is a legal matter, and it will be dealt with accordingly."

## What next

Civil aviation officials from both countries are due to meet within about 10 days, and a further round of Pancheshwar technical talks is planned before Shah leaves. Final dates and the programme are to be announced by the two governments. Delegation-level talks are expected on November 23.
