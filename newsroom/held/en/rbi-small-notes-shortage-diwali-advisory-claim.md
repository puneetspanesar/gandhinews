## What we found

The message is **false**. A WhatsApp forward framed as a public advisory from the Reserve Bank of India claimed that new ₹10, ₹20 and ₹50 notes could run short before Diwali. On 2 November **PIB Fact Check labelled the claim fake** and said the RBI had made no such announcement. The forward was still being shared, and reported on, through Tuesday.

The government's most recent statement on small notes points the other way. In March, Minister of State for Finance Pankaj Chaudhary told the Rajya Sabha: "There is **no shortage of lower-denomination currency notes** of INR 10, INR 20 and INR 50 in circulation in the country."

## The evidence

### 1. What the forward said

The message, passed around on WhatsApp and then other platforms, presented itself as an RBI warning to the public that fresh ₹10, ₹20 and ₹50 notes might not be available in enough numbers for the festival season, and urged people to plan ahead.

### 2. The government's fact-checking unit rejected it

The Press Information Bureau's fact-checking unit posted on 2 November: "The claim is FAKE." It said **the RBI had made no such announcement** about a shortage of these denominations and asked people to check the RBI's website and verified accounts before believing or forwarding messages about money.

Sources: [Organiser, 3 November 2026](https://organiser.org/2026/11/03/423652/bharat/fact-check-viral-whatsapp-claim-about-shortage-of-rs-10-rs-20-and-rs-50-notes-ahead-of-deepawali-is-fake/); [NewsBytes, 2 November 2026](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/india/pib-fact-check-debunks-whatsapp-claim-about-diwali-note-shortage/tldr)

### 3. What Parliament was told about supply

Answering a question in the Rajya Sabha on 10 March 2026, Chaudhary said the RBI keeps assessing how many notes of each denomination are needed and recommends the mix the government should print, and that demand for small change is met by a mix of notes and coins. He gave the RBI's supply figures. **Supply of ₹10 notes more than doubled** in 2025-26, while ₹50 supply up to 26 February was lower than the year before.

## Why this claim spread

The forward arrived days before Diwali, when the Supreme Court has allowed green crackers in Delhi-NCR on 7 and 8 November and demand for cash rises. A warning about scarce small notes lands on a real worry, and it told people to plan ahead. But a local shortage at a counter is not a national one, and **any RBI notice would appear on its own website**, not only in a WhatsApp forward. Notes about cash have been a steady target: PIB Fact Check has earlier flagged a false claim that the government would ban ₹500 notes.
