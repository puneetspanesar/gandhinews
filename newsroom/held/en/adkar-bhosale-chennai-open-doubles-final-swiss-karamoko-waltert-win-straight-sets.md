Vaishnavi Adkar and Rutuja Bhosale came up short in the **Chennai Open doubles final on Sunday, November 8**, beaten in straight sets by Switzerland's Naima Karamoko and Simona Waltert at the SDAT Tennis Stadium. The Swiss pair took the second set **6-0**, and with it the WTA 250 title.

The result ends a week in which the two Indians made history. Their semi-final win over Weronika Falkowska and Alana Smith, **6-2, 2-6, 10-8** on Saturday, made them the first all-Indian pair to reach a doubles final on the WTA Tour. On Sunday they met a team that had not dropped a set in its own semi-final, a 6-4, 6-4 win over Isabelle Haverlag and Panna Udvardy.

## How the final went

Waltert's baseline game, and above all her backhand, gave the Swiss the first break, and Karamoko's work at the net kept the Indians under pressure. The Swiss pair led **4-0 in the first set** before Adkar and Bhosale broke Waltert's serve in the fifth game. The Indians had one more chance to get back into the set, but Bhosale put a return into the net on a break point against Karamoko, and the Swiss closed it out.

The second set slipped away early. Bhosale lost her serve after a double fault and a run of errors, and the Swiss led 3-0. Adkar then dropped her own serve after another double fault on the second break point against her. From there the Swiss pair kept the pressure on and finished the set without losing a game. Unforced errors and unconverted points in the close games cost the Indians the match.

The first-set score is reported differently by different sources, so it is not given here. All agree on the straight-sets result and the 6-0 second set.

## The champions

Waltert, 25, from Graubünden, is ranked No. 133 in singles, the highest of the four finalists on individual rankings. This is her **second WTA doubles title**; the first came in Lausanne in 2021, with Susan Bandecchi.

Karamoko, 29, from Geneva, won her first title in her first WTA final. She plays only doubles and is Switzerland's highest-ranked doubles player at No. 99. Her best singles ranking was 730th, in August 2024, and she no longer has one. Within about two weeks she is due to make her debut for Switzerland in the Billie Jean King Cup in Thailand.


## What the week meant for Indian tennis

Adkar is 21 and Bhosale 30.

The title stayed out of reach, but the run stands. After the semi-final Bhosale called reaching the final together "**really special**" and "surreal", while Adkar, who said she had been surprised when Bhosale asked her to partner her, said she was "really glad" to reach her first WTA final. Bhosale had gone one round further than her semi-final exit at last year's event.

The week also gave Indian women's tennis a second headline. Wild card Maaya Rajeshwaran Revathi, 17, reached the singles quarter-finals, before losing to Robin Montgomery.

None of the reports available says where Adkar and Bhosale play next, or what ranking points they take from the week.
