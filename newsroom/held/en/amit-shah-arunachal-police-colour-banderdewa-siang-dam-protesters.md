Union Home Minister Amit Shah said on Saturday that the proposed Siang Upper Multipurpose Project (SUMP) in Arunachal Pradesh will be taken forward "**only with your trust**", answering a memorandum sent a day earlier by two Adi organisations that want the dam dropped. He spoke at the Police Training Centre in Banderdewa, after presenting the President's Police Colour to Arunachal Pradesh Police.

## What Shah told the Siang region

Mr Shah described the 11,200 MW project as **important for the state's development and India's energy security**. He then turned to the Adi community, whose villages and farmland lie in the reservoir area. "I want to assure my Siang brothers and sisters that we will have progressive discussions with you", he said, and promised that the way forward would keep their culture "and everything associated with it as a priority". He asked people to take their concerns to the state government and the Home Department.

The speech was a public address. No report so far says he met the protest leaders face to face, although officials had indicated before the visit that he might meet organisations leading the movement.

## The memorandum he was answering

On Friday, November 20, the **Adi Ba:né Kébang United (ABKU)** and the **Siang Indigenous Farmers' Forum (SIFF)** emailed a joint memorandum to the Home Minister's Office. It carried six demands: an immediate halt to pre-feasibility report (PFR) work, the release of detained protesters, withdrawal of FIRs against them, withdrawal of central forces including the Rapid Action Force from Upper Siang and Siang districts, a judicial inquiry into the October 23 violence, and a clear assurance that the project will not proceed without public consent.

The groups say the proposal was rejected twice by earlier state governments and revived in 2024 without proper consultation. They say they are not against hydropower as such and prefer smaller dams, and are ready to talk on equal terms, but argue that talks held in the presence of armed personnel would not build trust. They also pointed to **conflicting statements from Chief Minister Pema Khandu and Arunachal East MP Tapir Gao**: Mr Khandu has said no dam will be built without public consent, while Mr Gao has said it will go ahead regardless. The memorandum questions the national security case for the dam in view of China's planned dam on the Yarlung Tsangpo.

## How the dispute reached Banderdewa

The opposition turned violent on October 23, when a 12-hour bandh in Siang, Upper Siang and East Siang districts was followed by a night in which a police station, the NHPC office and the homes of state BJP president Kaling Moyong and two MLAs were attacked in Pasighat. The organisations say tear gas and stun grenades were fired at protesters that day. Mr Khandu condemned the vandalism on October 24 and offered to meet opponents "anywhere" convenient. Our [explainer on the dam](/explainer/siang-dam-explained-what-sump-is-why-adi-protest) sets out what SUMP is and why the Adi object, and our [report on the Pasighat violence](/news/pasighat-vandalism-siang-dam-bandh-khandu-khera) covers the bandh night.

Before Mr Shah's arrival on Friday night, the Arunachal Congress asked him to ensure the project is not imposed on indigenous people and said the visit should "go beyond ceremonial engagements", with written assurances and time-bound commitments.

## 'Not a single local terrorist left'

At the same ceremony Mr Shah turned to Kashmir. "In Pulwama, Kashmir, the Kashmir Police neutralised the last known active Kashmiri terrorist affiliated with Lashkar", he said, adding that "there is not a single local terrorist left in the Kashmir Valley. All those who remain are foreign terrorists." He said the abrogation of Article 370 had "put an end to the sentiment of separatism among the youth of Kashmir".

The reference is to Lashkar-e-Taiba's Latif Bhat, killed on November 18. Police and our [earlier report](/news/latif-bhat-lashkar-last-local-terrorist-kulgam-amit-shah-anti-terror-conference) place that encounter in Zangalpora, Devsar, in Kulgam district, not Pulwama. The claim that no local militant remains active is the Home Minister's own and has not been independently tallied.

## The Police Colour

The President's Police Colour is conferred on a force for distinguished service. Mr Shah presented it to Arunachal Police at the parade ground of the Police Training Academy in Banderdewa, near Itanagar, and was due to join the force at a Bada Khana. On November 6 he handed the same honour to [Uttarakhand Police in Dehradun](/news/amit-shah-presidents-police-colour-uttarakhand-police). Mr Khandu said newly recruited police and forest personnel would receive appointment letters at the event.

## What happens next

On Saturday afternoon Mr Shah was due to chair two review meetings at Lok Bhavan in Itanagar, one on development projects and border security and one on Home Ministry matters with the state government. On **Sunday, November 22** he unveils the **Arunachal Pradesh Cooperation Vision Document 2047** at the Dorjee Khandu State Convention Centre, presents the Cooperation Awards and inaugurates the Saras Mela at 10:30 am. At 3:45 pm he opens the **12th Battalion campus of the NDRF at Hollongi**, then returns to Delhi.

Whether the Siang groups get the talks Mr Shah promised, and on what terms, is the open question. The memorandum asks that survey work stop first; the reports of his speech do not mention pausing it.
