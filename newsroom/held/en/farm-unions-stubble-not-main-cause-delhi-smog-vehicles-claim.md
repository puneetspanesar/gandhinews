## What we found

The claim is mostly true on the point that farm fires have fallen, and not yet proven on the point that they are not the principal cause. On November 12, 2026, the Bharatiya Kisan Sangh's **Mohini Mohan Mishra** said "farmers are not the primary cause of pollution" and the All India Kisan Sabha's **Rajan Kshirsagar** said stubble burning "is not the principal reason" for pollution in Delhi and the NCR. Both welcomed the Supreme Court's November 6 order. That order **records a substantial decline in stubble burning**, but it does not rank the causes of Delhi's smog by share.

## The evidence

### 1. What the court wrote

The order, in the suo motu case on air pollution in the National Capital Region, is signed by a bench of the Chief Justice, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V Mohana. After looking at the amici's chart of compliance with the Graded Response Action Plan from October 21 to November 3, it says the data "indicates a **substantial decline in incidents of stubble burning**" in Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana and Rajasthan. It adds that the same data brings out "a converse and equally pressing concern, namely, the **sharp increase in issues attributable to vehicular emissions**." The order gives **no percentage or count** for either, and it does not call vehicles the main cause.

### 2. The fall in farm fires

A written reply by the Environment Ministry in the Lok Sabha on November 25, 2024, gave paddy residue burning events from September 15 to November 18. Punjab had **48,489 in 2022, 33,719 in 2023 and 9,655 in 2024**. Haryana had 3,380, 2,052 and 1,118. Uttar Pradesh's NCR districts went the other way: 72, 108 and 192. In December 2025 the Commission for Air Quality Management reported roughly a 92% overall reduction in NCR against 2021 in this burning, without giving counts.

### 3. What the record does not show

The same ministry reply lists vehicular pollution among several sources of Delhi's pollution and gives no share for it. The order also does not mention the Covid-19 lockdown, although a hearing account says the bench raised it. Nothing in these documents says how much of any given day's PM2.5 came from fires, so "not the principal cause" is a **reasonable reading of falling counts, not a measured finding**.

## Why this claim spread

The order landed in the first week of Stage III curbs, when blame for the smog usually falls on farmers. The unions had long argued that fines and cases against farmers missed vehicles, and a court order pointing at traffic gave them a public platform. The unions' demand to withdraw cases and refund fines is a separate matter that the order does not address.
