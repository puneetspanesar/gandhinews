Carlos Alcaraz beat Ben Shelton **7-6(5), 7-5** in the Paris Masters final in Nanterre on Sunday, November 8, to win the title for the first time. The match lasted 1 hour 57 minutes.

It was the Spaniard's **ninth Masters 1000 title** and his fifth tour-level title of 2026. At 23, he already has seven Grand Slam titles and a first trophy from the indoor Masters in Paris.

## How the final was decided

Shelton, the American third seed, had the early chances. Alcaraz, the second seed, **saved six break points in the first set**, which went to a tie-break and took 65 minutes. He won the tie-break 7-5.

In the second set Shelton broke for a 3-2 lead and served two aces in a row, but Alcaraz broke straight back to level at 4-4. At 6-5 he broke Shelton again to take the set 7-5. He **closed out the match on his second match point** with a two-handed backhand winner down the line.

"Coming here every year, not finding my best tennis but we never gave up," Alcaraz said afterwards. He added that his team had "always said try again" after painful losses in Paris. Boris Becker, a former Paris Masters winner, presented the trophy.

The win also ended a long wait for Spain. Alcaraz is the **first Spanish winner of the Paris Masters since David Ferrer in 2012**, and the title took him to 29 career titles, nine of them at Masters 1000 level and seven at the Grand Slams.

## A reversal of the US Open

The result avenged the match that ended Alcaraz's US Open title defence. Shelton won that quarter-final in New York two months ago in five sets, after 4 hours 28 minutes. Alcaraz has now won **four of their five meetings**.

Shelton was seeking his eighth career title and third Masters 1000. He had won both of his earlier Masters finals, at the Canadian Open in the last two editions, so this was his first defeat in a Masters final. There was a sub-plot on Shelton's side of the net: he has recently added Juan Carlos Ferrero to his team, the coach who parted ways with Alcaraz last year. Both men congratulated each other's shots during the match, and Alcaraz walked around the net to hug Shelton at the end.

## The road to the title

Alcaraz reached the final the hard way. In the quarter-finals he **saved two match points against Taylor Fritz**, and on Saturday he beat Casper Ruud 6-4, 6-4 in the semi-finals. Shelton had beaten Luca Van Assche 5-7, 6-3, 6-4 in his semi-final. The Paris field was missing the top seed: Jannik Sinner withdrew before the tournament with a right knee injury.

The win extends a run that began after a right wrist injury cost Alcaraz time earlier this year. He returned in late August and has since won the Japan Open, the Vienna Open and now Paris. He has won his last nine matches. Only Sinner, who is injured, has more tour-level titles this year, with six.

In the first set Alcaraz held his nerve at the points that mattered, and in the second he found the break back within a game of falling behind, the pattern that decided the match. Shelton, for his part, had won his own semi-final from a set down, a sign of the form he carried into Sunday.

## What happens next

Attention now turns to Turin. The ATP Finals run from November 15 to 22, and Alcaraz is seeking his first title at the season-ending event. He arrives with three titles since returning in August and nine straight match wins. In Paris he had won the French Open twice before, but never this indoor Masters.
