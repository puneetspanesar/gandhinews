## What we found

The claim is **misleading**. The Election Commission's own description of the 13 crore figure is that it counts electors placed in the **absent, shifted, dead or duplicate** (ASDD) category in the draft rolls, across three phases of the Special Intensive Revision. It is not a count of names struck off the final rolls. That does not make the opposition's concern empty: most of those entries are, in practice, entries that will not be on the final lists, and no all-India final figure has been published.

## The evidence

### 1. What the opposition said

Congress leader Pramod Tiwari said outside the Supreme Court in mid-October that "more than 13 crore votes have been cut in the name of SIR." Rahul Gandhi has used the word "stolen" for the same 13 crore, and BJP spokesperson Sambit Patra rejected it in Varanasi on November 2, saying that removal of names during verification is not theft. Aam Aadmi Party's Sanjay Singh has written to the Prime Minister about the "deletion of more than 13 crore names."

### 2. What the Commission says the number is

In statements reported on October 2 and 7, the Commission said the 13 crore figure is misleading. It was drawn from the ASDD list, which covered about **2.8 crore deceased, about 3 crore absent or untraceable, about 6.3 crore permanently shifted and nearly 1 crore duplicate entries**. The Outlook count puts the total at 13.39 crore, out of 95.40 crore voters scrutinised across three phases. J P Nadda gave the same breakdown in his reply to Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge.

### 3. What happens to a name in that category

The Commission says more than 10 lakh booth-level officers checked the ASDD lists, and that the lists were shared booth by booth with over 22 lakh booth-level agents of political parties. Voters had one month after the draft rolls to file claims, a genuine voter left out could apply on Form 6, and decisions can be appealed. It says 2.37 crore voters aged 18 to 28 have been added since the SIR began on June 24, 2025. Those steps are why it says the draft marks are not final deletions.

### 4. Where the opposition has a point

A draft mark is not a deletion, but the names that were not contested did not stay on the rolls. In Uttar Pradesh, our November 8 fact check found the final roll had **2.04 crore fewer electors** than the roll frozen before the revision. Sanjay Singh cited Bihar, where he said only 315 foreign nationals were found among about 65 lakh names removed. We could not check that figure. The Commission has not published a single all-India count of final deletions against additions, and without it neither side's number can be tested.

## Why this claim spread

"13 crore" is a large, round number that fits the opposition's "vote chori" charge, and the Commission's first public answer came days after it spread. Both sides use the same figure for different things. The measure that settles the matter is the final roll against the roll before the revision, state by state, with additions shown. Until the Commission publishes that, this rating stands.
