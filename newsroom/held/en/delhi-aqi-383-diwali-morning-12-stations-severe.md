Delhi woke to a smoggy Diwali on Sunday, with the city's overall air quality index at **383 at 8 am**, in the "very poor" category, according to the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB). The reading was a shade higher than Saturday's 364 at the same hour.

**Twelve stations were in the severe band**, above 400. Jahangirpuri recorded 450, followed by RK Puram at 448, Punjabi Bagh at 446, Bawana at 444 and IIT Delhi at 443. Dwarka Sector 8 (438), Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium (431), CRRI Mathura Road (426), Chandni Chowk (423), Mundka (421), Burari (416) and Alipur (410) were also above the line. ITO stood at 396, IGI Airport Terminal 3 at 359, Anand Vihar at 350 and Badarpur at 349.

## Around the capital

The neighbouring cities fared slightly better. Gurugram recorded 322 and Noida 319, both very poor. Ghaziabad (298), Faridabad (293) and Greater Noida (285) were in the "poor" band.

Experts have pointed to wind speeds below 10 kmph, which leave little ventilation to disperse pollutants. Firecrackers set off through the festival, vehicle emissions and cooler air near the ground add to the load. Crop-residue burning was **589 cases across six states on Friday**, with Punjab alone at 357. Stubble fires contributed about 1.7% of Delhi's pollution on Saturday and were expected to account for 5.4% on Sunday.

## Curbs in force

The Commission for Air Quality Management invoked **Stage 3 of the Graded Response Action Plan** across Delhi-NCR on Friday. It restricts older petrol and diesel four-wheelers, bars dust-heavy construction, and moves schools up to Class 5 to hybrid classes. Since November 1, commercial goods vehicles and buses meeting only BS-IV or lower standards are barred from entering Delhi.

Doctors advise children, older people and those with asthma, COPD or heart disease to limit time outdoors, avoid strenuous activity and wear N95 masks when they must step out. People with breathlessness or chest tightness should seek medical help promptly.

Readings are likely to move through the day as the festival evening begins, and the early warning system has already forecast severe air across the city over the Diwali weekend.
