The Supreme Court on Wednesday, November 25, **set aside every order** of the Allahabad High Court that had asked the CBI and the Enforcement Directorate to verify allegations that Rahul Gandhi holds assets beyond his income. A bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana sent the petition back to the High Court, where a new bench will start the hearing from the beginning.

"All the impugned orders passed by the High Court are set aside," the bench said, as quoted in reports of the hearing. The Chief Justice of the Allahabad High Court is to constitute the fresh bench, and the proceedings **begin afresh in open court**.

## What the Supreme Court directed

The petition was filed by Vignesh Shishir, a BJP worker, who asked the High Court to order a probe into Gandhi, the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and members of his family. Gandhi was not a party to it. The Supreme Court told Shishir to **make Gandhi a respondent** and gave Gandhi the chance to contest whether the writ petition can be heard at all.

The bench kept the merits and the maintainability question open. "All contentions of the parties, including those pertaining to the maintainability of the writ petition, are kept open," it said. Nothing in the order finds the allegations true or false. Gandhi had also asked for the case to be moved from Allahabad to the Delhi High Court; that transfer petition was reported to have been disposed of with the order.

The case title in the Supreme Court is Rahul Gandhi v S. Vignesh Shishir and Others. Senior Advocate Kapil Sibal appeared for Gandhi, and Shishir argued his own case.

## How the case reached this point

In **May 2026** the High Court, acting on Shishir's petition, expected the complaint to be verified as the law allows and told the CBI and the ED to take permissible steps and report on progress. The exact date of that order was not reported.

On **July 20** a bench of Justices Brij Raj Singh and Rajesh Singh Chauhan said the CBI's counter-affidavit did not comply with the earlier direction. It noted that the ED had taken the required steps and could proceed if its inquiry turned up material. The matter was posted for August 20.

Gandhi went to the Supreme Court. On **August 17** it issued notice on his petition, told the High Court to defer further proceedings and asked why orders touching him had been passed without hearing him. It also told the CBI and the ED not to file reports in response to the High Court's directions.

## The two sides in court

For Gandhi, Sibal called the High Court proceedings a "**witch hunt through a process not recognised by law**". He questioned Shishir's standing and good faith, pointing to his repeated petitions against Gandhi and what he said was a failure to disclose an earlier High Court order criticising him. Gandhi's side also asked how confidential steps in the verification had come to be published in newspapers.

Additional Solicitor General S V Raju, appearing for the agencies, called the allegations "very serious". He said the CBI was **only verifying the complaint** and had not registered an FIR.

The Chief Justice observed that the police need no court's permission to act in a case such as murder, but that when a court itself issues directions it is expected to follow the principles of natural justice. No statement from the Congress or the BJP on the order had been found when this report was written.

## Why it matters

For Gandhi, the order removes, for now, a court direction under which the central agencies were examining his finances without his having been heard. For the petitioner, the High Court route stays open, but the first question will be whether his petition can proceed. The Supreme Court did not say how the new bench should decide that.

Any later direction to the CBI or the ED would now have to come from a bench that has heard Gandhi first.

Shishir has a separate petition in the High Court alleging that Gandhi holds a British passport, which he says conflicts with citizenship law. A direction in that case to register an FIR was held back by the High Court, and the matter remains pending.

## What happens next

The Chief Justice of the Allahabad High Court will constitute the new bench. Shishir must first amend his petition to add Gandhi as a respondent, and Gandhi will then be heard on maintainability. No date has been reported for the first hearing before the fresh bench.
