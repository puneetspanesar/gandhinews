Indian households should brace for **a long spell of expensive food**, economists said on Tuesday, as the weakest monsoon in more than a decade hits crops, animal feed and sugar output at the same time.

In Rajewadi village in Maharashtra's Jalna district, where rain has been **about 48% below average** this year, 78-year-old farmer Amarsingh Gushinge cannot sow wheat or chickpeas for the winter. "There's not enough moisture in the soil to sow the next crop, and no money left to do so either," he said.

## From grain to protein

Retail food inflation **rose to 7.74% in September**, while headline inflation reached 5.54%, close to the Reserve Bank's upper limit of 6%. Devendra Pant, chief economist at India Ratings and Research, expects food inflation to **reach about 10% in the October-December quarter**, and the RBI to raise rates by a further 50 to 75 basis points, starting with a second straight hike in December.

Protein is the hardest part to tame. Corn and soybean feed make up 60% to 70% of the cost of raising poultry, said Uday Singh Bayas, president of IPEMA-Poultry India, and producers have not yet passed on all of it. "Prolonged protein inflation could hurt lower-income households and force them to alter their diets," said Madan Sabnavis, chief economist at Bank of Baroda.

Sugar is the sharpest pinch, **up nearly 30% over the past year**. Consumption is expected to top 29 million tonnes against output below 27.5 million, even after the government allowed 1 million tonnes of duty-free imports. With world prices above India's and a weak rupee, imports offer little relief.
