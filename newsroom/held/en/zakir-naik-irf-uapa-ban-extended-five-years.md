The Union Home Ministry has extended the ban on the Islamic Research Foundation (IRF), the organisation founded by preacher Zakir Naik, for **five more years** under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA). The notification is dated Friday, November 13, 2026, a day before the earlier ban was due to run out.

It is the third time the Centre has declared the Mumbai-based group an unlawful association. The order was issued under Sections 3(1) and 3(3) of the 1967 law and **takes effect immediately**, with the five years counted from its publication in the Official Gazette, subject to any order of the UAPA tribunal under Section 4.

## What the notification says

The ministry says the IRF has been engaged in activities **prejudicial to the security of the country** and capable of disturbing peace and communal harmony and undermining India's secular fabric. It names the foundation's founder and president, Dr Zakir Abdul Karim Naik, and says some of his speeches were "objectionable and subversive".

According to the notification, Naik encouraged religious enmity and hatred between communities and inspired young people in India and abroad to commit terrorist acts. It says his statements reached millions of people through satellite television, the internet, print and social media. The ministry also fears that, if the restrictions were lifted, the group could **regroup its absconding activists**, support militancy and secessionism, and weaken India's sovereignty and integrity. These are the government's allegations; no response from the IRF or Naik was available on Sunday morning.

## Shah: "zero tolerance"

Home Minister Amit Shah announced the extension on X. He said it reflected the government's "zero tolerance towards terrorism and anti-national activities", described the group as "led by fugitive terror preacher Dr Zakir Naik" and accused it of "sinister anti-India designs", including inciting communal hatred and promoting terrorism. He added that it was "propagating a venomous ideology of terrorism and separatism".

## How we got here

The IRF was set up in Mumbai in February 1991. The Centre first banned it under the UAPA on **November 17, 2016**, a few months after a cafe attack in Dhaka on July 1, 2016, that killed more than 20 people, including 17 foreigners. One of the attackers had said he was inspired by Naik's speeches. Naik, who denies any role in violence, left India that year.

The ban was renewed on **November 15, 2021**. In each case the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Tribunal examined the government's grounds and confirmed the declaration, in orders dated May 22, 2017, and March 30, 2022. The 2026 order follows the same path: under the Act, the Centre must now refer the declaration to the tribunal, which decides whether there is sufficient cause for it.

Naik now lives in Malaysia, where he was granted permanent residency while Mahathir Mohamad was prime minister. India has repeatedly sought his extradition on money laundering and incitement charges. He visited Pakistan twice, meeting Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif in Islamabad in October 2024 and travelling there again in February 2025.

## What an unlawful association ban means

Under the UAPA, an association declared unlawful can have its premises and funds notified and seized, and **membership or support of it becomes an offence**. In practice the declaration keeps the IRF from operating in India, holding events or raising money, and gives investigators a firmer legal footing to act against anyone who works for it.

For readers, the immediate effect is limited: the foundation has been barred since 2016, and the notification means that bar does not lapse on November 14. Without it, the group could have resumed work in India from the next day.

## What happens next

The Centre is expected to send the declaration to the tribunal, which will issue notice to the IRF to show cause why it should not be declared unlawful. Hearings in the earlier rounds ended in orders in May 2017 and March 2022, about six months and four months after the declarations. The IRF can contest the notification before the tribunal and then in the courts. Naik's extradition from Malaysia remains pending, and no date has been reported for any step in that process.
