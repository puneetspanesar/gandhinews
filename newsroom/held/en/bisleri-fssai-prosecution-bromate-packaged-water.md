The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has **sanctioned prosecution of Bisleri International** Pvt Ltd, the country's best-known packaged drinking water brand, after a regulatory sample was declared unsafe. The regulator announced the decision on Sunday, November 22, 2026, in a social media post. Bisleri responded on Monday, November 23, saying its own tests found the water within limits and that it had **never received any notice** from the regulator.

## What the regulator says

According to reports of the FSSAI announcement, a regulatory sample of "Packaged Drinking Water (other than Mineral Water)" was collected from Bisleri's premises. It was found non-compliant with the prescribed bromate limits and declared unsafe under the Food Safety and Standards (FSS) Act, 2006. After the company appealed, the sample went to a referral food laboratory, which **also declared it unsafe, this time for polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)**. FSSAI said the findings established violations relating to unsafe food and granted sanction to prosecute.

The same announcement sanctioned prosecution against four other food businesses, Vasant Masala, Tit-Bit Foods India, Mimansa Industries and Asha Confectionery. It also suspended the licences of Urmi Lifesciences LLP and Gaur Ayurvedic Store and ordered Asha Confectionery to recall chocolate-filled cookies.

## The sample, the batch and the plant

Reports say FSSAI drew **six samples at Bisleri's Goa plant on September 30, 2025**. Five met every applicable standard. The sixth showed bromate at **12 micrograms a litre, against a limit of 10 micrograms a litre**. Bisleri says the case concerns a single batch made at Goa in September 2025. No batch number has been published. The Goa unit is one of 143 plants in the company's network.

Bisleri appealed on November 22, 2025, exactly a year before the sanction, and the sample went to the referral laboratory. According to the company, that laboratory found bromate within the prescribed limit. Reports say it separately flagged PAHs, and the regulator's case now rests on that finding. The reports do not give the PAH readings or say which compounds were found.

## Bisleri's response

In its statement the company said **its drinking water is safe**. It said it had the sample re-tested at an FSSAI-approved laboratory, which also found bromate within limits, and sent those results to FSSAI's Western Regional Office in a letter dated February 2, 2026. After that, it said, it heard nothing and understood the matter to be closed. It said it had received the reported notice neither by email nor in hard copy and could not find it on the FSSAI website. It described the case as about 14 months old.

The company said its process involves **more than 90 quality checks across 10 stages**, that "drinking water safety is a priority for Bisleri", and that it would cooperate with the regulator and pursue the matter through the regulatory process. Its independent results address bromate only; the statement as reported does not answer the PAH finding.

According to a report on the dispute, FSSAI plans to test more Bisleri samples across the country as well as packaged water from other brands, and a government source said the affected batches had been recalled and later testing found no water-quality problems. Neither point has been confirmed in an FSSAI document seen by this desk.

## What bromate is and what the limit says

Bromate is a chemical that can form when water containing bromide is disinfected with ozone, a method many packaged water plants use. Long-term exposure above safe levels is a concern because bromate is classed as a possible human carcinogen. The Indian standard for packaged drinking water, IS 14543, caps it at **10 micrograms a litre (0.01 mg a litre)**, and FSSAI's packaged water rules adopt the same limit. PAHs are a family of compounds that can enter water through environmental contamination, and some of them are carcinogenic.

## What prosecution under the FSS Act means

Under the Act, food that fails the standards in a way that makes it injurious to health is "unsafe food". The Commissioner of Food Safety sanctions prosecution, after which the case is filed against the food business operator in court. A company can first contest a result by asking for the sample to be tested at a referral laboratory, which Bisleri did. Reports do not say which sections FSSAI invoked.

Penalties for selling unsafe food are set in Section 59 and rise with the harm caused: **up to six months in prison and a fine of up to ₹1 lakh** where no injury results, up to one year and ₹3 lakh for non-grievous injury, up to six years and ₹5 lakh for grievous injury, and seven years to life with a fine of at least ₹10 lakh where a death results. Nothing in the reports suggests anyone was harmed. Penalties fall on the company and, in some cases, on its responsible officers.

## What it means for consumers

The finding concerns one sample from one batch made in September 2025, not Bisleri water generally. Consumers need take no special step beyond what applies to any bottle: check the **batch number and best-before date** on the label, buy sealed bottles with a visible FSSAI licence number and an ISI mark, and avoid bottles left in sun or heat. Complaints can be lodged with FSSAI through its food safety connect app or helpline.

## What happens next

The case now moves to the courts, where Bisleri can contest both findings. The company has not said whether it will challenge the sanction itself or the PAH result. FSSAI has yet to publish the order, the batch details or the lab reports, and the wider sampling reported by the regulator is still to be confirmed. Readers can expect the first court listing to be the next public marker.
