Tamil Nadu signed **78 investment agreements worth ₹58,214 crore** on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, at the second Vettri Tamil Nadu Investment and Conversion Conclave, held near Kovalam in Chengalpattu district outside Chennai. Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay presided. The state says the agreements could create **65,786 jobs**.

The agreements are memoranda of understanding, statements of intent between the state and companies. None of the ₹58,214 crore has yet been spent, and the reports from the day describe the figures as proposed commitments.

## What was signed

According to the figures released by the Chief Minister's Office, the day had four parts. Besides the 78 MoUs, Vijay laid **foundation stones for 15 projects worth ₹7,925 crore**, expected to employ 19,965 people. He also inaugurated six completed projects worth ₹1,430 crore, with 1,710 jobs, and handed appointment orders to 18 candidates selected by five companies. Taken together the investment comes to about ₹67,569 crore and the jobs to about 87,461.

Of the 78 MoUs, 58 are new factories or facilities (**64% of the investment value**), 18 are expansions and two cover both. Fourteen companies from Brazil, Finland, Germany, Singapore, South Korea, Sweden and the United States account for ₹14,798 crore of foreign investment.

## The big names and where the money goes

The largest single agreement is **₹8,140 crore from CSE Development India** for renewable energy projects across several districts, with about 2,000 jobs. JM Baxi Ports and Logistics signed for ₹5,200 crore and 7,500 jobs. Avigna Private Limited pledged ₹5,000 crore, though for only about 300 jobs.

Among the vehicle and electronics makers, **Royal Enfield committed ₹1,000 crore for electric motorcycles**, with about 500 jobs. TVS Motor signed for ₹2,000 crore in automotive components at Krishnagiri, with 1,000 jobs, and Titan for ₹1,500 crore in electronics, also at Krishnagiri. Brazil's WEG chose Dharmapuri for a ₹2,000 crore plant for EV motors, industrial drives and power equipment, with about 3,000 jobs. Sanmar Group will spend ₹1,950 crore on shipbuilding at Thoothukudi, Emami Agrotech ₹1,380 crore in Virudhunagar and Apollo Tyres ₹1,100 crore in Ranipet. The two Hyundai suppliers, Hwashin Automotive India and Seoyon E-Hwa Summit Automotive India, together pledged ₹1,370 crore.

The state also listed 12 aerospace and defence agreements worth ₹3,620 crore and about 3,275 jobs, 12 multimodal logistics parks worth ₹15,250 crore and about 8,375 jobs, and a drone industrial cluster at Kanadukathan in Sivaganga district. Amazon Seller Services signed to support e-commerce exports.

Eighteen MoUs, worth **₹17,413 crore and 16,411 jobs**, are meant for eight industrially backward districts: Sivaganga, Dharmapuri, Virudhunagar, Cuddalore, Villupuram, Ramanathapuram, Tiruvannamalai and Theni.

## What an MoU commits

An MoU records that a company intends to invest a stated sum at a stated place and that the state will help with land, approvals and incentives. It is not a licence, a loan or a payment. The money moves only when land is allotted, approvals are in and the company decides to build. The job figures are projections made at signing.

The jobs per rupee vary widely. Titan's ₹1,500 crore is projected to employ about 3,000 people, while Avigna's ₹5,000 crore is projected to employ about 300.

## How earlier pledges have fared

This is the second Vettri conclave. The first, in August, produced **97 MoUs worth ₹67,452 crore** and 1,06,998 projected jobs. The government says it has signed 190 MoUs worth about ₹1.73 lakh crore since taking office in May, with 1,98,557 jobs projected.

On conversion, of the 112 MoUs signed before November 25, worth about ₹1.15 lakh crore, **83 had reached land allotment or beyond, or 74.1%**, according to figures presented at the conclave. That counts a step before construction, not factories producing goods. The remaining 25.9% had not reached land allotment.

Industries Minister S. Keerthana told the conclave the state's "real competition is not another state, but the world's leading manufacturing economies." Of delays in clearing projects she said: "For the first time, delay has an owner." She also said: "Politicians usually tell you only where they are winning. I am telling you where the work is left."

The government listed the changes it says make it easier to invest. **Industrial approvals can come in 21 days or less**, more than 25 reforms were made through 23 government orders across 11 departments, and trade licences now stay valid for 10 years instead of three. A semiconductor incentive scheme began in October, and a project-wise status report is presented to the Chief Minister to track bottlenecks.

## The political argument

The opposition has questioned the government's record on investment. On November 13, AIADMK general secretary Edappadi K. Palaniswami said the government had fallen short in securing new industrial projects. DMK leader T.R.B. Rajaa, a former industries minister, said six months into office the government had not brought projects beyond those begun under the DMK government and was claiming them as its own. "Inherited momentum is NOT an industrial strategy," he wrote. He also claimed that Andhra Pradesh had attracted ₹5.35 lakh crore in investment MoUs in one day. Neither remark was a response to the November 25 deals, and no reaction to them had come by the evening.

## What happens next

The next test is conversion: whether the 78 new MoUs reach land allotment and construction, and whether the 29 earlier MoUs still short of land allotment get there. The government has said project-wise status reports go to the Chief Minister, but it has announced no date for a public update.
