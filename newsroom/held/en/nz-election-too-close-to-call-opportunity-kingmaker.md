New Zealand's general election was **too close to call** on Saturday night, with Chris Hipkins' Labour and Christopher Luxon's National level at roughly 30% each and a first-time party poised to decide who forms the next government.

With about 30% of votes counted at 7:13 pm local time (11:43 am IST), Labour had edged ahead on **29.9% to National's 29.4%**, after National led in the first returns. The Greens were on a record 14%, ACT on 10.5% and NZ First on 9.4%. Polls had closed at 7 pm New Zealand time, 11:30 am IST.

## Opportunity in the middle

The Opportunity Party stood at 5.4%, over the 5% bar needed to enter Parliament without winning an electorate, and is projected to win **about six seats** in its first election. Neither the centre-right coalition of National, ACT and NZ First nor the left bloc of Labour, the Greens and Te Pāti Māori looked able to reach a majority without it. Projections had the blocs within a seat or so of each other.

Opportunity leader Qiulae Wong told supporters: "You have voted to change New Zealand politics for good." Te Pāti Māori led in five electorates.

## National's vote falls

National's share is down sharply from the 38% it won in 2023. The party is expected to win more electorate seats than its party vote entitles it to, creating an overhang that would mean it adds no list MPs. Finance Minister Nicola Willis, Defence Minister Paul Goldsmith and Speaker Gerry Brownlee were reported to be at risk of losing their seats.

ACT leader and Deputy Prime Minister David Seymour called it "a record result for ACT tonight" and said: "It's going to take some time before it becomes clear." National's Mark Mitchell described the race as "very close", and Luxon had urged voters earlier to "stick to the plan that's working."

## Weeks, not hours

The Electoral Commission aims to release preliminary results from 95% of voting places by the end of election night. Special votes and ballots from New Zealanders abroad will be added, with the **official result due on November 27**, and negotiations over a government could run beyond that.

The campaign lasted six weeks and turned on the cost of living. National promised to return the budget to surplus and expand the national pension scheme, while Labour offered cheaper healthcare and transport. It is a rematch of 2023, when Luxon beat Hipkins.
