Paraguayan prosecutors on Saturday **charged former president Santiago Peña** with crimes linked to the collapse of Ueno Bank and asked a judge to hold him in pre-trial detention, a day after the Senate threw him out of office. The judge had not ruled by Sunday afternoon local time.

Peña is accused of breach of trust as an instigator and of self-serving administration as the perpetrator. He appeared before prosecutors in Asunción, testified and denies any wrongdoing. Prosecutors also charged Carlos Carvallo, the former Central Bank president, who is already in custody. Carvallo faces counts of breach of trust, producing false public documents and self-serving administration. He ran Ueno Bank before Peña appointed him to head the Central Bank.

## What prosecutors allege

According to prosecutors, **from 2023 to 2026 Ueno Bank took large deposits** from public bodies and the pension system while making allegedly irregular loans to companies linked to its owners, the Vázquez Group. Lawmakers who voted to remove Peña made a related case: that he used political influence to steer hundreds of millions of dollars in public funds into the bank and ignored risky insider lending. Peña was himself a shareholder in the lender.

Ueno Bank was a U.S.-owned digital lender serving about three million customers. On Tuesday, November 3, the Central Bank seized it because its solvency ratio no longer met legal standards. Savings were frozen overnight, and protests broke out across Asunción.

## How he lost the presidency in three days

Peña announced his resignation on Thursday, November 5, and apologised in a speech posted on social media: "I want to apologize to the Paraguayan people if I have failed you." Congress refused to accept it and pressed on with the impeachment. Opposition lawmakers wanted a removal on the record so that he could not step down with immunity.

On Friday, November 6, after a trial lasting about ten hours, the Senate voted at about 6:55 pm local time. **Forty-four of the 45 senators present voted to remove him**, including members of his own Colorado Party, finding him guilty of malfeasance in office. It was the first removal of a Paraguayan president since Fernando Lugo in 2012. Prosecutors ordered his arrest the same day.

Peña had been under house arrest. After protesters threw stones at police and tore down fences near his Asunción residence, he was moved overnight to the barracks of the Presidential Escort Regiment. On Saturday morning he went to the attorney general's office for questioning, and was not handcuffed.

## The new government

Vice President **Pedro Alliana was sworn in** and will serve out the term to August 2028. He has named Ariel Martínez interior minister and Juan Carlos Baruja public works minister. Both are close to former president Horacio Cartes, and Baruja also heads the Colorado Party. Alliana has suspended foreign travel to focus on the crisis and pledged that all affected depositors will recover their savings.


## Paying savers back

The Central Bank said on Saturday that **1.2 million Ueno customers have received** partial or full reimbursement of insured deposits. Customers with balances up to about $1,500 have started getting their money. Later payouts will extend to an initial maximum of 40 minimum wages, about 121 million guaraníes (roughly $19,300). Banks open on Sunday so affected customers can open new accounts and complete the paperwork.

## Reactions and what comes next

The opposition and street protesters are not satisfied. They accuse Alliana of failing to act in time to prevent the collapse, want him removed too, and are calling for **early elections in early 2027**.

The next step is the judge's ruling on whether Peña stays in custody while the case is prepared. On Monday, November 9, Paraguay's largest teachers' union, with more than 40,000 members, plans an **indefinite national strike over public pension funds**, over the money of public bodies and the pension system that prosecutors say Ueno held.
