An explosion and fire at the Habas iron and steel plant in Aliaga, in western Turkey's Izmir province, **killed 10 workers on Monday** and injured 15, Izmir governor Suleyman Elban said. Four people are believed to be still trapped under the wreckage, and search teams were working through the debris when the latest figures were released.

The blast came around noon local time (2:30 pm IST) at the plant in the Bozkoy neighbourhood of Aliaga, about 60 km north of the city of Izmir. A thick column of smoke rose over the site as firefighters, ambulances and rescue crews arrived. Two of the injured are in critical condition.

## What the governor's office says

The governor's office has given a preliminary cause: **an overhead crane broke loose and fell into an electric arc furnace** while liquid steel was being cast. It has not called that the final finding, and said an investigation has been launched to establish exactly what happened.

The toll climbed through the afternoon. The first statement spoke of three dead, 14 injured and about 10 people trapped. A later one said six were dead, after one seriously injured worker died in hospital and two bodies were recovered from the rubble. By about 4:45 pm local time (7:15 pm IST), the governor put the death toll at 10, with **15 people in hospital and four still missing**.

Izmir Mayor Cemil Tugay wrote on X that rescue teams were removing a collapsed platform, and that "unfortunately, we have received preliminary information indicating a large number of deaths and injuries" among the factory's workers. He said the exact number would be known only once the wreckage had been cleared.

## Inquiries opened

Justice Minister Akin Gurlek said **four public prosecutors had been assigned** to the case. Labour Minister Vedat Isikhan said the Social Security Institution had sent a chief inspector to investigate and that his ministry was following developments.

Interior Minister Mustafa Ciftci said emergency teams were coordinating firefighting, search and rescue, and that the injured had been taken to hospitals where they were being closely monitored. Vice President Cevdet Yilmaz sent condolences to the families of the dead, and Communications Director Burhanettin Duran said rescue and emergency operations were continuing. Trucks and heavy vehicles operating around the plant were halted to keep roads clear for emergency traffic.

## Witnesses

People near the plant described the force of the blast. Nuri Ali Ulker told the Anadolu news agency: "The ground shook. It was very intense." Deniz Ulker, a passer-by, said it felt like the earth's crust was shifting beneath them and that he thought it was an earthquake. Truck driver Turan Ilbay compared it to a bomb going off. Footage from the scene showed a large smoke cloud and flames engulfing a tall structure.

## The plant and its record

Habas was established in 1987 and says on its website that it can produce **4.5 million tons of steel a year**. The company had not issued a statement in the reports available by the evening.

The site had already seen deaths in recent weeks, according to the Evrensel daily. Eyup Eginli, 20, died on November 5 when a heavy vehicle's tyre exploded during inflation, and Hakan Dogan, 55, died on October 21 after falling about eight metres.

Aliaga is best known for its port and its shipbreaking yards, which critics say have caused serious environmental damage. The blast came on the day world leaders and campaigners were gathered in Antalya for the COP31 climate talks, which [we reported on](/en/news/cop31-opens-antalya-turkey-australia-negotiations-india-position).

## A hazardous year for Turkish workers

Workplace safety in Turkey has long been under scrutiny. The independent monitor ISIG, which counts informal and self-employed workers too, says at least **1,736 workers died on the job** in the first nine months of 2026, including 226 in September. September was the fifth month in a row with more than 200 deaths, the first such streak since the group began publishing reports in 2013. Its totals are revised as more deaths are documented.

The leading causes, it says, are traffic incidents, falls from height, and crushing or collapse. Construction and roadwork accounted for 50 of the September deaths, agriculture and forestry for 45, and transport for 28.


## What happens next

Rescuers will keep searching for the four missing workers, and the toll could change as the debris is cleared. The four prosecutors and the Social Security Institution's inspector are expected to examine the crane, the furnace and the plant's safety procedures, and to establish who was responsible. No date has been given for the findings.
