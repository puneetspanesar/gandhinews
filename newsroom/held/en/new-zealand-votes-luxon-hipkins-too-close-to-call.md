New Zealand is voting on Saturday in a general election that most polls say is too close to call, with Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's National-led coalition seeking a second term against a Labour Party led by Chris Hipkins.

Polling stations opened at 9 am local time and **close at 7 pm, which is 11:30 am IST**. The Electoral Commission expects to report preliminary results from half of the voting places by 10 pm local time (2:30 pm IST). Official results, including special votes such as overseas ballots, are due on **November 27**.

## A race inside the margin

The polls do not agree on who is ahead. One survey has Labour on 30.7% and National on 26.4%. Two others put National narrowly in front: 29.5% to 28.8% in one and 28.9% to 28.4% in the other.

"It is going to be close," Luxon said as the campaign wound up. Hipkins said he was "increasingly confident" that Labour would emerge as the biggest party, but that it was too early to talk about coalitions.

New Zealand elects 120 MPs by proportional representation, and a bloc needs a majority to govern. Only Labour, in 2020, has won an outright majority since the system began in 1996. National governs with ACT, led by David Seymour, and NZ First, led by Winston Peters, and either would be needed again.

## The small party in the middle

The centrist Opportunity Party has polled between **5.1% and 7.2%**, just above the 5% threshold for seats in Parliament. If it gets in, neither side may reach a majority without it. Luxon has ruled out working with Labour, the Greens, Te Pāti Māori and Opportunity, but not with ACT and NZ First.

Commentators expect it could take weeks to form a government. Left-leaning parties are aiming to make this the first one-term government in nearly 50 years.

## The economy decides

The campaign has centred on the cost of living. Growth is subdued, household budgets are stretched, and unemployment rose to **5.7% in the third quarter**, its highest in 11 years.

Turnout has been strong. About 1.67 million people had voted by Thursday, more than a third of those eligible, and advance voting in the first week reached 913,762 ballots, 28% more than at the same stage in 2023.

Election-day rules bar campaigning and anything that could sway voters until polls close, with fines of up to NZ$20,000 for breaches.
