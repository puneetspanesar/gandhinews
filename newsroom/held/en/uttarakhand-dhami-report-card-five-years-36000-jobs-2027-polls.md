Uttarakhand Chief Minister Pushkar Singh Dhami on Thursday released a **16-page report card of his government's five years** in Dehradun, saying it had delivered "almost 70 per cent" of what the BJP promised before the 2022 election and that the party was "ready to form government for the third time". The state votes early in 2027.

Dhami, who took over as Chief Minister in July 2021, said the party would "go to the public with this report card". He did not say which promises were still pending. Former Chief Ministers Vijay Bahuguna, Ramesh Pokhriyal Nishank, Tirath Singh Rawat and Trivendra Singh Rawat, Rajya Sabha member and state BJP president Mahendra Bhatt, ministers and MLAs were among those present.

## What the report card claims

Jobs come first. The document says **about 36,000 young people have got government jobs** in five years, which Dhami described as recruitment "without slip, without expenditure", a Hindi phrase meaning no recommendation and no bribe. He pointed to a **strict anti-cheating law** for recruitment exams, which provides for life imprisonment in serious cases and fines of up to ₹10 crore.

On youth schemes, 106 young people have been placed in jobs abroad under the state's global employment scheme, and 11,000 will get free online coaching for competitive exams in the first phase of the new Mukhyamantri Bhavishya Nirman Yojana. Dhami also said a Yuva Aayog, or youth commission, is being worked on and will be formed soon.

For women, the report card cites a **30% horizontal reservation in government jobs** and says over 3 lakh women have become "Lakhpati Didis". On health, it says 63.32 lakh Atal Ayushman cards have been issued. For farmers, it lists interest-free loans of up to ₹3 lakh.

The state's flagship legal change also features: Uttarakhand brought the Uniform Civil Code into force on January 27, 2025, which the report card presents as a first for any state. A relief package of about ₹200 crore for tourism, transport and culture in 2021, and the Vatsalya scheme for children orphaned by COVID-19, are listed as pandemic-era measures.

## Investment and the pilgrim count

The report card repeats the government's headline investment claim: **₹3.56 lakh crore in proposals** at the Global Investors Summit, of which over ₹1 lakh crore has, in the government's words, reached the ground, along with more than 30 new policies. It also says over 65 lakh pilgrims visited the Char Dham this year, up from the 60 lakh Dhami announced in October, and that Uttarakhand athletes won 103 medals at the 38th National Games.

The investment figure is the most contested number in the document. On October 18, Lok Sabha Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi called the summit "a sham in the name of investment" and said **₹35,279 crore of the ₹1 lakh crore came from state-owned power companies**. He asked Dhami to publish the full list of 3,300 "investors", as we reported at the time. We found no state government reply to that charge in the reports of Thursday's event.

## How we got here

Dhami has led the Uttarakhand government since July 2021, and the BJP is seeking a third consecutive term after the 2022 mandate his report card measures itself against.

The campaign for 2027 has been under way for weeks. On November 2, Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge said the BJP's "double-engine" government had **"betrayed the hopes and aspirations"** of the state's people after a poll review in Delhi with Rahul Gandhi, K C Venugopal, Kumari Selja and state Congress chief Ganesh Godiyal. Venugopal named leaked exam papers and the Ankita Bhandari case as grievances. Dhami's report card answers the first with the anti-cheating law and the jobs figure.

## Why it matters

These are the figures voters will hear until polling day. Government jobs, the exam-cheating law and women's reservation speak to the young and to families in a hill state where recruitment-paper leaks have been a live grievance. The investment number matters for a different reason: it is the one figure that has already been challenged on its method, and neither side has published the underlying list.

The numbers are the government's own and were not independently checked. Where reports differed, for instance on the count of Lakhpati Didis and how far the Rishikesh-Karnaprayag rail line is complete, we have used the lower, agreed figure or left the item out.

## What happens next

Dhami said the report card would be taken to the public, and the party has said it will fight on it in the Assembly election due early next year. Congress had not responded to the document at the time of writing. The Election Commission has yet to announce the schedule for the Assembly election.
