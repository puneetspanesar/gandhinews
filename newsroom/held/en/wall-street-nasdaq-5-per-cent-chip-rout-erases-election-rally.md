Wall Street **gave back all of its post-election rally** on Thursday, a day after its best session in 19 months, as a sell-off in chipmakers spread across the market on fears that a Democratic Congress will slow the race to build artificial intelligence data centres.

By 1 pm in New York (11:31 pm IST), the Nasdaq Composite was **down 5.27% at 24,378.98**, on course to close in a correction, more than 10% below its record. The S&P 500 had lost 4.02% to 7,379.33 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 1,672 points, or 3.13%, at 51,684.10. That fall was larger than the 1,569 points the Dow added on Wednesday.

The S&P 500 shed **more than $2 trillion in market value** by mid-morning. The Dow was on track for its worst second session after a midterm election in data going back to 1896, and both it and the S&P 500 were heading for their worst day since the tariff crash of April 2025.

## Chipmakers take the hit

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index **fell about 11%**. Micron and AMD were down by double digits, Nvidia lost more than 6% and Broadcom was among the hardest hit. Qualcomm slid as much as 13% after its smartphone chip sales for the quarter fell short of expectations, which the company put down to weak demand and less business from Apple.

The selling reached well beyond chips. Data-centre groups Nebius and CoreWeave, storage makers such as SanDisk and Western Digital, and **Elon Musk's SpaceX and Tesla** all fell sharply. AppLovin went the other way, up nearly 30% after strong quarterly results. Energy shares gained as oil climbed back, with Brent around $97 a barrel after Wednesday's slump of more than 8%.

## Why the mood turned

Democrats won control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate this week, including the governorship and a Senate seat in Texas, a centre of the data-centre build-out. Traders are now **pricing in tougher rules for AI infrastructure**. The sell-off also builds on doubts about AI spending that wiped more than $1 trillion off tech stocks on October 30.

Wall Street's fear gauge, the VIX, jumped more than 65% to as high as 27.45, its **biggest one-day rise in over two years**, according to Shay Boloor of Futurum Equities. Bitcoin briefly fell below $76,000.

The new Congress also changes the outlook for the Federal Reserve. Jonathan Pingle, an economist at UBS, said Democratic control of the Senate effectively blocks President Donald Trump's efforts to reshape the central bank.

Earlier on Thursday the Bank of England **raised its main rate by a quarter point to 4%** on an 8-1 vote. Governor Andrew Bailey said the move "shouldn't be seen as the first in an inevitable long sequence of hikes."

## What comes next

US markets close at 2:30 am IST on Friday, and the October jobs report is due later on Friday. Dalal Street, where the Sensex **rose for a sixth straight session** on Thursday on cheaper crude and the US election result, will open with this overnight rout as its backdrop.
