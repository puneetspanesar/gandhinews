The Supreme Court on Wednesday **issued notice to Delhi Police** on fresh bail petitions filed by Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam in the 2020 Delhi riots "larger conspiracy" case, but refused to take up their request for interim bail that day. A bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana said the matters would be placed before the Chief Justice to **constitute a three-judge bench**.

No date for the next hearing was reported. Khalid's counsel told the court he has been in custody for more than six years.

## What happened in court

Senior advocate Kapil Sibal, appearing for Khalid, pressed for interim bail, pointing out that he had spent more than six years in jail. He also noted that a bench hearing a co-accused's case, *Tasleem Ahmed*, had granted interim bail while it made a reference to a larger bench in May. Chief Justice Kant's answer to the request was short: "Not today."

Justice Bagchi said the interim prayer would get a reply from the other side: "You have made a prayer for interim relief; let it be responded." The Chief Justice indicated that the main petitions, not just the interim prayer, will be listed.

Senior advocate Siddharth Dave, for Imam, argued that the May reference has no bearing on these petitions. His client, he said, was exercising the liberty the court gave in January to apply again for bail. Solicitor General Tushar Mehta, for Delhi Police, said the reference had arisen out of the same FIR.

## The conflict over Najeeb

The reason for a larger bench is a difference between benches of the court on how long custody counts under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act. In its 2021 judgment in *Union of India v. K A Najeeb*, a three-judge bench held that long incarceration can justify bail even under the UAPA's strict restrictions. Chief Justice Kant wrote that judgment, and he said so in court.

"It was my judgment (Najeeb case) which is being discussed. So I am feeling little embarrassed," he said. Addressing Dave, he added: "Mr. Dave, in the light of the conflict of observation, I have to apply my mind." Describing the divergence, he said: "One bench says Najeeb means this, another bench says it does not mean..." On the preparation for the hearing, he said: "We have done our homework."

The conflict was set out in two earlier orders. A bench of Justices BV Nagarathna and Ujjal Bhuyan, in *Syed Iftikhar Andrabi*, criticised the January ruling in the *Gulfisha Fatima* case for diluting *Najeeb*. On May 22, a bench of Justice Aravind Kumar, noting a "perceived conflict" in the case law, referred the question to a larger bench in *Tasleem Ahmed*.

## How the two reached this point

On January 5, 2026, a bench of Justices Aravind Kumar and NV Anjaria **denied bail to Khalid and Imam** and granted it to five other accused, including Gulfisha Fatima, distinguishing their alleged roles. The bench described the two as "ideological drivers" of the conspiracy, held that the material attracted the bail restrictions in Section 43D(5) of the UAPA and said Article 21 could not be a "trump card" for bail. It allowed them to apply again once the protected prosecution witnesses had been examined or one year had passed, whichever came first.

They went back to the trial court, which rejected their fresh pleas on July 4, according to the listing of the case. The Delhi High Court declined to interfere on September 30. The present petitions challenge that judgment.

## What the petitioners say

In their petitions, Khalid and Imam argue that the examination of protected witnesses is unlikely to be completed within the period the January order set, and that the trial is moving slowly while they remain in judicial custody. Counsel told the court that the one-year window is about to run out and that the witness condition cannot realistically be met by **January 5, 2027**.

Delhi Police's position, put by Mehta, is that the reference order was passed in the same FIR. The prosecution's case rests on speeches, WhatsApp conversations and witness statements, and it alleges a conspiracy behind the violence in North East Delhi in February 2020, which followed protests against the Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019. The violence killed 53 people and injured more than 700. The allegations are yet to be tested at trial.

## Why it matters

The larger bench's reading of *Najeeb* will matter beyond these two men. UAPA bail rules decide how long an accused can be held before trial, and benches of the same court have read the earlier ruling in opposite ways. The question stays open until a three-judge bench is formed and decides it.

## What happens next

Delhi Police must now file its response to the bail petitions. The Chief Justice will decide, on the administrative side, who sits on the three-judge bench and when the matters are listed. The court has not said whether it will hear the *Najeeb* question first or the two petitions together. The January 5, 2027 date, when the one-year window from the earlier order ends, is the next fixed point both sides are watching.
