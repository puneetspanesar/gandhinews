The **TDP-led alliance was far ahead** in Andhra Pradesh's mandal and zilla parishad count on Saturday, taking Pulivendula, the home turf of YSRCP chief Y S Jagan Mohan Reddy, along the way.

The latest tally available, from about 2:25 pm, showed the alliance of the TDP, Jana Sena and BJP having **won or led in more than 170 of the 659 ZPTC seats** and in over 2,000 of the 9,821 MPTC seats. Officials had aimed to finish counting by 4 pm, but a final statewide tally was not yet out when this report was filed. It will come from the State Election Commission once verification ends.

## Kadapa falls

The result drawing the most attention came from Kadapa. At Pulivendula, **the TDP's Bharathi beat the YSRCP's Savitri by 1,463 votes** in the ZPTC contest. Local reports said the alliance also took 64 of the 65 MPTC seats in the Pulivendula Assembly segment, though some had been decided unopposed before polling. At Mydukur, the TDP's Krishna Kishore won the ZPTC seat by a reported 18,903 votes.

Elsewhere the pattern held. In Guntur, the TDP's Mikkili Sravani won the Jadavalli MPTC seat by just 74 votes, and in Tenali the TDP's Dasari Ratna Kumari took Halfpet by 32. In Chittoor, the TDP won five MPTC seats around Kuppam, and in Salur mandal of Manyam it took the Ganjaibhadhra and Sarika seats. Jana Sena's Yadla Mahalakshmi won Vedangi-1 in West Godavari.

## Tension at counting centres

Counting was not entirely calm. At the **Hindupur counting centre**, YSRCP workers objected to an MLA's personal assistant being allowed inside, tried to jump the gates and clashed with police, who later dispersed them. In Kaikaluru mandal of Eluru, 64 of 66 postal ballots were declared invalid; the two valid votes went to the TDP.

## What is still pending

The parishad count follows polling on November 5, when turnout was 70.72%. State Election Commissioner Anil Chandra Punetha said decisions on **23 pending municipalities and nearly 13,000 gram panchayats** would come soon.

The YSRCP won these polls in 2021. This time it has so far been left with a handful of seats, and Jagan has already called the process neither free nor fair.
