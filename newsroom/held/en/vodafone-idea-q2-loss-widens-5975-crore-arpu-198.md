Vodafone Idea on Thursday reported a **net loss of ₹5,975 crore** for the July to September quarter, wider than the roughly ₹3,750 crore it lost in the April to June quarter, as a one-off accounting charge outweighed gains in revenue and customer numbers.

The results, announced on November 12, 2026, came in worse than analysts had projected. The widening was mainly due to an **exceptional loss of ₹462 crore**, which arose when the company reassessed the fair value of Vodafone Plc shares that it receives over five years under a 2018 settlement. In the first quarter the same line had worked the other way, with an exceptional gain of ₹1,611 crore that cushioned the bottom line.

## What the numbers show

Revenue from operations rose to **₹11,884 crore**, up about 6% from ₹11,195 crore a year earlier and 1.7% from ₹11,689 crore in the June quarter. Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, or EBITDA, were ₹4,981 crore, **up 6.3% on the year** but down 1.1% from ₹5,034 crore in the previous quarter. The EBITDA margin eased by 120 basis points to 41.9% from 43.1%.

The figure telecom analysts watch most closely, average revenue per user, or ARPU, **rose to ₹198**, a gain of 9.7% in a year and, according to reports, the fastest among the major operators. The company attributes the rise to customers moving to higher-value 4G and 5G plans. A year earlier, in the September 2025 quarter, ARPU was ₹180.

## Customers and data

The total subscriber base stood at **194.4 million**, up from 193.1 million in the June quarter. That makes six months of net additions in a row, the first such run since the 2018 merger of Vodafone India and Idea Cellular. Postpaid customers, who pay more and churn less, rose to 33.9 million from 31.9 million, the strongest quarterly postpaid addition in nine quarters.

Subscribers on 4G and 5G networks reached **131 million**, against 127.8 million a year earlier. Average daily data use was 93.6 petabytes, up 27.1% on the year, a sign that customers are streaming and downloading more on the network.

Chief executive Abhijit Kishore said the company's "focus continues to remain firmly on execution", pointing to subscriber additions, network expansion and customer experience as the drivers of the quarter.

## Funding and the debt overhang

The loss comes as Vodafone Idea tries to finance a network upgrade it has put off for years. It raised **₹4,000 crore in the September quarter**, taking funds raised so far this financial year to about ₹10,500 crore, including ₹6,400 crore in the first quarter. That is part of a target of ₹25,000 crore to ₹35,000 crore through funded and non-funded facilities, and the company says discussions with banks are continuing. It plans to place more capital spending orders for network expansion.

The company's room for manoeuvre widened in May, when the Department of Telecommunications cut its adjusted gross revenue, or AGR, dues by 27%, from ₹87,695 crore to ₹64,046 crore, to be repaid by the 2040-41 financial year without further interest. Reports at the time said the company was in talks for a loan of about ₹25,000 crore led by the State Bank of India, though those talks were not final.

Relief on AGR has not removed the other large obligation. Spectrum liabilities were about ₹1.25 trillion at the end of December 2025, and the company owes roughly **₹49,000 crore over three years**: ₹7,000 crore in 2026-27, ₹15,000 crore in 2027-28 and ₹27,000 crore in 2028-29. Mr Kishore said in January that the company would not seek a moratorium on those payments.

## Why it matters

For the roughly 194 million people who use a Vodafone Idea connection, the company's ability to spend on towers, 4G coverage and the first phase of 5G decides how good their service is. For the market, it decides whether India keeps three private operators or drifts towards two. Higher ARPU is the clearest sign that the company can raise what it earns from each customer without losing them, and the subscriber gains suggest that, for now, it has not.

The company also offered 9.5 million employee stock options and performance stock units under its 2024 scheme at ₹12.88 a share, based on the November 11 market price. The stock closed at ₹12.98 on the BSE on Thursday.

## What happens next

The next test is the bank funding. Lenders have so far been cautious, and the size and timing of the loan will determine how fast capital spending can rise. The company's next quarterly results, for October to December, are due around February 2027. Until then, the first instalment of the spectrum payments in 2026-27 and any announcement of capital spending orders will show whether the turnaround plan is moving ahead of the losses.
