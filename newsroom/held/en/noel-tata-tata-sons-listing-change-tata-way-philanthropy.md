The Reserve Bank of India has told Tata Sons in writing that it must **list on the stock exchanges**, a week before the group's board meets to decide how to respond. In a letter dated November 16, 2026, the central bank directed the holding company to comply with the rules for upper-layer non-banking finance companies, "including the requirement of listing by your company", according to reports that reviewed the letter.

Tata Sons' board is reportedly preparing to **place responsibility for compliance on the board and its chairman**, N Chandrasekaran, at its meeting on November 26. That plan is said to be backed by a legal opinion from senior advocate Harish Salve. It sets Tata Sons against Tata Trusts chairman Noel Tata, whose own proposal would keep the company unlisted.

## What the RBI wrote

The letter was addressed to Tata Sons' chief financial officer, Saurabh Agrawal, and answered a letter from the company dated October 6 that asked the RBI for guidance on complying with applicable regulations. The RBI noted that Tata Sons **had not named any specific rule**, and said the request was "perhaps with respect to" the listing issue discussed at its board. It then told the company to follow all upper-layer rules, listing included.

It is the first time the regulator has used the word listing in a letter to the company. Noel Tata had maintained that earlier communications did not mention it. The letter sets no fresh deadline and neither approves nor rejects the Trusts' restructuring plan. It leaves open the chance for Tata Sons to **seek clarity on specific provisions** if it sends the relevant details.

## How the rule applies

The RBI placed Tata Sons in the upper layer of its scale-based framework in September 2022. Companies in that layer, where the threshold is assets of ₹1 lakh crore or more, must list within three years, and for Tata Sons **that window closed in September 2025**. Tata Sons reported total assets of about ₹2.01 lakh crore at March 31, 2026.

The company applied in March 2024 to surrender its core investment company registration after repaying its borrowings, a step that would have taken it out of the framework. **The RBI rejected that application on September 11, 2026.** Tata Sons' board met on September 17, resolved to begin compliance measures and reappointed Chandrasekaran by a 4:1 vote, with Noel Tata dissenting.

## Two plans for the board

Tata Sons' management reportedly holds that listing can no longer be delayed because it is now a regulatory matter. An executive was quoted as saying so, and the company is said to argue that the RBI's September order left no route to avoid a public offering. Its presentation on November 26 is reported to reject the restructuring plan the Trusts sent to the RBI directly in September.

That plan would **merge Tata Electronics Systems Solutions and Tata Consulting Engineers into Tata Sons**, bringing operating businesses into the holding company and changing its regulatory profile so that it stays unlisted. It needs the approval of the Tata Sons board and an RBI no-objection. Noel Tata is reportedly backed by trustees Neville Tata and Bhaskar Bhat in wanting the board to challenge the RBI's September order. Tata Trusts are reportedly due to meet on November 20 to revisit their stand.

On November 14, Noel Tata said at a leadership summit in New Delhi that "listing, which is what some people are proposing, will change the Tata way". He warned that **with 30,000 to 40,000 shareholders**, "profit may take precedence over philanthropy", and said the Trusts would "play a constructive role with regulators and others".

## The Shapoorji Pallonji question

The Shapoorji Pallonji Group, which holds 18.37% of Tata Sons, favours a listing. Its chairman, Shapoor Mistry, has argued that it would bring transparency and accountability, and the group is reported to want to **raise at least ₹25,000 crore** by monetising part of its stake to reduce debt.

The board may consider a proposal for ₹25,000 crore of liquidity for the group through a capital reduction, in which Tata Sons would buy back and cancel shares over 24 months. Tata Sons is reported to say that request lies outside the remit of the Trusts' chairman, who raised it in September. Chandrasekaran is reportedly due to meet Mistry privately before November 26 to discuss the stake. The board may also fix a date for its delayed annual general meeting.

Within the Trusts, trustees Venu Srinivasan and Vijay Singh wrote on November 11 to fellow trustee Darius Khambata that charity-owned companies can be public, citing Novo Nordisk, Hershey, Lundbeck and Demant and listed Tata companies such as TCS, Tata Steel, Tata Power and Tata Motors. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman [said on November 2](/en/news/sitharaman-tata-critical-component-settle-amicably-investors/) that the quarrel "cannot be spilling out like this in the public".

## Why it matters

Tata Sons holds the shares of the roughly $165 billion Tata Group, and a listing would put a market price on the holding company for the first time. For investors, that would offer a direct way to own the group rather than its listed units separately. For the Shapoorji Pallonji Group, it is a route to cash to reduce its debt. For the Trusts, which fund charitable work through their holding, the question is whether a wider set of shareholders would change the group's priorities, as Noel Tata fears.

## What happens next

The Trusts are due to meet on November 20, and the **Tata Sons board meets on November 26**. Tata Sons has not announced an IPO, filed an offer document or disclosed a listing date. The RBI's letter set no new deadline, so the board's decision on November 26 is the next point at which the company's route becomes clear.
