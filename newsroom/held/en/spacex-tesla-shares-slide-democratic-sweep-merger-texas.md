Shares of SpaceX and Tesla **tumbled more than 10% each** on Thursday, as investors priced in what Democratic control of Congress, and a Democratic win in Texas, could mean for Elon Musk's companies and their plans to merge.

By late morning in New York, about 10 pm IST, SpaceX had **lost about $200 billion in market value** and Tesla about $170 billion, among the biggest losses in a broad sell-off that sent the Nasdaq down 5%. Both stocks had already fallen more than 9% earlier in the session.

## Texas at the centre

Both companies are headquartered in Texas, and that is where the worry is sharpest. The state elected a **Democratic governor this week**, and Democrat James Talarico won its Senate seat. Musk had feuded with Talarico online before the vote.

Gina Hinojosa, the governor-elect, has promised a moratorium on data centres in the state. Wells Fargo estimates that Texas holds **27% of planned US data-centre capacity**. That matters for SpaceX, whose AI business brought in 52% of its income in the September quarter.

## A harder road for the merger

Dan Ives, a partner and managing director at Yorkville, said the deal could still happen, but on a slower track.

> "We will believe a SpaceX/Tesla merger happens but now faces a much tougher regulatory roadmap to get this deal done in 2027."
> Source: Dan Ives, partner and managing director, Yorkville, November 5, 2026

Ives said the new political map in Washington and in Texas **makes that timeline harder**. The losses came a day after Wall Street's best session in 19 months, when investors first cheered the end of election uncertainty. US markets close at 2:30 am IST on Friday.
