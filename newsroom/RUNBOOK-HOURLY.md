# Writing run (every hour at :03, 6 am to 11 pm and 12:03 am IST; the run name stays hourly-HH)

Even hours (6, 8, 10 am ... 10 pm) and 12:03 am: the FULL run below, 4 stories. Odd hours (7, 9, 11 am ... 11 pm): the LEAD RUN at the end of this file, 1 story. The owner's rule of November 12 is that the lead is never more than 2 hours old, so the site needs a new lead-worthy story with a real photo every hour.

Between runs the stall check and the GitHub job .github/workflows/home-rotate.yml rotate the other headlines without AI. Markets figures are updated by every run.

Read newsroom/RUNBOOK.md first: its rules (A), setup (B), pictures (C), gate (D) and publish (E) are part of this runbook. This file only adds what is particular to the hourly run.

Each run publishes 4 new news stories (at least 3; owner, November 8: fewer, better stories, each built on a primary source), refreshes the homepage and keeps the markets band current. No explainers or editorials: the long-form runs write those.

## Time budget (minutes from the start time setup.sh printed)
- 0 to 6: setup (a fresh container installs for 2 to 3 minutes), held stories, front-page check.
- 6 to 22: desks write the stories and place their main pictures; then `audit.py --new` on them.
- 22 to 28: pictures the desks could not place (C), and the markets work.
- 28: picture deadline. Every story still without a correct main picture gets its infographic with `--nophoto` (RUNBOOK.md rule 11a) and publishes; it stays out of the top five.
  THE LEAD IS ALWAYS A PHOTOGRAPH (owner, November 8). A lead-worthy story (politics, national, states, world) never goes up on an infographic alone: mix 2-3 real photos of its people and place or thing with `python3 newsroom/scripts/lead_cover.py <id> "<alt>" "<Hindi alt>" <photo> <photo> [<photo>]` (library photos may be reused for a cover), Read the result, and it can lead. refresh_home.py and the build gate refuse an infographic lead.
- 28 to 40: refresh_home and gate.sh (about 3 minutes), review_pack and the editor agent, apply_verdict, gate.sh again only if something was held or fixed.
- by 45: publish.sh. If the gate still fails at 42, hold the failing stories and publish the rest. The run ALWAYS publishes, at the least a refreshed homepage and current markets figures, because the site must change every hour.

## 1. Front-page check (5 minutes at most)
Read newsroom/editor-notes.md (the owner's current priorities); its items are BIG STORIES. Then two WebSearch calls (mode extended) for India's top news of the last few hours, and WebFetch of newsonair.gov.in and one or two front pages that allow it. A story leading two or more of them is a BIG STORY. Write the BIG STORIES, ours or missing, to /home/claude/gn-work/frontpages.txt, one per line. Missing BIG STORIES are written first this run, even if they broke up to 24 hours ago; BIG STORIES we have but whose latest development is missing get a development story. Delete an editor-notes item once it is published.

## 2. Desks (four agents in ONE message, in parallel; each finds its own pictures)
Write /home/claude/gn-work/window.txt with: the date and time now (`date`); the window (the last 2 hours; the 6 am run covers from midnight); the BIG STORIES; and these limits, which override every brief: "HOURLY run. News only, and every news story is reported news, not a list of facts (owner, November 8). 600 to 900 words; the lead story of a run 800 to 1,100. Each one has: a lead that says what happened, to whom, where and when, with the key figure; what the primary record actually says (the order, release, data or statement, in specific detail); how we got here (the earlier developments with dates, linking our own earlier stories); reactions on the record, from more than one side where there are sides; why it matters to readers, in concrete sourced terms (money, rights, services, prices, safety), never opinion; and what happens next (the next date, deadline, hearing, vote or release). At least one chart, timeline or fact box when there is data. No padding: every paragraph adds a fact a reader did not have. No briefs: an item too small for 600 words is not written. Your desk writes the number of stories in its DESK line, and at least one of them must be able to lead: a fresh national, state or world story of real weight with a real photo, never markets, sport or science (those never lead unless the front-page check pins a BIG STORY). In-story photo blocks: at most 2 per story, each a different person or place; no cover blocks. At most 6 WebSearch calls. Every story rests on a primary source read this run (release, order, judgment, filing, data, the person's own statement), named and linked; no story that only rewrites another outlet's report. WebFetch refuses a URL typed from memory: WebSearch for the release first so its URL appears in the results, then WebFetch that URL. If the primary source still cannot be opened, write the story from at least two independent published reports, say 'according to reports' (never name an outlet) and link them in sources. Never hand back nothing." Spawn every desk with `model: "sonnet"` (the owner's usage decision, November 6; the editor agent stays on the default model). Each desk prompt starts `Read /home/claude/gn-work/run-brief.md and follow it. DESK = <desk>.`:
1. national: politics, Parliament, the Union government, courts. 1 story.
2. states: state politics, governments, disasters, crime and civic news from the states. 1 story.
3. world-business: 1 story: world news on runs at even hours from 6 am (6, 10, 14, 18, 22), economy, companies, RBI or SEBI on the others (the homepage World desk needs 4 world stories in 48 hours, which this gives). At most one markets story per run, and only when the market really moved (Sensex or Nifty over 0.5%, or a big single stock); monthly sales figures as one roundup a day.
4. sport-science: 1 story: science, tech, space, health or climate on runs at 8, 12, 16 and 20, sport (cricket first) on the others. A foreign tennis or football result is not news for this site unless an Indian is in it or it is a final.
5. ONLY in the 12:03 pm and 10:03 pm runs, a fifth desk, opinion-and-fact-check (also Sonnet): 1 editorial and 1 fact check on the biggest issues since the last long-form run that have none yet, following RUNBOOK-LONGFORM.md desks 2 and 3 (shapes, word counts, primary sources only). Its window.txt line: "LONG-FORM pieces inside a writing run: editorial 500 to 800 words with 1 or 2 visuals; fact check against primary sources only." Between these and the three long-form runs the opinion and fact-check sections change several times a day.
That is 4 stories a run, at least 3 (owner, November 8: fewer, better stories; about 40 a day with the long-form runs instead of 57). The 48-hour pool still gives the hourly rotation enough to change every headline.
EVERY STORY RESTS ON A PRIMARY SOURCE read in this run: the official release, order, judgment, filing, data table or the person's own statement or post, named and linked in the text and sources. A story that would only rewrite another outlet's report is not written, unless it is a BIG STORY, and then it adds what the primary record shows. (Owner, November 11: the site went a whole day with one or two stories a run.) When the primary source cannot be opened in this run (site down, blocked, PDF unreadable), the story is still written: it rests on at least two independent published reports, says so in the text ("according to reports", naming no outlet), and links them in sources. An unreadable source is never a reason to publish nothing. A run that ends with fewer than 3 new stories has failed: the desks write replacements for held stories before minute 35. Choose the story where the desk can add something: the figure in context, what the order actually says, what changed since last time.
PICTURES ARE THE DESK'S JOB, done before it hands back (run-brief.md "Main picture"): the people library first (`one_photo.py <id> --person "<name>"`, seconds), then quick_photo.py, then ONE finder call for whatever is left, then the infographic with `--nophoto` (never a hold for want of a photo). The run then only checks the pictures and does the finder for anything a desk could not place.
An event older than the window is not news; only a real new development of it is (new id). Desks put the event time in `verification.notes`.
PICTURE FIRST (owner, November 12): a desk finds the main picture before it writes the story, and runs the desk check itself before handing back (run-brief.md, "Picture first" and "Before you hand back"). At most ONE story of a run goes up on an infographic.
When they finish: `python3 newsroom/scripts/audit.py --new --skip home,site,style` checks only the files this run wrote (text and pictures); it must pass (fix the file, or delete that story in both languages), then `git add content public/photos && git commit -q -m "desk HH"` locally. Never push here.

## 3. Pictures
For stories the desks could not picture: RUNBOOK.md section C, tag `h<day><HH>`. Upgrades in the same finder call: `python3 newsroom/scripts/audit.py --hours 24 --skip content,home,site,style` lists stories of the last 24 hours that show an infographic although they are about a person or place. Add those people and places to this run's queries and replace each graphic with the photo (one_photo.py replaces the map entry).

## 4. Markets and data (content/data/markets.json, format in newsroom/markets-format.md)
Market hours (9:15 am to 3:30 pm, Monday to Friday): current index levels, change and % change on the previous close, `status: "open"`, `as_of` now; also rupee, Brent, gold, silver, the 10-year yield, top Nifty gainers and losers, sector moves. After 3:30 pm: closing values, `status: "closed"`, the close appended to each index `history` (last 30 sessions), FII and DII flows once NSE has published them. Every number from a source read in this run; a value you could not find keeps its old value and date. Economy releases (CPI, IIP, GDP, GST, forex reserves, repo) also update content/data/economy-dashboard.json and its Hindi twin. At most 6 WebSearch calls.

## 5. Homepage, gate, editor, publish
RUNBOOK.md sections D and E (gate.sh, the editor, apply_verdict, publish.sh). `--lead <id>` when the biggest story of the front-page check is not the lead (refresh_home.py replaces a held, over-8-hour, clock-stale or markets lead by itself); `--pin <id> ...` for BIG STORIES. Every headline must differ from last hour's: refresh_home.py does this by itself; a nation- or world-changing story stays only when this run added new facts to it (new `updated:`) and passes it with `--critical <id>`, or as a new development story. During a big election count, update content/data/results.json every run from sources read in this run (`active: false` 48 hours after counting ends).
AFTER publish.sh (owner, November 12): look at what you published, on the live site. `python3 newsroom/scripts/verify_live.py` must print OK, and Read the collage of every new story (public/photos/t-<id>-640.jpg): a plain photo, a wrong centre, a cut-off head or an unrelated tile is fixed now with a second publish, not left for the stall check.
Run name for the lock and the record: `hourly-HH`, HH the hour the run started. Message the owner (SendUserMessage) only when a story was held or the site could not be deployed.

## Owner's rules, November 8 (evening)
- The top of the homepage is the newest news. Lead under 6 hours old; beside the lead, news under 12 hours old, newest first. Fresh news (under 6 hours) may stay in the headlines the next hour and moves down as newer stories arrive; the lead still changes every hour. refresh_home.py and check_fresh.py enforce this.
- A lead cover (lead_cover.py) never shows the same person twice. Pair the person with the place or event, or use one photo. lead_cover.py refuses panels whose captions name the same person.
- A broken cover is kept off the homepage with `"nohome": "<reason>"` on its photos.map.json entry; remove the flag once the story has a proper photo.

## Story page design C (owner's pick, November 9)
- A story with a real photo opens split: headline on a tinted panel, photo on the right. Infographic heroes keep the stacked opening.
- Under the opening, a strip of 3 or 4 key numbers comes from `glance:` (list of `{icon, value, label}`, Phosphor icon names) or, failing that, `infographic.stats`. Give every photo story a `glance:` of 3 or 4 real figures from the primary source.
- `infographic.timeline` / `infographic.steps` show as a timeline at the foot of the story; the dateline's state shows as a "Where" map in the rail.

## Card photos: the house collage treatment (owner, November 9)
- Every real photo in a card slot (homepage, section pages, rails) shows its treated collage: the subject cut out over 2-3 related scene photos from our own library, toned to the house red, on paper beige, with a red arch and small line marks. Story pages show the photo as taken.
- The "Treat photos" GitHub workflow builds these automatically after each push that changes content/photos.map.json. To do it in a run: `python3 newsroom/scripts/treat_photo.py <id>` (needs `pip install rembg[cpu] cairosvg`).
- Only licensed photos, as always: never PTI, ANI, DD News, Getty or any other outlet's pictures, cut out or not. A collage of an agency photo is still the agency's photo.
- (Later, November 9) EVERY photo everywhere now shows the collage, story pages included. Infographic stories get one too when we hold a solo photo of the headline's person (cut out), or 3-4 clearly related scene photos; their story page keeps the infographic.
- Owner wants many more stories with photos: find a real licensed photo for every story (PIB first, then Wikimedia Commons, official handouts and press kits). An infographic-only story is the last resort, not the default.
- (November 9, evening) Freshness applies to the TOP of the homepage only (lead, beside the lead, the headline column, Must read). Every section below runs its newest stories, up to 7 days old, and is never dropped for being short of fresh news.

## Photos: owner's ruling, November 9 night (read before choosing any picture)
- An infographic is NOT a picture. Every story about a named person gets that person's real photo: PIB first, then our own library (newsroom/library/people.json has licensed portraits of about 300 people; `treat_photo.py` downloads them itself). Never publish an infographic-only story about Modi, Advani, Shah, a CM or anyone in the library.
- Never a cover of one person twice. Never a background tile from another place or subject (no White House behind an India-Ukraine story). treat_photo.py enforces both: a tile must match the story's topic and share a proper noun with the headline; a portrait must show that person alone; when nothing fits, the collage has no tiles.
- Before publishing: open every new collage (public/photos/t-<id>-640.jpg) and look at it. Fix anything wrong before the push, not after.
- (Owner, November 11) A photo shown plain, untreated, in a card is an error. Never set `notreat` to get rid of a bad collage: fix the cause (give the story a better main photo, or a solo photo that cuts out) and make the collage again. The collage's subject is always the story's own main photo or the headline person's portrait; it never takes a centre picture from a related story.

## LEAD RUN (odd hours: 7, 9, 11 am, 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 pm; owner, November 12)
ALWAYS WRITE AND PUBLISH A NEW LEAD, even when the current lead is still under 2 hours old (owner's rule, November 13: the 7:03 am lead run of November 13 saw a 17-minute-old lead and published nothing, so the lead crossed 2 hours before the 8 am run published). The current lead was published by the previous run at about minute 45; it must be replaced now, or it passes 2 hours before the next run publishes. A lead run that publishes nothing is a failed run.
The lead is never more than 2 hours old, so the hours between full runs get one new lead. Everything in RUNBOOK.md applies; this run is the full run cut down to one story, and it publishes by minute 35.
1. Setup as usual (`setup.sh hourly-HH`), held stories. A two-search front-page check: what is the biggest India story of the last 90 minutes that we do not have, or have only an older version of?
2. ONE desk agent (Sonnet), ONE story: that story, national, state, world or economy news (never markets, sport, science or tech unless it is a BIG STORY), 600 to 900 words, on the same rules as a full run (primary source or two reports, Hindi twin, `seo.title`, `upcoming:` when a date is ahead). PICTURE FIRST: it is chosen only if it has a real photo wide enough to lead (PIB, the people library, quick_photo, 900 px or more), or you can build a cover of 2-3 real photos with lead_cover.py. A story that can only go up on an infographic is not this run's story.
3. Markets figures during market hours (section 4), at most 3 searches.
4. `refresh_home.py --lead <id>`, gate.sh, review_pack and the editor agent on the one story, apply_verdict, publish.sh, then the look at the live result (verify_live.py and the story's collage).
5. If the story is held or fails: write a second one. This run does not end without a NEW lead, published in this run, on the live site. If truly nothing new has happened in India or the world in 2 hours, write the most important new development of the current lead's story as its own story (new id).
