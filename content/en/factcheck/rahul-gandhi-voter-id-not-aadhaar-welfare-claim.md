## What we found

The claim is **misleading**. A Union minister did say what Rahul Gandhi says he said. On October 30, 2026, Panchayati Raj Minister **Lalan Singh** (Rajiv Ranjan Singh) told a national workshop of his ministry in New Delhi that the Aadhaar card would no longer be the basis for payments and that beneficiaries would be paid using their biometrics and their voter ID (EPIC) number.

But Gandhi's post, on November 6, presents that remark as a decision already taken: benefits "will now be determined not by Aadhar, but by Voter ID". There is **no order, notification or circular** behind it, no scheme has been named and no date has been set. The law that governs welfare payments still makes Aadhaar their basis.

## The evidence

### 1. What the minister said

Speaking at the National Workshop on the People's Plan Campaign 2026-27, Singh said that "the Aadhaar card will no longer serve as the basis for making payments to beneficiaries" and that "payments to beneficiaries will be processed using their biometrics and their voter ID (EPIC) number". He argued that Aadhaar was too easy to get: "An Aadhaar card can be issued to anyone in just five minutes." He did not say **which schemes would change or when**.

### 2. The ministry's own account says nothing about it

The government's preview of the workshop described a meeting of about 400 officials from the states on village development plans, Finance Commission grants and own revenue for panchayats. The account of the day built on the ministry's statements covered the release of a campaign booklet, an agreement on electric vehicle charging points on panchayat land and the new **Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana**. Neither mentions Aadhaar, voter ID or biometrics, and no separate release on a change in payments has been issued.

### 3. His ministry pays no one directly

The Ministry of Panchayati Raj has **no scheme that credits money** straight into the bank accounts of individual beneficiaries. Its funds go to panchayats. The direct-payment schemes that use Aadhaar, such as PM-Kisan, rural housing under PMAY-G and the National Social Assistance Programme pensions, are run by other ministries, and none of them has announced a change. Some schemes of the Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying ministry, which Singh also heads, require Aadhaar for payments, and no change to them has been announced.

### 4. The law still runs on Aadhaar

Section 7 of the Aadhaar Act, 2016 lets the Centre and the states require a person to authenticate with Aadhaar, or show an Aadhaar number, to receive a subsidy, benefit or service. The Unique Identification Authority of India told every ministry and state in a memorandum of August 11, 2022 that the section "provides the backbone for delivery of benefits, services and subsidies based on Aadhaar number". A person without Aadhaar can be paid on other identity proof only **while their enrolment is pending**. Schemes ask for Aadhaar through their own notifications under this section, and none has been withdrawn or replaced.

## Why this claim spread

The remark landed in the middle of the fight over the **Special Intensive Revision** of electoral rolls. The CPI(M) said on November 5 that more than 13.4 crore voters had been left out in the revision and that tying welfare to the voter card would shut out the poorest. Gandhi took the same line a day later, warning "Today, Panchayati Raj schemes. Tomorrow, rations?" and calling it "not just vote theft" but "rights theft".

That fear explains why the post travelled fast. It does not change what is on the record: a minister's stated intention, with **no rule yet written** to carry it out. If the government does notify a switch for any scheme, this rating will be revisited.
