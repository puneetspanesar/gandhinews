## What we found

Piyush Goyal's headline figure is **mostly true**. Indian airlines have ordered more than 1,200 aircraft, and the government's own aviation minister counts more than that. What the claim blurs is who does the ordering and what the 2,000 figure rests on.

## The claim

Speaking to reporters in Cairo on November 16, 2026, the Commerce Minister said: "We have ordered more than 1,200 planes, and we are ready to order another 1,000 planes. We need more than 2,000 planes for our requirements." The remark was reported widely the next day and took off as proof of a shortage of aircraft and a boom in demand.

## The evidence

### 1. The big three orders

A Rajya Sabha written reply of **July 31, 2023** records the government's in-principle approval for **Air India to import 470 aircraft and IndiGo 500**, to arrive by 2035. Akasa Air's own release of **January 18, 2024** says the 150 Boeing 737 MAX jets it had just ordered take its order book to **226 aircraft**. Boeing's release says the same.

### 2. The arithmetic

470 plus 500 plus 226 is **1,196**, a little under Goyal's figure. IndiGo has also ordered 30 Airbus A350 jets, according to reports we could not confirm from Airbus itself, which would take the total to 1,226. Either way the claim sits at the edge of what three official or company records show, and it is stated as a floor ("more than").

### 3. The government's own count is higher

On March 29, 2026, Civil Aviation Minister K Ram Mohan Naidu said, "1,700 orders for aircraft are pending, and it will take up to 15 years for them to arrive." That is the government's own number for aircraft still to be delivered, and it is **about 500 above** the figure Goyal gave. The two measure different things, orders placed against orders pending, and Akasa's 226 includes planes ordered in 2021 and since delivered. They do not contradict each other, but they show Goyal's figure is not inflated.

### 4. The parts that are not a record

Goyal said India is "ready to order another 1,000 planes". Aircraft are ordered by airlines, not by the government, and no airline, manufacturer, value or date was named when he spoke. That makes it a **statement of intent**, which no document can confirm or refute. His "more than 2,000" need is also his own estimate. We found no ministry paper that sets a requirement of that size, though Naidu's 1,700 pending orders and a rising pilot requirement point the same way.

## Why this claim spread

Aircraft shortages are visible to passengers in delayed routes and high fares, and a round number such as 1,000 more planes is easy to repeat. The claim also arrived while air traffic was soft: our report on the speech notes that September passenger numbers were lower than a year earlier, so the constraint he describes is supply, not demand.

## What would change this rating

If a ministry or DGCA document shows the airlines' total order book is below 1,200, the rating would fall to Misleading. If an official paper puts the national need at 2,000 or more, the last open part of the claim would be settled in his favour. A signed order for 1,000 more aircraft would turn his intention into a fact.
