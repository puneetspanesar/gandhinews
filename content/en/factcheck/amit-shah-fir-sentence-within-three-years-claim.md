## What we found

The pledge is **misleading**. Union Home Minister **Amit Shah** said in Dehradun on November 6 that for any FIR registered after May 2027 the accused would be "sentenced, all within a span of three years". The new criminal laws do give deadlines to individual steps. They do not give one to a case from FIR to sentence, the courts that must deliver the sentences are carrying a record backlog, and the promise was framed differently five weeks earlier.

It is a target, so it cannot be proved wrong today. What can be checked is what the law actually requires.

## The evidence

### 1. The law times steps, not cases

The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), in force since July 1, 2024, sets limits for stages. Under section 258 a judge must deliver judgment **within 30 days of the end of arguments**, extendable to 45 days with reasons recorded in writing. Charges are to be framed within 60 days of the first hearing on charge, and committal in a sessions case is to be completed in 90 days, extendable to 180. We found **no provision capping the time from FIR to sentence**, and no provision for appeals. A legal summary notes that courts have treated such limits as guidance rather than hard bars; we have not checked the case law.

### 2. The backlog the courts carry

The Minister of State for Law and Justice told the Lok Sabha on February 13, 2026, that on December 31, 2025, **4,76,57,328 cases** were pending in district and subordinate courts, up 5.84% in three years. The High Courts held 63,66,023 and the Supreme Court 92,101, up 11.40%. The last criminal and civil split we could open, from February 2020, put about 72% of pending subordinate-court cases (2.29 crore of 3.19 crore) in the criminal column. A rule for new FIRs does not clear what is already there, and the same judges hear both.

### 3. Courts are not the Home Ministry's to command

Replying to the Lok Sabha on August 8, 2025, Minister of State Arjun Ram Meghwal said case resolution is "within the exclusive domain of the judiciary". Fast-track special courts for rape and POCSO cases, set up to speed up trials, had **2,00,349 cases pending** on June 30, 2025. The pledge depends on bodies the Home Minister does not run.

### 4. The end point has moved

At Bhopal on October 3, Shah said justice would be delivered within three years "from the filing of an FIR to the Supreme Court" once the laws were fully implemented, with no start date. At Dehradun on November 6 the end point was a sentence and the start was FIRs after May 2027. A sentence at trial and a final decision after appeals are different things.

## Why this claim spread

The pledge came at a ceremony that drew national coverage, and it is easy to repeat: one number, three years. It also answers a real public complaint, that cases take decades.

If a High Court or the Centre publishes how long cases registered since July 2024 are taking, this rating will be revisited.
