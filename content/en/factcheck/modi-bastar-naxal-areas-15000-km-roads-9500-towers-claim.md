## What we found

The Prime Minister's figures are **misleading**. In his Diwali address to security forces at Karanpur in Bastar on November 8, Narendra Modi said more than 15,000 km of roads and more than 9,500 mobile towers had been built in Naxal-affected areas. The Home Ministry's own published counts are lower: **12,000 to 14,928 km** of road and **8,640 towers**. The numbers on schools and training centres are close to the record.

## The evidence

### 1. Roads

The ministry's written reply in the Lok Sabha on August 12, 2025 gives two road figures. Its main text says **14,928 km** were built under the Road Requirement Plan and the Road Connectivity Project for LWE-affected areas. Its annexure, covering developmental achievements since 2014, totals **12,012 km**. A PIB backgrounder of December 2025 says over **12,000 km** were completed between May 2014 and August 2025. The reply does not explain the gap between the two figures. Modi's "more than 15,000 km" is above all of them.

### 2. Mobile towers

The same reply counts **8,640** towers commissioned. The Prime Minister's figure is more than 9,500. It would need roughly 860 more towers in the 15 months since, and we found no official count after August 2025. PIB's later backgrounders report towers by scheme: 2,343 in the first phase, 2,542 sanctioned in the second with about 1,150 operating, and 8,527 4G towers approved. Approved is not built.

### 3. Schools, ITIs and skill centres

About 175 Eklavya schools sits just below the 179 the ministry lists as functional (220 sanctioned). Nearly 50 ITIs matches 45 to 46 functional ones, and around 50 skill centres matches 49. Of the speech's development claims, these are fair.

## Why this claim spread

The speech came on Diwali, in uniform, and was released by PIB the same afternoon, so the figures were quoted widely as the summary of what the campaign built. Round numbers are easy to repeat, and they were the largest of the figures available. If the Home Ministry publishes a newer count that supports 15,000 km and 9,500 towers, this rating will be revisited.
