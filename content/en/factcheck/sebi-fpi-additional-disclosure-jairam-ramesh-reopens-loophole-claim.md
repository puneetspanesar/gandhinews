## What we found

Jairam Ramesh's claim is mostly true on substance and unproven on motive. The Congress general secretary said on November 22, 2026, that SEBI's plan to scrap the Additional Disclosure Framework for foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) **reopens a loophole closed under pressure** after the January 2023 revelations about the Adani group.

SEBI's own board papers do show that the rule was built to catch what Ramesh describes: funds used to hide who really owns shares. They do not say it was a response to the Adani reports, or to pressure of any kind.

## The evidence

### 1. What the rule was for

SEBI's board memorandum on the framework says some FPIs concentrate a large share of their Indian equity in one company or group, and that promoters or allied investors "could be using the FPI route for circumventing regulatory requirements", so the free float shown may not be the true one. It also says the route could be misused to get round Press Note 3, the rule on investment from land-border countries. Because owners below the money-laundering thresholds are not captured, the memo says, "it is not possible to determine whether the above scenario may be playing out."

The tests were **more than 50% of Indian equity in one group**, or **more than ₹25,000 crore** of equity. A fund caught by either must name everyone with ownership, economic interest or control, down to natural persons, with no minimum holding. The consultation paper behind it was issued on May 31, 2023.

### 2. Nothing about Adani or pressure

The 2023 memo does not mention Hindenburg, Adani or any specific company or group. Ramesh's link between the rule and the January 2023 revelations is therefore his inference. The timing is consistent with it, since the consultation came four months later, but SEBI's papers do not make it.

### 3. The rule was already loosened

SEBI's April 2025 memo records that the framework came from the August 2023 amendments and the August 24, 2023 circular, and that the board approved it on June 28, 2023. It proposed raising the size limit from ₹25,000 crore to **₹50,000 crore**, leaving the 50% test untouched, after 27 of 28 comments on the figure backed it. One commenter wanted ₹10,000 crore, citing opacity; SEBI rejected that, pointing to existing know-your-customer and beneficial ownership rules. So the rule was not intact until November, as "reopened" implies.

### 4. What is proposed now

SEBI's November 20, 2026 consultation paper proposes removing the framework, with comments open until December 11, according to our earlier report of it. We could not open the paper itself. It is a proposal only.

## Why this claim spread

Ramesh tied the proposal to the Adani matter by citing SEBI's September 2026 order closing the minimum public shareholding case against the group, a point we did not check and do not rate. The regulator's side, as reported, is that the rule is stricter than those abroad and counts as one factor in foreign outflows. Both sides can cite the same board papers, which settle what the rule was for but not why it came when it did.
