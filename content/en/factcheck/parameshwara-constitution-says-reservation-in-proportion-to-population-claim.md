## What we found

Parameshwara's claim is misleading. On October 13, 2026, a day after the Karnataka High Court struck down the state's SC/ST quota increase, he said: "The Constitution says that reservation should be provided in proportion to the population." He added that there is "scope for increasing reservation under special circumstances", and that the Cabinet would decide whether to go to the Supreme Court.

For **government jobs and college seats**, the court record does not say that. The High Court held that population size is not a ground for crossing the 50% ceiling, and the Supreme Court's 1992 Indra Sawhney ruling, on which it relies, kept quotas under 50% even though the OBC share of the population was larger.

## The evidence

### 1. What the Act did

The Karnataka Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Reservation of Seats in Educational Institutions and of Appointments or Posts in the Services under the State) Act, 2022, was enacted in January 2023. It raised SC reservation from 15% to 17% and ST reservation from 3% to 7%. With OBC reservation kept at 32%, **the total rose from 50% to 56%**.

### 2. What the High Court held

A bench of Chief Justice Vibhu Bakhru and Justice K S Hemalekha ruled on October 12, 2026. According to reports of the judgment, it struck down Section 3 to the extent it took reservation beyond 50%, and Section 4 on state services. It said the ceiling "has to be understood as circumscribing the power of the State". Karnataka had argued, citing the Nagamohan Das Commission and other reports, that SC and ST populations had grown and that over 90% of the state's people belonged to SC, ST or OBC groups. The court said accepting that "would be destructive of the principle of equality enshrined under the Constitution", and that the state could not use the exceptional-circumstances route merely on that basis. It added that the reports might support internal reservation, but not a higher total.

### 3. The Supreme Court's rule

In Indra Sawhney, the Supreme Court's conclusions say reservation under Article 16(4) should be confined to a minority of seats and "not to exceed 50%". The judgment records that the Mandal Commission found OBCs were about 52% of the population and recommended only 27% reservation "in deference to legal limitation". The ruling itself shows that a population share was not treated as a measure of the quota.

### 4. What Article 16(4) says

Article 16(4) lets the State reserve posts for a backward class of citizens that it believes is not adequately represented in its services. As we read the page we could open, the test is inadequate representation, not a population share.

## Why this claim spread

The claim came as Karnataka's Congress government defended a law passed by the earlier BJP government. Former Chief Minister Siddaramaiah called the verdict a "serious setback" and asked why the BJP raised quotas before the 2023 election without making them constitutionally safe. Parameshwara said the Congress government had continued the arrangement it inherited. The population argument is the state's main answer to the ceiling, and it will now be tested where the 50% rule was made.
