## What we found

Rahul Gandhi's figure does not match the rolls. At the closing session of the Bharat Summit in Hyderabad on November 14, 2026, he said about **70 lakh new voters** were added in Maharashtra between the Lok Sabha and Assembly elections of 2024. The Election Commission's own totals show the electoral roll grew by **40,81,229, about 41 lakh**. The 70 lakh he cites is close to a different number: the rise in votes actually cast, about 69 lakh.

He also said "every single one of those 7 million voters votes for the BJP". No public record can show that, because the ballot is secret.

## The evidence

### 1. The rolls: 41 lakh, not 70 lakh

According to the Election Commission figures, Maharashtra had **9,29,43,890 electors** before the Lok Sabha polls and **9,70,25,119** when the roll was published in October 2024 before the Assembly election. The difference is 40,81,229. This is a net figure: it already counts names removed for death, shifting or duplication.

### 2. Where 70 lakh does appear

Votes polled in Maharashtra were **5,71,79,131 in the Lok Sabha election and 6,40,88,195 in the Assembly election**, a rise of 69,09,064, according to the Chief Electoral Officer's data as tabulated by Alt News. That is turnout, not registration. Turnout rose, and the electorate grew, so both numbers rose; calling the vote rise "new voters" mixes them up. Many of those who voted in November had been on the roll in June and did not vote then.

### 3. The Commission's earlier answer

Gandhi first made the 70 lakh claim on February 3, 2025. On December 24, 2024, answering Congress's similar allegation of an "unprecedented increase", the Commission called it "misleading and factually incorrect". It said only **six constituencies**, not 50, had added more than 50,000 electors between the two polls, and that there were no bulk or unusual deletions: an average of 2,779 electors per constituency had been removed.

### 4. What is fair in his point

A 41 lakh rise in about five months is large, and the Congress has asked for the roll data and for an audit. That is a legitimate question about how the rolls were revised. But it is a smaller, different claim from 70 lakh, and the record does not say whom the new electors voted for.

## Why this claim spread

The line came in a closing speech watched across the opposition, in a season dominated by the Special Intensive Revision of the rolls. It repeats a figure Gandhi has used for nearly two years, so it travels easily. We could not check his other remark, that about 14 crore people who voted in 2024 were later declared non-existent, because he named no document and we found no primary data for it. It is not rated here.
