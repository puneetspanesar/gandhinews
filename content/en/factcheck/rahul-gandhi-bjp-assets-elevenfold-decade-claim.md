## What we found

Rahul Gandhi's claim is mostly true. The BJP's declared assets were **Rs 780.75 crore in 2013-14** and **Rs 9,181.16 crore in 2023-24**, a rise of about 11.8 times, so "elevenfold in a decade" is, if anything, a little low. Taken to the latest year, 2024-25, the party declares **Rs 12,171.18 crore**, about 15.6 times the 2013-14 figure. Two cautions: the numbers are the party's own book values, and they are not adjusted for inflation.

## The claim

At an event in Hyderabad, as reported on November 14, 2026, the Leader of the Opposition said Indian democracy had been destroyed in 2014 when large amounts of money entered politics, and that the BJP's **assets had grown "elevenfold in a decade"**. He did not name a start year or a source for the figure.

## The evidence

### 1. The yearly figures

Every recognised party files audited accounts with the Election Commission each year, including a statement of assets and liabilities. The Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR) tabulates them. For the BJP the series reads Rs 780.75 crore (2013-14), Rs 759.96 crore (2014-15), Rs 893.88 crore (2015-16), Rs 2,904.18 crore (2018-19), Rs 6,046.81 crore (2021-22), Rs 7,052.75 crore (2022-23), **Rs 9,181.16 crore (2023-24)** and Rs 12,171.18 crore (2024-25).

### 2. How the growth came

The rise was not steady. Between 2013-14 and 2016-17 the figure moved from Rs 780.75 crore to Rs 1,213.14 crore, barely half as much again. It then **almost doubled in 2018-19**, from Rs 1,483.35 crore to Rs 2,904.18 crore, and kept climbing: Rs 4,847.79 crore in 2019-20, Rs 4,990.20 crore in 2020-21. The last two years in the series added about Rs 2,130 crore and **about Rs 2,990 crore**, so more than a third of the 2024-25 total was added in the final two years. That is why the multiple depends so much on where the decade ends.

### 3. Which decade you pick

Without a stated start year, three readings are fair. From 2013-14 to 2023-24 the rise is **11.8 times**. From 2014-15 to 2023-24 it is 12.1 times. From 2014-15 to 2024-25, the most recent ten years in the series, it is **16 times**. All three are at or above eleven.

### 4. What the number does and does not show

The figures are what the party declares, at book value, with no correction for rising prices. They do not say where the money came from, and an increase in assets is not in itself a finding of wrongdoing. The BJP's income in 2023-24, as separately reported by ADR, was Rs 4,340.47 crore, but this check does not test how it was raised.

### 5. A cross-check

ADR's October 2017 report on the same accounts gave the BJP's assets at Rs 893.88 crore for 2015-16, up 627 per cent from Rs 122.93 crore in 2004-05. That matches the 2015-16 entry in the yearly series, which is the one point we could check against a separate document.

## Why this claim spread

The speech linked the party's wealth to a wider charge about money in politics, and a single round number is easy to repeat. The BJP has been the richest party by assets for years, so the claim sounds right, and in this case the declared figures agree.

## What would change this rating

If the party's filed 2023-24 or 2024-25 statements differ from ADR's tabulation, the multiple would move. The Election Commission's copies of the audited reports are the document to read.
