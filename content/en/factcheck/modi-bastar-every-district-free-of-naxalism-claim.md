## What we found

Narendra Modi's claim is mostly true. In a written reply to the Rajya Sabha on July 29, 2026, the Home Ministry said **no district is now classified as LWE-affected**, down from 126 before April 2018. The Prime Minister's line, that this is "the first Diwali where every single district in the country is free from Naxalism", matches that official category.

Three qualifiers matter. The same reply keeps **37 districts as "Legacy and Thrust"** and one, West Singhbhum in Jharkhand, as a "District of Concern". Violence has not stopped: the ministry counts **33 Maoist-violence incidents up to July 21, 2026**. And the figure of "more than 230 districts in nearly 20 states", as reported from the speech, does not appear in the official data we read.

## The evidence

### 1. The district count is zero

The ministry's reply traces the count from 126 districts before April 2018 to 90 in April 2018, 70 in July 2021, 38 in April 2024 and 8 in December 2025. For 2026 every state shows nil. According to reports at the time, the ministry told nine states on April 8, 2026 that "no district in the country falls under the LWE-affected category" after a security review completed past the March 31 target date, and that the categorisation had been discontinued.

### 2. "Free" does not mean off the watch list

A "Legacy and Thrust" district is one where the insurgency has been eliminated but support continues to prevent a return. The ministry lists **37 such districts and one "District of Concern"**, where violence is contained and the organisation dismantled but work remains. So the label "affected" is gone, while 38 districts are still tracked.

### 3. Violence is far lower, not zero

The ministry's figures for violence by Maoists show a steep fall: 486 incidents in 2023, 375 in 2024, 234 in 2025 and **33 up to July 21, 2026**, with 6 civilians and 5 security personnel killed this year. Chhattisgarh accounts for 18 of the 33 incidents and 4 of the 5 security-force deaths. Jharkhand has 13 and Telangana 2. The Prime Minister's claim concerned districts, not incidents, so it is not contradicted, but "free from Naxalism" should not be read as "no violence".

### 4. The 230-district peak is not in the official record

In his speech, as reported, Modi said more than 230 districts in nearly 20 states had once been affected. The Home Ministry's own peak is **126 districts in 10 states** under its security-expenditure scheme, and a 2022 reply says violence was reported in 96 districts in 2010. We found no official document that supports 230 or 20 states, though a broader historical count may exist elsewhere.

### 5. The other numbers

Modi said there were "as many as 2,000 Naxal attacks in a single year". The ministry's 2022 reply gives 2,258 incidents in 2009, the all-time high, so the scale is consistent. His figures of 76 CRPF personnel killed at Dantewada in 2010 and more than 1,000 security personnel killed in Chhattisgarh in 2004 to 2013 could not be tested against a primary release.

## Why this claim spread

It was the centrepiece of the Prime Minister's Diwali address to CoBRA and CRPF personnel at Karanpur in Bastar on November 8, 2026, an annual event the Prime Minister has held with the forces since 2014, and it follows the government's own March 31, 2026 deadline to end Left Wing Extremism. The desk has no sourced data on how widely the line was shared.
