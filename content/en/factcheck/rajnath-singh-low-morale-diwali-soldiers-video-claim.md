## What we found

The claim is false. A clip circulating on X from November 9 has a voice sounding like Defence Minister Rajnath Singh saying: "I know that your morale is very low. Along with you, our morale is also low." It was posted with the caption "Rajnath Singh Celebrates Diwali with Soldiers". The Indian Army's fact-check account the same day called it a **digitally manipulated video** and said Singh had not made those remarks.

## The evidence

### 1. What Singh actually said

On Sunday, November 8, 2026, Singh celebrated Diwali with Army personnel at the Sherathang War Memorial near the Nathu La pass in Sikkim, and paid tribute to the soldiers commemorated there. Reports of the event quote him saying: "Your discipline is your greatest strength," and "Your service to the nation will never be forgotten. Every generation of Indians will remember your sacrifice." He told the troops he was proud of their faces, their enthusiasm and their passion, and said citizens could sleep peacefully because of them. Neither report of the visit contains a remark about low morale.

### 2. The original video

The video of the interaction was uploaded to his official YouTube channel on November 8 under the title "Interaction with Indian Army personnel in Sikkim on the occasion of Diwali". According to checks of the clip by others, it shows him paying tribute to the fallen and saying that the soldiers' smile, energy and courage are "the biggest lights of this Diwali". The words in the viral clip do not appear in it. We could not open the upload ourselves.

### 3. What the Army said

The Indian Army's fact-check account on X posted on November 9 under the heading "Detect the Deepfake" that the clip was digitally manipulated, according to reports of the post. It named the accounts spreading it and asked people to check the original speech.

### 4. The audio

Two voice and image checkers, Hive Moderation and the Hiya Deepfake Voice Detector, rated the clip's audio as likely AI-generated, according to reports. Detectors give probabilities, so the finding does not rest on them. It rests on the fact that the speech as delivered, in the reports we read, says nothing of the kind.

## Why this claim spread

The clip was dated to Diwali week and set at a border post, which made it look like a real moment from a real visit. Singh's trip was widely reported, so a viewer could see the visit was genuine, and the false words were then laid over it. It came days after another doctored Singh video about India-China tensions was debunked by others, which shows how quickly such clips are recycled around a real event. The Army's quick response on November 9 is the reason this one is on record as manipulated rather than left as a rumour.
