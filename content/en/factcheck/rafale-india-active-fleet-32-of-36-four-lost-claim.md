## What we found

The number is real and the conclusion drawn from it is not supported. FlightGlobal's World Air Forces 2027 directory, reported on November 20, 2026, lists **32 active Rafales** in the Indian Air Force. Posts since then say the gap from 36 means **four jets were shot down** in the May 2025 conflict with Pakistan. We found nothing official that says so, and the directory itself does not say so either. We rate the claim **misleading**.

## The evidence

### 1. What the official record says

The Ministry of Defence told the Lok Sabha in a written reply on November 20, 2019, released by PIB, that the deal covers **36 Rafale fighters under an inter-governmental agreement signed on September 23, 2016**. That is the purchase, and it is the only official figure we could open. We found no Ministry release, Parliament reply or Air Force statement reporting that any Rafale has been lost or struck off.

### 2. What the directory is

The directory's fleet figures are an estimate drawn from a commercial database that collects open information: military publications, press reports, manufacturers' releases, accident tracking and sightings. It is not an Air Force return. The report on it says the estimate gives **no reason for the revision** and that the 2026 edition had already cut the count to 35 for one loss it could corroborate by images. The broader Pakistani claims of shoot-downs it treated as unverified. A fall from 35 to 32 in a year, with no event named, is a change in an estimate, not a recorded loss.

### 3. Why "active" is not "owned"

An air force's active count leaves out jets in deep maintenance, upgrade or storage. The Air Force does not publish how many of its aircraft are flying on any day. An Air Headquarters request for proposal of June 2026 was reported to seek a five-month bridge support package covering **all 36 Rafales**, which suggests the force still counts 36 on its books. We could not open that document, so we do not rest the rating on it.

### 4. What officials and others have said

Ministry of Defence sources were reported on November 20 as saying the figures did not come from the Ministry or the Air Force. Air Marshal Narmdeshwar Tiwari (retd), a former Vice-Chief of the Air Staff, called the count "patently false" in a post on X. These are reported statements, not published records, so they weigh less than a document would. They do not confirm the claim, and no official has said four jets are missing.

## Why this claim spread

The May 2025 conflict left a long dispute over aircraft losses. Pakistan said it downed Indian jets, India has not published a loss list, and a precise, foreign-sourced number fell into that gap. Shared without the words "estimate" and "active", the figure reads as a confession. The one fact that would settle it is a published inventory, which the Air Force has never released.

## What would change this rating

A Ministry reply in Parliament, or an Air Force statement, giving the number of Rafales on strength would settle it either way. If it said fewer than 36, the claim of a fall would stand, though the cause would still need its own record. Until then, 32 is a number about an estimate, not about the fleet.
