## What we found

The claim is **mostly true**. On Diwali, November 8, 2026, at the CoBRA operations base in Karanpur, Prime Minister Narendra Modi said this was "the first Diwali when every district of the country is free from Naxalism". By the government's own classification, that is right: **no district in India is listed as affected by Left Wing Extremism** any more.

What the sentence cannot mean is that Maoist violence has stopped. Official figures show incidents and deaths still being recorded in 2026, in much smaller numbers.


## The evidence

### 1. The official count of affected districts is zero

A research document published by the Press Information Bureau on August 1, 2026 says the number of districts affected by Left Wing Extremism fell from **126 in 2014 to zero by March-April 2026**. The most-affected category fell from 35 districts to zero. The document says India became "effectively free from Naxalism" on March 31, 2026, the target the government had set on August 24, 2024.

### 2. Violence has fallen sharply but not to nothing

The same document puts violent incidents at **870 in 2014 and 33 in 2026 up to July 29**, and fatalities at 310 in 2014 against **11 in 2026** up to the same date. A written reply in the Rajya Sabha in July 2026 shows the same pattern: Maoist-linked incidents down from 486 in 2023 to 234 in 2025, then 33 so far this year. Eleven deaths in seven months is a long way below the past, and it is not zero.

### 3. What the Prime Minister did not claim

Modi did not say that no Maoist remains. According to reports of the speech, he warned that supporters of the ideology, whom he called "dimagi Naxals", are still present in political ranks. That part of the speech could not be checked against a full text.

## Why this claim spread

The remark came on Modi's 13th consecutive Diwali with the security forces, and it is the political payoff of a target set more than two years ago. It will be repeated as the end of a decades-old conflict. The fair reading is narrower: **a classification reached, a threat much reduced**, and residual violence that the Home Ministry's own numbers still record.
