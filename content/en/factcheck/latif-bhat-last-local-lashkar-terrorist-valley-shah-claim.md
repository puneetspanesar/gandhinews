## What we found

Home Minister Amit Shah's description of Latif Bhat is **mostly true, with a caveat about what can be checked**. Bhat, a Lashkar-e-Taiba commander from Khrewan in Kulgam, was killed in the early hours of November 18, 2026, at Zangalpora in the Devsar area, after he took shelter in a mosque and refused repeated calls to surrender. The Jammu and Kashmir Police and the Army's Chinar Corps both confirmed it. Mr Shah then called him the **last known active local Lashkar terrorist in the Valley**.

The sentence is built with four limits. He said "known", not "actual". He said "local", which leaves out foreign fighters. He said "Lashkar", not every group. And he said "Valley", not the whole Union Territory. Within those limits the claim agrees with what security officials have said for months. It cannot be audited, because the list it rests on is not public.

## The evidence

### 1. The killing is confirmed

The J&K Police and the Chinar Corps, which named the operation Op Zangalpura, said Bhat was shot after he opened fire from inside the mosque. Senior Superintendent of Police Kulgam Anayat Ali Chowdhary said the forces tried to persuade him to surrender, including through a video call with the Commanding Officer of 9 Rashtriya Rifles. Police said his body and a weapon were recovered. The same operation closed an eight-day hunt that began after three CRPF men were wounded at Akhran on November 11. His associate Umar Nisar Dar had been killed at Laroo on November 16.

### 2. What the Home Ministry said

The Home Ministry release of November 18 names the forces Mr Shah congratulated: the J&K Police, the Army, the CRPF and the intelligence agencies. It describes Bhat as the last active Lashkar terrorist in the Valley and carries Mr Shah's line: "The nation salutes the brave Jawans of JKP, Army and CRPF and intelligence agencies." We could not open the release page in this check, so the wording of the "last" description is taken from the desk's own reading of it on November 18 and from two reports that agree. The release gives no number and no list.

### 3. The count behind the claim is not public

An August 3, 2026 report said J&K was down to **one active local terrorist** according to security records kept by the J&K Police and the CRPF, and that officials estimated 40 to 50 foreign terrorists still operated in the Union Territory. The report names no official and publishes no register. That matches Mr Shah's claim in substance, and it means the claim has had the same unnamed basis for more than three months. We found no Home Ministry statement, Parliament reply or police document that publishes the number of active local militants today, so the figure can be believed but not independently counted.

### 4. Foreign terrorists remain

Lieutenant Governor Manoj Sinha said on November 18, at a police passing-out parade in Udhampur, that **only a handful of foreign terrorists** remain in Jammu and Kashmir. That is consistent with Mr Shah's wording and a reminder that "last local" is not "last terrorist". Bhat is also tied by police to a July 22 attack on police in Anantnag and is suspected in the killing of two migrant workers on August 1, so his death ends a particular threat, not the militant presence as a whole.

## Why this claim spread

The line came the day before Mr Shah opened the two-day Anti-Terrorism Conference in New Delhi, on cross-border terrorism, and it fits the government's message that home-grown militancy in the Valley has collapsed. It was shared widely as a bare headline, "last local terrorist killed", with the limits dropped. The limits are what make it hold. Read without them, it overstates what the public record can show; read with them, it matches what officials have said and the one report of the count.

## What would change this rating

A published register from the Home Ministry or J&K Police would settle it either way. If an active local Lashkar militant in the Valley were later named, the word "known" would carry the claim, but the headline version would fail. Until then the rating stays at mostly true.
