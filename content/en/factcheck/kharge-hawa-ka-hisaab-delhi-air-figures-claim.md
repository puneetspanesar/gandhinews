## What we found

Mallikarjun Kharge's numbers on Delhi's air are **mostly true**. The blank monitors, the unspent environmental compensation and the low use of clean-air funds all match what the records show. The death toll he cited is **a modelled estimate, not a count**, and the Union government disputes drawing a direct line from it to pollution.

## The claim

On November 10, 2026, the Congress president posted on X asking why India is becoming a "Vishwaguru" in air pollution and demanding a "Hawa Ka Hisaab" from the Prime Minister. He said PM2.5 readings disappeared from 36 of Delhi's 59 monitoring stations at the season's peak; that an estimated 17,188 Delhi deaths in 2023, nearly 15% of the total, were tied to particulate pollution; that 1,69,400 children under five died of air-pollution-related causes in India in 2021; that ₹1,154 crore, about 60%, of Delhi's environmental compensation was unspent, per a September 2026 RTI reply; and that Delhi used only 17% of the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) money released to it.

## The evidence

### 1. Blank monitors: accurate

On Monday, November 9, **36 of the 59 stations** in the Central Pollution Control Board's Delhi network were blank overnight, with 125 station-hours missing. The Delhi Pollution Control Committee (DPCC) then released the missing hourly values, which showed PM2.5 at **3,744 micrograms per cubic metre at Mundka at 2 am**. An official put the gap down to technical glitches. The restored hours were provisional when published, so the count of blank stations describes the first reports, not a later correction.

### 2. Environmental compensation: accurate

The RTI figures, dated September 16, 2026, show Delhi collected about **₹1,935.44 crore** as Environmental Compensation Charge and that **₹1,153.93 crore remains unspent**, which is 59.6%. Kharge's "₹1,154 crore, about 60%" is correct. The Supreme Court has directed Delhi to spend the balance on long-term measures and on interim recommendations of the Commission for Air Quality Management. The reply itself is not public and the report does not name who answered it.

### 3. NCAP funds: matches the RTI, disputed by the minister

An RTI reply obtained by environmentalist Vimlendu Jha showed Delhi was allocated **₹81.36 crore over five years and used ₹14.1 crore**, or 17.3%. Delhi's Environment Minister Manjinder Singh Sirsa rejected the figures in January 2026, saying use was complete last year on road works and mechanical sweepers and that about ₹80 crore was given to the MCD this year. Kharge's figure is the RTI's; whether it is the right measure is what the two sides dispute.

### 4. Deaths: an estimate, with a caveat

The **17,188 deaths in Delhi in 2023** come from Global Burden of Disease 2023 data from the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation, analysed by the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air, and amount to nearly 15% of deaths. The 1,69,400 child deaths in 2021 come from the State of Global Air 2024 report, which works from the Global Burden of Disease 2021 study. Both are model-based estimates. Minister of State Kirti Vardhan Singh told the Rajya Sabha on July 24 that air pollution is one of many contributing factors and that isolating it as the sole cause of death is not scientifically accurate.

## Why this claim spread

It came the day after Diwali-night data went missing and then reappeared, in a week of GRAP Stage III curbs, so a list of figures that fit that story travelled fast. The figures are real; the weak point is that the post links none of them.

## What would change this rating

If the RTI replies are published and show different totals, or the Delhi government produces records that the NCAP money was spent as it says, this rating will be revisited.
