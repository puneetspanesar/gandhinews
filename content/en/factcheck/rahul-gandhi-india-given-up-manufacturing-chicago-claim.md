## What we found

The claim is misleading. In Chicago, Rahul Gandhi said India had given up on production and manufacturing in favour of services. The official accounts say otherwise: **manufacturing output grew 10.88% a year** at constant prices between 2022-23 and 2025-26. What is true is that the sector has **not gained share**: it was 14.7% of the economy's gross value added in 2022-23 and 14.8% in 2025-26.

## The claim

In an address at the University of Chicago, circulated in a video on November 18 and 19, 2026, the Leader of the Opposition argued that India had moved away from making things, that China's weight in the world comes from being its biggest producer, and that artificial intelligence is now eroding the services India turned to. We checked the first of these, the one that can be tested against published data.

## The evidence

### 1. Output is rising, not abandoned

The statistics ministry told the Lok Sabha on August 12, 2026 that manufacturing gross value added (GVA), the sector's contribution to output, grew at a **compound rate of 10.88% a year** from 2022-23 to 2025-26 in the revised series with base year 2022-23. Year on year it grew 12.7% in 2023-24, 9.3% in 2024-25 and 10.7% in 2025-26.

### 2. The latest quarter

For April to June 2026 the ministry's press note puts manufacturing growth at **9.2%**, up from 8.3% a year earlier, against 10.0% for the tertiary (services) sector and 8.2% for total GVA. Within manufacturing the industrial production index shows electrical equipment up 27.0% and capital goods up 15.2% in the quarter. The sector is not shrinking.

### 3. The share has stayed put

Here the speaker's frustration has support. In current prices manufacturing was **14.7% of GVA in 2022-23, 14.7% in 2023-24, 14.5% in 2024-25 and 14.8% in 2025-26**. A sector that grows about as fast as the rest of the economy keeps its share; it does not gain one. In April to June 2026 the nominal figure was ₹10,34,961 crore, up only 7.7% against 11.5% for all GVA, which on our arithmetic puts its first-quarter share near 12.9%, from 13.3% a year before. A first-quarter share is not comparable with an annual one, and MoSPI has said the new series can show a falling price index for manufacturing, which pulls the nominal figure down.

### 4. Jobs

The Periodic Labour Force Survey annual report for 2025, released on March 27, 2026, puts **12.1% of workers in manufacturing**, up from 11.6% in 2024, with 43.0% still in agriculture. The report warns that 2025 uses a revised sample design, so the one-year rise should not be over-read. Manufacturing is a small employer, but it is not a shrinking one in this series.

### 5. What the data cannot settle

Neither source says whether India makes enough, or whether the parts in its products are made at home. The anecdote about Chinese motors and batteries in foreign drones is not a statistic and is not tested here.

## Why this claim spread

The remark came in a talk to a foreign campus, in the middle of a political row over the Leader of the Opposition's US trip, and it was clipped and shared by both sides: supporters as a verdict on policy, critics as proof that he runs the country down abroad. A sweeping sentence about a whole sector is easy to quote and hard to test.

## What would change this rating

If the ministry's next quarterly estimate, for July to September, shows manufacturing growth falling well below the rest of the economy, or the labour survey shows fewer workers in factories, the sector's decline would have data behind it. A share rising clearly above 15% would weaken the argument that it is stuck.
