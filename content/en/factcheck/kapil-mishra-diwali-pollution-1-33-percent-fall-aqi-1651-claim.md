## What we found

The Delhi minister's claims are **misleading**. His count of nearly 1,200 Punjab farm fires on Diwali is close to the state's own **1,152**. His **AQI of 1,651 at Sangrur** cannot be an official reading, because the national index ends at 500. And his statement that Delhi's post-Diwali pollution **fell 1.33% this year** names no measure, and the published readings do not produce it.

## The claim

At a press conference in Delhi on November 9, Kapil Mishra said satellite data showed fires at about 100 to 125 places a day on November 5, 6 and 7, rising to nearly 1,200 on November 8. He said the AQI in Malout, Abohar and Sangrur went past 1,200, with Sangrur at 1,651 in the early hours of Monday. He also said post-Diwali pollution in Delhi had risen 43.33% in 2023 and about 10% in 2024, and fell 1.33% this year.

## The evidence

### 1. The fire count: close

Punjab's Remote Sensing Centre detected **1,152 farm fires on November 8**, the worst day of the season, after 163 on November 7. "Nearly 1,200" is a fair round-up. His figure of 100 to 125 for November 7 is lower than the state's 163.

### 2. AQI of 1,651: not an official figure

The Central Pollution Control Board's National Air Quality Index has six bands, from Good (0 to 50) to **Severe (401 to 500)**. Nothing sits above 500. A reading of 1,200 or 1,651 therefore cannot be an official AQI. Readings that high show up on private sensor apps and in raw particle counts, which are not the CPCB index. The reports of the press conference do not say where the minister's numbers came from.

### 3. The 1.33% fall: no published basis

The minister named no pollutant, station set or dates. Delhi's city average on Diwali eve was 369, on Diwali day 376, and at 7 am the morning after 368. From Saturday to Monday that is a fall of about 0.3%; from Sunday, about 2.1%. Neither is 1.33%. The more useful comparison is the day after Diwali across years, and there this year is the **highest of the last four**.

## Why this claim spread

It came in a charged week: GRAP Stage III curbs were in force, Delhi's air stayed in the very poor band, and the Delhi and Punjab governments were trading blame. A single press conference carried a believable fire count, a dramatic AQI and a tidy percentage, so the whole set travelled together. The sharpest rival claim, from Delhi's opposition, that AQI crossed 3,000 on Diwali night, runs into the same ceiling of 500.

## What would change this rating

If the minister publishes the series behind the three percentages, or the CPCB bulletins show a different day-after-Diwali comparison, this rating will be revisited.
