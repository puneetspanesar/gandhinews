## What we found

The viral clip is **false**. It is real footage of Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov from October 31, 2026, with **a different audio track laid over it by AI**. In the original, he talks about whether Russian President Vladimir Putin would meet Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. He does not mention India.

## The claim

From November 2, 2026, a video carrying an M World logo was shared on X with the caption that Lavrov had said India is only an **oil-trading partner** and has no right to advise Russia. Several accounts posted it, among them two with large followings. At least one of the two deleted the post later.

## The evidence

### 1. The original video says something else

The source clip was uploaded on October 31, 2026, by M World. In it, Lavrov rejects Ukrainian claims that Putin refused to meet Zelenskyy. He says Zelenskyy must travel to Moscow if he wants a direct meeting. The subject is the Russia-Ukraine war. **India is not mentioned.** This matches the context of our own report on Lavrov's remarks about Zelenskyy and a possible peace deal.

### 2. The viral version matches the original frame for frame

Keyframes from the viral clip, put through a reverse image search, lead to the October 31 upload. Clothing, background, head movements and gestures are the same in both, and both carry the same visual glitch and the M World logo. Only the sound differs. In the viral version, Lavrov's lip movements and the audio fall out of step, a common sign of **synthetic audio laid over real video**.

### 3. Russia's embassy disowned it

The Embassy of Russia in India posted on X on November 3 that the clip was one of "silly AI fakes". The Ministry of External Affairs' fact-check handle is also reported to have flagged the video as fake on the same day. We could not open either post ourselves; they are listed in our sources.

## Why this claim spread

The claim played on a live argument in India about Russian oil and the country's standing with Moscow, and it came from a source that looks authoritative: a foreign minister's own voice and face. Ordinary signs of a fake were easy to miss on a phone: a news-style logo, a familiar speaker and a clip that was a few seconds long. Another deepfake in the same week used the same pattern on India's own External Affairs Minister.

## How to check a clip like this

Search for the speaker's name with the event and date, and find the longer original. Compare what the lips do with what the audio says. When a foreign government's embassy or India's MEA fact-check account has spoken, look there first.
