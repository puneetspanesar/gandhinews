## What we found

The claim is **true**. The Mangaluru cavern is India's largest underground LPG store, at **80,000 metric tonnes**, against 60,000 tonnes at Visakhapatnam, the only other one found. What the claim does not say is how small that is next to national demand: **about one day of use**.

## The claim

On November 11 the Prime Minister's Office said Mr Modi would dedicate "India's largest underground LPG storage cavern" at Mangaluru, with a capacity of 80,000 metric tonnes and a cost of over ₹850 crore, along with a 9.4 km pipeline from New Mangalore Port. On November 13 in Bengaluru he did so, and tied it to the case for energy security during the Gulf war.

## The evidence

### 1. The size: matches the record

The PMO release states **80,000 metric tonnes, over ₹850 crore**. Trade reports on the build, by Megha Engineering for HPCL, describe two chambers, one for propane and one for butane, and call it the largest and only the second such cavern in the country. The first, at Visakhapatnam, is reported at **60,000 tonnes**. We found nothing larger.

### 2. The scale: what it covers

India used **33.212 million tonnes of LPG in 2025-26**, according to Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell data as reported. That is about 91,000 tonnes a day. In March 2026, when supply was disrupted, use fell 13% to 2.379 million tonnes, about 77,000 tonnes a day. Either way, the cavern holds **between 0.9 and 1.0 days of national use**. Nearly 60% of the LPG India uses is imported, much of it through the Strait of Hormuz.

### 3. The supply it sits against

The storage claim is only half of the energy-security argument, so it helps to see the supply side. India's LPG production was **13.1 million tonnes in 2025-26**, up from 12.8 million tonnes a year earlier, and 1.4 million tonnes in March 2026, against 1.1 million a year before. Against consumption of 33.2 million tonnes, that is **less than 40% from home refineries and gas plants**, which squares with the roughly 60% import dependence reported. In March, when Gulf supply was disrupted, household LPG use fell 8.1% to 2.219 million tonnes, while industrial and bulk use fell far more, by about 48% and over 75% respectively. In other words, the shortage was absorbed mostly by rationing business use, not by drawing on storage.

A cavern does one job well: it holds a cushion near a port, close to where cargoes arrive, so a delayed ship does not empty bottling plants on the same day. At 80,000 tonnes it is a useful cushion, not a reserve. That is a limit on what the claim can be read to mean, not a mistake in it.

## Why this claim spread

The cavern was the headline item of a ₹15,600 crore programme, and it arrived in a week of attacks on shipping in the Gulf, when fuel storage is a political subject. A "largest in the country" line travels easily, and here it is correct. The risk is in how readers take it: that one cavern makes a meaningful dent in supply risk. It is one of two, and together they are well under two days of demand.

## What would change this rating

A larger underground LPG cavern in operation, or a different capacity in the speech text or in HPCL's own record, would change it. A figure for how much LPG the state-owned firms hold above ground, which this fact check did not find, would give the fuller picture of how many days India is covered.
