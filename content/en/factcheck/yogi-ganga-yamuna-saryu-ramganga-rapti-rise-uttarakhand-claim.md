## What we found

The claim is mostly true. At the Uttarakhand Mahotsav in Lucknow on November 11, 2026, Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath said, according to reports of his speech, that the Ganga, Yamuna, Saryu, Ramganga and Rapti **all rise in Uttarakhand**. Four of the five do. The Rapti that flows into Uttar Pradesh, the West Rapti, **rises in Nepal**. He also cited Govind Ballabh Pant, Uttar Pradesh's first Chief Minister, as a son of the Almora hills, and that part holds.

## The evidence

### 1. The Ganga and the Yamuna

India-WRIS, the government's water resources portal, says the Ganga "originates as Bhagirathi from the Gangotri glaciers in the Himalayas at an elevation of about 7010m." The Uttarkashi district website calls Gangotri the origin of the river, called Bhagirathi at the source, which takes the name Ganga from Devprayag. The Central Water Commission says the Yamuna **originates from the Yamunotri glacier** in Uttarkashi district at about 6,387 m, and the same district website calls Yamunotri the source of the Yamuna.

### 2. The Ramganga

The Central Water Commission says the Ramganga "originates from Doodhatoli ranges in the district of Pauri Garhwal, Uttarakhand," and joins the Ganga on its left bank near Kannauj. A separate Ramganga (East) rises from the Namik glacier in Pithoragarh, per that district's website, and is a different river.

### 3. The Saryu

The Pithoragarh district website says the Sarju originates **in the extreme south of the adjacent Almora district**, and that it forms the boundary with Almora before joining the Kali near Pancheswar. The source usually given for it, Sarmul in Bageshwar district, is not stated on any official page we could open, so we say only that it rises in Uttarakhand.

### 4. The Rapti

Nepal's Water and Energy Commission Secretariat groups the West Rapti with rivers that "originate in the Midlands or the Mahabharat Range," not in the Himalaya. General references describe it as flowing through Nepal before it crosses into Uttar Pradesh and joins the Ghaghara. **No official record places its source in Uttarakhand.**

### 5. Pant's birthplace

The Almora district website describes Khunt, near Shitla Khet between Ranikhet and Almora, as the birthplace of "the first chief minister of the province of Uttar Pradesh, Pandit Govind Ballabh Pant," where a centenary memorial stands. A standard reference work also places his birth at Khunt near Almora and records him as head of the United Provinces government from 1937 to 1939 and from 1946 to 1954.

## Why this claim spread

The line came in a reply to Samajwadi Party chief Akhilesh Yadav, who on October 24, 2026, said the biggest infiltrators in Uttar Pradesh were from Uttarakhand, as we reported earlier. The list of rivers turns a political argument into a point about shared heritage, and four of its five names are correct. The Rapti is the slip: it is a famous river of eastern Uttar Pradesh, and its Nepali origin is less widely known.
