## What we found

Mallikarjun Kharge's figures are **mostly true**. The official Delhi price series from the Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell (PPAC) shows petrol at Rs 71.41 a litre on April 16, 2014, and Rs 71.51 on June 7, 2014, with diesel at Rs 56.71 on May 16, 2014. A Delhi listing for November 15, 2026, gives **Rs 102.12 for petrol and Rs 95.20 for diesel**. That is a rise of about 43% for petrol and about 68% for diesel.

The claim hides a choice, though. The same PPAC table has petrol at Rs 61.33 and diesel at Rs 50.51 by December 16, 2014, after prices fell in the second half of that year. From that low, petrol is up about 67% and diesel about 88%.

## The claim

Reports of a Congress statement after the fuel price hikes of May 2026 quote Kharge as saying petrol has gone up 43% and diesel 68% since 2014. The statement came as the party attacked the government over what it called the fourth increase in ten days. The comparison with 2014 is the party's standing line on fuel.

## The evidence

### 1. The 2014 prices

PPAC's table of Delhi retail prices lists revisions through 2014. Petrol was Rs 72.43 on January 4, Rs 71.41 on April 16 and **Rs 71.51 on June 7**. Diesel was Rs 56.71 on May 16 and Rs 57.28 on June 1. The government took office on May 26, 2014, so both fall in the same fortnight.

### 2. Today's prices

A daily listing dated November 15, 2026, gives Rs 102.12 for petrol and Rs 95.20 for diesel in Delhi. PPAC's own page for current prices points to a PDF we could not read, so this figure is not from the official sheet and should be treated as such.

### 3. The arithmetic

Rs 102.12 against Rs 71.41 is +43.0%, and against Rs 71.51 it is +42.8%. Rs 95.20 against Rs 56.71 is +67.9%, and against Rs 57.28 it is +66.2%. Kharge's numbers sit inside that range.

### 4. What the percentages leave out

A price rise since 2014 is not the same as a rise in the cost of fuel. We did not open data on crude prices or on central and state taxes, so this check says nothing on how much of the increase comes from each. Retail petrol and diesel prices include both.

## Why this claim spread

Fuel prices touch every household and every transporter, and the 2014 comparison is easy to say in one line. Each new increase brings it back. The ruling side usually answers with the tax and crude price picture, which this check does not test.

## What would change this rating

If PPAC's current sheet gives a Delhi price that differs from Rs 102.12 or Rs 95.20, the percentages move. If the Congress president used a different base month, the figures change with it. A rating of Misleading would need a base that was clearly picked to exaggerate; the mid-2014 prices used here are the ones in force when the government took office.
