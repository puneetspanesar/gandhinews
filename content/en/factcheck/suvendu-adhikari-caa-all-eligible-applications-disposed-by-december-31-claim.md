## What we found

The claim is **misleading**. On Friday, November 6, 2026, West Bengal Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari said in Aranghata, Nadia: "All eligible applications under the CAA will be disposed of by December 31." He put the pending number at about **2.5 lakh applications** in Nadia and North 24 Parganas.

The law does not work that way. Under the Citizenship (Amendment) Rules, 2024, an application goes to the **Empowered Committee through a district-level committee**, and neither step has a deadline in the rules. The chief minister's own numbers show a pace far below what the promise needs. No official count after November 1 was found.

## The evidence

### 1. Who decides

Rule 11A says an application under section 6B "shall be submitted by the applicant in electronic form" to the Empowered Committee through the District Level Committee "as may be notified by the Central Government". The committee, headed by a designated officer, **verifies the documents** and administers the oath of allegiance, then forwards the file to the Empowered Committee. Rule 13A says the Empowered Committee, after such inquiry as it considers necessary, "may grant citizenship". The state government is not named in either rule. An explainer of the rules says the state-level Empowered Committee is headed by the Director of Census Operations, and the designated officer is a postal superintendent. Reports in May 2024 said Bengal's first grants came from the state's Empowered Committee.

Adhikari has said hearings run through district magistrates and sub-divisional officers, and on August 24 that the Government of India had "entrusted this responsibility" to him. We found **no order or delegation** that moves the decision to the state. If one exists, it has not been published in anything we could open.

### 2. Is there a deadline?

The rule text we read sets no time limit for verification or for the committee's decision. A year-end date is the chief minister's own target, not one set by the law. It has also moved. On August 1 he said pending applications would be disposed of within six months; on November 6 the date was December 31.

### 3. The pace so far

On August 24 he said about **23,000 certificates** had been issued, out of 2.30 lakh applications, with about 2.07 lakh pending. On November 1 he said about **25,000 people** had received citizenship in Bengal. That is about 2,000 more in 69 days. Clearing 2.5 lakh applications between November 6 and December 31, 55 days, would need about **4,500 decisions a day**, which is more than the state has recorded in ten weeks. The two counts come from different speeches and may not be measured the same way, and the statewide and two-district pending figures do not match each other.

### 4. What "disposed of" means

Disposal covers rejections as well as grants. The promise was reported as citizenship for every eligible applicant, which is a stronger statement. A candidate whose papers fail the December 31, 2014, entry test is not eligible, and a decision against them is still a disposal.

### 5. What would settle it

Three numbers would settle the question: how many applications the district-level committees have verified, how many files the Empowered Committee has decided, and how many of those were grants and how many refusals. **None of the three has been published** in the material we found. The chief minister's figures are the only counts in public, and they are spoken numbers, not a record. Until the Centre or the committee releases a dated tally, December 31 cannot be tested against anything but the pace he has himself described.

## Why this claim spread

The date is **easy to remember**, the number is large, and the audience was the Matua community, which is waiting for its certificates. A promise that has a date and a figure is easy to repeat. Readers should hold it as a state target until the committee's decisions, which the rules place elsewhere, are published.
