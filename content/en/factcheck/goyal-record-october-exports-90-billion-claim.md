## What we found

Piyush Goyal's claim holds up against the Commerce Ministry's own numbers. October's goods exports were **$49.47 billion, the highest for any month**, in the Commerce Secretary's words. Together with services, exports came to **$89.99 billion**, which is "nearly $90 billion". Two details need care: the services figure is an estimate, and the same data show a goods trade deficit at a one-year high.

## The claim

Speaking at the inauguration of the India International Trade Fair in New Delhi, as reported on November 14, 2026, Goyal said India had recorded an all-time high in exports in October, reaching nearly $90 billion, made up of about $50 billion in goods and $40 billion in services. He said exports for the first seven months of the year reached $570 billion, that international trade should grow 15 to 16 per cent this year, and that India is on course to meet its **$1 trillion export target**.

## The evidence

### 1. The October figure: accurate

The ministry's figures, released on November 13, give goods exports of **$49.47 billion**, up 45.06% from $34.11 billion in October 2025, and total goods and services exports of **$89.99 billion**, up 29.9% from $69.27 billion. Goyal's "$50 billion and $40 billion" is a rounding of $49.47 billion and $40.51 billion.

### 2. The services half: an estimate

The $40.51 billion for services is **not a reported figure**. The ministry said the October services numbers are estimates because the latest Reserve Bank of India services data runs only to September. A record for goods is firm; a record for the total depends on that estimate holding.

### 3. Seven months and the target: consistent

April to October exports of goods and services are estimated at **$568.84 billion**, up 16.66%, which rounds to the $570 billion he gave. Goyal also said exports were about $480 billion at the six-month mark, 48% of the target. Subtracting October from the seven-month total gives $478.85 billion, so the two statements agree. The target needs about **$431 billion in five months**, roughly $86 billion a month, below October's level. His 15 to 16 per cent growth outlook is a forecast, and the year-to-date rate is 16.66%.

### 4. What the claim leaves out

Goods imports were $89.35 billion in October, up from $74.61 billion in September, and the goods deficit reached **$39.88 billion, a one-year high**. The petroleum import bill was $22.82 billion, up 54.3% on the year, and gold imports were $5.51 billion. The growth in exports is also measured against a weak base: goods exports of $34.11 billion in October 2025 were well below this year's run. Against that, October 2025's goods deficit was $42.58 billion, so the gap is smaller than a year ago.

## Why this claim spread

The figures came out the day before the trade fair opened, with a minister who had a $1 trillion target to defend and a new monthly record to show. A round "$90 billion" travels more easily than a split between goods and an estimated services number.

## What would change this rating

If the RBI's October services data come in well below $40.51 billion, or the ministry revises the goods figure, the record for the total could fall away. The November data are due in mid-December.
