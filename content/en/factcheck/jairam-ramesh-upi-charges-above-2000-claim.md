## What we found

Jairam Ramesh's claim is mostly true. Parliament amended the Payment and Settlement Systems Act in August 2026 so that the Centre decides by notification which payments are shielded from charges. The September 14 notification shielded only UPI payments up to ₹2,000 and RuPay debit card payments. The next day NPCI set a **0.4%** merchant discount rate (MDR) on merchant payments above ₹2,000 from October 15.

The claim leaves out who pays. The fee falls on merchants, person-to-person transfers stay free at any value, and NPCI's FAQ bars merchants from passing the fee on to customers.

## The evidence

### 1. What Ramesh said

On September 15, 2026, UNI reported that Ramesh, Congress general secretary in-charge communications, accused the government of "opening the door to charges on Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions". He said the new notification removed a safeguard against fees on transactions above Rs 2,000, and that it vindicated a warning he gave on August 6.

Source: [UNI, September 15, 2026](https://www.uniindia.com/national/politics-upi-ramesh-modi-congress/180101)

### 2. The law now lets the Centre pick protected payments

PRS records that the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 was introduced on August 4, passed by the Lok Sabha on August 6 and by the Rajya Sabha on August 10, and that it amends the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. Section 10A earlier barred charges on payments made through modes prescribed under Section 269SU of the Income-tax Act. The amendment replaced that link with modes "as the Central Government may, by notification, specify". The Bill itself did not impose any charge.

Sources: [PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-taxation-and-other-laws-amendment-bill-2026); [LiveLaw, August 11, 2026](https://www.livelaw.in/news-updates/parliament-passes-taxation-laws-amendment-bill-paving-way-for-possible-upi-charges-545218)

### 3. Only payments up to ₹2,000 kept protection

The Finance Ministry's Department of Financial Services issued notification S.O. 5067(E) on September 14, 2026, naming RuPay debit cards and UPI payments up to **₹2,000**. It quoted the notification: "No bank or system provider shall impose, whether directly or indirectly, any charge upon a person". The bar covers both payer and payee.

Sources: [LiveLaw, September 15, 2026](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/centre-prohibits-charges-on-upi-transactions-up-to-rs-2000-rupay-debit-card-550015); [News On AIR, September 15, 2026](https://newsonair.gov.in/upi-payments-of-up-to-2000-rupees-to-remain-free-of-bank-charges/)

### 4. A merchant fee starts on October 15

The NPCI-led UPI and Services Steering Committee set a 0.4% MDR on person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000 from October 15, 2026, with a ₹300 cap. Railways, telecom, insurance and fuel pay a flat ₹5. Small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month keep zero MDR and P2P stays free. SCC Online's summary of NPCI's FAQ says merchants "will not be permitted to pass MDR charges on to customers".

Sources: [Deccan Chronicle, September 15, 2026](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/business/npci-announces-04-merchant-charge-on-upi-payments-above-2000-1987634); [SCC Online, September 16, 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/16/npci-released-upi-mdr-faqs-explained/)

## Why this claim spread

Ramesh had raised the issue while the Bill was before Parliament, and later dated his warning to August 6. He alleged that the amendment may have been prompted by the US Trade Representative's criticism of India's zero-fee UPI and RuPay system. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman replied publicly on August 6. UPI merchant payments have carried zero MDR since January 2020, so the September framework brings back a merchant fee on some UPI payments for the first time since then.
