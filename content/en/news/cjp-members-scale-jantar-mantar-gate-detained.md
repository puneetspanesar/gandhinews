Leaders of the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) climbed over a locked gate into Jantar Mantar early on Tuesday, carrying the national flag, and were detained by Delhi Police. The site was removed from the city's list of designated protest venues over the weekend.

Delhi Police detained CJP leaders Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka as they tried to reach the protest ground. Party founder Abhijeet Dipke said Ajinkya Shinde, the party's national organisation in-charge, and several other team members were also picked up. Police have not said how many people were held or where they were taken.

## Over the gate

A video Dipke posted shows Das and Ranka climbing the gate with the Tricolour while the group shouts "Bharat Mata ki Jai" and "Gyanesh Kumar isteefa do", a call for the Chief Election Commissioner to resign. Police rushed in and took them away.

"Cockroaches with the Tiranga sneaked into Jantar Mantar early this morning before they were picked up by Delhi Police," Dipke wrote on X. Tagging Prime Minister Narendra Modi, he added: "You cannot denotify the resistance."

Ranka posted his own clip of the climb with the caption "Hands Off Jantar Mantar!"

## A site taken off the list

Jantar Mantar, close to Parliament, has long been Delhi's designated protest venue. Under an order from Police Commissioner Anurag Kumar, based on recommendations of a Union Home Ministry committee, it no longer is. Ramlila Maidan, Burari Ground and CBD Ground in east Delhi have been named instead.

The CJP, a youth movement that began online in May, wants Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar to resign over the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls. Its call for a march to Jantar Mantar on 10 October ended with police detaining thousands of people across central Delhi.

Das and Ranka were held that day too, along with Dipke, after their flight from Mumbai landed in Delhi. All three were released on Sunday on a ₹50,000 surety bond to keep the peace for a year.

Delhi Police have said a security review of the three new protest sites is complete and deployment is being reviewed daily. They have also asked for 273 companies of central armed police to stay in the capital until 20 October, citing the possibility of further CJP protests.

## In court today

The Supreme Court on Tuesday hears the petitions over the weekend detentions and a contempt plea against the Union Home Secretary, the Delhi Police Commissioner and the Delhi Metro chief. At a special sitting on Sunday, a bench of Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana said nobody should be detained "save and except by procedure established by law", particularly people in peaceful, unarmed assemblies.

The court is also due to hear a plea from the Supreme Court Bar Association over disruption to lawyers and court work during the protests. Separately, the Delhi High Court has pleas before it on the protests and on the closure of Jantar Mantar as a protest site.
