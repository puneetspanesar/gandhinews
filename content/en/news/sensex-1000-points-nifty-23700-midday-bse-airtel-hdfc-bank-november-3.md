Indian shares **extended their rally for a fourth day** on Tuesday, with the Sensex climbing about 1,000 points by early afternoon as banks and telecom stocks drew steady buying.

At midday the Sensex stood at 76,588.30, **up 895.52 points or about 1.2%** from Monday's close of 75,692.78. The Nifty 50 was up 1.06% at 23,689.85, and soon afterwards the gains widened: the Sensex pushed about 1,000 points higher and the **Nifty climbed back above 23,700**, with FMCG and realty stocks joining the move.

## Who led the gains

The exchange operator **BSE was the top performer, up 4.87%**. Bharti Airtel gained 3.61% and HDFC Bank 3.12%, giving the index heavyweights in banking and telecom the biggest say in the day's rise. State-run explorer ONGC slipped slightly, one of the few large stocks in the red.

The rally came despite a weak start across Asia, where South Korea's KOSPI fell about 2.4% in the morning as investors sold technology shares over worries about heavy spending on artificial intelligence. Brent crude, which fell 7% on Monday, was **steady at about $104 a barrel**, a level that eases pressure on India's import bill.

## What to watch

Cheaper oil and renewed buying by foreign investors have powered the four-day run. Traders will watch whether the Nifty can hold 23,700 into the close, and **State Bank of India's September-quarter results**, due on Tuesday, for the next lead on bank stocks.
