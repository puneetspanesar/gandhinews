Union road transport minister **Nitin Gadkari announced highway projects worth about ₹75,000 crore** for West Bengal on Thursday, at an event in Kolaghat in Purba Medinipur district with Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari on the stage. He also opened or laid foundation stones for 22 national highway projects worth more than ₹8,700 crore.

The list includes a 250 km ring road around Kolkata, an eight-lane road between Dankuni and Kolaghat, and a four-lane highway to the Petrapole border crossing with Bangladesh. Most of it is announced, not sanctioned work with dates, and the minister made clear where he thinks delays come from. "Acquire the land. Get the forest clearances done. We will do the rest," he told the state government.

## What the ministry's release lists

The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways says **national highway works worth about ₹40,000 crore are complete** in the state and about ₹35,000 crore are under way. The new announcements are on top of that. The biggest items, with the ministry's estimated costs:

- **Dankuni to Kolaghat**, 58 km, eight lanes, about ₹20,000 crore. The ministry expects it to cut about 1.5 hours off the drive from Kolkata towards Kharagpur, Balasore and Bhubaneswar, and to improve the link to Haldia port.
- **Kolkata ring road**, 250 km, about ₹14,000 crore, connecting Kolkata with Howrah and North and South 24 Parganas and joining the Kona, Belgharia and Kalyani expressways.
- **Moregram to Kishanganj**, a 245 km four-lane high-speed corridor of about ₹12,000 crore, of which 91 km and ₹4,500 crore fall in Bengal.
- **Barasat to Petrapole**, 75 km, four lanes, about ₹8,000 crore, linking Barasat, Basirhat and Bangaon and the border crossing.
- **Belgharia expressway**, a 7 km six-lane elevated road of about ₹1,000 crore towards the Kolkata airport.

Smaller works include a ₹1,000 crore four-lane bridge over the Mayurakshi in Birbhum, the widening of the NH-116B between Nandakumar and Digha for ₹2,700 crore, and **repairs at 59 accident black spots** estimated at ₹5,100 crore: 25 in Malda, 20 in Nadia, nine in Jalpaiguri and five in Paschim Bardhaman.

The named projects in the release add up to less than ₹75,000 crore, and the release does not itemise the rest of the total. It gives **no start or completion dates** for any of the new corridors.

## What opened on Thursday

Two finished projects were dedicated: the **63.83 km Palsit to Dankuni stretch of NH-19**, now six lanes, which links Howrah, Hooghly and Bardhaman along the Kolkata to Durgapur industrial belt, and a four-lane bridge over the Ganga at Farakka, built for ₹521.20 crore, which shortens the crossing between Murshidabad and Malda.

Foundation stones were laid for 20 more works. They include 57 vehicle and light vehicle underpasses on NH-12 and NH-19, a bypass at Ramnagar on NH-116B, and two bridges on the Norghat and Dwarkeshwar rivers under the Setubandhan scheme, meant to ease the road to Digha, Mandarmani, Tajpur and Bakkhali.

## The bigger corridors

Gadkari also pointed to longer routes that pass through or end in Bengal. The ministry says a **570 km six-lane greenfield highway from Gorakhpur to Siliguri** has been approved at an estimated ₹38,000 crore, and that the project report for a Siliguri to Guwahati high-speed corridor is complete. He mentioned the Kharagpur to Bardhaman to Moregram greenfield expressway, the Varanasi to Kolkata greenfield highway and a proposed Raxaul to Haldia corridor.

## What was said on the stage

Gadkari put the state's share of the work plainly. "I have no shortage of money. You will get tired of asking me, and I will not get tired of giving," he said, as his speech was reported. He praised the state government in the release for land acquisition, removal of encroachments and clearances over the last six months.

Adhikari recalled Gadkari telling him, "Arrange the land, Suvendu babu. I will arrange a ₹1 lakh crore project." He answered on land: "We will give the land." He asked for a wider Kolkata to Bangaon road, a bridge over the Hooghly between Falta and Bagnan, and an expanded road from Kakdwip to Gangasagar.

The two leaders used different totals. The ministry's own release gives **about ₹75,000 crore**, and that is the figure this report uses; the ₹1 lakh crore figure was spoken at the event and is not in the release. No reaction from the Trinamool Congress was found by the time of writing.

## Why it matters

For people in and around Kolkata, the ring road and the Belgharia link are the projects meant to take long-distance trucks off city roads. The Petrapole highway serves a border crossing with Bangladesh, and the black-spot works are aimed at stretches the ministry has marked as prone to accidents.

## What happens next

Each corridor now needs a final project report, land and tenders before construction. The ministry and the state will be judged on whether the Dankuni to Kolaghat and ring road projects reach the bidding stage, the step that turns an announced cost into a contract.
