The Indian Council of Medical Research has found that 41.8% of children aged 12 to 59 months and **55.9% of women aged 15 to 49 are anaemic**, in the first national anaemia estimates it has published from venous blood tests. The findings of the SAMPADA survey, carried out in 2023-25 by the ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition in Hyderabad, were released by the Department of Health Research on November 23, 2026.

The figures are lower than the last national round, the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019-21), which found 67.1% of children aged 6 to 59 months and 57% of women anaemic. The gap is large for children and small for women. The two surveys also did not measure the same way, which matters for how the fall is read.

## What the survey recorded

SAMPADA, short for Survey for Assessment of Markers of Population Health, Activity, Diet and Anthropometry, covered **more than 2 lakh people in over 81,000 households** across 183 districts in 35 states and Union Territories. More than 1.7 lakh blood samples were tested for haemoglobin.

Among other groups, the report puts anaemia at **46.3% for adolescent girls** aged 10 to 19, 22.8% for adolescent boys, 22% for men aged 20 to 59, 58.1% for lactating women and 43.6% for pregnant women. For women aged 60 and above it is 55.7%, and for men of that age 44.7%.

Most cases are mild. About half of anaemic children, adolescent girls and women have mild anaemia, and about three-quarters of anaemic men and adolescent boys do. **Severe anaemia is below 2%** in most age groups.

## Iron is only part of the cause

The survey tested a sub-sample for iron, folate, vitamin B12 and markers of inflammation. Iron deficiency accounts for **about half of anaemia** in women, adolescent girls and children, and is concentrated in those three groups. Iron deficiency was found in 23% of children aged 12 to 59 months, 36.3% of adolescent girls and 34.1% of women aged 15 to 49.

Folate and vitamin B12 deficiency were similar in anaemic and non-anaemic people, so the release concludes they contribute little to anaemia in India. The report points instead to infections, inherited blood disorders and inflammation from chronic disease.

The state figures vary widely. Anaemia among children aged 12 to 59 months is highest in **Andhra Pradesh at 57.5%** and lowest in Kerala at 14.5% and Mizoram at 14.8%. Among adolescent girls, Ladakh (70.6%) and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands (70.3%) are highest. In rural areas, 60.5% of lactating women are anaemic against 50.6% in cities.

## Why the comparison is not like for like

NFHS-5 measured haemoglobin from a **finger-prick blood drop on a portable device** in the field. SAMPADA drew blood from a vein and ran it on automated analysers in field laboratories within four to six hours, which ICMR calls the gold standard. Age bands also differ: 6 to 59 months in NFHS-5 against 12 to 59 months now, and 15 to 19 years for adolescent girls against 10 to 19 years.

The release itself says only that prevalence is lower than in previous national surveys and does not set out a comparison. ICMR Director General Dr Rajiv Bahl said: "It is encouraging that prevalence of anaemia is lower in SAMPADA compared to previous national surveys." The lower numbers may therefore **reflect the new method as well as any real change**, and the published data do not separate the two.

## How the data came to be separate

The health ministry told Parliament on July 28 and July 31, 2026 that it had dropped haemoglobin testing from NFHS-6 because of doubts over the reliability of finger-prick estimates, and that it would adopt ICMR's venous-blood figures instead. NFHS-6, released on May 29, 2026, left out all anaemia indicators. In an RTI reply dated September 1, ICMR-NIN gave no date for releasing the results.

Anaemia Mukt Bharat, launched in 2018, supplies iron and folic acid, deworming and testing to six groups of beneficiaries. It aims to cut anaemia in mothers and children by 3 percentage points a year. NFHS-5 had shown the opposite: children's anaemia rose from 58.6% in NFHS-4 (2015-16) to 67.1%, and women's from 53% to 57%.

## What it means and what comes next

For families and health workers, the survey points to where effort should go. ICMR-NIN director Dr Bharati Kulkarni called for targeted, population-specific approaches across a person's life, and for more research and surveillance. The report says focused iron interventions are needed for children under five, adolescent girls and women, and that "treatment and tracking until resolution is essential". Because half of cases are not iron deficiency, **iron tablets alone will not close the gap**.

SAMPADA is a new baseline, since NFHS-6 has no anaemia numbers to set beside it. The release does not say when further SAMPADA findings will follow, and the health ministry has not announced any change to Anaemia Mukt Bharat's targets.
