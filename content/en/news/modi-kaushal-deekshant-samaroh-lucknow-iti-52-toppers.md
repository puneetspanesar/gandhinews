Prime Minister Narendra Modi told a gathering of skill trainees in Lucknow on Saturday that **ITI students are "no less than" IIT students**, and asked parents to stop hiding the fact that their children are training at an Industrial Training Institute. He was speaking at the fifth Kaushal Deekshant Samaroh, the national skill convocation, at Indira Gandhi Pratishthan in Gomti Nagar, at about 5:30 pm.

The ceremony honoured **52 national-level toppers** from Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs), National Skill Training Institutes (NSTIs) and Institutes for Training of Trainers (IToTs), according to the Prime Minister's Office. It was the first time the annual event was held outside Delhi, according to reports.

## What the Prime Minister said

Modi's central message was about status. "Be it IIT or ITI, for me, students are the treasure, strength and future of the country," he said. He asked families who feel embarrassed about an ITI certificate to change their attitude, and said that mindset "must change".

He argued that skilled workers will be in demand in the years ahead and that the ITIs are preparing people for those openings. Large companies, he said, have two or three degree holders at the top but need **hundreds of ITI-certified workers** to keep operating. He said the future would favour skilled ITI students over "suited-booted" degree holders.

Modi tied the point to trade. The free trade agreements India has been signing, he said, create demand for skilled people, and he named ITI graduates specifically. He also claimed that the major Gulf countries have recognised the resilience of Indian skilled workers. He said he had flown from West Bengal to Lucknow to hand certificates to what he called his "ITI children".

## A history of skills, and the gold appeal

The Prime Minister also gave the issue a long historical frame. He said foreign invaders had weakened India's traditional skill networks and targeted its artisan traditions, alleging that craftsmen's thumbs were cut to destroy their skills, and that the British later brought in an education system meant to produce clerks. Indian society, he said, had once been organised around skills, but their standing declined over the years. These are the Prime Minister's own statements; he offered no figures for them in the address as reported.

Near the end he turned to his earlier appeal to citizens to avoid buying gold. He had first asked people in May 2026 not to buy gold for a year unless strictly necessary, and repeated the request in September as part of his swadeshi push. In Lucknow he credited people for **cutting non-essential gold purchases**, linked that to cooling bullion rates, and compared the response with Lal Bahadur Shastri's 1960s call to skip one meal a day. He said citizens had told him they saved money by following his advice, and he expects a similar response to his call for respect for skills.

## The ceremony and the scheme behind it

The Directorate General of Training under the Union Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship instituted the Kaushal Deekshant Samaroh in the 2021-22 academic session. It recognises skill-development excellence and the move of trained youth into work. The toppers come from the Craftsmen Training Scheme and the Crafts Instructor Training Scheme. Uttar Pradesh Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath felicitated Modi at the event, and senior Union ministers attended, according to reports.

This year's edition highlighted PM-SETU, the scheme to upgrade ITIs and link them with industry. In October the steering committee [cleared ₹1,194.92 crore for five ITI clusters](/en/news/pm-setu-1195-crore-five-iti-clusters) covering 25 institutes, taking total approvals under the scheme to ₹3,687 crore.

## How the day went

Lucknow was the last of three stops in the Prime Minister's programme on November 21. At about 9:45 am he attended the platinum jubilee celebrations of IIT Kharagpur in West Bengal and interacted with 75 students of the institute, which was founded in 1951 as the first IIT. He then went to Bihar, where he [dedicated the Nitish Setu and projects worth ₹34,470 crore in Vaishali](/en/news/nitish-setu-modi-dedicates-vaishali-34470-crore-tejashwi-credit-row).

The Lucknow remarks also carry on a theme from earlier in the week. At the FICCI annual meeting on November 19, Modi [asked industry whether tax cuts had raised jobs and pay](/en/news/modi-ficci-agm-india-first-quality-gandhi-swadeshi) and urged it to put India's trade agreements to use. The skills speech addresses the other side of that question: who will do the work if the agreements bring orders.

## Why it matters and what comes next

For the young people who train at ITIs, the Prime Minister's words put official weight behind a long-standing complaint that vocational training is treated as a second choice to a degree. Whether that changes depends on placements and pay, and the government has not yet released a detailed assessment of how this year's awardees will be placed in jobs, according to reports.

No response from opposition parties had been published when this report was written. The next step to watch is the rollout of the PM-SETU clusters already cleared, for which the Centre has approved **₹3,687 crore** so far.
