Florida has charged Raúl Castro, the 95-year-old former leader of Cuba, with **conspiracy to commit first-degree murder** and secured a state warrant for his arrest over the shooting down of two civilian planes three decades ago.

Attorney General James Uthmeier announced the charge at Florida International University in Miami on Monday. He said the evidence shows Castro ordered the Cuban military in 1996 to shoot down two planes that had taken off from South Florida, killing four men. They belonged to Brothers to the Rescue, a Miami-based group that flew over the sea searching for Cuban migrants.

"The people of Miami have waited too long," Uthmeier said. "Well, today the wait is over."

He acknowledged the warrant is unlikely to be served soon, though Castro could be extradited to Florida if he travels to certain countries. "I'm not ignorant of the fact that Raúl Castro is an older man who's probably not looking to march himself into Miami," he said. "We'll be ready to bring the case however needed."

Castro was indicted by federal prosecutors in May over the same attack. Jason Reding Quiñones, the US Attorney appointed by President Donald Trump, said that under the principle of dual sovereignty the state and federal governments can each prosecute breaches of their own laws. "That means two separate prosecutions, each standing on their own," he said.

The announcement came on the first day of early voting in Miami-Dade County. José Javier Rodríguez, the Democrat challenging Uthmeier for attorney general, called it a "political distraction" and a "smokescreen". "If we can arrest the murderous Raúl Castro from Tallahassee, I will be the first to do it as Attorney General," he wrote.

Cuba's government has not commented.
