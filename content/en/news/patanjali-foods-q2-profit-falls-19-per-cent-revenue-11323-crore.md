Patanjali Foods said on Saturday that its **profit after tax fell about 19%** to ₹419.8 crore in the quarter to September 30, against ₹516.7 crore a year earlier, even as quarterly sales rose 15.55%.

Revenue from operations rose **15.55% to ₹11,322.58 crore**, from ₹9,798.84 crore in the same quarter of 2025. Profit before tax came in at ₹409.01 crore, down from ₹505.10 crore. The quarter's revenue was almost flat on the April to June quarter's ₹11,337.45 crore.

## Thin margins on edible oil

The squeeze is visible in the operating numbers. **Operating EBITDA was ₹425.63 crore**, a margin of 3.76%, and gross profit was ₹1,439.14 crore, or 12.62% of revenue. Net profit margin narrowed to about 3.7% from 5.3%.

Most of the sales come from edible oils, where margins are thinnest. The segment posted **record revenue of ₹8,532.25 crore, up 22.39%**, but its EBITDA was ₹256.22 crore, a margin of 3%.

## Food and home care carry the profit

The FMCG business earned far more per rupee of sales. On revenue of ₹2,923.48 crore it made EBITDA of ₹221.33 crore, a **margin of 7.57%**, and contributed 45.04% of the company's segment EBITDA, excluding unallocable income.

Biscuits had a record quarter, with revenue of ₹638.31 crore, up 27.69%, and the EBITDA margin rose to 11.91% from 9.79%. The Doodh brand accounted for about ₹475 crore of that. Ghee brought in ₹306.34 crore, consumer staples ₹838.84 crore and textured soya under the Nutrela brand ₹185.56 crore, up 16.40%.

Home and personal care revenue grew **11.06% to ₹713.88 crore**. Skin care rose 25.58%, home care 17.53%, hair care 8.13% and dental care 4.56%. The upstream oil palm business reported revenue of ₹742.29 crore, with 1,27,265 hectares under cultivation as of September 30 out of 6.63 lakh hectares allocated to the company.

## Half-year picture

For April to September, revenue from operations was a record **₹22,660.03 crore, up 22.06%**. Operating EBITDA was ₹969.03 crore, a margin of 4.28%. Profit after tax for the half-year rose to ₹755.65 crore from ₹697.10 crore, which means the first quarter earned about ₹336 crore, against about ₹180 crore a year earlier, and the second quarter gave back part of that gain.

## What the company says

Chief executive Sanjeev Asthana said the quarter "reflected the resilience of our integrated business model". He pointed to festival timing: Navratri began in October this year rather than September, and Pitru Paksha pushed some festive purchases later. The company said it expects growth momentum to continue into the wedding season, and listed premiumisation, scaling both edible oils and FMCG, expanding oil palm and improving efficiency as its priorities.

Its commentary also pointed to cost pressure. Raw material prices for wheat, milk and sugar have been trending upward, and retail inflation has risen through the year. The government cut the import duty on crude palm and soybean oil to 11% from 16.5%, and on crude sunflower oil to 5.5%, from September 24, 2026, which should lower the landed cost of the oils that make up most of the company's sales.

## Why it matters

Patanjali Foods sells cooking oil, biscuits, ghee, soya chunks, skin care and home care products to households across the country, so its margins track the food prices families pay. Retail inflation hit [6.09% in October](/en/news/retail-inflation-october-2026-6-09-two-year-high/), with food prices up 8.94% from a year ago. A company that moves a lot of volume on a 3% edible oil margin has little room to absorb costs without passing them on or taking the hit.

Investors have not been kind. The stock closed more than 2% lower at ₹421 on the BSE on Friday, the last session before the results, and is down about 30% over a year, behind both the Sensex and the BSE FMCG index.

Another consumer goods maker reported very different numbers this month: [Marico's profit rose 31%](/en/news/marico-q2-profit-31-per-cent-revenue-3897-crore/) on India gross margins that widened by about 600 basis points.

## A recall in the background

In October, the Madhya Pradesh Food and Drug Administration ordered a recall of one batch of cow ghee after a sample was found unsafe under food safety rules. The company's response was not available at the time of writing.

## What to watch

The company has pointed to the wedding season to lift demand in the October to December quarter. The test will be whether the duty cut on imported oils shows up in edible oil margins, and whether festival sales that slipped from September into October lift FMCG volumes enough to offset higher wheat, milk and sugar costs.
