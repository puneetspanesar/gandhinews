The Union home ministry's cybercrime unit has advised WhatsApp users to **log out of WhatsApp Web after every session**, warning that a logged-in session on a computer can be misused to take over a person's account. The advisory, carried in an official statement on the evening of Monday, November 23, 2026, comes from the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), through its National Cybercrime Threat Analytics Unit.

It is aimed at ordinary users rather than companies. An earlier warning in August was addressed to finance teams. This one covers anyone who links a phone to a desktop browser.

## What the advisory says

The unit says an "authenticated WhatsApp web session can be misused to gain unauthorised access" to a user's account. Once in, an attacker can **read and send messages while posing as the user**, and can see contacts and group chats. That access can be used for fraud, phishing and social engineering that trades on the account holder's trusted name.

The advice is practical:

- Do not handle official, financial or sensitive information through WhatsApp Web on a computer.
- Do not stay logged in permanently; log out at the end of each session.
- Do not log in on cyber cafes, public kiosks, hotel business centres, borrowed computers or other untrusted systems.
- Watch for **messages sent from your account that you did not write**, odd replies from contacts and other unexplained activity.
- Keep the operating system, browser and security software updated, and avoid suspicious downloads, unknown files, cracked software and untrusted attachments.

The statement carries no figures on how many accounts have been affected.

## How the warnings have built up

I4C has now put out repeated notices on the same weakness. The Press Information Bureau release of August 7, 2026, described a "boss scam" in which fraudsters send malicious .zip files disguised as account statements or notices from the Reserve Bank of India and the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Opened on a Windows computer, the file **hijacks the WhatsApp Web session**. The attackers then pass the file to the victim's contacts and, posing as senior executives, ask for funds to be moved to mule accounts.

That release said chartered accountants, company directors, chief financial officers and finance teams were the main targets, with recent incidents in Delhi, Gujarat, Maharashtra and Rajasthan. It said **more than 58,000 potential victims had been alerted by SMS** under the header "I4CMHA-G" in the previous 30 days, and that more than 10,000 Indians had been protected by geo-blocking the attackers' command servers through the Sahyog portal. It followed an I4C advisory of June 22, 2026, on account takeover through malicious Windows programs.

## The court case in the background

The advisory arrives while the Supreme Court is examining how platforms and banks respond to online fraud. In its order made public on November 10, a bench led by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant asked WhatsApp and other intermediaries to **tell the court what measures they propose** and to send the same plan to I4C. WhatsApp's counsel told the court the company had acted against 9,400 accounts. Our report of that order is [here](/en/news/sc-digital-arrest-whatsapp-intermediaries-measures-november-23), and our explainer on the scam is [here](/en/explainer/digital-arrest-scam-explained-supreme-court-banks-whatsapp).

## What it means for users

A WhatsApp Web login is a standing key to the account. Anyone who opens it on a shared computer and forgets to sign out leaves that key where the next user, or any malicious program on the machine, can reach it. Messages sent from a hijacked account reach people who trust the sender, which is why the advisory stresses contacts and groups.

For a worker who uses a shared office or home computer, the steps are simple: sign out when finished and watch for anything sent from the account that the owner did not write. A sudden request for money or a file from a known contact is worth confirming by a phone call before acting.

## What happens next

The advisory sets no deadline and imposes no rule on WhatsApp or on users; it is guidance. I4C has said in the past that it blocks attacker servers through the Sahyog portal and alerts potential victims by SMS. The Supreme Court case on platform and bank responsibilities continues, and any directions to WhatsApp would come from that bench.
