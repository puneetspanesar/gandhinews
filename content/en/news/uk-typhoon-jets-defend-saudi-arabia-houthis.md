Britain is sending Royal Air Force Typhoon fighter jets to help defend Saudi Arabia against missile and drone attacks by Yemen's Houthis, the Ministry of Defence said on Tuesday.

The jets will fly "defensive counter air missions" over the kingdom, the ministry said, stressing that they would operate in a defensive role and would not attack the Houthis. Britain is also extending its air-to-air refuelling support for Saudi air operations. It did not say how many aircraft were going or where they would be based.

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting called the Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia "absolutely despicable" and said the Typhoons "will fly defensive air missions" to help protect Saudi airspace from drones and missiles.

The Houthis, also known as Ansar Allah, have fired missiles and drones at Saudi oil facilities and Riyadh's main airport in recent weeks. A Saudi-led coalition has hit back with air strikes on Houthi positions, as fighting between the group and Yemen's internationally recognised government, which Riyadh backs, has intensified.

Rising energy prices are partly behind London's decision, since events in the Gulf feed directly into household bills in Britain.

Saudi Arabia's other partners are moving more slowly. Egypt's parliament delayed for a second day a vote on President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi's request to authorise military support for the kingdom if it asks. The next session is set for 26 October.
