Neha Bora, president of the All India Students' Association, takes her fight against a police "peace" bond to the Delhi High Court on Thursday, a day after two jailed Cockroach Janta Party leaders were freed on similar bonds.

Justice Subramonium Prasad is due to hear her petition. He took it up during the Dussehra break after she sought an urgent hearing, and then put it off to Thursday at her counsel's request.

## The notice

Delhi Police issued the notice on 17 October under Section 126 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita. It asked her to appear before a special executive magistrate on 19 October and show cause why she should not sign a personal bond of ₹10,000, with one surety, "for keeping peace for a period of enquiry".

Bora was taken into preventive detention on 16 October on her way to the CJP's "Lehra Do Tiranga" march and was released the next day. She has been a prominent face of the protests over alleged irregularities in the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.

Her petition, prepared by advocate Abhik Chimni, wants the notice quashed and the police barred from coercive action. It calls the notice vague and factually wrong, says it names no period, occasion or event, and says there is no material to suggest she was likely to breach the peace. The proceedings are, it argues, a "colourable exercise of preventive powers to suppress peaceful dissent" that violates her rights to free speech, peaceful assembly and personal liberty.

## Elsewhere

At the Bombay High Court, the Kolhapur circuit bench had listed a petition by Prathamesh Govankar and others for 22 October when it took the case up in late September. The petitioners want every voter deleted from Maharashtra's rolls during the SIR restored. The bench had said then that it saw no urgency.

Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka, released from Tihar on Wednesday, must appear before Special Executive Magistrate Ritu Raj with their sureties on Friday at 3 pm. The Supreme Court's regular benches return from the Dussehra break on 26 October.
