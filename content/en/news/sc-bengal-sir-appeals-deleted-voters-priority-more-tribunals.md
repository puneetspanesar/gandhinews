The Supreme Court on Monday, November 23, told the Election Commission of India to **increase the number of appellate tribunals** hearing West Bengal's Special Intensive Revision (SIR) appeals, in consultation with the Chief Justice of the Calcutta High Court. A bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana also ruled that appeals by voters deleted from the rolls be heard first.

The Commission told the court that **38.31 lakh appeals** are before the tribunals: 22.21 lakh from people seeking restoration of deleted names and 16.10 lakh challenging names that were added.

## What the court ordered

The tribunals must now sort their pending files into two sets: appeals by excluded voters and appeals against inclusions. The order says the first set "shall be given preference in the matter of final disposal". Of the second set it says: "The second set of appeals does not involve any urgency, and those appeals may, for the time being, be deferred."

Within the first set, **Kolkata Municipal Corporation voters come first**, because civic elections are due. The bench also asked the Commission to find suitable premises in different parts of Kolkata, central or spread across the city, so that appellants can reach a tribunal more easily and files move faster.

The court expressed no final view on the SIR exercise itself or on appeals against inclusions, and those questions stay open.

## Who is filing against whom

The sharpest argument was over the inclusion appeals. Senior Advocate Gopal Sankaranarayanan, for the petitioners, said the Commission's own affidavit showed that the state's Chief Electoral Officer had filed appeals against inclusions. Under Rule 23 of the electoral rules, he argued, an appeal from an Electoral Registration Officer goes to an officer designated by the Commission, so the Commission would in effect be appealing to its own authority. Counsel for the petitioners put it as "effectively an appeal from the Election Commission to itself".

The bench did not decide the point and said it would need a final order. Deferring the inclusion appeals, for now, means no tribunal decides them while the question is open.

Advocate Prashant Bhushan told the court that just over one lakh of the appeals had been decided and that **93% of those went in favour** of the deleted voter. He said more than two lakh voters in the Kolkata municipal area were excluded and their appeals are pending. These are counsel's figures, not findings of the court.

Voters who lost welfare benefits such as ration entitlements after being left off the rolls were told they may approach a competent court, including the High Court. The state was asked to reply to a plea against a food department order on deleting beneficiaries from the public distribution system. Justice Bagchi said the proceedings before the bench are confined to electoral rolls. Solicitor General Tushar Mehta asked why affected people were not seeking relief in the High Courts.

## How we got here

The tribunals exist because of the court itself. After its order of March 10, 2026, the Commission notified **19 appellate tribunals** on March 21, led by former High Court judges, with former Chief Justice T. S. Sivagnanam heading one. Appeals could be filed online or at District Magistrate and sub-divisional offices.

On April 13 the court noted that the tribunals were fully functional, that **more than 34 lakh appeals** had been filed and that more than 60 lakh objections had been decided by judicial officers. It made clear that "the mere pendency of appeals" did not entitle anyone to vote, and ordered supplementary rolls for appeals decided before the April 23 and April 29 polling dates.

On October 9 the same bench heard a plea to move deleted voters' appeals up the queue before the Kolkata and Howrah civic polls, and said it was alive to the problem; our [report on that hearing](/en/news/supreme-court-sir-cannot-delay-local-body-polls/) has the detail. The count of pending appeals has since grown from about 1.70 lakh in the two civic areas to a state-wide 38.31 lakh on the Commission's tally.

## What it means for voters and the civic polls

For a deleted voter, the order decides the queue. A person from the Kolkata area who has appealed should now be heard before an appellant elsewhere, and well before a challenge to another voter's name. More tribunals and new premises are meant to cut the wait.

No Kolkata civic election has been notified. On November 6 the Calcutta High Court barred the State Election Commission from notifying Kolkata Municipal Corporation polls until a challenge to the redrawing of wards from 144 to 209 is decided, as we [reported](/en/news/calcutta-hc-defers-kmc-elections-ward-delimitation-144-to-209/). Whether an appeal is decided before a poll is notified will matter, because, as the April order stressed, only a tribunal decision, and not a pending appeal, puts a name back on the roll.

## What happens next

The Election Commission now has to decide how many tribunals to add, and where they sit, after talking to the Calcutta High Court Chief Justice. The reports of the hearing give no next date and record no response from the Commission. The unresolved question of the Chief Electoral Officer's appeals against inclusions will come back for a final order.
