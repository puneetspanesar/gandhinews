The Saudi-led coalition said on Sunday it had **intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles** that the Iran-aligned Houthi movement fired towards Riyadh. Loud booms were heard in the Saudi capital, and no casualties or damage have been reported.

The Saudi Civil Defence had issued an **early-warning alert for the Riyadh region** shortly before the booms, an eyewitness said. It later announced that the danger had passed. The coalition later added that it had destroyed two cruise missiles over Riyadh and the southern region, without giving a time.

Earlier in the morning the coalition said it had stopped a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at the Eastern Region, where Civil Defence had sounded alerts in Dhahran, Dammam, Abqaiq and Al-Ahsa before giving an all-clear. The Saudi and Qatari foreign ministers also spoke by phone about **de-escalation in the region**, according to the live reports.

## A truce that has collapsed

The fighting has broken the truce that the United Nations brokered in 2022. Saudi Arabia led an Arab coalition into Yemen in 2015 to restore the internationally recognised government after the Houthis seized Sanaa. Since hostilities resumed in July, the Houthis have **fired missiles and drones at Saudi airports, ports and oil facilities**, and Saudi Arabia has carried out air strikes on Houthi targets in Yemen.


The Houthi-run health ministry said on Saturday that **Saudi air strikes near Sanaa killed at least 33 people**, a higher toll than the 25 first reported. The Saudi authorities said they were investigating. The casualty figure has not been independently confirmed.

The Houthis are part of a wider Middle East war that began after US and Israeli attacks on Iran in February. The Strait of Hormuz remains at the centre of the stand-off between Washington and Tehran.
