A team of senior Election Commission officials, led by a **senior deputy election commissioner**, begins a review of Uttar Pradesh's election preparations in Meerut on Monday, the first stop of a phased tour of the state ahead of Assembly polls due early next year.

In Meerut, the officials will look at the **readiness of the grassroots machinery** in some of the divisions of western Uttar Pradesh and meet police and administrative officers. Further phases will follow, though the Commission has not announced dates for them.

Uttar Pradesh is the biggest of the five states that go to the polls next year. Its 403-member Assembly's **term ends on 22 May 2027**, while the terms in Manipur, Goa, Punjab and Uttarakhand run out on different dates in March 2027. The Representation of the People Act, 1950 allows the Commission to hold elections up to six months before a House's term expires.

Similar review visits to Uttarakhand, Manipur and Punjab are already over, and a tour of Goa is being arranged. The full Commission usually visits poll-bound states shortly before it announces the election schedule, so the officials' rounds are an early sign of how close that moment is.

Work on the voting machines has also begun. Uttar Pradesh and Goa have started **first-level checks of EVMs and paper trail units**, in which engineers from Bharat Electronics and Electronics Corporation of India examine each machine for mechanical faults. Faulty units go back to the makers. A mock poll is held in front of party representatives, and the machines that pass are marked "FLC OK" and sealed with a pink paper seal.

The review comes as the Commission faces sharp political attacks over its revision of electoral rolls, with opposition parties demanding the removal of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.
