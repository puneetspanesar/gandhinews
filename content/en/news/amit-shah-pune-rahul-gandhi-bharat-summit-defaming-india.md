**Union Home Minister Amit Shah** said on Sunday that Rahul Gandhi was "defaming India at international platforms" and that "if he aspires to return to power, he should stop travelling abroad and start visiting villages across India". Speaking at a function near Pune, Shah was answering the Leader of the Opposition's closing speech at the Bharat Summit in Hyderabad a day earlier. No response from Congress or Gandhi to the Pune remarks was available when this report was written.

## What Shah said

Shah spoke after the ground-breaking ceremony for a cooperative engineering venture and the launch of a Bharat Taxi service, an event also attended by Maharashtra Deputy Chief Minister Eknath Shinde. The political part of his speech was aimed at the Congress leader. He said Gandhi had used the summit to speak against India's democratic institutions and Prime Minister Narendra Modi's record, and that such remarks abroad would **not help him come to power**.

He described the summit, held on November 13 and 14, as a gathering to which the Congress had invited "Left-wing" promoters from India and abroad, and said Gandhi was "venting his frustration over continuous electoral defeats". He urged Congress workers to question their own leadership. If the party followed Gandhi's approach, he said, it could **stay out of power for 30 more years**. He also asked voters to give Congress a "fitting reply" in the coming elections.

Shah then moved to a comparison of governments. He alleged that scams worth **₹12 lakh crore** took place during Congress rule at the Centre from 2004 to 2014, and said no corruption allegation had been made against the Prime Minister in 12 years. These are his claims and the reports did not test them. He listed the lunar south pole landing, the growth of airports from 76 to about 150, the new education policy, startups and railways as the Modi government's record, and said the Centre had created a separate Ministry of Cooperation after Congress had long demanded one.

## What Gandhi said in Hyderabad

The speech Shah was answering came on **November 14** at the Hyderabad International Convention Centre. Gandhi told the closing session that Congress and its allies "are going to defeat PM Modi and we are going to get rid of PM Modi". He said the line between business and politics, "between Adani and PM Modi, has evaporated", and claimed that 70 lakh voters were added in Maharashtra in three months before the 2024 Assembly election. He offered no documents for these claims, which are his own.

Reports of the speech also describe Gandhi criticising the Centre over foreign delegates who were reportedly refused entry or had visas cancelled. That row had begun before the summit opened.

## How the dispute built up

The Bharat Summit was organised by the Congress-ruled Telangana government. On the day it opened, Chief Minister A Revanth Reddy said the Centre had **cancelled visas at the last minute** for guests and journalists, and Lok Sabha member Shashi Tharoor alleged that 115 of 315 cleared speakers were affected. That count has not been independently confirmed. The Ministry of External Affairs said visa matters are "sovereign functions of any country or any government", and BJP leaders called the event political.

## Why it matters and what next

The exchange sets out the line each side is likely to take before the elections Shah referred to: Gandhi arguing that democracy has been damaged since 2014 and that business and politics have merged, and the BJP presenting criticism made abroad as harm to India's name. Shah's speech also adds a new charge, that the summit invited "Left-wing" promoters, which is his description and was not tested in the reports.

Congress has not yet answered Shah. Its reply, and any BJP response to Gandhi's voter-roll claims, will shape the argument as Parliament prepares for a Winter Session that is likely to begin in the [first week of December](/news/winter-session-first-week-december-delimitation-womens-quota-bill), according to government sources.
