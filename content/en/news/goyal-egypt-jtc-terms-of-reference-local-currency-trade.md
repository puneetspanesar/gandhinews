India and Egypt agreed on Sunday to write the **terms of reference for a bilateral trade agreement**, the first formal step towards a preferential or free trade pact between the two countries. The decision came at the 7th India-Egypt Joint Trade Committee in Cairo, the first meeting of the committee held at ministerial level, which Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal co-chaired with Egypt's Minister of Investment and Foreign Trade, Mohamed Farid Saleh.

Goyal said a **joint team will work on a time-bound basis** to finalise the terms. Officials have not said what a deal would cover or when talks on tariffs would begin. Terms of reference only set the scope of what the two sides will study, and neither country has committed to a pact. Goyal said an agreement could improve market access and make trade between the two countries more competitive, and that the sides would also look at customs cooperation and mutual recognition of testing agencies.

## Trade in rupees and Egyptian pounds

The announcement that may matter most to businesses was on payments. Goyal said the **central banks of the two countries** are working to promote bilateral trade in local currencies, which would let exporters and importers settle in rupees and Egyptian pounds rather than routing every deal through the dollar. He did not say when such settlement would begin, and no official notification was available when this was written.

He also proposed linking India's **Unified Payments Interface, or UPI, with Egypt's digital payment systems**, and named fintech among five areas where the two economies complement each other. The others were pharmaceuticals and healthcare, engineering and automobiles, agriculture and food processing, and culture and museums. Cooperation in education was also on the list.

## What the numbers say

The meeting followed a strong run in trade. India's merchandise trade with Egypt rose about 64% to **$5.04 billion in April to September 2026**, according to Commerce Ministry data, against about $3.08 billion a year earlier. In 2025-26 India exported $3.87 billion of goods to Egypt and imported $2.60 billion, leaving a surplus in India's favour. Machinery and petroleum products are among the main Indian exports, and crude oil and fertilisers among the main imports. One report says the two sides have set a target of $12 billion in bilateral trade, a figure we could not confirm in an official text.

Saleh, for his part, put a proposal on the table for businesses. He suggested a **dedicated zone for joint Indian-Egyptian enterprises** and offered land in the Suez Canal Economic Zone, the Golden Triangle or elsewhere in Egypt. "We had agreed on many things, but this I'm putting on the table for the businesses to decide," he said.

Goyal urged Indian companies to use Egypt as a base for serving Africa, the Middle East and Europe. "Visit the Suez Canal Economic Zone," he said. He is travelling with a delegation of more than 130 business leaders and start-ups.

## The four-point agenda

At the business forum, Goyal set out a four-point plan ahead of the 80th anniversary of diplomatic relations in 2027: resolve identified trade barriers within a set time, encourage Egyptian investment in Indian states and industrial clusters, link large companies' suppliers with smaller businesses, and hold annual reviews to track progress. "Made in Egypt, with India. Made in India, with Egypt," he said.

The talks covered non-tariff barriers, customs procedures, standards and conformity assessment, product registrations and regulatory procedures, and the sectors discussed included industrial goods, energy, fertilisers, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, engineering, textiles and agricultural products. Goyal also held bilateral talks with Saleh and with Egypt's Industry Minister, Khaled Hashem. The meeting followed a September meeting between Prime Minister Narendra Modi and President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi on the sidelines of the BRICS summit in India.

## Why it matters

For Indian exporters, Egypt is both a market and a door to others. A pact would matter most for the **non-tariff barriers** that Indian traders say slow shipments there, such as product registration and testing rules, which the committee discussed. Settlement in local currencies, if it comes, would cut the cost of converting through the dollar and reduce exposure to exchange-rate swings on both sides.

## What happens next

The two sides were due to sign a startup partnership agreement on Monday, November 16, and hold the first Egypt-India Startup Forum; the outcome was not available when this was written. The joint team now has to finalise the terms of reference, and the date for that has not been announced. Goyal's visit to Egypt ends on Monday. His other remarks in Cairo, on the US trade deal, are covered in [our earlier report](/en/news/goyal-cairo-us-preferential-access-egypt-jtc).
