The BCCI has **put on hold** the IPL stakeholders' meeting that was due to bring franchise owners, broadcasters and state associations together in Mumbai on Thursday evening, leaving decisions on the 2027 season, including the future of the Impact Player rule, for another day.

The conclave was to be held at the Taj Santacruz. In a memo, BCCI secretary Devajit Saikia gave two reasons. The first was Prime Minister Narendra Modi's felicitation of the Asian Games teams in New Delhi on the same day. "Accordingly, BCCI officials will be required to attend the felicitation ceremony," the memo said.

The second came from the invitees themselves. "Requests have been received from various stakeholders seeking a postponement of the meeting," it said, adding that the board wanted "maximum participation in person". Some franchise owners and broadcast executives were travelling abroad.

No new date has been fixed. Late October or early November is being talked about as a likely window.

## What is on the table

IPL 2027 will be the league's 20th season, and the board wanted a wider meeting than usual. When he first called it, Saikia wrote that "the BCCI proposes to convene a joint meeting of all key stakeholders" and that "the State Associations play a central role in the successful staging of the League."

The agenda covers retention limits, Right-to-Match cards and purse caps, overseas player availability, venue and fan facilities, and broadcasters' concerns over match timings. The Impact Player rule, criticised by several Indian captains and franchise coaches, remains the most debated item.

The calendar does not wait for the meeting. Franchises have until November 15 to submit their lists of retained and released players, and the mini-auction is expected in Goa in the second week of December. It will be the last auction of the 2023 to 2027 cycle, and the first held in India in recent years after editions in Saudi Arabia, Dubai and Abu Dhabi. The trading window is already open.
