Lashkar-e-Taiba commander **Latif Bhat was killed in Kulgam district** of south Kashmir in the early hours of Wednesday, November 18, 2026, after a stand-off in which he took shelter inside a mosque and refused repeated calls to surrender, according to the Jammu and Kashmir Police and the Army's Chinar Corps. Union Home Minister Amit Shah, congratulating the forces, described Bhat as the **last known active local Lashkar terrorist in the Valley**.

Mr Shah said on Wednesday: "The nation salutes the brave Jawans of JKP, Army and CRPF and intelligence agencies." He praised the coordination between the agencies, and the Home Ministry carried the message in a release. On Thursday, November 19, he opens a two-day Anti-Terrorism Conference in New Delhi.

## What happened at Zangalpora

Acting on specific intelligence, joint teams of the **J&K Police, the Army and the CRPF** cordoned off Zangalpora in the Devsar area of Kulgam. Bhat, a resident of Khrewan in the district, took cover in a mosque.

Senior Superintendent of Police Kulgam Anayat Ali Chowdhary said the forces tried repeatedly to persuade him to give himself up, including through **a video call with the Commanding Officer of 9 Rashtriya Rifles**. "Security forces have maintained maximum restraint because of the mosque," he said.

Officials said Bhat refused and opened fire, and was killed. The Chinar Corps named the operation Op Zangalpura and said he was forced out of the mosque before he was shot and that the building was not damaged. Police said his body and a weapon were recovered.

## The hunt of the past eight days

Bhat had been wanted since the middle of the month. CCTV footage released by the Jammu and Kashmir Police shows him and **Umar Nisar Dar visiting Mir Bazar, Akhran, Hablish and Kilam on November 10**, which police say was reconnaissance. On November 11, gunmen on a motorcycle fired at a CRPF checkpoint at Akhran and wounded three personnel of the 46th Battalion, as [we reported](/en/news/kulgam-crpf-naka-attack-three-injured-nov-11).

On Monday, November 16, forces engaged the two at Laroo in Kulgam. **Dar was killed and Bhat escaped**. Nearby schools were evacuated before that operation.

Police also want Bhat in connection with an attack on police in Anantnag on July 22 that killed a policeman, and are investigating his suspected role in the killing of two migrant workers in Kulgam on August 1. **A reward of ₹15 lakh** had been announced for information leading to his arrest.

## What the Home Minister said

The Home Ministry release names the forces Mr Shah congratulated: the Jammu and Kashmir Police, the Army, the CRPF and the intelligence agencies. It describes Bhat as the last active Lashkar terrorist in the Valley, and Mr Shah described the operation as a major success against terrorism.

Lieutenant Governor Manoj Sinha, speaking earlier on Wednesday at a police passing-out parade in Udhampur, said only **a handful of foreign terrorists** remain in Jammu and Kashmir.

## What it means on the ground

"Last known active local" is a count of Lashkar's home-grown fighters, not of all militants. The Lieutenant Governor's remark the same day says foreign terrorists remain in the region. The killing removes the man police tied to the November 11 attack, to the July attack in Anantnag and, as a suspect, to the migrant workers' deaths in August.

For residents of Kulgam, where schools were evacuated on Monday, the immediate effect is the end of a search that ran for more than a week through villages along the Akhran and Mir Bazar stretch. No further operation in the district had been announced by Wednesday evening.

## The conference on Thursday

On Thursday, November 19, Mr Shah inaugurates a **two-day Anti-Terrorism Conference in New Delhi**. The Home Ministry release places it under the Prime Minister's zero-tolerance policy on terrorism. The theme is cross-border terrorism.

The conference is organised by the National Investigation Agency and is the sixth in the series, according to reports. It brings together senior police officers from states and Union Territories, officers of central counter-terrorism agencies, and experts in law, forensics and technology. Ministers of State for Home Nityanand Rai and Bandi Sanjay Kumar, the Home Secretary, the Secretary of R&AW and the NIA Director General are due to attend.

Discussions are to cover operational experience and best practice, the use of emerging technology against terror networks, international legal cooperation and dismantling terror ecosystems. Mr Shah is expected to review progress on the recommendations of last year's meeting.

## What happens next

Police are still investigating Bhat's suspected role in the August 1 killing of the two migrant workers in Kulgam. The Anti-Terrorism Conference opens on Thursday, November 19, and runs for two days, with Mr Shah's inaugural address on cross-border terrorism the first item.
