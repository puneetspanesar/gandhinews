Union Minister **G Kishan Reddy** asked on Thursday why the Telangana government is spending public money on what he called a Congress political programme, a day before the Bharat Summit opened in Hyderabad. The summit runs on **Friday and Saturday, November 13 and 14**, and is expected to bring about 1,000 delegates from India and roughly 70 countries to the city.

Speaking to reporters in New Delhi, the Telangana BJP leader said the state government and the Congress were jointly organising the event and wanted an explanation for the use of state funds. No figure for the cost of the summit has been published, and no reply from the Congress or the state government to his remarks had been reported by Friday morning.

## What the BJP leader said

Kishan Reddy said Chief Minister **A Revanth Reddy** and the Congress were presenting the summit as a way to showcase Rahul Gandhi's vision. "What exactly is Rahul Gandhi's vision?" he asked. He alleged that Telangana was being used as a platform for forces that spread anti-India sentiment and attack constitutional institutions, and tied the event to an agenda promoted by the financier George Soros, a charge he did not back with any detail.

He also argued that the summit would hurt investment and development, even though the state government plans its own investment summit. Turning to the Congress's record, he claimed the party had **lost 100 elections** under Rahul Gandhi and asked which of the guarantees and promises from the 2023 Assembly election had been implemented. The Congress has not responded to these claims in the reports available.

## What the summit offers

The theme is "Forging a New World". The organisers describe it as an effort to revive the legacy of Afro-Asian solidarity and the Non-Aligned Movement, and it is tied to India's 80th year of Independence and the 137th birth anniversary of Jawaharlal Nehru, which falls on Saturday.

The programme has plenary sessions, international panels, an exhibition and a set of Telangana sessions. The international panels take up artificial intelligence, the emerging world order, economic collaboration, climate change, youth in governance, gender justice, reform of multilateral institutions and the future of media. Delegates named in the previews include policymakers, former ministers, senators and scholars from the Netherlands, Portugal, Indonesia, Côte d'Ivoire, South Korea, Brazil, Japan and Thailand.

The state's own agenda sits inside this frame. Nine **Telangana sessions** cover Future City and urbanisation, economic justice, social justice, welfare policies, cooperative federalism, culture and society, youth, sports and politics, and electoral integrity in Telangana and across India. The Future City session is meant to present the state's urban blueprints and its aim of making Hyderabad a global hub for investment and innovation.

Revanth Reddy, Deputy Chief Minister Mallu Bhatti Vikramarka, IT and Industries Minister D Sridhar Babu and Ministers N Uttam Kumar Reddy, Ponnam Prabhakar and Mohammed Azharuddin are listed as speakers. On Thursday, Bhatti Vikramarka met Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge and Karnataka Deputy Chief Minister D K Shivakumar separately and handed them formal invitations. Whether either will attend has not been confirmed.

## Rahul Gandhi's keynote

**Rahul Gandhi**, Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, is due to give the keynote at the valedictory session on Saturday. He is expected to set out an alternative view of India's foreign policy built on principled multilateralism, strategic autonomy, peaceful coexistence and democratic values.

The Hyderabad host committee has framed the event as a matter of pride. Chamala Kiran Kumar Reddy, chairman of the Telangana Pradesh Congress Committee's media and publicity cell and the Bhongir MP, called hosting it "a matter of immense pride for Telangana". It is the second time Hyderabad has hosted a Bharat Summit.

## Why the row matters

Hyderabad has been a stage for the BJP and the Congress to trade charges for two weeks. On October 25 the Bharat Rashtra Samithi's K T Rama Rao [called Telangana an ATM for Rahul Gandhi](/en/news/ktr-telangana-atm-rahul-gandhi-420-promises) and asked what had happened to the Congress's 420 promises. On November 2, Home Minister Amit Shah [used the same word for Revanth Reddy's government](/en/news/amit-shah-revanth-telangana-atm-congress-evm-hyderabad) at the end of a BJP padayatra in the old city. Kishan Reddy's question about the bills moves that argument from rhetoric to the state's accounts.

It also lands as the state sits through a politically sensitive stretch. Its voter list [was on course to shrink by about **26%**](/en/news/telangana-sir-electorate-may-shrink-26-per-cent) after the Special Intensive Revision, and electoral integrity is one of the summit's own Telangana sessions.

## What happens next

The summit closes on Saturday, November 14, with Rahul Gandhi's address. The state government has not said how much it has spent, and until it does, Kishan Reddy's demand for an account of public money will stay the sharpest line of attack from the BJP.
