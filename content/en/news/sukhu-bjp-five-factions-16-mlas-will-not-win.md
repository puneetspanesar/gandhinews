Himachal Pradesh Chief Minister Sukhvinder Singh Sukhu on Friday dismissed the BJP's claim that it will sweep the 2027 Assembly election, saying the opposition party in the state is split into rival camps.

"The BJP is divided into five factions," Sukhu told a press conference in Shimla. "These claims are being made to boost the morale of different groups within the party."

The BJP has said it will return to power in the hill state with a two-thirds majority. Sukhu said the party's own internal surveys pointed the other way, suggesting it could lose seats, and stood by a prediction he has made before. "I have said that 16 of the BJP's 28 MLAs will not win the next election," he said.

He also took aim at the Leader of the Opposition, former Chief Minister Jai Ram Thakur. Sukhu said he did not attach much weight to Thakur's daily statements, which he suggested were meant to show the BJP's central leadership that its state unit was active.

"Let them say whatever they want. The people will decide," he said.

The Congress won power in Himachal in 2022, and Sukhu has led the government since. The next Assembly election in the 68-seat House is due in late 2027, and the contest between the two parties is already shaping the state's politics a year ahead of the vote.
