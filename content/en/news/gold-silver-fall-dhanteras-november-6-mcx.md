Gold buyers on Dhanteras found the metal **cheaper than a day earlier**. By about 10:45 am on Friday, gold futures on the Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX) were down ₹2,034 at ₹1,42,061 per 10 grams, after opening at ₹1,42,161 against Thursday's close of ₹1,44,095. They touched ₹1,41,808 before trimming the loss.

Silver fell harder. MCX silver was **down ₹3,105 at ₹1,84,035** a kilo, against ₹1,87,140 on Thursday, after dipping to ₹1,83,700.

The fall followed world prices. On Comex, gold opened at $4,098.50 an ounce against a close of $4,113.50 and slipped to about $4,061. It had climbed to a two-week high on Thursday as the dollar weakened after the US midterm elections, gaining more than $250 this week from a one-year low of about $3,870. On Friday **a firmer dollar and profit-taking** reversed part of that move.

The next test comes this evening, when **US jobs and wage data are due at 7 pm IST**. Strong numbers could add to fears of a US rate rise and weigh on gold. Independent analyst Anuj Gupta said markets were pricing a 68% chance of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December.

Ponmudi R, chief executive of Enrich Money, called the near-term bias "cautiously weak". He said gold needs to hold above ₹1,43,700 to steady, and a break below ₹1,40,000 could take it towards ₹1,38,300.

Even after Friday's fall, gold is well below its peak. MCX gold's high this year is ₹1,80,779 per 10 grams.
