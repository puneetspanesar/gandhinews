Indian shares **held their ground on Friday morning** while the rest of Asia sold off. At 11 am the Sensex was up 394.27 points, or 0.5%, at 79,704.28, and the Nifty 50 was up 63 points, or 0.26%, at 24,599.60.

The day began quietly. The Sensex opened at 79,371, barely above Thursday's close of 79,310.01, and the Nifty 50 opened 22 points lower at 24,514. Buyers came in within the first hour, and the Sensex climbed to a high of 79,959.48, **just short of the 80,000 mark**, before settling back a little.

## Asia takes the hit

The calm in Mumbai stood out against the region. Overnight, Wall Street had its worst day in more than a year as chip makers crashed, and the selling carried into Asia. **Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 5.88%** to 57,485 in morning trade, South Korea's Kospi lost about 5% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng 1.11%. Shanghai was the exception, up 1.14%.

Hariselvan Radhakrishnan of HST Wealth had expected India to stand apart, at least at first. India "may initially demonstrate relative resilience" in the global correction, he said before the open, pointing to stable crude prices and the strength in bank stocks. He said a fall below 24,330 on the Nifty would show that global fear was winning over domestic support.

## IT up, small companies down

Indian IT companies, which sell software services rather than chips, **led the gains instead of the losses**. The Nifty IT index rose 1.9% in the first half hour, with Mphasis, Persistent Systems and TCS among the gainers. By 11 am M&M, Titan and ICICI Bank were the top Sensex gainers, while Asian Paints, Power Grid and Bajaj Finance had been the biggest drags early on.

Foreign investors helped set the tone. They **bought shares worth ₹14,974.68 crore on Thursday**, their fifth straight day of buying, while domestic institutions sold ₹10,444.54 crore.

The broader market told a different story. The Nifty Midcap 100 fell 1.14% and the Smallcap 100 1.69% at the open, and quarterly results punished several companies. **Astral fell as much as 12.43%** to ₹1,215.50, a 52-week low, after its September-quarter profit fell 5.9% to ₹127 crore. Happy Forgings dropped 9.9% to ₹1,695, Schneider Electric fell 8.6% after its profit dropped 42.3% to ₹30 crore, and Groww's parent Billionbrains Garage Ventures slid 6.9% to ₹208.55 after large block deals.

Traders are also watching the US jobs report, due on Friday evening India time, for clues on the Federal Reserve's next move.
