Indian shares were set for a **sharp gap-up on Thursday** as the pre-open session put the Sensex up 1,623 points, or 2.10%, at 78,925 shortly after 9 am. The Nifty 50 stood at 24,378, up 417 points or 1.74%, and was closing in on the 24,400 mark.

The two indices had ended Wednesday at 77,301.40 and 23,960.60, after a fifth straight day of gains. Thursday's move, if it holds through the session, would wipe out much of the recent pullback in one go.

## Oil and the US vote

Two things drove the buying. The Democrats' win in the US midterm elections has raised hopes of a **faster end to the West Asia conflict**, and crude has fallen hard as a result. Brent traded **below $100 a barrel** after dropping 8% on Wednesday, with January futures near $93, down another 1% in Asian hours. For India, which imports most of its oil, cheaper crude eases worries about inflation and the import bill.

Wall Street set the tone overnight. The Dow rose 3.03%, the S&P 500 1.58% and the Nasdaq 1.44%. US futures gave back some of their early gains on Thursday morning.

The rupee also strengthened, **gaining 29 paise to 94.36** against the dollar from Wednesday's close of 94.65. Gold and silver futures rose 1.7% and 1.9%.

## A weak Asia

The rest of Asia did not share the mood. South Korea's Kospi was **down 3.6%** by mid-morning as traders sold chip and technology shares, and Japan's Nikkei 225 slipped 0.39%.

Chart watchers say the Nifty needs to climb above 24,780 before the broader trend can be called better. Bombay Coated & Special Steels makes its market debut on Thursday.
