A former gang leader was sentenced in Las Vegas on Tuesday to **40 years to life** in prison for the 1996 murder of rapper Tupac Shakur, in a case that went unsolved for almost 30 years.

Duane "Keffe D" Davis, 63, once a leader of the South Side Compton Crips, is the only person ever charged in Shakur's death. He had been found guilty of first-degree murder with the use of a deadly weapon.

"The world never forgot about him and his death, and neither did Las Vegas," Judge Carli Kierny said as she passed sentence.

## The 1996 shooting

Shakur was shot in Las Vegas on September 7, 1996, while sitting in the passenger seat of a black BMW stopped at a red light, after attending a Mike Tyson heavyweight fight. He died six days later, aged 25. The shooting came about two hours after a confrontation between Shakur's entourage and Davis's nephew, Orlando Anderson.

Investigators suspected Davis for decades but lacked the evidence to charge him. What changed was Davis himself: he talked about his role in media interviews and in his 2019 memoir, *Compton Street Legend*, where he admitted handing the gun to companions in a white Cadillac. He is the only one from that car still alive.

A 2008 agreement with federal agents had given him limited immunity for statements made in private. Prosecutors argued that his public accounts voided that protection.

## Family in court

Prosecutor Marc DiGiacomo said Davis was trying to profit from a killing he committed and was "guaranteed to die" behind bars. Shakur's sister, Sekyiwa "Set" Shakur, told the court: "This man's life mattered." His brother, Maurice "Mopreme" Shakur, called the murder "devastating, heartbreaking and sad".

Asked if he wished to say anything more, Davis replied: "No, I'm good." He asked for seized property, including personal photographs, to be returned, and said he would "appeal this case for sure".
