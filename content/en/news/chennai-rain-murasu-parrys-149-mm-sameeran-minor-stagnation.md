Chennai woke on Monday to **waterlogged roads after a night of torrential rain** that fell hardest on its northern neighbourhoods and also soaked the neighbouring districts of Chengalpattu and Tiruvallur, as Cyclone Murasu closed in on the Andhra Pradesh coast.

Parrys, the old commercial heart of north Chennai, recorded **149.2 mm in the 24 hours to 6 am**, according to the Greater Chennai Corporation's Integrated Command and Control Centre. New Manali Town and Manali were close behind. Five locations had very heavy rain, 28 heavy and 25 moderate. Madhavaram logged 11 cm at the IMD's stations, with Ennore Port and Nungambakkam at 10 cm each.

Rough waves pounded the city's beaches. Corporation Commissioner G S Sameeran inspected stagnant water on Besant Road in Triplicane and then went to the command centre at Ripon Building to review the relief work. He said there was "some minor water stagnation" in a few areas and **no major flooding**.

Only **two of the city's 22 subways** saw minor waterlogging, which was cleared quickly, and all of them stayed open. Pumps were draining the worst-hit streets and field teams with backup motors were posted at vulnerable spots. Sameeran said he was working with the city police on traffic and on the risk of wave surges along the coast.

Schools and colleges in Chennai are shut for the day under an orange alert for very heavy rain. Chennai airport has cancelled **11 IndiGo flights** to and from Vijayawada, Rajahmundry, Kadapa and Tiruchirappalli for the day.

Murasu, now a severe cyclonic storm, is expected to cross the Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Narsapur, close to Ongole, around midnight, with winds of 100 to 110 km/h gusting to 120 km/h.
