India will send 168 athletes to the Asian Para Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, a squad little more than half the size of the one that went to Hangzhou three years ago.

The contingent, named on Tuesday, has 112 men and 56 women across 11 sports. They will be supported by 98 team officials and 15 contingent staff. The Games, the fifth edition, run from October 18 to 24, with 45 nations competing for medals in 492 events across 18 sports.

Badminton player Pramod Bhagat, a Tokyo Paralympic gold medallist, and table tennis player Bhavina Patel will be India's flagbearers at the opening ceremony.

## Familiar names lead the way

The squad is built around India's best-known para athletes. Avani Lekhara, also a gold medallist in Tokyo, is in the 13-member para-shooting team. Archer Sheetal Devi is in the 11-strong para-archery group, and Bhagat is part of a 28-member badminton squad, the second-largest in the contingent.

Para-athletics remains the backbone of the team with 62 athletes, followed by badminton with 28 and swimming with 16. Shooting, archery and table tennis each have between 10 and 13 places, and India will also field a full ten-member squad in football 5-a-side, the game for visually impaired players.

At the other end of the list, eight powerlifters and seven judokas make the trip, along with two boccia players and a single para-cyclist.

## Why the team is smaller

India sent 309 athletes to Hangzhou. The biggest cut this time is in athletics, where the Olympic Council of Asia initially allotted India only 60 quota places, against the 123 athletes India took to China. The Paralympic Committee of India secured two more spots, taking the athletics squad to 62.

The ministry has also chosen not to send teams in seven sports: taekwondo, goalball, sitting volleyball, wheelchair basketball, wheelchair fencing, wheelchair tennis and wheelchair rugby.

Between the tighter quotas and the dropped sports, India will have 141 fewer athletes than in Hangzhou. The selection itself ran late, and some places are still unsettled. Court appeals are pending in a few sports, so the final numbers could change before the team flies out.

## What comes next

The opening ceremony is on October 18, and competition runs for a week. With fewer entries than in Hangzhou, India's medal hopes will rest largely on the athletics squad and on proven names such as Bhagat, Lekhara and Sheetal Devi.
