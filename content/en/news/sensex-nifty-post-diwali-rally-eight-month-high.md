The Sensex **jumped about 1,100 points to 82,212** in early trade on Wednesday, November 11, and the Nifty 50 rose above 25,300, as Indian stock markets reopened after a one-day Diwali-Balipratipada holiday and picked up where the festive rally left off.

By about 9:30 am the Sensex was up 1,106 points, around 1.4%, from its November 9 close of 81,106.65. The Nifty was up about 276 points at 25,324, a gain of roughly 1.1% on its previous close of 25,044.65. The benchmarks touched an **eight-month high** at the open, with the Sensex peaking at 82,520.40 and the Nifty at 25,424.35. Both indices have gained roughly 9% to 10% in November so far.

## A rally with no laggards

The buying was unusually broad. **Every Sensex constituent traded in the green** in the first hour. IndiGo, Mahindra & Mahindra, State Bank of India, Axis Bank, Eternal and Asian Paints were the top gainers, with moves of up to 5%.

Banks led the way. The BSE Bankex rose 1,294 points to 68,528, and the Nifty Bank index gained about 1.8%, with all 14 of its constituents higher. The BSE Auto and Capital Goods indices each added more than 1,000 points. Only Sun Pharma and Titan were among the laggards at the open in one early tally.

IndiGo got a further lift from cheaper fuel and from its September quarter results, after which brokerages stayed positive on the airline. Lower crude is expected to ease aviation turbine fuel costs.

## Oil and foreign money

Two forces explain most of the move. The first is **crude oil**. Brent fell below $90 a barrel on Tuesday for the first time since August, and was trading near $92.45 on Wednesday, up almost 1% on the day but near a 10-week low. Our earlier reports tracked Brent at far higher levels, above $116 at one point in October, so the slide has been steep for an economy that imports most of its oil. Easing tension between the United States and Iran, after remarks by President Donald Trump, has been cited for the drop.

The second is **foreign portfolio money**. Foreign investors have bought ₹53,739 crore of Indian equities in November so far, the **highest monthly inflow since September 2024**, according to market data cited on Wednesday. Another account put purchases at up to ₹55,000 crore over the last eight sessions. That is a sharp turn from the selling we reported through October, when outflows reached ₹53,337 crore by the tenth week of heavy selling.

Bank deposit growth has also quickened to above 17% year on year, driven by inflows into FCNR-B deposits, according to one market report.

## Asia up, Wall Street soft

Asian markets rose in the morning. Japan's Nikkei gained 1.27% to 61,230, Taiwan's Weighted index rose 2.54% to 43,815 and South Korea's Kospi jumped 2.27% to 5,350. Wall Street, by contrast, closed lower overnight, with the Dow down 0.3%, the S&P 500 down 0.38% and the Nasdaq down 0.74%, after US consumer prices for October rose 0.4% and revived fears of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December.

## What the analysts say

Geojit's Anand James said the upside path of **25,400 to 25,700** on the Nifty remains intact, but he raised the downside marker to 24,926 and trimmed the near-term target to 25,327, citing a higher chance of mean-reversion declines after such a steep run. In plain terms, a pause after a six-session surge of more than 6% would not be unusual.

## Why it matters

Market capitalisation on the BSE rose to **₹511.21 lakh crore** in the morning. For households, the rally lifts the value of equity mutual funds and SIPs, and the crude slide, if it holds, eases pressure on petrol and diesel costs, airfares and the rupee. A strong run of foreign buying also supports the currency. The risks are the same two drivers in reverse: a rebound in oil if US-Iran talks falter, and a hawkish turn from the Fed.

## What next

Traders will watch whether the Nifty can hold above the 25,327 to 25,400 zone into the close, the next set of FPI data from the depositories, and the US-Iran talks. Closing figures for the day will be reported after the 3:30 pm close.
