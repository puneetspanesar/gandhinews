Novak Djokovic's season is over. **Australian qualifier Rinky Hijikata** came from a set down to beat the seven-time champion 2-6, 6-3, 6-3 in the second round of the Paris Masters on Wednesday night, a result that also ends the Serbian's hopes of a place at the ATP Finals in Turin.

Djokovic, seeded 10th, needed at least a semi-final in Paris to stay in the race for the season finale, which runs from November 15 to 22. He raced through the first set, breaking at 4-2, and looked set to repeat their earlier meetings, all won by Djokovic in straight sets.


## How the match turned

Hijikata, **ranked 83rd in the world**, grew bolder in the second set. Djokovic survived a six-deuce game early on, but Hijikata did not face a single break point in the set, broke Djokovic at 4-3 and served out the set to love. In the decider he saved three break points at 2-1 to stay level, then took the match away late on. Serving at 3-5 to stay alive, **Djokovic double-faulted twice** to open the game and could not recover.

The Australian finished with **37 winners to Djokovic's 18** in two hours and 14 minutes. He said he had been struggling with neck pain since the day before. "Honestly, I wasn't sure whether I was going to be able to take the court today," Hijikata said. "I think the last few months, I've maybe gotten a little bit passive under pressure."

Before the match the 25-year-old had said: "I feel level wise, this is probably the best season I've had in my career so far." He came through two qualifying rounds in Paris and will meet **Tomas Martin Etcheverry in the last 16**.

## A day of upsets

It was a wild Wednesday at the Paris La Défense Arena. Alexander Zverev, playing his first match as world No. 1, lost **7-6(5), 6-2 to Lorenzo Musetti**. "Yeah, I was bad," the German said. Fourth seed Daniil Medvedev went out 6-4, 6-4 to Ignacio Buse, and eighth seed Alex de Minaur lost to Etcheverry.


Quentin Halys beat seventh seed Frances Tiafoe 6-3, 1-6, 7-6(9) after Tiafoe wasted four match points. That result **sent Arthur Fils to Turin**, the first Frenchman to qualify for the ATP Finals since Gael Monfils in 2016.
