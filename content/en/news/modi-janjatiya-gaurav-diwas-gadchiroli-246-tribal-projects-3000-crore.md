Prime Minister Narendra Modi will **inaugurate 173 projects and lay foundation stones for 73 more** on Sunday, November 15, 2026, in a tribal-welfare package the Prime Minister's Office puts at about ₹3,000 crore. He will take part in the Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas programme at Gadchiroli in Maharashtra at 11 am by video conference and address the gathering on the 151st birth anniversary of Dharti Aaba Bhagwan Birsa Munda, according to the Office's statement of Saturday, November 14.

The 246 projects cover tribal education, health, livelihoods and community infrastructure across the country. The event closes the Janjatiya Gaurav Pakhwada, a fortnight of tribal outreach observed nationwide from November 1 to 15.

## What the Office's statement lists

The largest inaugurations are schools and service centres. **Thirty-eight Eklavya Model Residential Schools** for tribal students are to be opened, along with **100 Multi-Purpose Centres**, which the statement describes as hubs for delivering services and for community activity. The rest of the 173 inaugurations are further projects under PM-JANMAN, the Pradhan Mantri Janjati Adivasi Nyaya Maha Abhiyan aimed at the most backward tribal groups, and under the Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan.

Two buildings stand out in the list. He will inaugurate the **Tribal Research Institute building in Itanagar**, Arunachal Pradesh, and the **Ropuiliani Tribal Freedom Fighter Museum in Aizawl**, Mizoram. The museum is named after Ropuiliani, a 19th-century Mizo chieftainess who resisted British rule, and honours Mizo tribal freedom fighters and their history.

The 73 foundation stones comprise **19 new Eklavya Model Residential Schools**, 12 Tribal Multipurpose Marketing Centres meant to widen market access for tribal producers, and 42 other projects under the two schemes. The statement does not give a cost for each item or a split of the ₹3,000 crore.

## How we got here

The Prime Minister has used Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas to open tribal projects away from Delhi, at Khunti in Jharkhand in 2023 and at Dediapada in Gujarat in 2025. On November 15, 2025, at Dediapada in Gujarat, on the 150th anniversary, he inaugurated and laid foundation stones for works **worth over ₹9,700 crore**, according to the Press Information Bureau. They included 42 Eklavya schools worth about ₹1,900 crore, 228 Multi-Purpose Centres and the housewarming of one lakh homes built under the two schemes.

This year's package is about **a third of that size** in rupee terms: 38 schools against 42, 100 centres against 228, and 19 new Eklavya foundation stones against 50 a year ago. The 2025 total included roads, buses and a language chair that have no counterpart in this year's statement.

The scheme behind much of the list, the Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan, was launched on October 2, 2024, at Hazaribagh in Jharkhand. The Bureau's release then gave it an **outlay of ₹79,156 crore**, of which the Centre's share is ₹56,333 crore, covering about 63,843 tribal-majority villages, more than five crore people, 549 districts and 17 line ministries over five years.

## Why Gadchiroli

The venue is a tribal-majority district in eastern Maharashtra. State tribal development minister Ashok Uike announced on November 12 that the Prime Minister would address the state-level programme by video link. Maharashtra is marking Bhagwan Birsa Munda Gaurav Week from November 11 to 18, with aid under the Nucleus Budget scheme, help camps for Aadhaar and other documents on November 16 and health and nutrition programmes on November 17. Minister Ashish Shelar said the state has appointed over 3,000 contractual teachers in tribal residential schools.

## What the Prime Minister said last year

No text of Sunday's address exists yet, so none is quoted. At Dediapada a year ago he said **gram sabhas had been made the pivot of development**, put the spending on PM-JANMAN at ₹24,000 crore, and said over 60,000 villages had joined the Dharti Aaba campaign. His stated aim was "let no one lag behind in progress, let no one be left out of development". The same release recorded his claim that tribal enrolment in Eklavya schools had risen 60% and that six crore people had been screened in the sickle cell campaign. These are the government's figures, not independently audited ones.

## Why it matters

For tribal families, the practical items are the residential schools and the multi-purpose centres. The Eklavya schools are residential schools for tribal students, and the centres are meant to be hubs where services and community activity are delivered in the habitation itself. In 2025 the Prime Minister said the Centre had allocated over ₹18,000 crore for Eklavya schools in the previous five to six years. How many of the new schools have teachers and students in place is not part of Saturday's statement.

The Itanagar and Aizawl buildings take the package to the north-east. Last year's event likewise opened a Tribal Research Institute building, in Imphal, Manipur.

## What happens next

The Prime Minister speaks at **11 am on Sunday, November 15**. The Pakhwada ends the same day, and Maharashtra's tribal week runs to November 18, when the state holds its Tribal Economic, Educational and Social Empowerment Day. The Prime Minister's next scheduled engagement is the [ASEAN-India summit in Manila](/en/news/modi-asean-india-summit-manila-november-16-18) from November 16 to 18.
