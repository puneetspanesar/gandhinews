The Union Cabinet on Wednesday, November 25, approved the **continuation of the Postal Operations scheme with ₹1,141 crore** for the five years from 2026-27 to 2030-31, the period of the 16th Finance Commission cycle. The Central Sector scheme pays for upgrading the Department of Posts' network, from village post offices to parcel hubs, as mail and parcel traffic shifts towards e-commerce and digital services. Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw briefed reporters on the decision after the meeting, chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

## What the Cabinet approved

The Press Information Bureau's release describes a scheme that runs on six sub-schemes, and the allocations add up exactly to the ₹1,141 crore outlay.

**Mail Network Optimization gets ₹575 crore**, the largest share. It pays for centralised delivery, mechanisation of sorting, the Advanced Postal Technology (APT 2.0) IT system, GIS-based services, **smart 24x7 letter boxes**, and upgraded facilities and transport workshops.

**Parcel Network Optimization gets ₹278 crore** to build Parcel Hubs and expand Parcel Packaging Units, with the stated aim of speeding up parcel movement, cutting handling, and managing growing e-commerce and rural volumes.

**Rural Business and Access to Postal Network gets ₹120 crore** to turn rural post offices into citizen-focused service hubs, with standardised branding and better use of resources, to widen financial inclusion and last-mile delivery.

Marketing functions and quality of service get ₹100 crore, for multi-channel campaigns, expanded Dak Chaupal outreach, market research, ICT upgrades and third-party evaluation of service quality. Philately gets ₹60 crore for exhibitions, school clubs and new stamp designs, which the release lists as scented, holographic and 3D, along with the Dhai Akhar letter-writing competition, the SPARSH scholarship and modernised production centres. International mail infrastructure gets ₹8 crore to upgrade Foreign Post Offices and Sub Foreign Post Offices, so that India Post can process export consignments faster.

## The network behind the money

The release says India Post operates about **1.65 lakh post offices, of which 1,49,655 are in rural areas**. Beyond letters and parcels, the same counters carry the Post Office Savings Bank, India Post Payments Bank, Postal Life Insurance, Aadhaar enrolment and updates, passport services through Post Office Passport Seva Kendras, KYC verification, and support for rural artisans through Dakghar Niryat Kendras.

That spread is why a scheme of this size matters outside the mail room. A rural post office that is upgraded into a service hub is, for many villages, the nearest place to deposit savings, buy insurance or update Aadhaar, and the parcel hubs are meant to move rural and e-commerce consignments with less handling. The release frames the scheme as supporting Digital India, Atmanirbhar Bharat and Viksit Bharat.

## What the release does not say

The release gives no earlier outlay for the scheme, no year-by-year spending and no targets such as the number of post offices to be upgraded or hubs to be built. Nor does it say when guidelines will be issued. The approval covers the five financial years from 2026-27 to 2030-31.

## One of five Cabinet nods

The postal scheme was one of five decisions taken at the same meeting, which together carry a price tag of ₹19,934 crore according to reports of the briefing. Petroleum Minister Hardeep Singh Puri listed them in a post on X, saying the decisions would "create new opportunities towards a Viksit Bharat". The others were a manufacturing scheme for construction and infrastructure equipment, which we reported separately, a second container terminal at Kamarajar Port in Tamil Nadu, a greenfield airport at Doloo in Silchar, Assam, and the continuation of the Estates Management Scheme for postal buildings.

The port project is the largest of the set after the equipment scheme. It is a ₹4,288 crore terminal with a capacity of 2 million TEUs a year, built in two phases by a private developer on a design, build, finance, operate and transfer basis. Phase 1 is due within 24 months of the award and Phase 2 by month 48. The port handled 700,000 TEUs against an existing capacity of 800,000, an 88% utilisation, according to details given with the Cabinet note.

## What happens next

The Department of Posts will now have to turn the approval into spending: guidelines for each sub-scheme, tenders for parcel hubs and mechanised sorting, and the choice of rural offices to be upgraded. The release names no dates for any of these steps.
