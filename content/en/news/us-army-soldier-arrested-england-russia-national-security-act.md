A senior serving soldier of the **US Army was arrested in England on Tuesday** on suspicion of helping Russian intelligence, the Metropolitan Police announced, in one of the most unusual espionage cases Britain has handled since Moscow's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The 41-year-old man was detained near Gloucester on November 10 and is being held at a police station in London. He has not been named.

## What police say

Counter Terrorism Policing London arrested him on suspicion of offences under **Sections 1 and 3 of the National Security Act 2023**. Section 1 covers obtaining or disclosing protected information. Section 3 covers assisting a foreign intelligence service. Officers are searching two locations in Gloucestershire, and police say the allegations relate to Russia.

"The allegations are serious and our investigation is very much ongoing," said Deputy Assistant Commissioner Vicki Evans, Senior National Coordinator for Counter Terrorism Policing. She said the arrest came out of close collaboration between UK and US authorities. Police say the case is not linked to any other counter-terrorism investigation now under way, which separates it from the [suspected Iran-linked plot at RAF Fairford](/en/news/uk-says-iran-highly-likely-behind-raf-fairford-plot).

Under the Act, a suspect can be held for up to 14 days without charge, so police have until about **November 24** to charge him or release him. Police do not usually name suspects before charge.

## The soldier and his base

US Army Counterintelligence Command described him as a "senior soldier" but gave no rank, unit or role, and declined to say more. He was reported to be based at Imjin Barracks at Innsworth, near Gloucester, which houses the headquarters of NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps and where a small US intelligence contingent also works. Nothing published says what information he is suspected of having passed on, or whether it concerned NATO.

## Washington's reaction

Brigadier General Sean Stinchon, who leads US Army Counterintelligence Command, thanked the UK and the FBI for their cooperation and called the arrest "a sobering reminder that insider threats are real and persistent." He urged anyone who sees suspicious activity to report it.

FBI Director Kash Patel said the case "involves allegations of espionage, which is of the utmost concern to the United States and our allies," and that the FBI has "no tolerance for anyone who breaks their oath." He said the bureau has made **167 counterintelligence and espionage-related arrests** in the past 21 months, a 53% rise on the previous comparable period. A source told one US broadcaster the soldier could face action from both British authorities and the US Army, where military espionage charges can carry life imprisonment. No official has confirmed that.

No response from the Kremlin or from British ministers had been found at the time of writing.

## How we got here

This is believed to be the first time a US soldier has been suspected of spying for a foreign government while serving in Britain. It follows a run of Russia-linked arrests under the same Act. In **September 2025** three people, two men and a woman, were arrested on suspicion of spying for Moscow, and in **October 2025** three men were held on suspicion of helping Russian intelligence under Section 3. Western officials have blamed Russia for around 200 sabotage and other malign incidents across Europe since 2022, according to reports.

Britain has been one of Ukraine's largest backers, and relations with Moscow are at their lowest point. The arrest comes as Russia, Ukraine and the US are in talks over a possible settlement. Our reports on the [Abu Dhabi talks](/en/news/abu-dhabi-talks-end-prisoner-exchange-agreed-kushner-medinsky) cover where those stand. The [re-arrest of two men on October 20 over the Fairford plot](/en/news/uk-police-rearrest-two-men-iran-intelligence) was a separate case.

## Why it matters

The arrest puts a question to the alliance about its own insiders. The soldier served on a base that houses NATO's rapid-reaction headquarters, and the case comes while Washington and London are backing Ukraine against Moscow. For readers in India, the case is part of the wider contest between Russia and the West that shapes sanctions, oil and defence supply chains; India has not commented, and nothing in the police statement links the case to it.

For the US and UK, the practical question is what, if anything, was compromised. Neither government has said.

## What happens next

Police must charge or release the man by about **November 24**, the Act's 14-day limit being the ceiling. His name is expected to be released after any charge. A separate US military process is possible, and the investigation, in Evans's words, is "very much ongoing."
