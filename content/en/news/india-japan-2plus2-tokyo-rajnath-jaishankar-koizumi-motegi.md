Japan's Self-Defense Forces will use an Indian firing range for the first time in 2027, with **live-fire training that includes surface-to-ship missiles**, after India and Japan held their fourth 2+2 foreign and defence ministerial meeting in Tokyo on Thursday, November 19. A **letter of intent on the use of firing ranges in India** was signed, and the two defence authorities will now work on early implementation, according to the joint statement.

Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar sat down with Japan's Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi. Japan's foreign ministry says **the meeting ran 90 minutes, from 2:40 pm to 4:10 pm**, and that a joint statement was issued afterwards. Koizumi wrote on X that "we agreed that next year, the Self-Defense Forces will use an Indian firing range for the first time."

## What the joint statement says

The statement, published by Japan's foreign ministry, runs to 20 paragraphs. Its defence and industry commitments are the most concrete part.

- **Exercises:** the ministers welcomed the Veer Guardian 26 air exercise, which brought Japanese Air Self-Defense Force fighters to India for the first time, and plan more complex drills, unmanned systems and short-notice bilateral and multilateral exercises.
- **UNICORN:** they expect early arrangements on the Unified Complex Radio Antenna for ships and India's **Acceptance of Necessity by February 2027**, the formal first step before the Indian Navy can buy it.
- **Industry:** they back deeper work under "Design with Japan, Make in India", and welcomed the third India-Japan Defence Industries Forum held in Tokyo in October.
- **Maritime security:** they welcomed the memorandum on maritime security cooperation signed in August, will focus on maritime and air domain awareness, search and rescue and disaster relief, improve mine countermeasures and explore **joint naval shipbuilding and design**.
- **Coast guards:** the fifth Coast Guard Gap Study will be hosted in India in February 2027.

Singh, in his bilateral meeting with Koizumi earlier in the day, asked for "joint development, manufacturing and long-term technological partnerships" and faster work on niche, underwater and missile technologies. He also pressed for an early Maritime Ship Repair Arrangement, which would let each navy use the other's repair facilities.

## Economic security and terrorism

The statement voices "grave concern" over **economic coercion, including arbitrary export restrictions** and non-market practices that cause overcapacity. It names no country. Jaishankar spoke of the "weaponisation" of resources, connectivity and supply chains. The two governments commit to project-based cooperation in semiconductors, critical minerals, ICT, clean energy and pharmaceuticals, following the Joint Declaration on Economic Security Cooperation adopted at the 16th Annual Summit in July.

On terrorism, the ministers **strongly condemned terrorism in all forms, including cross-border terrorism**, and called for action against UN-listed groups, naming Al Qaeda, ISIS/Daesh, Lashkar-e-Tayyiba and Jaish-e-Mohammad and their proxies. They asked for safe havens to be removed, terror financing cut and cross-border movement of terrorists stopped.

On the UN Security Council, each side pledged support for the other's candidacy for a permanent seat, and for non-permanent seats in 2028-29 and 2033-34.

## How we got here

Singh arrived in Tokyo on November 18 and visited the Yokosuka naval base with Koizumi, as we reported before the talks. This is the **third Singh-Koizumi meeting of the year**, after New Delhi in August and Cebu on October 28. Japan's use of Indian ranges matters to Tokyo because it has limited room for missile tests at home; it already has a similar arrangement with Australia, according to reports.

The 2+2 began in November 2019, and the second round in Tokyo on September 8, 2022, started Joint Service Staff Talks and the first bilateral fighter exercise plan. The joint statement also welcomes last week's Quad leaders' summit in Manila; our report is at [Modi in Manila](/en/news/modi-manila-quad-leaders-summit-dylan-johnson-modi-leaves/).

## Why it matters

For the Indian Navy and defence industry, the practical items are the UNICORN approval, the ship repair arrangement and the joint shipbuilding study, because they decide what Indian yards build and where Indian ships can be serviced. The statement also asks for undersea cable cooperation, a renewed cybersecurity memorandum and an early Space Dialogue.

The wording on economic coercion lands as Japan's ties with China have been strained, a context reports draw, though neither government names Beijing in the text.

## What happens next

India's Acceptance of Necessity for UNICORN is targeted for **February 2027**, the same month the Coast Guard Gap Study meets in India. The Japanese forces' range training is planned for 2027, and 2027 is also the **75th anniversary of diplomatic ties**, which both governments have named the "Japan-India Year of Shared Horizons". The next 2+2 will be held in India on a date to be agreed.
