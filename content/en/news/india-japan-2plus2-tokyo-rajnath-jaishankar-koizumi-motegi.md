Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar reached Tokyo on Wednesday, November 18, for the **fourth India-Japan 2+2 ministerial dialogue**, where they will sit down with Japan's Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi to draw up a plan for closer defence cooperation.

India's ambassador to Japan, Nagma Mallick, received both ministers. Singh was also met by Lt Gen Norimichi Shirakawa, vice chief of Japan's Joint Staff. The Ministry of Defence says the visit, on **November 18 and 19**, "aims to further deepen bilateral defence ties". The dialogue itself is due on Thursday, November 19, according to reports, and Singh holds a separate bilateral meeting with Koizumi.

## What is on the agenda

The Ministry of Defence's release of November 17 lists a full review of strategic and defence cooperation, an exchange of views on regional and global developments, and a fresh pledge to a "free, open, inclusive and rule-based Indo-Pacific". The practical items are more telling:

- **Defence equipment and technology** collaboration.
- **Maritime security**.
- Joint exercises of **larger scale and greater complexity across all three services**: army, navy and air force.
- Deeper industrial partnerships between defence firms, and "a roadmap for enhanced operational and industrial synergy".

The release does not name any project, purchase or agreement to be signed. Nothing of that kind should be assumed until the two governments announce it.

Singh, in a post on X before leaving, said India and Japan "share a Special Strategic and Global Partnership rooted in mutual trust" and that he looked forward to "productive discussions".

## Third meeting of the year

For Singh and Koizumi this is the **third meeting this year**. They held defence talks in New Delhi in August on maritime security, defence technology and industrial collaboration. On October 28 they met on the sidelines of the ASEAN Defence Ministers' Meeting-Plus in Cebu, in the Philippines, where the subjects included mine-countermeasure capabilities, naval shipbuilding and design, air-force engagement and defence-industry cooperation.

Those topics suggest where Wednesday's bilateral talks are likely to go, though neither side has said so. Jaishankar's programme, according to reports, also includes the 19th India-Japan Foreign Ministers' Strategic Dialogue with Motegi, for which no date has been given.

## How the 2+2 format got here

The 2+2 brings the two countries' foreign and defence ministers together in one room. The first India-Japan meeting was held in November 2019. The second took place in Tokyo on **September 8, 2022**, and Japan's own record of it shows what the format can deliver.

At that meeting the ministers agreed to start Joint Service Staff Talks between Japan's Joint Staff and India's Integrated Defence Staff. Japan took part in the multilateral MILAN naval exercise for the first time, and the reciprocal supplies and services agreement between the two armed forces was put to use there. The two sides committed to keep up the Dharma Guardian, JIMEX and Malabar exercises and planned a first bilateral fighter exercise. They also noted work on unmanned ground vehicles and robotics. On that occasion Japan told India it would strengthen its defence capabilities within five years and was considering "counterstrike capabilities"; India said it supported enhanced security and defence cooperation.

The third 2+2 was to be held in India, as the 2022 statement said, which makes this fourth round a return to Tokyo.

## Why it matters

India and Japan now talk at both foreign and defence minister level, and the topics on this agenda are practical ones. Exercises, shared work on naval technology and industrial tie-ups are the kind of outcomes that shape what Indian shipyards and defence manufacturers build and how the Navy trains. A bigger, tri-service exercise calendar would also put Indian and Japanese forces together more often across the Indo-Pacific.

## What happens next

Singh and Koizumi meet on Wednesday and the 2+2 follows on Thursday. The ministers' joint statement, and any announcement on new exercises or equipment projects, will show whether the "roadmap" in the Ministry of Defence's release carries dates and named programmes.
