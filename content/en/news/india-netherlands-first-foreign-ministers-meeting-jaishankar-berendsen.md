External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar and Dutch Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen **co-chaired the first India-Netherlands Foreign Ministers' Meeting** in New Delhi on Sunday, November 22, a new dialogue created to review the strategic partnership that Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Prime Minister Rob Jetten announced in The Hague in May. Berendsen is on an official visit to India on November 22 and 23.

The talks covered the bilateral agenda and the wider world: the war in Ukraine, the Black Sea, the conflict in the Gulf and developments in the Indo-Pacific, according to the account of the meeting. Jaishankar said the roadmap agreed in May "allows us a pathway to proceed forward".

Jaishankar wrote on X that the two sides "reaffirmed our shared commitment to deepening cooperation in trade, semiconductors, AI, digital and cybersecurity". According to reports, **semiconductors, defence and clean energy** were named as the emerging sectors both want to deepen, alongside agriculture, water and health. Berendsen said these are "the topics where we continue the cooperation and strengthen the cooperation", and asked for results: "It should be progress on very important topics that we both share." He said he and Jaishankar have met six times this year, more than he has met any counterpart outside the EU.

On Ukraine, Berendsen spoke of the war's effects on Europe and on global food security. "We very much appreciate the role that you and also your Prime Minister take in working toward a solution there," he said, according to reports.

## What was said

On trade, Jaishankar said India looks forward to the **operationalisation of the India-EU free trade agreement**. "We look forward to the operationalisation of the FTA," he said, and pointed to progress in the wider India-EU relationship, including a security and defence partnership. Negotiations on the FTA concluded in January 2026, the May joint statement records, and both leaders then called it a link between "the world's second and fourth largest economies".

He named two areas for closer work. "I think terrorism is one, climate change is another, where it's important we work together," he said. The May joint statement had condemned the April 2025 Pahalgam attack and backed India's call for a UN Comprehensive Convention on International Terrorism.

On people, Jaishankar thanked the Dutch government for looking after the Indian diaspora. "The diaspora and the Hindustani Surinamese between them form a living bridge," he said. **About 3 lakh people of Indian origin live in the Netherlands**, the largest such community in mainland Europe, and about 2 lakh of them belong to the Surinamese Hindustani community, according to the Ministry of External Affairs. More than 3,500 Indian students study there.

Both sides plan to mark the **80th anniversary of diplomatic relations in 2027**. India and the Netherlands established ties in 1947.

## What the partnership covers

The May roadmap, a seven-page document for 2026 to 2030, sets out cooperation in political ties, trade and investment, defence and security, cyber, critical technologies including semiconductors, space, AI and quantum, health, agriculture, water, climate and energy, maritime development and education. Jaishankar said the partnership, which began with water, agriculture and health, has since grown into technology, semiconductors, defence and renewable energy.

Several of those pieces were signed in The Hague. They include a memorandum on semiconductors, a **letter of intent on defence cooperation**, a memorandum on critical minerals, a migration and mobility memorandum and a green hydrogen roadmap. A Joint Trade and Investment Committee and a Fast Track Mechanism were set up for business. The Netherlands said it would join the Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative and supports India's bid for a permanent UN Security Council seat.

## The trade and investment base

The Netherlands is a larger economic partner than its size suggests. Merchandise trade came to **$27.8 billion in 2024-25**, about 2.4% of India's total, according to the Ministry of External Affairs. India sold $22.7 billion of goods there, which makes the Netherlands India's third largest export destination worldwide and its largest in Europe, helped by the Port of Rotterdam as a gateway into the continent.

The Netherlands is also the fourth largest investor in India, with cumulative investment of about $55 billion since 2000. More than 300 Dutch companies work in India, among them Philips, Signify, Akzo Nobel, KLM, Rabobank and Heineken. Indian firms have put about $28 billion into the Netherlands, and more than 300 Indian companies operate there, including TCS, Infosys, Wipro and Tata Steel.

## How we got here

Modi visited the Netherlands on May 16 and 17, his second visit, when the two governments moved from a long-standing relationship to a strategic partnership. The meeting in Delhi is the first held under the dialogue mechanism that follows from that step. Berendsen, of the Christian Democratic Alliance, became foreign minister in February 2026.

It follows a run of high-level contact this month, from the India-Japan 2+2 in Tokyo, covered in our [report on the Tokyo talks](/en/news/india-japan-2plus2-tokyo-rajnath-jaishankar-koizumi-motegi), to the trade rounds with Canada described in our [report on the CEPA round](/en/news/india-canada-cepa-sixth-round-concludes-goyal-sidhu).

## What happens next

On Monday, November 23, Berendsen is scheduled to meet Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw, the minister for electronics and information technology, the area where the semiconductor strand of the partnership sits. The agenda of that meeting has not been published.

The larger test is the India-EU trade agreement. Jaishankar's remarks show New Delhi wants the Netherlands, a major EU member and India's biggest European market for exports, to press for its entry into force. The two governments will next look to 2027, when the 80th anniversary of diplomatic ties gives them a date to show results from the roadmap.
