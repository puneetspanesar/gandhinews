West Bengal police killed **Ratan Sheikh, the prime accused in the shooting of a constable at Santiniketan**, in an encounter at Nanoor in Birbhum district on Sunday, November 15, 2026. Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari defended the action within hours, saying Sheikh fired at the police and the personnel "did what needed to be done on the spot". It is the **fifth police encounter since the change of government** in the state six months ago.

## What police and the government say

According to police sources, Sheikh, a resident of Saota village in Nanoor, was traced on Sunday and, in custody, disclosed where he had hidden a firearm. Police took him to a brick kiln in the Uchkaran area near Charkol village to recover it. Police sources allege that he produced a gun and fired, **hitting a police vehicle**, and that officers returned fire in self-defence. He was taken to Nanoor Health Centre and **declared dead at about 5:45 pm**, and the body was sent to Bolpur hospital for a post-mortem.

Other reports carry a slightly different sequence, saying he was shot while trying to open fire on a police party. The details of the final minutes have not been independently established, and no official police statement setting out the sequence was available when this report was written.

## The shooting that started it

The trail goes back to Friday evening. Three police personnel from Santiniketan police station went to the banyan tree area at **Layek Bazar on the Bolpur-Nanoor road** to arrest Sheikh, who had been granted bail in a robbery case and had returned to his locality that day. Police say he opened fire and **hit Constable Biswajit Das in the left leg**, then went into hiding.

Adhikari visited Das on Saturday at Bardhaman Medical College and Hospital, where doctors described his condition as stable. The chief minister announced Rs 5 lakh toward his treatment and said his promotion would be considered. He told reporters that police "have not been given firearms and ammunition to keep in their pockets" and that **"if you shoot at the police, don't expect a shower of flowers"**, adding that police had been given a free hand against criminals under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita.

## The chief minister's line

Speaking after the encounter, Adhikari said: "There is no place for goons and rapists in West Bengal." He warned remaining criminals and those holding illegal arms to **leave the state**. He said Sheikh had **36 cases against him**, though other reports put the count at more than two dozen, covering theft, robbery, illegal firearms and drug trafficking. He also called such individuals "Trinamool-backed goons", a political charge for which no evidence was cited in the reports.

Adhikari said he would look into **how Sheikh obtained bail during the court's Puja vacation**, a question that points at the courts and the prosecution rather than at the police.

## Ministers back the police

Three ministers echoed the chief minister. **Agnimitra Paul called Sheikh a "dreaded criminal with 36 cases"** and said attacks on police would not go unpunished, adding that the police were best placed to explain the circumstances. Umesh Rai said the government follows a zero-tolerance policy on rape and hooliganism. Sharadwat Mukherjee said the police acted in self-defence and to keep the peace.

## A pattern of encounters

The Birbhum killing follows four earlier encounters since the new government took office, the earlier ones reported from Baruipur, Gaighata, Samsherganj and Raiganj. The government has repeatedly framed these as a break from what it describes as lawlessness under the previous administration; its earlier statements on police action and recruitment are in our report on [police attacks and the Asansol recruitment drive](/en/news/wb-suvendu-police-attacks-asansol-recruitment-29000).

Police encounters in which the accused dies are routinely examined under the Supreme Court's guidelines, which require that the death be recorded and independently investigated, and in some cases magisterial inquiry follows. Whether the guidelines will be applied in the Nanoor case was not stated in the reports reviewed.

## Why it matters

For residents of Birbhum, the case combines two concerns that have run through the state's politics this year: **armed criminals released on bail**, and the use of lethal force by police in response. The constable's injury shows the risk police face in arrests of this kind, while the pace of encounter killings raises the question of oversight, since in each case the account of the final moments comes from the police themselves. Opposition parties had not been quoted in the reports reviewed.

## What happens next

The post-mortem at Bolpur hospital and the usual inquiry into a death in police firing are the next steps. The chief minister's examination of Sheikh's bail during the Puja vacation could lead to action on the bail order or the prosecution's role. Constable Das remains under treatment at Bardhaman, with a decision on his promotion pending.
