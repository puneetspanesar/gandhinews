Chief Minister **Yumnam Khemchand Singh chaired a high-level security review** in Imphal on Saturday, November 14, after two attacks in two days killed five civilians in Manipur's Kangpokpi and Ukhrul districts. The meeting told forces to continue combing operations and to strengthen their posts, and the state's Deputy Chief Minister appealed for dialogue. A Naga apex body meanwhile gave the Centre and the state **a three-day ultimatum** and said families would not claim the dead.

## What happened on the highway

The deadlier attack came on Friday afternoon at New Haven, under Litan police station in Ukhrul district. Armed men opened fire on two cars travelling together, and **four people died on the spot**, two men and two women, according to police. Bullet marks were found on the windscreens of both vehicles.

Police named the dead as Kapangkhui R S and Haoleia R S of Shangshak village, and August Ngalung and Khanmila of Tashar village. All were from the Naga community. Officers said the gunfire came from near Yaolen, a Kuki settlement close to the site, and that the ambush is suspected to be the work of Kuki militants. No group has been named by the state, and the government statement spoke only of "unidentified armed miscreants". Manipur Police, the BSF, the Army and the Assam Rifles were sent to the area and searches began for the attackers.

The Tangkhul Naga Long, which calls itself the apex body of the Tangkhul Nagas, says this was **the fourth attack at the same spot**.

## The Kangpokpi shooting

A day earlier, on Thursday, a 42-year-old Kuki woman, Kimneineng Chongloi, was killed and two others were injured in firing between Kuki Taphou and Kailenjang villages, under Senapati police station limits in the Kangpokpi belt. Kuki groups blamed cadres of the NSCN-IM and ZUF-K. The Committee on Tribal Unity called a **48-hour bandh in Kangpokpi district** from midnight on November 12 and demanded that central and state forces be withdrawn from the district's hill areas.

## What the review decided

The meeting at the Cabinet Hall of the Chief Minister's Secretariat at Babupara was attended by Home Minister Govindas Konthoujam, Chief Secretary Rajeev Singh Thakur, Security Advisor Kuldiep Singh, DGP Mukesh Singh and Additional DGP (Law and Order) Lhari Dorjee Lhatoo, with senior officers of the Assam Rifles, CRPF, BSF and central agencies. According to the government statement, **combing operations will continue** to apprehend those involved in crime and violence, personnel at posts were told to tighten security in their areas of responsibility, and measures for vulnerable areas were discussed. The meeting also stressed vigilance and coordination among the agencies.

The state has been meeting forces all week. On Wednesday the Chief Minister met the newly appointed CRPF Range Imphal DIG, B Gangadhar, on paramilitary deployment, and on Thursday he met CISF Inspector General Senthil Krishna Raj on protection of vital installations, after a large arms recovery in Bishnupur district.

## Reactions

Deputy Chief Minister Losii Dikho, a senior Naga leader, called for **dialogue and reconciliation** and condemned the killing of innocent people. Violence and retaliation, he said, cannot heal the losses, and those who choose conflict will be held accountable.

The Tangkhul Naga Long was far harder. It demanded the immediate removal of Kuki militants from Naga areas, **transfer of the case to the National Investigation Agency**, and withdrawal of all central security forces, saying it had "lost complete faith" in them. It said families would not take the bodies until the forces are withdrawn, and warned of an indefinite agitation if the demands are not met within three days, which falls around November 17.

The Meitei body COCOMI also condemned the killings, too. Its convenor, Y K Dhiren, speaking at the JNIMS mortuary in Imphal, said "protecting ordinary citizens must take precedence over non-essential security duties" and asked for personnel on VIP protection to be moved to vulnerable stretches. He noted that earlier highway attacks were usually fired from a distance, whereas this one was at close range, which he called a worrying change.

## How we got here

The two attacks fit a pattern that has built up in the hill districts. Reports cited in coverage say **at least 50 people have died in Kuki-Naga clashes** since February 2026. Our earlier reports covered the ambush in Kamjong that killed a Kuki-Zo woman and left a couple missing on October 15, the firing at Chassad two days later, and the transfer of the Kamjong police chief on October 18 that Naga women protested. In each case the state's answer has been more forces and more patrols.

## Why it matters

For people on Manipur's highways the question is practical. The Imphal-Ukhrul and Imphal-Dimapur roads are the main routes for families, traders and patients from the Naga hills, and a fourth attack at one spot raises doubts about whether road security is working. The Naga body's refusal to claim the bodies, and the Kangpokpi bandh, show how quickly each killing shuts roads and markets in a neighbouring district.

## What happens next

The Tangkhul Naga Long's three-day window runs out around Tuesday. Whether the Centre agrees to hand the case to the NIA, and whether the state answers COCOMI's call to pull forces off VIP duty, will decide if the agitation goes ahead. The Chief Minister's office has not said when the next review will be held.
