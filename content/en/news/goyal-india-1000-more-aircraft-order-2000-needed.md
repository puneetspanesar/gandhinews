Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal said on Monday that India is **ready to order another 1,000 aircraft**, on top of more than 1,200 that Indian airlines have already ordered, and that the country needs more than 2,000 planes to meet its requirements. He was speaking to reporters in Cairo at the end of a two-day visit to Egypt.

"We have ordered more than 1,200 planes, and we are ready to order another 1,000 planes," Goyal said. "We need more than 2,000 planes for our requirements. So we are pursuing the established manufacturers of aircraft for faster supplies." He described India as facing an **acute shortage of civil aircraft**.

## What was and was not said

Goyal did not name the manufacturers he is talking to, put a value on the proposed order, say which airlines would take the aircraft or give a date by which an order would be placed. The remark was one of intent. The only firm numbers are the two he gave: more than 1,200 aircraft already booked, and a stated need of more than 2,000. The suppliers that dominate the Indian market are Airbus and Boeing, which together buy components and services worth about **$2 billion a year from Indian companies**, according to the figure that accompanied his remarks.

He tied the point to air links with Egypt, which are today operated directly by EgyptAir. "We need to see more connectivity," he said. "As we get more aircraft, we are sure we will be able to have more flights to Egypt." He also met Egypt's Transport Minister, Lt Gen Kamel al-Wazir, to discuss transport and logistics cooperation, including airport development. Goyal's [Cairo trade talks](/en/news/goyal-egypt-jtc-terms-of-reference-local-currency-trade) on Sunday produced an agreement to write the terms of a possible trade pact.

## The orders already placed

The 1,200 figure is the sum of the big deals of the last three years. According to civil aviation figures cited in reports, **IndiGo ordered 500 Airbus A320-family jets in 2023** and 30 A350s in 2024, and had 901 aircraft still awaiting delivery as of March 2026. The Air India Group ordered 470 aircraft from Airbus and Boeing in 2023. Akasa Air has ordered 226 Boeing 737 MAX jets. Those four orders alone add up to about 1,226 aircraft.

The fleet they are meant to add to is much smaller. Scheduled Indian carriers operated about **853 aircraft in 2025-26, up from 395 in 2013-14**, a little more than double, while passengers carried rose from 7.64 crore to 13.3 crore over the same period, according to the figures cited. In August 2025, the Civil Aviation Ministry told Parliament that scheduled carriers were operating 846 aircraft.

## Why supply, not demand, is the limit

The shortage Goyal describes is about how fast planes arrive. Orders are delivered over several years, and Airbus and Boeing have both struggled with **production delays, component shortages and engine supply**, so a new order takes years to turn into aircraft in service. Goyal's own wording was about "faster supplies" from the established makers.

It is not a story of runaway demand. Domestic air traffic in India has been soft this year. In August the airlines carried about 1.21 crore passengers, **6.34% fewer than a year earlier**, according to DGCA data. In September they carried 1.18 crore. For January to September 2026 the total of 12.24 crore passengers was 0.82% lower than in the same period of 2025.

## A market held by two groups

Capacity is concentrated. In September IndiGo carried 77.3 lakh passengers, a **65.4% share**, and the Air India Group (Air India and Air India Express) carried 31.65 lakh, or 26.8%. Together the two held 92.2% of the market. Akasa Air had 5.6% and SpiceJet 0.6%, down from 1.2% in August. Any new order would in practice be placed by a handful of airlines, and their fleets decide how many routes to smaller cities can be added.

Aircraft shortages feed through to passengers in familiar ways: **fewer seats on busy routes**, higher fares in peak weeks, delays in opening new routes and old aircraft being kept in service longer. The same constraint limits how many international flights Indian carriers can add, which is the link Goyal drew with Egypt.

## Making planes in India

The announcement came with a note on manufacturing in India. Airbus has two final assembly lines in India, one for military transport aircraft, built with Tata Advanced Systems, and one for helicopters. Brazil's Embraer has a strategic collaboration with the Adani Group to explore regional aircraft manufacturing in India. None of these is a commercial passenger jet line, and the minister did not announce one.

## What happens next

No order has been placed, and no timetable was given. Any deal would have to come from the airlines themselves. The next data point is DGCA's October traffic release, due in the coming weeks, which will show whether the slide in passenger numbers has stopped.
