The India Meteorological Department (IMD) expects the Bay of Bengal depression to become **a cyclonic storm on Monday, November 16**, and to take the name Akvan, the third cyclone to threaten the east coast in about three weeks. A newer bulletin had not been published when this report was written, so the naming is still due rather than done. What the IMD's full press release of Sunday afternoon adds is reach: warnings for rain, wind and sea now run across **Tamil Nadu, Bengal, Jharkhand, Telangana, Chhattisgarh** and the north-east, well beyond the Andhra and Odisha coast named so far in our reports.

## What the release says

The release, issued at 2 pm on November 15 from observations at 8:30 am, describes a depression over the southeast and adjoining southwest Bay moving north-north-westwards. It expects a **cyclonic storm over the central Bay on November 16** and a **severe cyclonic storm over the west-central Bay on November 17**, then a move north-westwards towards the Andhra Pradesh coast over the following two days. Our [report on the very severe forecast](/en/news/akvan-very-severe-nov-19-120-130-kmph-imd-sunday-evening) gave the peak stage as early on November 19.

For the central Bay the release gives winds of 60 to 80 km/h rising to 90 km/h on Monday evening, then **120 to 130 km/h, rising to 145 km/h**, from the morning of November 18 in its table. The sea over the central Bay is very rough on Monday, high to very high on November 17 and 18, and very high to exceptionally high from November 19. Fishermen at sea were told to return by November 15, and **must not venture out** over the central Bay from November 16 to 20 and over the southwest Bay from November 15 to 19.

## Rain beyond the coast

The release forecasts heavy to very heavy rain in coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam on **November 19 and 20**, with isolated extremely heavy rain, which the IMD defines as above 204.4 mm in a day. Rayalaseema gets heavy rain on the same two days. Odisha gets heavy rain on November 18 and isolated very heavy rain from November 19 to 21, with gusts of 50 to 60 km/h reaching 70 km/h.

The change from earlier reports is in the south. Tamil Nadu, Puducherry and Karaikal are forecast heavy rain on November 17 and 20 and **very heavy rain on November 18 and 19**. Our Sunday reports carried only light to moderate rain and no direct impact for Tamil Nadu. Gangetic West Bengal is warned of heavy rain on November 19 and 20, Jharkhand on November 20 and 21, Telangana and southern Chhattisgarh on November 20, northern Chhattisgarh on November 21, and Mizoram and Tripura on November 19.

The IMD lists the likely impact as broken branches, damage to kutcha houses and roads, waterlogging, flooding, landslides in low-lying areas and disruption to road, rail and helicopter services. Farmers in coastal Andhra, Odisha and Tamil Nadu are advised to **harvest mature paddy, cotton, groundnut and vegetables** and drain excess water from fields.

## How the states are preparing

Andhra Pradesh has been on alert since Arnab and Murasu. Prakhar Jain, managing director of the State Disaster Management Authority, said on Sunday: "Under its influence, North Andhra may experience heavy rain accompanied by strong winds on Thursday and Friday." Heavy rain is forecast from November 18 in Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Visakhapatnam, Anakapalli and Alluri Sitarama Raju districts. Our [Andhra report](/en/news/akvan-andhra-apsdma-north-andhra-nov-19-eight-districts-fishermen-return) lists the eight districts on rain alert.

In Odisha, Ganjam Collector V Keerthi Vasan chaired a preparedness meeting on Saturday and told departments to "remain alert and discharge their responsibilities with utmost urgency." He cited the lessons of Cyclone Arnab and the floods that followed, and set a target of **zero casualties**. Pregnant women are to be moved to safer places in advance, shelters checked for water, food, lighting and toilets, and stocks of chuda, jaggery, biscuits and bottled water kept. Fishermen must be ashore by November 16. Odisha's orange alerts for November 19 to 22 are in our [earlier report](/en/news/akvan-severe-cyclone-forecast-nov-18-odisha-orange-warnings-nov-19-21).

## A coast already hit twice

Cyclone Murasu crossed near Ongole on November 3 as a severe storm, and Andhra's first damage estimate was about **₹1,919 crore**, as our [damage report](/en/news/murasu-andhra-damage-1919-crore-naidu-bapatla-guntur) records. A storm with winds of 120 to 130 km/h would be stronger than Murasu was at landfall, and the same fields and coastal villages are exposed again.

## Why it matters

For readers in Chennai and the delta districts the warning is new: very heavy rain on two days means waterlogging risk even if the storm never turns towards Tamil Nadu. For farmers from Odisha to Telangana the advice is practical, to cut and secure paddy before November 18. For coastal families, the fishing ban over the central Bay runs to November 20.

## What happens next

The IMD's Monday bulletins are the first that can confirm the cyclonic storm and name Akvan, which is due by evening. A landfall coast and time are **not yet named**; the IMD has said the track will be clearer once the storm forms. The severe stage is due on November 17, and the heaviest rain in north Andhra on November 19 and 20.
