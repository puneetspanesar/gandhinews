Indian companies and residents invested **$4.65 billion abroad in October 2026**, about 17% more than the $3.96 billion of October 2025 and roughly 4% above September, the Reserve Bank of India said in its monthly release on overseas direct investment on November 19, 2026. The mix changed sharply: equity put into foreign ventures fell, while loans to overseas subsidiaries roughly doubled.

## What the data shows

Equity investments stood at **$2.05 billion, down from $2.35 billion** a year earlier, a fall of about 13%. According to an analysis of the data file that accompanies the release, loans extended to overseas entities came to about $1.49 billion, against $735 million in October 2025, and guarantees issued by Indian parents for their foreign arms were $1.12 billion, up 27.5%. The file lists 603 entries in all.

The month was dominated by a handful of transactions. The largest was a **$1 billion loan by Adani Infra (India) to AIIL Trade and Investment**, its subsidiary in Mauritius. Without that single loan, the month's lending would have been about $486 million. Second was **Sun Pharma's $998.5 million equity investment** in a subsidiary in the Netherlands. Together the two account for about 43% of the month's total.

The next three were smaller: a $361.8 million guarantee by Zydus Medtech for a venture in France, a $346.3 million guarantee by Inventurus Knowledge Solutions for its US subsidiary, and a $340 million equity investment by Tata Steel in a holding company in Singapore. The top five deals together make up about two-thirds of the month.

## Where the money went

By destination, **the Netherlands took $1.16 billion, Mauritius $1.14 billion and Singapore $713 million**, according to the same analysis. The three together hold nearly 65% of the month. All are common locations for holding companies, so the file does not show where the money was finally deployed. The United States followed with 150 entries worth $581 million, and the UAE with 131 entries worth $254 million.

Wholesale and retail trade was the largest sector at about $1.2 billion, most of it the Adani Infra loan. Small entries were numerous: 433 of the 603 were below $1 million and together added up to just $74 million, about 1.6% of the month.

## How we got here

Outward investment has climbed steeply over the past two years. According to reports, it reached **$26.7 billion in the financial year ended March 2026**, nearly double the level two years earlier, and about $22.7 billion left in the five months to August. The biggest single deal of the year so far was Coforge's purchase of Encora at an enterprise value of $2.35 billion.

The trend has run into the weakness of the rupee, which has fallen more than 5% this year on steady dollar demand and capital outflows. The Reserve Bank has already tightened corporate hedging rules and tried to curb importers' dollar buying; our report on [the RBI's daily cash reserve ratio change](/news/rbi-raises-daily-crr-minimum-to-99-per-cent) covers another of its recent liquidity moves.

In the week before October 20, according to reports, the RBI gave major banks a **standard checklist for overseas investments above $1 billion** and for deals beyond the regulatory limit of four times the investor's net worth. Banks are expected to verify valuations, including acquisition premiums and share-swap ratios, rather than rely on the valuation reports they receive, and to look at the investor's earnings quality, past overseas deals and ability to absorb shocks. The RBI had not publicly commented on the checklist, and it has not placed new limits on overseas investment itself.

## Why it matters

Money sent abroad for overseas investment adds to demand for dollars, so a month of $4.65 billion matters at a time when the **rupee has lost more than 5% this year**. The exchange rate feeds into the cost of imported fuel, electronics and foreign education.

The October figure was driven by two deals, so a single month says little about direction. Individuals face a limit of $250,000 a year on foreign transactions, and according to reports wealthy Indians sometimes use the overseas direct investment route, through holding companies, as an alternative.

## What happens next

The RBI publishes the overseas direct investment series every month, so **the November figures will follow in a month's time**. The thing to watch is whether the banks' tighter scrutiny of deals above $1 billion shows up as fewer very large entries.
