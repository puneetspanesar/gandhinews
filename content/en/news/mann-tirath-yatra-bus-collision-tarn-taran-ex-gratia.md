Punjab Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann on Sunday, November 15, 2026, announced **₹5 lakh for the family of each person killed** and ₹1 lakh for each person injured when two buses carrying pilgrims under the state's Mukh Mantri Tirath Yatra scheme collided head-on in Tarn Taran district on Saturday evening. He also said the government would bear the full cost of treatment for the injured.

Five people died: four pilgrims and a woman riding pillion on a motorcycle. Mann went to a hospital in Amritsar on Sunday, spent time with the injured and then spoke to reporters.

## What the Chief Minister announced

The package has three parts. **Each bereaved family gets ₹5 lakh**, every injured person gets ₹1 lakh, and treatment is free. Mann also directed the civil and police administrations to give the families and the injured all help, and to make sure proper treatment reaches everyone still in hospital.

"This is an extremely painful incident," Mann said, according to the statement. He said the government "stands shoulder to shoulder" with its people in every difficult hour.

He said most of the injured are out of danger and are expected to recover soon. Mann put the number of injured at 11. The police and district administration have given higher figures, between 15 and 18, and the counts have not been reconciled.


## How the crash happened

The collision took place on **National Highway-54 near Naushehra Pannuan**, Tarn Taran district, on the evening of Saturday, November 14. Two buses carrying 37 people between them were involved. One was returning towards Faridkot after a visit to Tarn Taran, and the other was going the opposite way, towards Amritsar and Muktsar.

Tarn Taran Senior Superintendent of Police Surendra Lamba said the driver of one bus tried to avoid a motorcycle, lost control, crossed the road divider and hit the oncoming bus. A guide travelling on the Faridkot-bound bus, who suffered a fractured leg, said a motorcycle took a sudden wrong turn in front of it. Mann gave a similar account: a vehicle suddenly turned on the road and the driver swerved.

The front of both buses was badly damaged and some passengers were trapped. Local residents began the rescue before teams from the civil administration, police, the Sadak Surakhya Force and ambulances arrived. Deputy Commissioner Nirmal Ossephchan and Senior Superintendent Lamba visited the site and the hospital.

Four people died at the scene and a fifth died later. Five seriously injured people were referred from the Civil Hospital in Tarn Taran to Guru Nanak Dev Hospital in Amritsar, and the rest were treated locally. Official accounts differ on how many were critical, with the district administration counting six and police counting five.

The cause has not been formally established. No investigation findings have been made public.

## "Not a lapse in arrangements"

Mann rejected any suggestion that the scheme or its arrangements were at fault. "It was a sudden accident, not a lapse in arrangements," he said. He added that the incident "should not be attributed to any fault on the part of the government," and appealed to opposition parties not to politicise a tragedy.

He did not order an inquiry or a review of the scheme's bus arrangements, according to reports from Amritsar. Mann cited the scheme's record: **7,02,203 pilgrims have travelled on 17,531 bus journeys** to Amritsar, Sri Anandpur Sahib, Haridwar-Rishikesh, Khatu Shyam and Mathura-Vrindavan, and more than 70 buses run every day, carrying over 3,000 pilgrims. He said no accident of this kind had happened before, and credited the state's Sadak Surakhya Force with bringing down road accidents.

Deputy Commissioner Ossephchan said the government would provide "complete assistance" to the families.

## Reactions

Prime Minister Narendra Modi said on X that he was "deeply saddened to hear of the loss of lives in an accident in Tarn Taran, Punjab," offered condolences to the bereaved families and wished the injured a speedy recovery.

Congress MP Amarinder Singh Raja Warring, a former president of the Punjab Congress, called the accident heartbreaking, offered condolences to the families and wished the injured a speedy recovery. He did not criticise the government in the remarks reported so far. No other opposition statement had been reported by Sunday evening.

## The scheme and why it matters

The Mukh Mantri Tirath Yatra scheme is a Punjab government welfare programme. It covers residents aged 50 and above and provides **free air-conditioned sleeper bus travel, accommodation and food** on pilgrimage routes. Its passengers are therefore mostly older people, who are more likely to suffer serious injuries in a collision and take longer to recover.

For the families, the ₹5 lakh is immediate relief, and the free treatment matters because several of the injured have head injuries and fractures. For the government, Mann said no accident of this kind had occurred before on a scheme that runs more than 70 buses a day.

The accident came four days after a state-run PRTC bus hit a flyover wall in Sangrur on November 11, killing three and injuring more than 20, as reported in our [earlier story](/news/sangrur-prtc-bus-flyover-crash-three-killed). That crash was a PRTC service, not a pilgrimage bus.

## What happens next

The injured remain in hospitals in Amritsar and Tarn Taran, and doctors have said all are out of danger. The police investigation into how the driver and the motorcyclist came to be on the same stretch continues, and no findings have been made public. Mann has so far declined a review of the scheme's buses.
