Pakistan declared their first innings at **434 for 9 in 106.3 overs** on the second day of the first Test against Sri Lanka at the Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium on Tuesday, then took two early wickets to leave the visitors on 114 for 3 at stumps, 320 runs behind.

The innings carried five half-centuries, three of them from debutants. Pakistan resumed on 281 for 5 after a first day on which a new-look batting order had already put runs on the board against a seam attack that found help on a greenish pitch.

## How Pakistan got to 434

The hosts began the day with Saud Shakeel anchoring the middle order, and he went on to **make 93 off 199 balls**, seven runs short of what would have been a hundred. Asitha Fernando ended his stay, and Shakeel's sixth-wicket partnership with Ghazi Ghori had added 96. Ghori made 48.

Earlier, the innings had been built on quick scoring at the top. Azan Awais (30) and debutant Tayyab Arif, who made **57 off 51 balls**, put on 86 for the first wicket. Debutant Mohammad Taha then made 51 in a 69-run third-wicket stand with Babar Azam, who scored 50, before Sonal Dinusha removed Taha.

Arafat Minhas, who made 52 off 72 balls with nine fours and a six, struck his first Test half-century, and Razaullah added 10. Extras contributed 27. Babar declared once the lead was secure.

For Sri Lanka, Fernando took 3 for 107 and Dilshan Madushanka 3 for 108. Lahiru Kumara (1 for 104), Milan Rathnayake (1 for 72) and Dinusha (1 for 23) took one each. Fernando became **the sixth Sri Lankan fast bowler to reach 100 Test wickets**.

## Sri Lanka's reply

Sri Lanka's openers had little time to settle. Pasindu Sooriyabandara, promoted to open in only his third Test, was **out for a duck to Mohammad Abbas**. Debutant Saqib Khan then had Dinesh Chandimal caught for 1, and Sri Lanka were 12 for 2.

Lahiru Udara counterattacked, scoring 42 off 52 balls, before Saqib bowled him as well to leave Sri Lanka on 65 for 3. Saqib finished the day with 2 for 54 and Abbas with 1 for 23. Razaullah went wicketless for 32 and Minhas, who bowled one over, conceded a run.

Kamindu Mendis, on 40, and Dhananjaya de Silva, on 22, then added an unbroken stand of more than 50 to take Sri Lanka past 100. **Bad light ended play early**, with about 26 overs lost on the day, and the two batters will resume on the third day.

## What it means for the match

Sri Lanka need 121 more runs to avoid the follow-on if the usual 200-run margin applies, with seven wickets standing, and the batters at the crease are their most experienced pair. Bad light has now cost play on both days, so time may become a factor for a side hoping to bowl twice.

For Pakistan, the day was as much about the new faces as the total. Three of their five half-centuries came from debutants, and Minhas reached his first Test fifty, which gives the team management a deeper batting group to draw on in the matches that follow.

## What happens next

Play on the third day resumes on Wednesday, November 11, with Mendis and de Silva back at the crease. Sri Lanka's first aim is to wipe out the 320-run deficit as far as they can, and to bat long enough that Pakistan cannot enforce the follow-on. The Test is scheduled to run until November 13.
