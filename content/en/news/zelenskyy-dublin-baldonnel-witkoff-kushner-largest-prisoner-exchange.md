President Volodymyr Zelenskyy met US special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner at Baldonnel Aerodrome, outside Dublin, on the night of Thursday, November 12 and came away saying Ukraine and Russia are preparing **the largest prisoner exchange since the full-scale invasion** in February 2022. He told reporters that swaps of "1,000 for 1,000, maybe 1,500 for 1,500" had been discussed. No date has been announced.

The envoys had flown to Ireland straight from Moscow, where they spent part of the day with Vladimir Putin at the Kremlin. Zelenskyy was in the country for the summit of the European Political Community, and Ireland lent a venue for the talks. By Sunday morning Kyiv, Moscow and Washington had all confirmed that a large exchange is being worked on, and none had given a date.

## What Zelenskyy said

Zelenskyy set out three outcomes in a Telegram post overnight into Friday. "First: a humanitarian outcome, prisoners will be released, and we are counting on the largest exchange since the beginning of this war," he wrote. The second, he said, is that "we are engaging European representatives even more actively in diplomacy" so that they can take part in negotiations on an equal footing. "Third: we are also preparing further multilateral meetings. Diplomacy will remain active."

He thanked the envoys for "the intensity of communication" and thanked Ireland for hosting the summit and a venue for talks. Kyiv has argued for months that European states, whose security is tied to the outcome, cannot be left outside the room.

## The American side

Kushner said after the meeting that the talks had helped finalise the exchange, and that the deal cannot be concluded "without the Europeans being at the table". Writing on X, he called it the "largest prisoner exchange of the war" and said it could reunite "thousands of people with their families". He added that nothing apart from humanitarian steps will be final until a comprehensive agreement is reached, and that the negotiating tracks and schedule would be announced "shortly".

On Saturday the White House said Witkoff and Kushner, on President Donald Trump's instructions, had begun work on what could become the largest prisoner exchange of the war, in the words of Deputy Press Secretary Anna Kelly. Witkoff said Putin had agreed to let European representatives join the next round, and that another meeting is scheduled.

## What Moscow says

The Kremlin's account is more guarded. It also expects the exchange to be the largest of the war but says the terms are still being agreed. Russian state media suggested it could take place within two to three weeks, a timeline neither government has confirmed.

Putin told the envoys that the humanitarian track had produced "positive results", including exchanges of detainees, and that another major step was under way. He thanked Trump for helping to open contacts with Kyiv. He rejected "grey zones" along the front, saying Russia recognises only the state border as the line of separation, and opposed foreign troops in Ukraine under any settlement, warning that Russia would treat them as a "legitimate target". Kremlin foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov said Europeans must decide between peace and war, and spokesman Dmitry Peskov warned that European troops in Ukraine would mean fighting on a much larger scale.

## How we got here

Russia, Ukraine and the US met in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11, the first trilateral round in nine months. The sessions ended with [a large prisoner swap agreed in principle](/en/news/abu-dhabi-talks-end-prisoner-exchange-agreed-kushner-medinsky) but no deal on the war itself. On day two Zelenskyy said Russia's delegation had claimed about half of the Donbas land Ukraine still holds, [a claim Kyiv rejected](/en/news/zelenskyy-russia-claims-half-ukrainian-held-donbas-abu-dhabi-day-two). Thursday's Kremlin meeting, reported in [our account of the Moscow talks](/en/news/putin-witkoff-kushner-moscow-talks-kostiantynivka-humanitarian-trilateral), produced a plan for a humanitarian mission in Kostiantynivka and an agreement to keep trilateral contacts going.

For scale, the largest swap in recent years was the 1,000-for-1,000 exchange completed in May 2025. A swap of 1,500 for 1,500 would be half as large again.

## The grid and the front

The exchange is the one result both sides will own. The rest of the agenda is stuck. Zelenskyy said Ukraine's delegation in Abu Dhabi asked for a halt to strikes on energy infrastructure and calm in the Black Sea, and that Russia's negotiators answered that they had no permission to discuss sparing the grid. He said Russia has hit Ukrainian energy facilities more than 310 times since the start of November, and called the reply a sign that Moscow wants to keep striking. Russia has not responded to that account in anything we have read.

Territory remains the hardest issue. Russia holds about a fifth of Ukraine, and Kyiv refuses to give up the rest of Donbas.

## Why it matters

For families on both sides, the exchange is the part of the diplomacy that changes lives within weeks. One report linked to the White House announcement says 486 Ukrainian women are still held in Russian captivity. Prisoners returned in earlier swaps have been the main source of accounts of conditions in detention.

The European role may matter more over time. If Brussels and national capitals join the next round, as Kushner and Witkoff say Putin has accepted, security guarantees for postwar Ukraine will be negotiated alongside territory. Moscow's rejection of any foreign troops sets that discussion up as the next point of conflict. The war also touches readers far from Europe: Black Sea shipping of grain and the price of energy both depend on whether strikes on infrastructure continue, a point left unresolved this week.

## What happens next

Kushner says the tracks and schedule of the expanded talks will be announced shortly; Witkoff says another meeting is planned, with no date or venue given. The first thing to watch is the exchange itself. Kyiv has said it will be the biggest of the war, Moscow says the terms are not final, and the two estimates of its size and timing have not been reconciled.
