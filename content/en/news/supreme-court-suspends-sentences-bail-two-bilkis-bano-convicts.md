The Supreme Court has **suspended the life sentences** of two of the 11 men convicted in the Bilkis Bano case and granted them bail while their appeals are pending. The two are Bipinchand Kanaiyalal Joshi and Pradip Ramanlal Modhiya. The order, passed by Justices MM Sundresh and PB Varale, is dated **October 28, 2026**. It became public on **November 16, 2026**, when it was reported from the court's website; according to reports it had been uploaded a day earlier, on Sunday.

The order is not an acquittal. The convictions for gang-rape and murder during the 2002 Gujarat riots stand, and the appeals against them continue.

## What the order says

According to reports of the order, the bench noted that the two appellants have been **in custody for more than 17 years** and that one of them has a severe handicap. Its operative reasoning is short: "Certainly, there are issues to be decided in the appeal, both on fact and law." It then said: "we suspend the sentence and grant bail to the appellants."

Bail is to be granted "on such terms and conditions as may be imposed" by the concerned trial court. According to reports, the order itself lists no conditions, so what the two men must do on release, and where they may live, will be set by that court.

The appeals are against the **Bombay High Court judgment of May 4, 2017**, which upheld the convictions and life terms. The special leave petitions are numbered 5265-5266 of 2026, and the suspension application was heard after the court sought responses from Gujarat and Maharashtra in March 2026, according to reports.

## The arguments in court

Senior advocate Siddharth Luthra, for the appellants, argued that one of them was **not named in the FIR** and was implicated nearly three years after the incident. Additional Solicitor General Raja Thakre opposed the plea for Gujarat. He said both lower courts had considered the material on record, particularly as the offence was so serious.

## How the case got here

The case goes back to **March 2002**, when Bilkis Bano, then 19 and pregnant, was attacked during the Gujarat riots. Seven members of her family were killed, including her three-year-old daughter.

A special CBI court in Mumbai **convicted 11 men in 2008**. The High Court upheld that verdict in 2017. On **August 15, 2022**, all 11 walked out of jail after the Gujarat government granted them remission. The release set off protests, and Ms Bano went to the Supreme Court.

On **January 8, 2024**, the Supreme Court quashed the remission, holding that Gujarat was not the government competent to decide it because the trial had taken place in Maharashtra. The 11 were ordered back to jail and **surrendered on January 21, 2024**.

## The legal test

A convicted person who has appealed can ask the appeal court to suspend the sentence and release them on bail. The power is in Section 430 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, which replaced Section 389 of the old Code of Criminal Procedure. The court must record its reasons in writing. Where the sentence is death, life or ten years or more, the public prosecutor must first be given a chance to oppose release in writing.

Courts have been more ready to suspend fixed terms than life sentences, and have asked life convicts to show a gross and apparent error in the verdict. Our [explainer on suspension of sentence](suspension-of-sentence-explained-tejpal-supreme-court) sets out how that played out when the court refused it to Tarun Tejpal this month. In this case, according to reports, the bench relied on the length of custody, the handicap and the fact that the appeal raises questions of fact and law, rather than finding any error in the verdict.

## Reaction

Ms Bano, in a statement issued through senior advocate Shobha Gupta on **November 16**, said she was not informed of the application or heard before the order and learned of it from press reports. She said: "I will fight this order. I must." According to reports, she was not a party to the suspension application. Reports quote no statement from the Gujarat government on the order.

## The other nine convicts

The order covers only Joshi and Modhiya. No report says that the other nine, who surrendered in January 2024, have sought similar relief, and their position is unchanged.

## What happens next

The two men can leave jail once the trial court fixes the bail terms and they comply. The appeals against the 2017 judgment will be heard in due course, according to reports, and no date has been given. If the appeals fail, the men will have to return to complete their sentences.
