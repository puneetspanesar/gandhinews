Indian shares look set for **a sixth straight day of gains** on Thursday, even as a fresh rout in Asian chipmakers swept through Seoul on fears that the Democrats' sweep of the US Congress will bring tougher rules for artificial intelligence.

GIFT Nifty futures were trading near **24,286 at 7:50 am IST**, about 325 points above the Nifty's close of 23,960.60 on Wednesday, pointing to a gap-up open at 9:15 am. The Sensex ended Wednesday at 77,301.40. Foreign investors **bought shares worth ₹7,284 crore** on Wednesday, their fourth day of buying in a row, while domestic funds sold ₹3,712 crore.

## Seoul's sell-off

The mood in Korea was very different. The Kospi opened about 2% lower and the fall gathered pace as programme selling cascaded through large chip stocks. At 10:16 am in Seoul, 6:46 am IST, the exchange **triggered a sell-side circuit breaker**, suspending programme sell orders, with Kospi 200 futures down 5.25%. The index slid to the 5,400 level, about 5% down.

Samsung Electronics fell about 6% and **SK Hynix about 9%**. Investors fear that a Democrat-led Congress will tighten rules on AI data centres, hurting demand for memory chips. Overnight the Philadelphia chip index dropped 3.1% to a six-month low, even as the Dow **jumped 3% to 53,356.12** on Wednesday.

"Major futures indices including the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 turned lower after early counting showed Democrats leading," Kim Seok-hwan, a researcher at Mirae Asset Securities, said of Wednesday's swing in Seoul. Tokyo's Nikkei 225 was slightly lower in early trade.

## What it means for India

Dalal Street has had less to fear from the AI trade than Seoul or Taipei, and cheaper oil has helped it more. Brent was near $97 a barrel, down sharply over five sessions, which eases the import bill for India. Traders will watch whether the Nifty can hold **above the 24,000 mark** once trading opens.
