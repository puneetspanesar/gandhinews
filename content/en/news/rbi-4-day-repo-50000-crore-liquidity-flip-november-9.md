The Reserve Bank of India **lent banks ₹50,003 crore for four days** at a variable rate repo auction on Monday, November 9, a sharp change of direction after a week of taking cash out of the system. Banks bid ₹62,872 crore for the ₹50,000 crore on offer, and the central bank allotted the money at a cut-off rate of **5.51%**, one basis point above the 5.50% policy repo rate.

The auction window ran from 9:30 am to 10 am, and the money has to be returned on Friday, November 13. The weighted average rate was 5.54%. Because demand was higher than the amount offered, bids at the cut-off rate received **15.18% of the amount sought**, a partial allotment, according to the RBI's result notice (press release 2026-2027/1467).

## What the RBI said

The notice announcing the auction, issued on Friday, gave a single reason: "On a review of current and evolving liquidity conditions in the near term, it has been decided to conduct a Variable Rate Repo (VRR) auction." It did not say what in the conditions had changed. The RBI has made no other statement on the auction, and no bank has commented on the record.

## A week of going the other way

On the days just before, the RBI was removing money. On Wednesday, November 4, it took in ₹50,015 crore in an overnight variable rate reverse repo (VRRR) auction and another ₹1,180 crore in a second one, both at 5.49%. On Thursday the overnight auction drew only ₹7,767 crore of offers against ₹75,000 crore sought. On Friday a three-day auction took in ₹18,133 crore, against ₹25,000 crore on offer.

Those amounts fell as the week went on. Over the same days banks left between ₹1.37 lakh crore and ₹2.02 lakh crore a night in the RBI's standing deposit facility (SDF), which pays 5.25%.

## Surplus has shrunk but not gone

The RBI's money market tables show the banking system still held more cash than it needed. Net liquidity, counting all of the RBI's operations, was an **absorption of ₹1.55 lakh crore** on both Saturday and Sunday. On Sunday alone banks left ₹1,50,425 crore in the SDF overnight.

That is well under half the cushion of a month ago. On October 8 the surplus parked with the RBI was ₹3.88 lakh crore, as we reported when the central bank [tightened the daily cash reserve rule](/news/rbi-raises-daily-crr-minimum-to-99-per-cent). Banks' cash balances with the RBI were ₹8.46 lakh crore on November 7 and 8, against an average daily requirement of ₹8.17 lakh crore for the fortnight ending November 15. Overnight call money was trading at 5.20% on Saturday and 5.25% on Friday, below the repo rate, while the overall overnight segment averaged 5.57% on Saturday.

The four-day repo therefore does not mean the system has run dry. It means some banks wanted cash for several days at a price near the policy rate, and the RBI was willing to supply it at that price.

## How we got here

The Monetary Policy Committee [raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%](/news/rbi-raises-repo-rate-to-5-50) on October 7, its first increase since February 2023. Governor Sanjay Malhotra said the RBI would use a mix of liquidity tools to bring the call rate in line with the repo rate. [The daily CRR floor went to 99%](/news/daily-crr-99-per-cent-rule-takes-effect) from October 16, and the RBI sold government bonds and ran reverse repo auctions to drain cash.

## Why it matters

Rates on loans and deposits follow the cost of money in this overnight and short-term market. When the RBI supplies cash at 5.51% instead of absorbing it at 5.49%, it can be read as wanting short-term rates to sit at or just above the repo rate, which is what the October hike was meant to achieve. The RBI has not said so. The call rate had averaged about 14 basis points below the policy rate since August, which blunted the hike. Several state-run banks have already passed on the 25 basis point rise in their repo-linked lending rates.

## What happens next

The money returns to the RBI on **Friday, November 13**. The current reporting fortnight ends on November 15. The next RBI operations, whether another repo or a reverse repo, are announced a day or so ahead on the RBI's website. The Monetary Policy Committee next meets from December 2 to 4.
