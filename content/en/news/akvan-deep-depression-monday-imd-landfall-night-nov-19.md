The India Meteorological Department (IMD) now expects Cyclonic Storm Akvan to become a **severe cyclonic storm tonight**, November 17, and a very severe cyclonic storm by the evening of Thursday, November 19, before it crosses the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coast **around noon on Friday, November 20**, between Nellore and Tuni. At landfall the winds are forecast at 120 to 130 km/h, gusting to 145 km/h. The department warns of **extremely heavy rain of 21 cm or more** at isolated places in north coastal Andhra Pradesh on November 19 and 20.

The earlier outlook had the very severe stage arriving by the morning of November 19, and some reports had put landfall itself on the night of November 19. The IMD's track has not moved: the crossing is around noon on November 20. Akvan, a name contributed by Iran, is the third storm of the season to head for the Andhra coast.

## What the IMD bulletin says

In its latest national bulletin the IMD placed the storm near 12.2°N, 85.7°E, about **670 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam**, 610 km east of Chennai and 790 km south of Gopalpur in Odisha, moving north-northwest. After landfall it expects the system to weaken to a severe cyclonic storm by the evening of November 20, a cyclonic storm by the morning of November 21 and a depression by that evening.

For the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts the bulletin forecasts winds of 120 to 130 km/h from the night of November 19 until landfall, with a sea state that turns phenomenal, waves above 14 metres, from the evening of November 19. A **storm surge of 1 to 2 metres above the tide** is likely to flood low-lying areas of Prakasam, Bapatla, Krishna, West Godavari, Konaseema and Kakinada districts, with the peak over Bapatla, Krishna and West Godavari from the morning to the afternoon of November 20.

## Rain warnings and districts on alert

Beyond the coast, the IMD expects heavy to very heavy rain at isolated places in Odisha from November 19 to 21, in north Tamil Nadu, Puducherry and Karaikal on November 18 and 19, and in eastern Telangana on November 20. Heavy rain is forecast at isolated places in Rayalaseema on November 19 and 20, and in Chhattisgarh, Gangetic West Bengal, Jharkhand, Vidarbha and east Madhya Pradesh between November 19 and 21.

For November 20, **five Andhra districts are under an orange alert**: Alluri Sitharama Raju, Kakinada, Parvathipuram Manyam, Srikakulam and Vizianagaram. Many others, including Visakhapatnam, Nellore and Tirupati, are under yellow alert. The Andhra Pradesh State Disaster Management Authority (APSDMA) expects heavy to extremely heavy rain along the coast on Thursday and Friday.

The bulletin lists what the winds and surge can do on the Andhra coast: **major damage to thatched and kutcha houses**, power and communication poles down, trees uprooted, heavy losses to crops, plantations and orchards, and road, rail and air disruption with escape routes flooded. It advises coastal hutment dwellers to move to safer places, everyone else in affected areas to stay indoors, and fishermen to keep off the sea.

## Andhra orders continuous warnings

Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu held a teleconference with officials on Tuesday and said there was **no need to panic**, but that the public must not be adversely affected. He ordered that warnings reach people continuously, including directly through the ward and village secretariat staff, and that fishermen be stopped from going to sea. He told officials to clear fallen trees and electricity poles at once, restore power and roads, make real-time assessments, prepare rescue and rehabilitation, and prevent breaches in tanks and canals.

District collectors have followed. In Krishna, Collector D.K. Balaji told departments to stay on high alert and coordinate closely so that lives and property are protected. Helplines are 112 nationally, 1070 and 1800 425 0101 for the APSDMA, and the Mana Mitra WhatsApp service on 9552300009.

## Odisha begins evacuations

In Odisha, **evacuation of landslide-prone areas began on Tuesday morning** in Ganjam, Gajapati, Kandhamal, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri, and collectors have been told to open relief centres. The state is deploying 74 rescue teams, 60 from the Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force and 14 from the National Disaster Response Force, with Puri also in focus depending on need. In Ganjam, 107 pregnant women have already been moved to safety.

Revenue and Disaster Management Minister Suresh Pujari reviewed the preparations and said the government had made "full arrangements to secure lives and property". Water has been released from dams on the Rushikulya, Nagabali, Bahuda and Vansadhara rivers to create room for the rain. Ganjam Collector V. Keerthi Vasan told officers to "not take any chances", to prepare for rainfall like that of Cyclone Arnab, and to keep three to four days of food and drinking water at every shelter.

The Bhubaneswar Meteorological Centre has issued **orange and yellow rain warnings** for Odisha districts between November 18 and 21, but no red warning. Orange covers 12 to 20 cm of rain. IMD Director General Mrutunjay Mohapatra said Odisha would get heavy rain but not extremely heavy rain like that of Arnab. Fishermen are told not to go to sea from November 18 to 20, and the IMD expects squally winds of 50 to 60 km/h, gusting to 70 km/h, on the Odisha coast from the morning of November 19.

## How we got here

The IMD first flagged the system on Friday, November 13, as a low that would become a cyclonic storm by Monday. It became a depression on Sunday and Cyclonic Storm Akvan at 5:30 pm on Monday, as our [explainer on the storm surge and warnings](/en/explainer/akvan-very-severe-cyclone-explained-storm-surge-landfall-warnings) sets out.

Cyclone Arnab struck the Andhra coast on October 28, and an official statement said at least 1.8 lakh people were evacuated; in Odisha it killed 11 people. Cyclone Murasu crossed between Nellore and Narasapur on November 3 as a severe cyclonic storm, and Andhra's damage estimate for it has since been [revised to ₹2,484 crore](/en/news/murasu-andhra-damage-revised-2484-crore-central-team-naidu). The two storms are reported to have killed 15 people between them.

## Why it matters

Akvan would be the **third cyclone to reach the Andhra coast in about three weeks**, and the landfall zone takes in Nellore, where Murasu crossed, and the Godavari delta. Districts still repairing roads, power lines and fields have under three days before the winds build. A storm of 120 to 130 km/h brings down trees and lines and damages kutcha houses, and a surge of up to 2 metres puts low-lying delta villages at risk of flooding. Roughly 400 km of coast lies in the landfall zone, so residents from Nellore to Kakinada, and in Yanam, are all within the area being prepared.

## What happens next

The first marker is **tonight**, when the IMD expects the severe cyclonic storm stage. Seas turn phenomenal from the evening of November 19, the very severe stage is due the same evening, and landfall is around noon on November 20. The IMD's bulletins through Wednesday and Thursday are the ones in which the landfall zone could be narrowed and the warnings raised.
