The India Meteorological Department (IMD) said in its early Wednesday bulletin, November 18, that Cyclone Akvan was **620 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam**, about 590 km east of Chennai and 620 km southeast of Machilipatnam, still moving only about 3 km/h. The storm is expected to become a **very severe cyclonic storm by Thursday**, November 19, and to cross the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts between Nellore and Tuni around noon on Friday, November 20, with winds of 120 to 130 km/h and gusts to 145 km/h. The orange cyclone warning for the Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts stays in force. Akvan, a name contributed by Iran, is the third storm of the season to head for the Andhra coast.

The bulletin also changed the picture for Odisha. After landfall the system is now expected to move north-northwest and **cross into the south of Odisha through the Malkangiri region**, a change from the earlier forecast that it would weaken into a deep depression before reaching the state. Malkangiri, Koraput, Nabarangpur and Nuapada are on alert. "By tomorrow (November 19) morning, it is likely to turn into a very severe cyclonic storm," said Manorama Mohanty, director of the IMD's Regional Meteorological Centre in Bhubaneswar.

Reports on Tuesday night said Akvan had already reached the severe stage. The Tuesday 11:30 am bulletin had put it 660 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam, so the storm has closed about 40 km in some 20 hours. The Tuesday reports also said the crossing could fall on the afternoon of November 20 between Nellore and Visakhapatnam; the IMD has not formally narrowed the 400 km stretch from Nellore to Tuni, and Andhra Pradesh's own advisories say the precise landfall location remains uncertain.

## What the outlook says

The very severe stage is due by the morning of Thursday, November 19, and landfall around noon the next day. Coastal winds of 60 to 70 km/h are forecast from Wednesday evening, and sea waves could exceed 14 metres. The IMD also advises a **total suspension of fishing operations** in the affected sea areas, where it expects very rough to phenomenal seas as the winds build. Reports of the outlook add that **storm surge of 1 to 2 metres above the tide** could flood low-lying areas of Prakasam, Bapatla, Krishna, West Godavari, Konaseema and Kakinada districts.

Heavy to very heavy rain is forecast along the Andhra coast on November 19 and 20, with isolated extremely heavy falls of 21 cm or more in the north. The IMD has moved the Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts from a yellow **pre-cyclone watch** to an **orange cyclone warning**. It adds that heavy to extremely heavy rain can fall over Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, coastal Andhra Pradesh and Odisha from November 17 to 21.

## Odisha starts early evacuations

Odisha's government has identified **1,902 vulnerable locations in 87 blocks of eight districts** and plans to move more than 2 lakh residents if conditions worsen, more than 1.21 lakh of them in Ganjam. Of 3,329 cyclone shelters listed, four were open on Tuesday and housed 276 people. Of 2,573 pregnant women in risk zones, 92 had been moved to safer places by Tuesday; the figure in Ganjam alone was 107 in the earlier count. The state has released ₹6.5 crore in advance aid: ₹5 crore for Ganjam, ₹1 crore for Gajapati and ₹50 lakh for Malkangiri.

Odisha has deployed **74 rescue teams**, 60 of the Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force and 14 of the National Disaster Response Force, concentrated in Ganjam, Gajapati, Kandhamal, Rayagada, Koraput, Malkangiri and Puri. Disaster Management Minister Suresh Pujari said the government had made "full arrangements to secure lives and property". Water has been released from dams on the Rushikulya, Nagabali, Bahuda and Vansadhara rivers to make room for the rain, and fishermen are barred from the sea from November 18 to 20. The IMD does not expect rain as extreme as Cyclone Arnab brought on October 28, and no red warning has been issued for Odisha. Arnab killed 11 people there, six are still missing, and most deaths were from landslides in Ganjam and Kandhamal. District administrations in the south have been told to begin **early evacuations from vulnerable areas**. All 30 districts are on alert, round-the-clock control rooms are running, and the National Disaster Response Force, the Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force and fire services are on standby. Farmers have been urged to harvest standing crops before the rain of November 18 and 19, and fishermen were told to be back on shore by Monday.

Ganjam Collector V. Keerthi Vasan said landslide-prone and low-lying spots have been identified and that personnel and machinery are ready. On Monday he ordered the evacuation of people from hilly and landslide-prone areas to start on **Tuesday morning**, and 107 pregnant women in Ganjam have already been moved to safer places. He told officers: "If required, people will be taken to safe locations and cyclone shelters." He asked them to check drinking water, food, lighting and toilets at every shelter. The IMD's Odisha outlook is for light to moderate rain from the evening of November 17, heavy rain on November 18, heavy to very heavy rain from November 19 to 21, and **thundersqualls of 50 to 60 km/h** on November 19 and 20. Orange warnings are out for Puri, Ganjam and Gajapati on November 19.


## Tamil Nadu, Bengal and the fishing ban

In Tamil Nadu, the Regional Meteorological Centre in Chennai forecasts **heavy rain with thunderstorms in Chennai, Tiruvallur and Chengalpattu from November 17**, with gusts of 40 to 50 km/h. From November 18 heavy rain is expected in Kancheepuram, Ranipet, Tiruvannamalai, Villupuram, Cuddalore, Mayiladuthurai and Puducherry. Fishermen in Tamil Nadu and Puducherry are advised not to go to sea between November 16 and 21. In West Bengal, heavy rain is forecast in coastal South 24 Parganas and Purba Medinipur from November 19 to 21.

In Andhra Pradesh the State Disaster Management Authority has told fishermen not to go to sea and expects squally winds of 40 to 60 km/h along the coast from Monday evening. The IMD asks fishermen to keep away from the southwest, central and northwest Bay until November 21, and port, shipping and offshore operators to review their preparedness. Helplines are 112 nationally and 1070 and 1800 425 0101 in Andhra Pradesh.

## How we got here

The IMD first flagged the system on Friday, November 13, as a low that would become a cyclonic storm by Monday. Over the weekend it raised the outlook to a severe and then a very severe storm, as our [explainer on the storm surge and warnings](/en/explainer/akvan-very-severe-cyclone-explained-storm-surge-landfall-warnings) sets out.

Cyclone Arnab struck the Andhra coast in late October, and an official statement said at least 1.8 lakh people were evacuated. Cyclone Murasu crossed between Nellore and Narasapur on November 3 as a severe cyclonic storm, and Andhra's damage estimate for it has since been [revised to ₹2,484 crore](/en/news/murasu-andhra-damage-revised-2484-crore-central-team-naidu). The two storms are reported to have killed 15 people between them.

## Why it matters

Akvan would be the **third cyclone to reach the Andhra coast in about three weeks**, and the landfall zone takes in Nellore, the district where Murasu crossed, as well as the Godavari delta. Coastal districts still repairing roads, power lines and fields have a few days between the first rain on Tuesday and landfall on Friday. A storm with winds of 120 to 130 km/h brings down trees and power lines and damages kutcha houses, and a surge of up to 1.5 metres puts low-lying villages in the delta at risk of flooding.

Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu held a review meeting on Tuesday morning and told officials to keep fishermen ashore. Danger signal No. 2 is up at several ports, SDRF and NDRF teams are deploying, pregnant women are being moved to hospitals and rail, road and air services may be regulated. Coastal districts between Nellore and Tuni, the most exposed, are preparing to move people from vulnerable areas before landfall. The landfall stretch is wide, and the IMD has not formally narrowed it beyond the reports of the 11:30 am bulletin. Residents from Nellore to Kakinada, and in Yanam, are therefore all within the area for which preparations are being made.

## What happens next

The first marker is **Thursday morning**, November 19, when the IMD expects Akvan to reach the very severe stage. Landfall is due around noon on Friday, November 20, and Odisha's orange warnings run from November 19 to 21, with extremely heavy rain forecast for Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri on November 20 and 21. The IMD's bulletins through Thursday are the ones in which the landfall stretch and hour could be fixed.
