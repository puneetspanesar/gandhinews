The India Meteorological Department (IMD) now expects Cyclone Akvan to cross the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts **between Nellore and Tuni around noon on Friday**, November 20, as a very severe cyclonic storm with winds of 120 to 130 km/h gusting to 145 km/h. The stretch is about 400 km of coast. It is the first time the department has named a landfall zone and an hour for the system, which was still a deep depression over the Bay of Bengal on Monday, November 16.

At 8:30 am the system was centred near 11.2°N, 86.4°E, **about 800 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam**, 700 km east-southeast of Chennai and 910 km south of Gopalpur in Odisha. It was moving north-northwest at about 6 km/h. The IMD expects it to become a cyclonic storm this evening, when it will be named Akvan, a name contributed by Iran, and a severe cyclonic storm on the evening of Tuesday, November 17.

## What the outlook says

The very severe stage is due near the Andhra coast around the night of Thursday, November 19, which is the timing the department gave in its 5:30 am bulletin. The newer outlook adds the coast and the hour: landfall around noon the next day. Reports of the outlook add that **storm surge of 1 to 1.5 metres above the tide** could flood low-lying areas of Prakasam, Bapatla, Krishna, West Godavari, Konaseema and Kakinada districts.

Heavy to very heavy rain is forecast along the Andhra coast on November 19 and 20, with isolated extremely heavy falls of 21 cm or more in the north. The Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts are under a **pre-cyclone watch**, the first of the IMD's four warning stages, issued as a yellow message.

## Odisha starts early evacuations

In Odisha, district administrations in the south have been told to begin **early evacuations from vulnerable areas**. All 30 districts are on alert, round-the-clock control rooms are running, and the National Disaster Response Force, the Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force and fire services are on standby. Farmers have been urged to harvest standing crops before the rain of November 18 and 19, and fishermen were told to be back on shore by Monday.

Ganjam Collector V. Keerthi Vasan said landslide-prone and low-lying spots have been identified and that personnel and machinery are ready. He told officers: "If required, people will be taken to safe locations and cyclone shelters." He asked them to check drinking water, food, lighting and toilets at every shelter. The IMD's Odisha outlook is for light to moderate rain from the evening of November 17, heavy rain on November 18, heavy to very heavy rain from November 19 to 21, and **thundersqualls of 50 to 60 km/h** on November 19 and 20. Orange warnings are out for Puri, Ganjam and Gajapati on November 19.

## Tamil Nadu, Bengal and the fishing ban

In Tamil Nadu, the Regional Meteorological Centre in Chennai forecasts **heavy rain with thunderstorms in Chennai, Tiruvallur and Chengalpattu from November 17**, with gusts of 40 to 50 km/h. From November 18 heavy rain is expected in Kancheepuram, Ranipet, Tiruvannamalai, Villupuram, Cuddalore, Mayiladuthurai and Puducherry. Fishermen in Tamil Nadu and Puducherry are advised not to go to sea between November 16 and 21. In West Bengal, heavy rain is forecast in coastal South 24 Parganas and Purba Medinipur from November 19 to 21.

In Andhra Pradesh the State Disaster Management Authority has told fishermen not to go to sea and expects squally winds of 40 to 60 km/h along the coast from Monday evening. The IMD asks fishermen to keep away from the southwest, central and northwest Bay until November 21, and port, shipping and offshore operators to review their preparedness. Helplines are 112 nationally and 1070 and 1800 425 0101 in Andhra Pradesh.

## How we got here

The IMD first flagged the system on Friday, November 13, as a low that would become a cyclonic storm by Monday. Over the weekend it raised the outlook to a severe and then a very severe storm, as our [explainer on the storm surge and warnings](/en/explainer/akvan-very-severe-cyclone-explained-storm-surge-landfall-warnings) sets out.

Cyclone Arnab struck the Andhra coast in late October, and an official statement said at least 1.8 lakh people were evacuated. Cyclone Murasu crossed between Nellore and Narasapur on November 3 as a severe cyclonic storm, and Andhra's damage estimate for it has since been [revised to ₹2,484 crore](/en/news/murasu-andhra-damage-revised-2484-crore-central-team-naidu). The two storms are reported to have killed 15 people between them.

## Why it matters

Akvan would be the **third cyclone to reach the Andhra coast in about three weeks**, and the landfall zone takes in Nellore, the district where Murasu crossed, as well as the Godavari delta. Coastal districts still repairing roads, power lines and fields have a few days between the first rain on Tuesday and landfall on Friday. A storm with winds of 120 to 130 km/h brings down trees and power lines and damages kutcha houses, and a surge of up to 1.5 metres puts low-lying villages in the delta at risk of flooding.

A 400 km landfall zone is wide, and the IMD has not narrowed it. Residents from Nellore to Kakinada, and in Yanam, are therefore all within the area for which preparations are being made. The department says the track will become clearer as the system develops over the Bay.

## What happens next

The first marker is **this evening**, when the IMD expects the deep depression to become a cyclonic storm and take the name Akvan. The severe stage is due on the evening of Tuesday, November 17, the very severe stage around the night of Thursday, November 19, and landfall around noon on Friday, November 20. The IMD's next bulletins, through Monday night and Tuesday, are the ones in which the landfall zone could be narrowed and the pre-cyclone watch raised to a cyclone alert.
