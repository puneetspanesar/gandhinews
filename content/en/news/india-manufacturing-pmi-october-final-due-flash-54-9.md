India's final **purchasing managers' index for factories** in October is due later on Monday morning, the final word on how the country's manufacturers fared in a month of costly oil and war in West Asia.

The flash estimate, released on October 23, put the HSBC India Manufacturing PMI at **54.9, a shade below September's 55.1**. Any reading above 50 means activity grew from the month before. The output index edged up to 57.6 from 57.5.

Flash numbers can move. September's figure was first put at 55.7 and later **revised down to 55.1** in the final survey, so Monday's number could differ from the early estimate.

The flash survey had good news on exports. "The steady pace of new order growth masks the **fastest increase in new export orders** since June 2025," said Pranjul Bhandari, chief India economist at HSBC. Firms reported demand from the US, the UK, West Asia and Bangladesh.

Hiring was the weak spot. Factory job creation slowed, and across the private sector employment grew at its weakest pace of the year, with companies saying their staff were enough to handle current work. Services firms kept headcount unchanged, ending a nine-month run of hiring.

Prices have cooled. Input cost inflation **eased for a seventh straight month** to its lowest in almost a year, even as firms mentioned dearer steel and transport. Factory selling prices rose at their softest pace in almost four years, a welcome sign for the Reserve Bank.

The flash composite index, which combines factories and services, rose to 56.7 from 55.9, its fastest growth since June. The final services PMI is due on Wednesday, November 4.
