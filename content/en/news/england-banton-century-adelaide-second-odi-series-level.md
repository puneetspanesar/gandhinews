England made **355 for 6** and then bowled Australia out for 220 in 44.3 overs to win the second one-day international at Adelaide Oval on Sunday, November 15, 2026, by **135 runs**. The result levels the three-match series at 1-1 and sets up a winner-takes-all third ODI in Hobart on Wednesday, November 18.

Opener **Tom Banton scored 121 off 99 balls**, with 12 fours and four sixes, for his second ODI century. Pace bowler **Gus Atkinson took 4 for 28**, a career best according to reports, and tore through Australia's top order after Mitchell Marsh sent England in to bat.

## Banton and Root build the platform

England lost Ben Duckett early, with 19 on the board, and then settled. Banton and Joe Root **added 137 for the second wicket**, which according to reports is an England record for that wicket in Australia. Root made 62 off 68 balls before he top-edged a ball from Cooper Connolly to cover. Banton's innings went on after he was struck on the grille of his helmet by a bouncer from Riley Meredith; he carried on batting.

Jordan Cox then hit **67 off 49 balls** and Will Jacks finished unbeaten on 54 off 40. England's last ten overs brought 110 runs, and the final over, bowled by Jack Edwards, cost 18. Xavier Bartlett was the best of the Australian bowlers with 3 for 72. England were not flawless: they put down chances, including Matt Renshaw on 27 and Cameron Green on 7.

According to reports, the total is England's highest in an ODI in Australia and the first time the hosts have conceded 350 or more at home in the format. England captain Harry Brook, who made only 5 himself, said the batting depth had made the difference. "The whole innings we looked like we were in control," he said, adding that the side "were able to go on and get 350".

## Atkinson ends the chase early

Australia needed 356 and were never in the game. Atkinson's opening burst removed Travis Head, caught at first slip by Root, then Connolly, caught by Brook at second slip, and Marsh, caught by Root diving to his left. **Three wickets fell for no runs in six deliveries**, and the hosts were 13 for 3. Josh Inglis then fell to a catch by Cox off Atkinson and Australia were **29 for 4 after seven overs**. Sonny Baker also took an early wicket.

Renshaw and Green, who made 63 and 64, steadied things with a fifth-wicket stand of 98, but it only delayed the end. Liam Dawson broke it by trapping Renshaw lbw as he tried a reverse sweep, and Sam Curran then took two wickets in successive deliveries, Edwards and Bartlett. Atkinson came back to dismiss Adam Zampa, and Jofra Archer bowled Spencer Johnson with a yorker to finish the match.

Marsh, who made 2, said afterwards that England had shown how complete a side they are. "Any time you are chasing 350 you are right under the pump from ball one," he said. Australia had rested Mahli Beardman, who took 6 for 24 in the first match at Perth.

## How the series got here

Australia won the opening ODI in Perth by seven wickets after bowling England out for 131, with Beardman taking six wickets. Our earlier report on [the squads for the Australia white-ball tour](/en/news/england-name-squads-australia-white-ball-tour-rew-bethell) has the names. After Adelaide it is 1-1 with one ODI to play.

## What happens next

The third ODI is at Bellerive Oval in Hobart on Wednesday, November 18, and the winner takes the series. Both sides will have to decide how to handle a short turnaround: England's bowlers, Atkinson, Archer, Curran and Dawson among them, took all ten wickets at Adelaide, while Australia's top order lost four wickets for 29.

The tour then moves to a five-match T20 series, starting with the first T20I at the Melbourne Cricket Ground on Saturday, November 21, followed by matches at Carrara on November 24, Brisbane on November 27, Sydney on November 29 and Canberra on December 2.
