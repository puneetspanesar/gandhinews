Indian shares go into Monday with something they have lacked for most of the autumn: momentum. The Sensex gained **1.83% last week to close at 74,459.53**, and the Nifty 50 finished Friday at 23,053.10, back above the 23,000 mark. Foreign investors were net buyers over the week, and domestic institutions added ₹3,109.79 crore.

The coming week will test that rebound from three directions at once: a heavy run of September-quarter results, Tuesday's midterm elections in the United States, and the stand-off between Washington and Tehran that has kept **Brent crude above $117 a barrel**.

## A crowded results calendar

ITC and Wockhardt open the week with results on Monday. **State Bank of India reports on Tuesday**, alongside the National Stock Exchange and Solar Industries. BSE, Zydus Lifesciences and Premier Energies follow on Wednesday.

## Washington and Tehran

Americans vote on Tuesday for control of Congress, and markets around the world will trade the outcome on Wednesday. In the same week, the US-Iran stand-off hangs over oil. President Donald Trump has said further strikes on Iran's nuclear sites could be done "in 10 minutes", and has suggested an attack could come after the elections. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has said Tehran is "prepared for all scenarios" while talks continue through Qatari mediators. The US ISM manufacturing survey, due on Monday, is the first big data point of the week.

For India, which imports most of its oil, every dollar on Brent matters. The rupee ended last week at **94.94 to the dollar**.

## What analysts expect

"Indian equities are expected to remain range-bound with bouts of volatility next week," said Siddhartha Khemka, head of research for wealth management at Motilal Oswal Financial Services.
