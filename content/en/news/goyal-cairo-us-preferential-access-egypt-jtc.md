Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal said in Cairo on Sunday that the United States has agreed that **Indian goods will get preferential access** to its market, with lower duties than those paid by competing exporters. "India will get preferential trade access in the US with lower duty than our competitor countries," he told reporters on the first day of a two-day visit to Egypt.

Goyal said the point needed no fresh negotiation. "This was already agreed between the leaders," he said, referring to President Donald Trump and Prime Minister Narendra Modi, who announced a broad framework for deeper trade ties a few months ago. He gave no tariff rate, no list of products and no date for a signing, and Washington has not published any figures of its own.

## What the minister said, and what he did not

The remark is the most specific public claim yet from the Indian government on what a trade agreement would give exporters. Until now the line from New Delhi has been that India wants a final rate comparable with competitors such as Vietnam. Goyal put it as a **comparative advantage over rivals**, which is what the senior official who spoke days earlier had also described, saying the US had assured India of a comparative tariff advantage.

That official added a caution that Goyal's remarks did not remove: the size of the advantage depends on how the American tariff framework finally takes shape. "We will have to see how the tariff framework unfolds," the official said. India's stated aim is to close the first tranche of the agreement by the end of the current financial year, that is, by March 31, 2027.


## Why the tariff structure is still open

The framework for the first phase was finalised in February. Since then the ground has shifted on the American side: the US Supreme Court struck down the reciprocal tariffs that had formed the basis of the earlier talks, and Washington is now using other provisions of its trade law to build a new tariff structure. That is why signing, once expected sooner, has been delayed while the two sides keep talking.

The history explains why Indian exporters watch the number so closely. Washington imposed tariffs of **up to 50% on most Indian goods** in August 2025 after talks broke down, and the February framework brought the rate down to 18%, as our report on the [Rubio-Jaishankar meeting in Mumbai](/en/news/rubio-jaishankar-mumbai-talks-close-to-trade-deal) recorded. Progress then stalled, and Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said early in October that the talks had plateaued, as we reported in the [India-US trade talks plateau](/en/news/india-us-trade-talks-plateau-russia-oil-tariff-law) story.

The recent run of statements has come quickly. On October 17 Trump [waived the Russia sanctions law](/en/news/trump-waives-russia-sanctions-law-india-tariff-threat), lifting a threat of 100% tariffs on buyers of Russian oil. Three days later Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the two countries were "about as close as you can be without having executed the agreement". On Saturday, External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar said the deal was in its [last stretch](/en/news/jaishankar-htls-india-us-trade-deal-last-stretch-national-interest) and that the remaining changes were "more at the American end than our end". Goyal's remark a day later adds a claim about what the finished deal will contain.

## Why it matters to exporters

The US is India's largest export market, and the duty paid at the American border decides how Indian goods price against those of rivals such as Vietnam, the comparison New Delhi has itself drawn. A lower rate than rivals is the point on which the government has now spoken most plainly. Goods exports hit a [record $49.47 billion in October](/en/news/india-october-exports-record-iitf-goyal-1-trillion-target), and Goyal said at the trade fair that India is on course for $1 trillion in exports this financial year.

Until the rates are published, the claim cannot be tested, and no American statement matching Goyal's description had been found by the evening of November 15.

## The Egypt visit

Goyal is in Egypt on November 15 and 16 with a delegation of more than 130 business leaders and start-ups. On Sunday he was due to co-chair the 7th India-Egypt Joint Trade Committee in Cairo with Egypt's Minister of Investment and Foreign Trade, Mohamed Farid Saleh. The committee has been raised to ministerial level.

India sells Egypt meat and poultry products, petroleum products, organic chemicals, cotton and machinery, and buys crude oil, fertilisers, cotton and mineral products. The agenda covers market access, non-tariff barriers and trade remedies, with interest in agriculture and food, pharmaceuticals, engineering goods, chemicals, fertilisers, textiles, renewable energy, green hydrogen, ports and logistics. The Suez Canal Economic Zone is a named area of cooperation.

Goyal said the two countries have complementary strengths and little direct competition, and that "we can be each other's support". He said several Egyptian business houses were interested in investing in India, particularly in education and food processing, and called Egypt a gateway to Africa: "Egypt will become the gateway to Africa." He said several announcements were expected during the visit.


## What happens next

On Monday, November 16, Goyal's schedule includes the India-Egypt Business Forum, the Start-up Forum and meetings with senior Egyptian ministers, along with engagement with the African Export-Import Bank. On the American side, the date to watch is the G20 summit in Miami on December 14 and 15, where Modi and Trump may meet, a trip the Ministry of External Affairs had not confirmed when we last reported on it. Until the US publishes the duties that apply to Indian goods, Goyal's statement is the Indian government's account of an understanding, not a published tariff schedule.
