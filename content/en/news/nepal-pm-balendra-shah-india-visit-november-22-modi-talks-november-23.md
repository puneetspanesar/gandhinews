Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah begins Monday, November 23, with a **ceremonial welcome at Rashtrapati Bhavan**, a wreath at Rajghat and a courtesy call on President Droupadi Murmu, before bilateral talks with Prime Minister Narendra Modi, according to the programme announced by the Ministry of External Affairs. As of 11:30 am IST no outcome of the talks, no joint statement and no list of agreements had been released. A number of documents are **expected to be exchanged**, and completed projects are to be inaugurated virtually, with foundation stones laid for new ones. The areas named are development partnership, energy, hydropower, railways, connectivity, climate change and disaster resilience, infrastructure and technology.

Shah reached Delhi on Sunday on his first visit to India since taking office in March, and Foreign Minister S Jaishankar called on him that evening. Jaishankar said in a post that India "cherishes the special friendship with Nepal." What follows is the earlier report of the arrival and the plans, kept as background.

Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah left Kathmandu on a Nepal Airlines flight at 1:45 pm on Sunday, November 22, and was due to **land in New Delhi at 3:45 pm** at the start of a two-day state visit. Minister of State for Commerce and Industry Jitin Prasada was to receive him at Indira Gandhi International Airport; unlike in Kathmandu, where he got no guard of honour, he was to be given state honours in Delhi, the Ministry of External Affairs has said. External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar was to **call on Shah at his hotel in the evening**, and talks with Prime Minister Narendra Modi follow on Monday, November 23.

At its briefing on Friday, November 20, the MEA called it a state visit. Spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said: "This is a state visit. He'll be offered all state honours." The ministry says Modi will hold bilateral talks with the Prime Minister of Nepal on November 23 and that Shah will call on **President Droupadi Murmu on Monday**, which settles the order of the two events that Jaiswal had left open. Shah is on his first visit to India since taking office in March.

The programme, as reported from the MEA announcement and Nepal's Foreign Ministry, also includes a lunch hosted by Modi, in some reports **floral tributes at Rajghat**, the Nepal-India Business Forum, the inauguration of energy projects and an exchange of agreements. The two leaders will address a **joint press conference** after the talks. Shah is travelling on a Nepal Airlines flight with Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle, Foreign Minister Shishir Khanal and Chief Adviser Kumar Byanjankar. Nepal Foreign Secretary Amrit Bahadur Rai said 34 government delegation members will go, and a separate 52-member business delegation is also travelling. Shah's only earlier foreign trip as Prime Minister was to the UN General Assembly in New York in September.

The MEA has said the talks will be wide. At an earlier briefing this month, Jaiswal said: "The entire range of bilateral relations that we have with Nepal will be discussed." He called it "a very special partnership". Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri, who carried Modi's invitation to Kathmandu on November 4 and 5, has said a detailed agenda is ready.

## What is planned

The visit was first reported as three days, November 22 to 24, and was cut back to two. Nepali sources have said Shah and Modi are set to hold a **30-minute one-on-one meeting**, followed by delegation-level talks. The MEA says the two leaders will review the full range of ties and exchange views on regional and international issues. Its listed agenda is development cooperation, people-to-people ties, energy and connectivity, and newer areas such as startups, technology, innovation and sports; Jaiswal singled out cricket as a growing link. The MEA places the visit in India's "Neighbourhood First" policy. India offered trips to Bengaluru or Ahmedabad; Shah is reported to have declined, keeping to the capital.

Nepal's Foreign Ministry says agreements will be signed and several projects launched. Reports of the MEA programme name the Lower Arun hydropower project and a petroleum pipeline from Siliguri to Charali in Jhapa among the energy items, and integrated check posts at Bhairahawa and Dodhara Chandani. The most visible is the **Ayodhya-Janakpur train**, a 604-km route promoted as part of the Ramayana circuit, which the two leaders are expected to inaugurate. A trial run was held on November 5. Other projects named are the Nepal Police Academy building at Panauti, a bridge in Darchula and the Integrated Check Post at Bhairahawa.

India is also expected to announce an economic package. Nepal has formally proposed about $2 billion, according to reports: $1 billion in credit lines, $500 million in grants and $500 million for reconstruction after the August 26 floods in the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi corridor, and India has said it will consider the reconstruction proposals. Other items on Nepal's wish list, which are not confirmed, include a cross-border LPG pipeline, religious and tourism circuits, branches of Indian institutes and help with cricket. Reconstruction after this year's floods is a major item: Shah thanked India on November 18 for help after the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi floods when Ambassador Sudhakar Dalela called on him in Singha Durbar. Dalela said the meeting with Modi would be "historic and significant" and that the agenda was final.

## Reaction in Nepal

The Nepali Congress, the main opposition party, said on Saturday, November 21, that it **backs the visit** and wishes Shah success, but faulted the government for not consulting parties first. Senior vice-president Bishwaprakash Sharma said the established democratic practice is to consult the main opposition before such trips. The party said it had prepared suggestions on cooperation but will not submit them unasked, and will judge the visit on results for Nepal's national interests rather than publicity.

## Air routes and power

Air connectivity looks like the most concrete deliverable. Nepal wants an entry route for Gautam Buddha International Airport at Bhairahawa; Mahendranagar, Janakpur and Nepalgunj have also been raised. India has asked for access to Nepal's eastern air route for flights from Bagdogra in West Bengal. The two sides are preparing an **agreement to exchange routes**, and technical talks have continued virtually.

On energy, Nepal wants to sell **more than 1,650 MW a year** to India and raise exports to Bangladesh through Indian territory from 40 MW. Nepal also wants quicker work on hydropower projects, including those involving NHPC, and help rebuilding plants damaged by floods, among them two half-century-old stations built with Indian assistance.

## The road to Delhi

Shah, 35, is a former rapper who was mayor of Kathmandu from May 2022 until he resigned in January 2026. After the September protests led by young Nepalis, in which 76 people were killed and the government fell, an interim government led by former Chief Justice Sushila Karki held an election on **March 5, 2026**. Shah's Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP) won 182 of 275 seats, against 38 for the Nepali Congress and 25 for the CPN (UML). President Ramchandra Paudel swore him in on March 27, and he has been described as Nepal's first Madhesi prime minister.

Until now he had declined diplomatic meetings citing protocol, and RSP leaders had said he would not travel abroad for at least a year. Ties with Delhi have carried a trust deficit: India points to the unilateral publication of a Nepali political map claiming Indian territory, and Nepali leaders say India overlooks their sensitivities. The last two Nepali prime ministerial visits to India listed by the MEA were April 1 to 3, 2022 and May 31 to June 3, 2023.

## What is unlikely to move

According to reports in Kathmandu, substantive progress is not expected on the **boundary dispute**, Nepal's widening trade deficit and transit arrangements, the recruitment of Nepali citizens into the Indian Army (suspended since 2020), or the stalled Pancheshwar Multipurpose Project, where water-sharing differences remain. On October 25 Nepal's foreign ministry told Parliament that encroachment exists along the border beyond Kalapani and Susta and that joint mapping continues; it gave no list.

The railway also has loose ends. The existing Janakpur-Jaynagar line is 51 km; the proposed Indian train would have 18 coaches against five on the Nepali side and run once a week at first. Agreement on security checks and on who bears operating and maintenance costs had not been reached, according to Nepali reports. Nepal Railway's Parajuli said: "Whoever operates the train will earn the revenue."

## What Monday holds

Nepali officials say the delegation-level talks with Modi will be followed by **memorandums of understanding on education, health, agriculture, sports and disaster management**, and by the inauguration of projects including the direct Janakpur-Ayodhya rail service. A joint vision for development and prosperity is to be released with a joint communiqué; its wording was still being negotiated on Sunday. Shah leaves Delhi on Monday evening, according to reports.

## Aid and air route, the open items

A senior Nepali foreign ministry official called the aid request and the air route the two main priorities. According to Nepali reports, New Delhi has responded **positively to Nepal's $2 billion proposal** of grants and lines of credit, and the two sides were in the final round of negotiation. Government sources in Delhi describe a "substantive" package of almost $2 billion, which would make it one of the largest India has given any country in its neighbourhood, though its final shape is not settled.

The air route is harder. India has proposed that its military aircraft be allowed to use the Bagdogra entry point; a Nepali official said: "We have made it clear that we can allow civilian flights, not military ones." The two sides also differ on altitude, with India proposing 39,000 feet and Nepal about 29,000 feet. Nepal also wants the L626 Kathmandu-Mahendranagar route made two-way, since it now lets international aircraft leave Nepal by the western border but not enter that way.

## Day by day

**Sunday, November 22:** Shah leaves Kathmandu at 1:45 pm (Mangsir 6 in the Nepali calendar), lands in Delhi at 3:45 pm, is received by Jitin Prasada and meets Jaishankar in the evening. **Monday, November 23:** he calls on President Murmu, attends the business forum and holds talks with Modi, followed by the exchange of agreements, project inaugurations and a joint press conference, and returns to Kathmandu in the evening. What is signed will be known only on the day.
