India and Nepal signed **eight memorandums of understanding** on Monday, November 23, after Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah held talks at Hyderabad House in New Delhi. Modi also announced a **Development Cooperation Package of 24,000 crore Nepali rupees** for Nepal's reconstruction after the August floods and for longer-term development, and six cross-border projects were inaugurated or had their foundation stones laid. The agreements cover space, policing, civil service training, health, traditional medicine, urban development and livestock and fisheries. It is Shah's first visit to India since he took office in March.

The Ministry of External Affairs had not put its list of outcomes or a joint statement within reach of our reading by 2:30 pm IST, and no transcript of Modi's remarks was available on the Prime Minister's pages. What follows rests on the MEA programme and spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal's posts as carried by All India Radio, and on agency reports of the signing and the announcements. Where a figure comes only from those reports, it is said so.

## What was signed

The eight agreements, as reported from the exchange at Hyderabad House:

- **Space:** ISRO and Nepal's Ministry of Information and Communication agreed a satellite communication network that uses the South Asia Satellite.
- **Forensics:** the National Forensic Sciences University in Gandhinagar and the Crime Investigation Department of Nepal Police agreed academic and research cooperation.
- **Police training:** Rashtriya Raksha University and Nepal's National Police Academy signed a second policing agreement.
- **Civil service:** the National Centre for Good Governance and the Nepal Administrative Staff College agreed a capacity-building programme for Nepali civil servants.
- **Health:** the two health ministries signed an MoU on cooperation in health and medicine.
- **Traditional medicine:** a government-to-government agreement on Ayurveda and other traditional systems.
- **Urban development:** technical cooperation on sustainable urban development.
- **Livestock and fisheries:** cooperation in livestock and fisheries development.

The list is narrower than the one Nepali officials described before the visit, which named education, sports, agriculture and disaster management. Education, sports and disaster management do not appear among the eight as reported.

## The package and the projects

Modi announced the **24,000 crore Nepali rupee package** after the talks. He expressed "deep condolences for the loss of lives caused by the severe floods in Nepal in August" and described the relationship as one of "close kinship and unbreakable trust", saying the money is meant to help affected families recover and to strengthen Nepal's ability to cope with future disasters. Nepal had asked for about $2 billion in credit lines and grants, including $500 million for rebuilding the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi corridor; the reports of the announcement do not say how the 24,000 crore is split or whether it is grant or credit, so we do not compare it with that request.

The projects, as reported:

- **Rail:** the Saryu-Kamala Express began service between Ayodhya and Janakpur, flagged off virtually.
- **Roads and border posts:** the motorable bridge linking Dharchula in India and Darchula in Nepal was inaugurated, a foundation stone was laid for an Integrated Check Post at Banbasa, and progress at the Bhairahawa, Sunauli and Dodhara Chandani check posts was reviewed.
- **Power:** the foundation stone of the **669 MW Lower Arun hydroelectric project** was laid. Nepal wants to sell more than 1,650 MW a year to India, so projects like this matter to the export plan.
- **Petroleum:** phase II of the Motihari-Amlekhgunj pipeline was dedicated and work began on a petroleum terminal at Jhapa, part of the proposed Siliguri-Jhapa pipeline.

## The ceremony and the talks

Shah was given a ceremonial guard of honour at Rashtrapati Bhavan with Modi present, laid a wreath at Rajghat and signed the visitors' book there, before the talks. Modi hosted a lunch for him and his 34-member delegation, which included Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle, Foreign Minister Shishir Khanal and Chief Adviser Kumar Byanjankar. Jaiswal said the welcome "reflected the special warmth and enduring friendship between India and Nepal" and that the leaders would "explore new avenues to further strengthen and deepen bilateral cooperation." A joint vision document titled "Samriddha Nepal, Samunnat Bharat" was expected to be released; we could not confirm its text.

## What the reports leave out

None of the reports of the day mention the **air route dispute**, the boundary, the trade deficit, transit or the recruitment of Nepali citizens into the Indian Army, which were the items our explainer on [what Shah and Modi can settle and what they cannot](/en/explainer/nepal-india-ties-explained-shah-modi-talks-border-power-trade) listed as unlikely to move. Silence in agency copy is not proof that they were not discussed; it means no outcome has been published. The two governments disagreed before the visit on the altitude of a new entry route (39,000 feet proposed by India against about 29,000 feet from Nepal) and on whether Indian military aircraft could use the Bagdogra entry point; Nepal's officials said it could allow civilian flights only. That remains the first thing to check when the MEA list appears.

## How we got here

Shah, 35, a former rapper and mayor of Kathmandu, became Prime Minister on March 27 after his Rastriya Swatantra Party won 182 of 275 seats on March 5. Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri took Modi's invitation to Kathmandu on November 4 and 5, and Ambassador Sudhakar Dalela discussed the visit with Shah on November 18. Shah flew in on Sunday, November 22, and Foreign Minister S Jaishankar called on him that evening. On October 25 Nepal told its Parliament that encroachment exists along the border beyond Kalapani and Susta, and joint mapping continues.

## Reaction

The Nepali Congress, the main opposition, said on November 21 that it backs the visit but will judge it by results for Nepal's national interests, and faulted the government for not consulting parties first. No statement from Nepal's government or opposition on the outcome had been published when this was written.

## What happens next

Reports said Shah would return to Kathmandu on Monday evening. The MEA's list of outcomes and any joint statement should settle the exact wording of the package, the grant and credit split and the status of the air route. This page will be updated when the primary documents are out.
