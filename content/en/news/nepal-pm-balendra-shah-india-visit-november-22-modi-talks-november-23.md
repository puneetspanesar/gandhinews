Nepal's Prime Minister **Balendra Shah will fly to New Delhi on November 22** for his first bilateral trip since taking office, and will hold talks with Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday, November 23, according to Nepal's Foreign Ministry sources. The stay is expected to last about 30 hours, including travel, and Shah is due back in Kathmandu the same day the talks end.

India's Ministry of External Affairs has said the talks will be wide. At a briefing this month, spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said: "The entire range of bilateral relations that we have with Nepal will be discussed." He called it "a very special partnership". Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri, who carried Modi's invitation to Kathmandu on November 4 and 5, has said a detailed agenda is ready.

## What is planned

The visit was first reported as three days, November 22 to 24, and was cut back. Shah and Modi are set to hold a **30-minute one-on-one meeting**, followed by delegation-level talks. Shah will also address a gathering of Nepali and Indian business representatives in the morning. India offered trips to Bengaluru or Ahmedabad; Shah is reported to have declined, keeping to the capital.

Nepal's Foreign Ministry says agreements will be signed and several projects launched. The most visible is the **Ayodhya-Janakpur train**, a 604-km route promoted as part of the Ramayana circuit, which the two leaders are expected to inaugurate. A trial run was held on November 5. Other projects named are the Nepal Police Academy building at Panauti, a bridge in Darchula and the Integrated Check Post at Bhairahawa.

India is also expected to announce an economic package. According to reports in Kathmandu, Nepal's wish list, which is not confirmed, runs to about $2 billion in grants and credit lines, a cross-border LPG pipeline, religious and tourism circuits, branches of Indian institutes and help with cricket. Reconstruction after this year's floods is a major item: Shah thanked India on November 18 for help after the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi floods when Ambassador Sudhakar Dalela called on him in Singha Durbar. Dalela said the meeting with Modi would be "historic and significant" and that the agenda was final.

## Air routes and power

Air connectivity looks like the most concrete deliverable. Nepal wants an entry route for Gautam Buddha International Airport at Bhairahawa; Mahendranagar, Janakpur and Nepalgunj have also been raised. India has asked for access to Nepal's eastern air route for flights from Bagdogra in West Bengal. The two sides are preparing an **agreement to exchange routes**, and technical talks have continued virtually.

On energy, Nepal wants to sell **more than 1,650 MW a year** to India and raise exports to Bangladesh through Indian territory from 40 MW. Nepal also wants quicker work on hydropower projects, including those involving NHPC, and help rebuilding plants damaged by floods, among them two half-century-old stations built with Indian assistance.

## The road to Delhi

Shah, 35, is a former rapper who was mayor of Kathmandu from May 2022 until he resigned in January 2026. After the September protests led by young Nepalis, in which 76 people were killed and the government fell, an interim government led by former Chief Justice Sushila Karki held an election on **March 5, 2026**. Shah's Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP) won 182 of 275 seats, against 38 for the Nepali Congress and 25 for the CPN (UML). President Ramchandra Paudel swore him in on March 27, and he has been described as Nepal's first Madhesi prime minister.

Until now he had declined diplomatic meetings citing protocol, and RSP leaders had said he would not travel abroad for at least a year. Ties with Delhi have carried a trust deficit: India points to the unilateral publication of a Nepali political map claiming Indian territory, and Nepali leaders say India overlooks their sensitivities. The last two Nepali prime ministerial visits to India listed by the MEA were April 1 to 3, 2022 and May 31 to June 3, 2023.

## What is unlikely to move

According to reports in Kathmandu, substantive progress is not expected on the **boundary dispute**, Nepal's widening trade deficit and transit arrangements, the recruitment of Nepali citizens into the Indian Army (suspended since 2020), or the stalled Pancheshwar Multipurpose Project, where water-sharing differences remain. On October 25 Nepal's foreign ministry told Parliament that encroachment exists along the border beyond Kalapani and Susta and that joint mapping continues; it gave no list.

The railway also has loose ends. The existing Janakpur-Jaynagar line is 51 km; the proposed Indian train would have 18 coaches against five on the Nepali side and run once a week at first. Agreement on security checks and on who bears operating and maintenance costs had not been reached, according to Nepali reports. Nepal Railway's Parajuli said: "Whoever operates the train will earn the revenue."

## Day by day

**Sunday, November 22:** Shah leaves Kathmandu in the afternoon (Mangsir 6 in the Nepali calendar) and reaches Delhi. **Monday, November 23:** he addresses business representatives in the morning, meets Modi for 30 minutes one-on-one, then leads delegation-level talks; agreements, the rail inauguration and a joint statement are expected, and he returns to Kathmandu the same day. The text of a joint communication was still being negotiated this week, so what is signed will be known only on the day.
