The Maharashtra cabinet on Tuesday approved **cash rewards of up to ₹1.5 lakh** for anyone whose information leads to the recovery of weapons that Naxals looted from security forces. The meeting, chaired by Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis at Mantralaya in Mumbai, also created a separate judicial district for Palghar, revised the state's textile policy and cleared an ordinance changing how public universities are run.

The decisions were reported in the evening of November 17. The state's own release was not available to Gandhi News when this story was written, so the account below rests on the published reports of the meeting.

## The reward scheme

The scheme came to the cabinet from the Home Department. The reports say it was meant to fill a gap: the state already pays for information that leads to arrests, to the breaking up of a Maoist network or to surrenders, but nothing rewarded an informant who helped bring back a looted weapon.

Rewards will depend on the type and number of weapons recovered. Automatic weapons, semi-automatic weapons, other firearms and ammunition each have their own provision, and the reports put the ceiling at about **₹1.5 lakh**. The per-weapon amounts were not published.

The cabinet also fixed who may sanction a reward. A **Superintendent of Police** can approve up to ₹10 lakh. The DIG of the Gadchiroli range can approve ₹10 lakh to ₹25 lakh, the Special Inspector General for anti-Naxal operations ₹25 lakh to ₹50 lakh, and the Additional Director General (Special Operations) ₹50 lakh to ₹1 crore.

According to one report, Maoists looted **175 weapons** from Maharashtra's security forces over the years, of which 50 have been recovered in encounters, in other states and through surrenders. That leaves 125 identified weapons untraced. Officials cited in the same report called the missing arms a grave concern, with the risk that they could reach criminals or terrorist groups or be used in fresh attacks on security personnel.

The government's case is that the threat has receded. The reports say the Red Corridor has been broken up and that Gadchiroli and Gondia, on its western fringe, are now declared free of Naxals, so attention is moving to recovering what was taken. We have reported earlier on the Prime Minister's claims about the decline of Naxalism, including in [Gadchiroli](/en/news/modi-gadchiroli-janjatiya-gaurav-diwas-bamboo-law-246-projects-address/).


## A district court for Palghar

Palghar, carved out of Thane on August 1, 2014, remains part of the Thane judicial district. The cabinet approved a **Principal District and Sessions Court**, a Chief Judicial Magistrate court and a District Public Prosecutor's office for the district, which has eight talukas: Palghar, Vasai, Dahanu, Talasari, Jawhar, Wada, Mokhada and Vikramgad.

The High Court's New Court Establishment Committee had already cleared the bifurcation of the Thane judicial district to create a separate Palghar district. For staffing, **79 new posts** will be created, 29 posts moved from existing courts and 39 positions filled through outsourcing. The magistrate's court gets 16 new posts and three outsourced positions, and the prosecutor's office five new posts and one outsourced position.

## Textile policy changes

The cabinet amended the Integrated and Sustainable Textile Policy 2023-28. Palghar moves from Zone 4 straight to **Zone 1**, because it is a tribal district. Zone 4 is abolished: Zone 2 districts move to Zone 1, Zone 3 districts to Zone 2, and the old Zone 4 districts to Zone 3. The move was reported as widening the industrial incentives open to Palghar units.

Installing new machinery worth **at least 10%** of the book value of the machinery installed in the previous financial year will now count as expansion. Projects that got bank loan sanctions before June 1, 2023, and self-financed units that paid for machinery in advance before that date, may choose between the 2018-23 and 2023-28 policies if they started production in the new policy period.

The changes followed demands from textile associations after the 2023 policy rolled out, and Fadnavis had assured them that industry-friendly revisions would follow. Boisar MLA Vilas Tare, who had pursued the Palghar demand for about 18 months, welcomed the decision and thanked Fadnavis, Deputy Chief Ministers Eknath Shinde and Sunetra Ajit Pawar and Textile Minister Sanjay Savkare. He said he would keep pressing for the release of pending textile subsidies.


## Universities ordinance

The cabinet approved an ordinance amending the Maharashtra Public Universities Act, which has been in force since March 1, 2017. The state government said the changes were needed because of the National Education Policy and shifts in higher education.

Under the amended Section 13(12), the **Chancellor can remove a Pro-Vice-Chancellor** on the recommendation of the university's Vice-Chancellor. Under Section 100(5)(a), construction and development work in universities will be carried out by the Public Works Department under the supervision of the university's management council. The Law and Judiciary Department may make procedural changes to the draft ordinance.

## What happens next

An ordinance has to be promulgated by the Governor before it takes effect, and the reports do not give a date. The reward scheme and the Palghar court posts will need government resolutions with the exact reward schedule and the dates from which the posts take effect. None of these had been published when this story was written.

For Palghar, the court approval is the step that separates its judicial district from Thane. For textile units there, the zone change decides which incentives they can claim under the 2023-28 policy.
