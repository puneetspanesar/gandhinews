Prime Minister Narendra Modi used the **23rd ASEAN-India Summit** in Manila on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, to put nine proposals to Southeast Asia, led by a long-term maritime cooperation plan and **free e-visas for tourists from ASEAN countries**. He then offered four more at the 21st East Asia Summit the same day. In the evening he joined US President Donald Trump, Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi for the first Quad leaders' meeting in two years, which ended with a commitment to a joint maritime exercise in 2027.

## What Modi proposed to ASEAN

According to the Prime Minister's Office, Mr Modi congratulated President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. on the Philippines' chairship of ASEAN under the theme "Navigating Our Future, Together", and on its work as India's country coordinator. He said India values its Comprehensive Strategic Partnership with the bloc, backs **ASEAN unity and centrality**, and supports a free, open, inclusive and rules-based Indo-Pacific whose future, he said, depends on cooperation rather than competition.

The nine proposals were:

- a **long-term ASEAN-India Maritime Cooperation Plan** covering connectivity, scientific research, disaster management, Coast Guard cooperation, maritime domain awareness and seafarers' security;
- a Joint Cyber Forensics Training Programme;
- ASEAN-India nuclear energy technical exchanges;
- an ASEAN-India Space Hackathon;
- cooperation on mining and critical minerals;
- an ASEAN-India Youth Connect, with a youth summit, exchanges for young parliamentarians and start-up hackathons;
- more scholarships for ASEAN students at Nalanda University;
- an ASEAN-India Creative Partnership Pavilion at WAVES 2027;
- free e-visas for ASEAN tourists.

Leaders adopted an **ASEAN-India Joint Statement on Cooperation in Disaster Management**. Mr Modi said India and ASEAN should co-create technology rather than only consume it, and that the Community Vision 2045 of ASEAN and India's Viksit Bharat 2047 goal could together meet shared aspirations. He noted that 2027 will mark 35 years of India-ASEAN ties and five years of the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership, and wished Singapore well as the next ASEAN chair.

## The East Asia Summit

At the East Asia Summit, which brings together ASEAN with India, China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, the United States and Russia, Mr Modi spoke of crises and conflicts and called for dialogue and mutual respect. He said they were hurting **food, fuel and fertiliser security** around the world, and asked members to guarantee the safety of commercial ships and seafarers, who continue to be targeted in international waters. India supports peaceful resolution of the conflicts in Ukraine and West Asia, he said, and he flagged terrorism as a threat to peace-loving societies.

His four proposals were a Seafarers' Safety Initiative on joint training and emergency response, an **EAS Cyber Crime Conference** at the National Forensic Science University, an EAS Maritime Heritage Festival at Lothal, and an EAS University Conclave at Nalanda.

## Three bilaterals

On the sidelines Mr Modi met Mr Marcos, Singapore's Prime Minister Lawrence Wong and Thailand's Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul, according to PMO readouts.

With Mr Marcos he reviewed the Strategic Partnership set up during the Filipino leader's state visit to India in August 2025, with defence and security, space, railway infrastructure, fintech and trade named as growth areas. They recalled meeting in New Delhi in September 2026, when Mr Modi invited Mr Marcos to the BRICS summit as ASEAN chair. The readout lists no signed agreements.

With Mr Wong he assessed progress under the **Roadmap for the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership** adopted during Mr Wong's visit to India in September 2025, across eight priority areas from economic ties and skills to defence and healthcare. With Mr Anutin he reviewed the Strategic Partnership raised during Mr Modi's April 2025 visit to Bangkok, and the two stressed BIMSTEC, IORA and the Mekong Ganga Cooperation.

## The Quad meeting

Mr Trump, Mr Modi, Mr Albanese and Ms Takaichi met on Tuesday, the first Quad leaders' meeting since Delaware on September 21, 2024, as [our report before the meeting](/en/news/modi-manila-quad-leaders-summit-dylan-johnson-modi-leaves) set out. Before the talks began Mr Trump called Mr Modi "a very tough man" and said the Japanese leader was "even tougher". Mr Modi called the Quad "a constructive and trusted partnership" for India and said "Manila and Quad have shared an age-old and special bond", recalling that officials of the four countries first met in Manila in 2007 and revived the dialogue there in 2017.

The joint statement, as reported, opposes efforts to **change the Indo-Pacific status quo by force or coercion** and voices concern about economic coercion, especially "arbitrary export restrictions". The leaders committed to reduce dependence on single suppliers of critical minerals used in semiconductors, batteries, electric vehicles and defence technology, backed freedom of navigation and safe shipping corridors, reaffirmed ASEAN centrality and condemned terrorism including cross-border terrorism.

## How we got here

Mr Modi last joined an ASEAN-India Summit on October 26, 2025, virtually. This year's trip, announced by the Ministry of External Affairs on November 12, put him in the room, and the Ministry had then described maritime security, trade and investment as India's main items. The AITIGA goods agreement, in force since 2011, is still under review, and India's trade deficit with ASEAN rose from $7.5 billion in 2011 to $44 billion in 2023. The Tuesday readouts do not mention a conclusion to that review.

## Why it matters

For travellers and exporters the concrete items are the free e-visa offer, which would apply to tourists from the 11 ASEAN member states if implemented, and the critical-minerals language shared by ASEAN and the Quad. ASEAN accounts for roughly a tenth of India's trade, and the maritime plan and the Quad's surveillance collaboration both concern the sea lanes it moves along. The Quad's shift to a named, dated joint exercise is a step from statements to operations.

## What happens next

The Quad Business Leaders' Forum is planned in India in early 2027, and the P-8 and P-3C exercise follows in 2027. Singapore takes over the ASEAN chair, and India and ASEAN will mark 35 years of ties in 2027. Mr Trump goes on to the APEC meetings in China this week. Mr Modi was due back in India on Wednesday, November 18.
