India's farms produced **a record 3,785.38 lakh tonnes of foodgrains in 2025-26**, according to the final estimates released by Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan in New Delhi on Thursday. That is 208.06 lakh tonnes more than the 3,577.32 lakh tonnes of 2024-25.

The same release gave the first estimate for the current kharif season. Despite El Niño, a late monsoon in June and drought-like conditions in several southern and western states, **kharif foodgrain production for 2026-27 is put at 1,671.43 lakh tonnes**, which the ministry says is 97.67 lakh tonnes above the average of the previous five kharif seasons.

## What the final numbers show

Rice and wheat both set records. **Rice rose to 1,551.54 lakh tonnes** from 1,501.84 lakh tonnes, and wheat to 1,220.64 lakh tonnes from 1,179.45 lakh tonnes. Pulses grew by 15.57 lakh tonnes to 272.40 lakh tonnes, with moong at a record 43.96 lakh tonnes, gram at 125.13 lakh tonnes and tur at 35.99 lakh tonnes.

The biggest single jump was in maize. **Maize output went from 434.09 to 550.25 lakh tonnes**, a rise of 116.16 lakh tonnes, which is more than half of the whole increase in foodgrains.

The release does not say so, but its own figures show that **coarse cereals other than maize fell**. Working back from the totals, coarse cereals came to 639.20 lakh tonnes in 2024-25 and 740.80 lakh tonnes in 2025-26. Take maize out and the rest, mostly millets, slipped from about 205.11 to 190.55 lakh tonnes, by our calculation. The release puts millets ("Shri Anna") at 172.09 lakh tonnes for 2025-26 but gives no figure for the year before.

Oilseeds barely moved. **Total oilseed output was 432.55 lakh tonnes**, up only 2.66 lakh tonnes on 429.89 lakh tonnes, even though groundnut (131.71 lakh tonnes) and rapeseed and mustard (137.66 lakh tonnes) both set records. Soybean stood at 126.36 lakh tonnes. Sugarcane was 5,037.29 lakh tonnes and cotton 280.06 lakh bales of 170 kg.

## Higher than the earlier estimate

The final figure is also above what the ministry said in May. The third advance estimate for 2025-26, released in May, was 376.56 million tonnes, or 3,765.6 lakh tonnes. **The final count adds about 19.8 lakh tonnes** to that. Wheat, estimated at 120.65 million tonnes in May, ended at 1,220.64 lakh tonnes, or about 122.06 million tonnes.

## The kharif season so far

Chouhan said sowing this year was hurt by the return of El Niño, the delay in the monsoon in June and rainfall he put at 35 to 40% below normal. The release names Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh as states where low soil moisture affected paddy, maize, tur, soybean and cotton.

Kharif sugarcane is projected at 4,543.39 lakh tonnes, cotton at 248.66 lakh bales and jute and mesta at 97.06 lakh bales of 180 kg.

## Why it matters

The minister's message was that India now has enough rice and wheat, and that **the push has moved to pulses and edible oils**, where domestic demand keeps rising and the country depends on imports. He pointed to the National Pulses Mission and the National Oilseeds Mission. The final figures show pulses growing faster than oilseeds: pulses added 15.57 lakh tonnes last year, oilseeds 2.66 lakh tonnes.

Chouhan also said rice exports grew by 5%, and that India ranks first in the world in rice, pulses, jute, millets and spices, and second after China in wheat, cotton and groundnut.

## How the estimates are made, and what comes next

The ministry says the numbers come from state and Union Territory reports, remote sensing, the weekly Crop Weather Watch Group, the Digital Crop Survey and the Digital General Crop Estimation Survey, checked through a three-tier validation. **The kharif numbers are preliminary.** They will be revised in the second advance estimates and the final estimates, once yields from crop-cutting experiments at harvest are in.
