Delhi's winter pollution curbs **came into force at midnight**, and for the next three months the city will look and move a little differently. Vehicles registered outside Delhi that meet BS-IV or older emission standards can no longer run in the capital, and the ban holds until January 31.

The Delhi government's Environment Department announced the package late on Saturday, saying it was meant "to tackle pollution directly at its source". CNG and electric vehicles are exempt, as are ambulances, fire tenders and police vehicles.

## Offices, building sites and parking

Government and private offices have been told to keep **physical attendance at 50%** until January 31, with essential services left out of the cap. Office hours are staggered to thin out the rush: Municipal Corporation offices will work from 8:30 am to 5 pm and Delhi government offices from 10 am to 6:30 pm.

Demolition and outdoor civil construction that raises dust are **banned until January 31**. Essential public infrastructure is exempt, and indoor finishing, plumbing and electrical work can carry on if dust is kept in check. A separate, complete halt on construction and demolition is planned from December 10 to January 20.

Parking will cost more too. Fees at authorised lots are **doubled from November 1 to February 28**, though lots run by the Delhi Metro Rail Corporation keep their old rates, a nudge towards the Metro.

## Cameras and challans

The Transport Department, Delhi Traffic Police, the Delhi Pollution Control Committee and the Municipal Corporation will enforce the rules. Number-plate recognition cameras and the AI-enabled Integrated Command and Control Centre will track vehicles, and those that break the ban will get **automatic e-challans** and may be impounded. Building sites caught flouting the order face stop-work notices, environmental compensation and possible sealing.

Chief Minister Rekha Gupta asked residents to cooperate. "Do not bring restricted vehicles registered outside Delhi into the city," she said, and urged people to "reduce non-essential use of personal vehicles, prioritizing public transit and carpooling instead."
