The **Senate Banking Committee has asked Jensen Huang**, who resigned as Nvidia's chief executive on Thursday, to testify at a public hearing on **December 3**, and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to appear on **December 10**. Chairman Tim Scott, a Republican from South Carolina, sent letters to both men on Thursday, November 12, and the committee made the requests public the next day. The questions are about how advanced AI chips reached China and why Washington did not act sooner against a Singapore customer of Nvidia.

Lutnick has said yes. His spokesperson, Kristen Eichamer, said the secretary "has proactively agreed to appear voluntarily before the Banking Committee to address their questions". Huang has not said whether he will come. Scott's letter says that if he does not, the committee "is prepared to use all tools at our disposal, including subpoenas".

## What the committee wants

Scott wrote that "recent reports raise serious concerns about our national security and the protection of sensitive U.S. technology". The Banking Committee oversees export controls, including those that govern Nvidia's sales of its most advanced processors. To Lutnick he wrote that the Commerce Department's **central role in export control rules** made his testimony "essential to our oversight". On X the chairman added: "The American people deserve answers."

The focus is **Megaspeed International**, a Singapore data-centre company and Nvidia customer that US officials suspect of helping Chinese firms get around the ban on advanced chips. According to trade records cited in reports, its Malaysian sister company bought at least **$4.8 billion** of AI equipment containing Nvidia chips between June 2024 and February 2026. Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security is reported to have opened a diversion inquiry in 2024, and the case was later said to have moved to the Justice Department, though neither agency has confirmed that.

Megaspeed has never been put on the Entity List, the Commerce register that cuts listed foreign firms off from US technology. The department has reportedly not added any company to it since October 2025. A former Megaspeed executive, Alice Huang, is not related to Jensen Huang. US officials are reported to have concluded she was a Chinese intelligence asset; China denies it, and Attorney General Todd Blanche has said the Justice Department has reached no conclusion.

## Washington turns on Commerce

On Friday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said his department and others, including State and the US Trade Representative, had favoured listing Megaspeed "for months", but that "**Commerce has the pen**". He declined to discuss reported friction with Lutnick. Tensions between the two were reported on November 11.

Senator Jim Banks, a Republican member of the committee, said Lutnick "used loopholes to allow some of these chip sales to occur", which he described as defying President Donald Trump's directive not to sell high-end chips to China. Senator Elizabeth Warren, the committee's senior Democrat, said Lutnick and Jeffrey Kessler, the under secretary who runs the export bureau, must explain why they have not closed the loopholes. She and Senator Chris Coons also asked Lutnick, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Stephen Miller to preserve their records on Nvidia chip exports to China.

The White House said Trump "maintains complete confidence" in Lutnick. House Foreign Affairs chairman Brian Mast has called for a look at Commerce's part but said he still has confidence in him.

## The House wants Huang too

Scott's request is the second from Congress this month. The chairmen of three House committees, Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford, had asked Huang for a briefing on November 18 over his contacts with Alice Huang (see [our earlier report](/en/news/nvidia-board-investigation-jensen-huang-megaspeed-congress-briefing-november-18)). They have since asked for a closed-door interview with Huang on December 1 and with Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter on December 2, with a Friday evening deadline to agree or face a subpoena. They also invited Kessler to a December 3 hearing. Mast said Huang has agreed to brief lawmakers. Moolenaar said Huang had been "one of the strongest proponents for not having export controls".

## The company and the market

Nvidia's board has hired Joon H. Kim, a former acting US Attorney for the Southern District of New York, to lead its independent investigation. Trump has said the FBI is looking into the affair. Nvidia shares closed down 4.3% at $166.19 on Thursday and have lost almost **$500 billion in market value** since the close on November 6. Reports on Friday identified more than $3 billion in Chinese computing projects built around restricted Nvidia processors.

China's foreign ministry has warned that tighter US export controls would threaten the stability of global supply chains.

## Why it matters in India

Nvidia is the main supplier of the chips that power AI training and data centres, in India as elsewhere. Indian cloud firms, start-ups and data-centre builders buy that hardware in the same global market, so a hardening of US export rules, a licensing slowdown at Commerce or a prolonged leadership vacuum at the supplier can touch delivery times and prices. Chip shares have also been the main drag on Wall Street this month (see [our report on the sell-off](/en/news/wall-street-close-dow-1833-nasdaq-correction-chip-rout)).

## What happens next

Nvidia reports quarterly results on **Tuesday, November 17**, its first under Kress. Huang has not yet said whether he will appear on **December 3**, and the committee has threatened a subpoena if he does not. Lutnick's arrangements for **December 10** are still being worked out.
