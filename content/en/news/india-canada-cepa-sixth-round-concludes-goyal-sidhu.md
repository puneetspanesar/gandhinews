India and Canada ended the **sixth round of negotiations on a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA)** in New Delhi on November 20, after ten days of talks that began on November 11. The Commerce Ministry announced the result on Saturday evening. No agreement was signed or initialled, and no tariff offers were published, but both governments repeated that they want to **conclude the deal by the end of 2026**.

## What the Commerce Ministry says

According to the ministry's release, the Indian side was led by chief negotiator Dr Brij Mohan Mishra and the Canadian side by chief negotiator Mary-Catherine Speirs. The teams worked across **six areas**: trade in goods, trade in services, rules of origin, trade remedies, intellectual property and investment promotion.

The release describes the discussions as "constructive and substantive" and says both sides made progress "towards advancing the negotiations". It does not say which chapters are closed, which are still open, or whether any of the market-access questions on farm goods or services are settled.

Near the end of the round, Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal met Glenn Purves, Canada's Vice Minister of International Trade, to take stock. Both sides reaffirmed their commitment to "an ambitious, balanced and commercially meaningful agreement" that delivers "tangible benefits to farmers, industries, businesses and Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) in both countries".

## The ministers who opened the round

The round opened on **November 11** with a meeting between Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal and Canada's International Trade Minister Maninder Sidhu. The Commerce Ministry says both underscored a "balanced and mutually beneficial" CEPA and guided their teams to move discussions along.

Canada's own readout, issued on November 12, adds that the two have **met more than a dozen times since May 2025**. Mr Sidhu pressed for government-to-government work to turn into commercial results in aerospace and space, clean technology and the energy transition, information and communication technology, life sciences and agri-food. He thanked Mr Goyal for India's support after a recent Team Canada trade mission.

## How the talks got here

Relations between the two countries froze in 2023 and have been rebuilt quickly this year. Prime Minister Mark Carney visited India from February 27 to March 2, the first bilateral visit by a Canadian prime minister in eight years, and the two governments agreed the terms for CEPA negotiations and the year-end target. The fifth round followed in Ottawa in early October, and when Foreign Minister Anita Anand came to Delhi in October, Prime Minister Narendra Modi asked for an early conclusion. Our report on the Modi-Carney call has the October background.

On the Canadian side, Mr Sidhu spent three days in India, taking in New Delhi and the CII Partnership Summit in Visakhapatnam. His office says he also met Civil Aviation Minister Kinjarapu Ram Mohan Naidu on code-share services and air connectivity, and IndiGo on its international expansion.

## What is at stake for traders

The numbers Canada has published show a modest trade that both want to grow. Two-way trade in goods and services was worth **C$30.4 billion in 2025**, of which C$11.0 billion was goods, according to the Canadian government. In 2025 Canada's merchandise imports from India were **C$9.7 billion, up 20.7%** on 2024.

Canada's top imports from India are precious stones and metals, machinery, pharmaceutical products, electronics, and iron and steel products. Its top exports to India are vegetables, mineral fuels and oils, wood pulp, fertiliser, and paper and paperboard. Canadian affiliates employed more than **68,900 people in India** in 2024, and Indian affiliates employed more than 37,900 in Canada.

Two of the six negotiating areas, rules of origin and trade remedies, determine how much of any tariff cut exporters of these goods can actually use. Neither government has said which tariff lines are on the table.

## What happens next

The release gives no date for a seventh round and no list of unresolved issues. The only deadline either side has named is the **end of 2026**, now about six weeks away. India's High Commission in Ottawa announced in October that Mr Modi will visit Canada on December 11 and 12, as we reported on October 18. Neither government has said whether the deal is meant to be ready by then.
