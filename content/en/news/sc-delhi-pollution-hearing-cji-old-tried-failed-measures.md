The Supreme Court on Friday asked the Centre and the Delhi-NCR authorities whether they have anything beyond the usual curbs to clean the capital's air, with Chief Justice of India Surya Kant describing **temporary bans as "old, tried and failed measures"**. Delhi's AQI was about 391 around noon, close to the 400 mark that separates very poor from severe air.

The CJI opened the suo motu hearing with a dry remark to the lawyers present: "We hope all of you are enjoying Delhi weather." The bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana then turned to what it called the most visible source, traffic.

## Cars from outside Delhi

The court said **vehicle emissions are a primary cause** of the deterioration, and noted bumper-to-bumper traffic in Delhi and on the roads to Ghaziabad, Greater Noida, Gurugram and Faridabad. Vehicles registered in other states can enter Delhi, it observed, and nobody can easily check at the border what fuel they run on or what condition they are in.

"Even if a vehicle enters Delhi for just two hours and then leaves, those two hours are enough to cause damage," the CJI said. The bench added that the people hit hardest are roadside vendors and daily-wage workers who cannot stay indoors.

## What the Centre told the court

Additional Solicitor General Aishwarya Bhati, appearing for the Union, listed five sources: industry, vehicles, construction and demolition dust, road dust and stubble burning. She said stubble burning accounted for about **14% of Delhi's pollution on November 5**. She also told the bench that nine major toll plazas, which handle roughly 90% of the traffic through 126 toll points, now have multi-lane free-flow tolling that is being tested.

Senior advocate Aparajita Singh, the amicus curiae, said earlier directions to the National Highways Authority of India and the Municipal Corporation of Delhi had been only partly carried out, and that commercial vehicles idling at border points add needless emissions. She asked the Commission for Air Quality Management to respond on toll-plaza idling.

On farm residue, the CJI suggested economic incentives: stubble could be bought from farmers at a notified rate and agencies such as the Food Corporation of India could store it for commercial use. Bhati agreed to place the court's suggestions before the Agriculture Ministry.

Justice Bagchi said the court would defer to expert bodies on how pollution restrictions are classified. He was reluctant to restrict school attendance and asked whether online classes are a workable alternative, and whether paediatricians had been consulted on the claims about children's health.

## Congress: "failed at every level"

Outside the courtroom, the opposition used the same air as a charge against the government. Speaking at Congress leader Rahul Gandhi's Diwali Milan in New Delhi on Friday, **Udit Raj blamed the BJP**, which holds the Centre, the Delhi government, the Lieutenant Governor's office and the local bodies. "They can no longer use the excuse that the smoke from stubble burning, drifting in from outside, is the cause," he said. "They have failed at every level and in every sector."

## What happens next

The bench said it would go through the status reports being prepared, could issue further directions soon, and would pass an order seeking various reports and updates. It wants compliance reports when the case is next heard, which it said would be listed immediately after the break. No date has been announced.

On October 29 the court had allowed certified green crackers to be made and sold, with use limited to fixed hours on November 7 and 8. The Commission for Air Quality Management put Delhi-NCR under Stage III of its Graded Response Action Plan on Friday, and forecasters expect severe air over the Diwali weekend.
