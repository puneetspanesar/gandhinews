Anuj Shrivatri of Madhya Pradesh became India's **100th chess Grandmaster** on Sunday, November 8, by winning the 7th ASEAN Plus Individual Open Chess Championship in Ozamiz City in the Philippines. He is 22.

He lost his first-round game and then won his way to the top, finishing on **7 points**, half a point clear of Nguyen Duc Hoa of Vietnam, who had 6.5. Nouri Alekhine of the Philippines took third place with 6. The title also makes Shrivatri the first Grandmaster from Madhya Pradesh.

## How the title was won

The tournament was played at the Asenso Global Youth Leadership and Sports Complex in Misamis Occidental province. After the opening-round defeat to Arca Christian Gian Karlo of the Philippines, a player who ended 32nd, Shrivatri **scored seven points from his remaining games** to take first place outright. India's Viani Antonio Dcunha finished fourth. The field included other titled players, among them Mongolia's Sharavdorj Dashzegve, who finished 11th, and the Philippines' Laylo Darwin and Quizon Daniel, who finished 18th and 22nd.

The result carried more than a trophy. All India Chess Federation president Nitin Narang described it on X as a "direct GM title" and called it a historic milestone. "Onwards to 200 Grandmasters," he wrote.

Shrivatri, who was born on July 21, 2004, earned his International Master title in 2020 and was then the youngest IM from his state. No Madhya Pradesh player had reached the Grandmaster level before him.

## From one to a hundred

Viswanathan Anand became India's first Grandmaster in 1988, after earning his third norm in 1987. The country then moved slowly: it took **nine years to go from one Grandmaster to three**, with Praveen Thipsay's title in 1997.

The count has risen sharply in the last four months. **The 96th to 100th Grandmasters were all added between July and November 2026**: Ethan Vaz, Harshvardhan GB, S Ashwath and V Rathnavel before Shrivatri. Rathnavel became the 99th in July.

Anand's influence on that growth is part of the story. His post opened with a Diwali greeting. He has played a major part in Indian chess beyond his own results, including through the Westbridge-Anand Chess Academy. The 100th title has now arrived **38 years after his own**.

## Reactions

Anand, a five-time world champion, congratulated Shrivatri on X. "Happy Diwali. A cracker of a 100. Congrats to @AnujShrivatri on getting India to this milestone," he wrote. He added: "Funnily only today I was thinking December 2027 will be the 40th anniversary of my GM title and literally this manifested."

Narang called it a proud moment for Indian chess.

## Why the number matters

Grandmaster is the highest title the world federation FIDE awards for over-the-board play. The arithmetic shows how fast the pace has changed: 99 Grandmasters arrived in 38 years, and **five of them, one in every twenty, came in the last four months**. In the first nine years, India had only three.

For Madhya Pradesh the title is a first: no player from the state had reached the Grandmaster level before Shrivatri.

## What happens next

Indian chess now turns to the world championship. Gukesh Dommaraju, the reigning champion, defends his title against Javokhir Sindarov in Geneva later this month, so an Indian player will again be at the centre of the sport's biggest event within weeks. Shrivatri's win has already moved the national tally to a round 100, and the federation's stated target is double that.
