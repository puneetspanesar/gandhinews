External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar met Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan in New Delhi on Saturday, **November 14**, while Pashinyan was passing through the Indian capital on a transit stopover. The two discussed bilateral relations and regional and international developments, including **the conflicts in the Gulf and in Ukraine**.

Jaishankar described the meeting on X. "Pleased to meet with PM @NikolPashinyan of Armenia during his transit stopover in Delhi," he wrote. "Our economic, security, cultural and people-to-people ties continue to gain momentum." He added that he appreciated Pashinyan's insights on recent developments in his region.

Neither side had said, by late evening, where Pashinyan was headed or how long the talks lasted, and no agreement or announcement came out of the meeting.

## A stopover, not a state visit

The readout shows the subjects the two ministers chose. The Gulf and Ukraine are the two wars that most shape India's energy bill and its diplomacy, and Armenia, which shares a border with Iran, has a direct interest in both.

India has its own stakes in each. The Gulf war has put the Strait of Hormuz at the centre of Indian energy planning, and our report on [two supertankers ablaze in the strait](/en/news/hormuz-two-supertankers-ablaze-irgc-4-million-barrels-india-gulf-crude-overtakes-russia) records how Gulf crude has become a larger share of India's imports. On Friday, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said a [gas carrier hit a mine](/en/news/trump-gulf-allies-hold-off-iran-strikes-gas-carrier-mine-hormuz) in the strait, and on Saturday Iran said three more oil tankers had hit mines and caught fire. Talks between Russia, Ukraine and the United States resumed in Abu Dhabi this week, the first round since February, as our [day-one report](/en/news/abu-dhabi-russia-ukraine-us-talks-first-since-february-day-one) described.

## How ties have moved this year

Saturday's meeting is the latest in a run of contacts. In June, **Prime Minister Narendra Modi phoned Pashinyan** to congratulate him and his party on winning Armenia's parliamentary elections and to discuss bilateral ties and regional developments. Modi also thanked Armenia for helping to bring out Indian nationals who were stranded in Iran during the West Asia tensions.

On August 31, Modi and Pashinyan met in person on the sidelines of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan, the first face-to-face meeting after Pashinyan was reappointed. Jaishankar, National Security Adviser Ajit Doval and Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri were in the room. According to the Indian account, the leaders covered **defence, trade, pharmaceuticals, space, information technology, mining, education and culture**, and Modi stressed that dialogue and diplomacy are essential for peace and stability in West Asia. Pashinyan briefed him on the implementation of the Armenia-Azerbaijan peace agreement.

Modi wrote afterwards that India-Armenia cooperation "is all set to grow in defence, IT, space, education, pharmaceuticals and more", and that the two countries would deepen trade and investment links.

On Friday, November 13, Armenian Parliament Vice-President Hayk Konjoryan, who heads the Armenia-India Parliamentary Friendship Group, met India's ambassador to Armenia, Armstrong Changsan, to discuss bilateral relations and parliamentary cooperation.

## The Azerbaijan factor

India's Armenia ties are now read alongside its widening contact with Baku. On October 23, Jaishankar [called on President Ilham Aliyev in Baku](/en/news/jaishankar-azerbaijan-visit-first-in-eight-years), the first visit by an Indian foreign minister there since April 2018. Azerbaijan's foreign ministry said the talks covered transport corridors, trade, energy and the normalisation process between Azerbaijan and Armenia, on which Foreign Minister Jeyhun Bayramov briefed Jaishankar.

Modi met Aliyev for the first time on the sidelines of the same Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Bishkek, according to our earlier report. Seen together, the sequence shows New Delhi talking to both capitals in the South Caucasus within weeks, and being briefed by each on the peace process between them.

## Why it matters to readers

For India, the interest in the South Caucasus is practical. Armenia is a growing defence and pharmaceutical partner, according to the areas Modi named in August, and the region is a link in the transport corridors that Azerbaijan pressed on Jaishankar in Baku. Indian nationals evacuated from Iran with Armenian help are the human side of that cooperation.

## What happens next

No follow-up meeting has been announced. The next fixed diplomatic date on India's calendar is Modi's trip to Manila for the [ASEAN-India summit](/en/news/modi-asean-india-summit-manila-november-16-18) on November 16 to 18, where the Gulf conflict is likely to come up again. Whether Pashinyan's stopover turns into a formal visit to India is not yet known.
