A SpaceX Dragon capsule is set to lift off from Florida on Tuesday carrying 2,860 kg of supplies and science to the International Space Station, including lab-grown models of the human brain and the station's last set of new solar panels.

The Falcon 9 rocket is due to launch the CRS-35 mission from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station at 6:33 am local time, 4:03 pm IST. It is the 35th SpaceX cargo flight under NASA's Commercial Resupply Services contract. A backup slot is available on Wednesday.

## What is on board

Among the cargo is the final set of ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays, which will boost the station's power supply.

NASA is sending four investigations from its biological and physical sciences division. One flies small, lab-grown models of the human brain for five weeks and will compare them with identical models kept on Earth, to see how a short trip to space affects brain tissue at the level of cells. Another tests whether crop diseases spread faster and more severely in weightlessness, using plants infected with a fungus.

A compact device called a microplate reader will be tested for the first time to see whether astronauts can measure antibodies, antigens and proteins in their own samples on board, rather than waiting for them to come back to Earth. And a long-running study of antibiotic-resistant bacteria, known as GEARS, returns for its fourth and final planned stay; crew members will swab surfaces across the station to track how superbugs survive and evolve.

The capsule also carries hardware to make artificial retinas in orbit, 3D heart tissue for drug testing, and samples to study how a new type of glass forms.

## Arrival and return

Dragon should dock on its own with the space-facing port of the Harmony module on Thursday at about 8:30 pm IST. It will stay about a month before splashing down in the Pacific off California with finished experiments. It is the only cargo craft now flying that can bring material back to Earth; Russia's Progress, Japan's HTV-X and Northrop Grumman's Cygnus XL burn up after their missions.
