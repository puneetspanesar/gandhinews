India Post will spend **₹2,632 crore over five years** on its post offices, buildings and mail and parcel networks, the Ministry of Communications said on Thursday. Union Communications Minister Jyotiraditya Scindia, Minister of State Chandra Sekhar Pemmasani and Secretary (Posts) Subrat Das set out the plans at a press briefing in New Delhi.

The money covers the 16th Finance Commission cycle, from 2026-27 to 2030-31. Of it, **₹1,491 crore goes to the Estates Management Scheme** and ₹1,141 crore to the Postal Operations Scheme.

## What the money pays for

The estates scheme is the bigger half and is mostly about buildings. According to the ministry, it will fund:

It also pays for **50 buildings to be reconstructed**, 150 residential colonies to be renovated and a permanent Integrated Technology Centre in Bengaluru for CEPT, the department's technology arm.

The ₹1,141 crore Postal Operations Scheme is spread over six areas: rural postal access, mail network optimisation, parcel network optimisation, international mail infrastructure, marketing and service quality, and philately.

## Approved, or proposed?

The ministry's two versions of the release do not say quite the same thing. The headline of both says the outlay follows Cabinet approval. But the English text says the Cabinet approved **continuing the Estates Management Scheme**, and calls the ₹1,141 crore for the Postal Operations Scheme a "proposed continuation". The Hindi text says the Union Cabinet approved continuing both. We found no separate release on a Cabinet decision about either scheme.

Spread evenly, the outlay comes to about ₹526 crore a year, by our calculation.

## The revenue push behind it

The spending comes with a sharp revenue goal. Secretary (Posts) Subrat Das presented a target of **taking India Post's revenue to about ₹19,803 crore in 2026-27**. The department earned ₹15,296 crore in 2025-26, up 16% from ₹13,218 crore the year before, Scindia said in April. Reaching the new target would mean growth of about 29% in one year.

The ministry says the growth is to come from operational efficiency, more parcel and logistics business, technology and better customer engagement. Das pointed to the integrated Insurance Management System, through which **a Postal Life Insurance policy can be bought in about 20 minutes**. It has handled premium transactions for around 2 lakh customers and brought in about ₹60 crore, he said. He also described a data analytics team, a Six Sigma team and a chief marketing officer working with six service verticals.

## What the minister said

Scindia thanked Prime Minister Narendra Modi for backing the Cabinet approval and thanked the department's staff.

> "Every member of the India Post family has worked with unwavering dedication and determination to bring about the transformation of this institution, whose potential extends far beyond national boundaries to the global stage."
>
> Source: Jyotiraditya M. Scindia, Union Minister of Communications, November 26, 2026

## What it means for users, and what comes next

For people who use the post office, the visible change is meant to be in the branches: **5,050 sub-post offices and rented post offices are to be upgraded** over five years, on top of the new and rebuilt buildings. The release does not give a year-by-year schedule, a list of locations or the states that will get the new buildings. Those details, and whether the parcel push brings the revenue jump the department is aiming for, will show in its accounts for 2026-27.
