A US congressional advisory body has asked Washington to open a **senior-level channel with the Government of India** on what happens when the 14th Dalai Lama dies. The recommendation is in the 2026 annual report of the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission, a bipartisan panel that advises Congress, and it drew attention in India on November 23, 2026.

The commission warns that the **Chinese government is preparing to turn the succession into an international contest over legitimacy**, and is likely to use "coercive tools" to shape the outcome. It says India, which hosts the Dalai Lama and the Tibetan government-in-exile, the Central Tibetan Administration, is the centre of that dispute.

## What the commission recommends

The proposals sit in Chapter 3 of the report, titled "China's Other Front: India and the Future of U.S. Strategy in the Indo-Pacific". They are recommendations to Congress, not law, and no action by Congress has been reported. In summary:

- Congress should make it US policy that the **Gaden Phodrang Trust is "the sole legitimate authority to identify and recognize the successor"**, and that the United States "will not recognize any successor selected or installed by the Government of the People's Republic of China".
- The Secretary of State should **establish "a senior-level channel with the Government of India for succession contingency planning"**, including sharing intelligence on Chinese influence operations, and report to Congress within 180 days.
- The Secretary of State should run an interagency tabletop exercise on succession scenarios and brief Congress within 180 days. The report names the National Security Advisor, the Director of National Intelligence and the Secretary of Defense as participants.
- Washington should hold Track 1.5 and Track 2 dialogues with Indian counterparts, which are talks that bring in former officials, academics and experts rather than serving ministers alone.
- The United States should work with allies to deny international legitimacy to a successor chosen by Beijing, and help the Central Tibetan Administration protect itself against Chinese cyber operations and AI-enabled disinformation.

## What the earlier paper found

The annual report builds on a research paper the commission published on August 26, 2026. That paper treats India as the central factor in how the contest will play out, and it is blunt about Delhi's readiness. It says India has **no standing Tibet policy and no succession contingency plan**, no interagency planning process and no single government point of contact on the subject.

It also records a shrinking exile base. The Tibetan community in India has **fallen by more than 40% since 2009**, from 110,228 to roughly 60,000 to 65,000 people, and monasteries, schools and settlement offices are operating below capacity. The paper says Chinese influence operations now reach Indian state media and a 2023 smear campaign, though they have not yet gained decisive traction and are concentrated among elite and academic opinion rather than the public.

Washington, New Delhi and Dharamshala, the paper adds, hold incomplete and partly inaccurate views of one another's positions on the succession, and no dialogue exists to correct them. It proposes a US-India channel that is formal where New Delhi will support one and informal, through Track 1.5 and Track 2 consultations, where it will not.

## How we got here

On July 2, 2025, ahead of his 90th birthday, the Dalai Lama declared that the Gaden Phodrang Trust, the body he set up in 2011, "has sole authority to recognise the future reincarnation". Beijing rejected that at once and insists any heir needs its approval.

India's response was careful. On July 4, 2025, Ministry of External Affairs spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said the government "has always upheld freedom of religion for all in India and will continue to do so", and that India takes no position on matters of faith and religion. The same day Union minister Kiren Rijiju, a Buddhist, said "there is no need for me or the government to say anything. Who would be the next Dalai Lama will be decided by him", adding that he spoke as a devotee and not for the government.

China's foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning answered that India "should be clear of the anti-China separatist nature of the 14th Dalai Lama", and urged New Delhi to handle Tibet-related issues cautiously to avoid harming relations.

## Why it matters to India

The Dalai Lama and the exile administration are based in India, at Dharamshala. A disputed succession would play out on Indian soil, with a Chinese-backed claimant on one side and the Gaden Phodrang Trust's choice on the other. Any Indian decision on who receives official recognition, access or security would be read in Beijing as taking a side.

The report also places the issue inside a wider India policy. It describes ties between India and China as gradually recovering since late 2024 but marked by "strategic distrust", points to the 2020 Galwan clashes as a turning point, and notes that India pursues strategic autonomy, so closer US ties do not mean automatic alignment. For the Government of India, a formal US channel on a religious succession would be a far more public commitment than the quiet consultations it now prefers. Our earlier reports on [the border talks](/en/news/jaishankar-no-agreed-border-china-stable-boundary) and on [China's renaming of places in Ladakh](/en/news/china-rejects-india-ladakh-28-place-names-mea-responds) show how sensitive that balance is.

## What happens next

The commission cannot compel anything. Its recommendations go to Congress, which would have to legislate or direct the State Department, and the 180-day deadlines run only if it does. The August paper also urged support for the Assuring the Future of Tibet Act of 2026 (H.R. 8982) and a public statement that Washington will recognise only a successor chosen through the Trust.

No reaction from the Ministry of External Affairs, the Chinese foreign ministry or the Central Tibetan Administration to the report was available on Tuesday morning.
