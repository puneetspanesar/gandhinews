The rupee ended the week near 97 to the dollar. It closed at a provisional 96.72 on Friday, October 9, up 16 paise from 96.88 on Thursday. Brent crude futures traded at $102.95 a barrel that day.

A day later, on Saturday, the Reserve Bank of India announced two sets of measures to ease pressure on the currency.

## A dollar window for oil companies

The RBI will sell dollars to meet "the entire daily dollar requirements" of three state-run oil marketing companies: Indian Oil Corporation, Hindustan Petroleum and Bharat Petroleum. The sales will go through one or more designated banks, starting Monday, October 12.

The facility "will remain in place until further notice". The RBI said the decision was based on its assessment of current market conditions. The window takes the oil companies' import demand out of the spot market, National Herald reported.

## Tighter rules on hedging

In two circulars issued the same day, the RBI also tightened rules for currency derivatives involving the rupee.

Users can no longer rebook a rupee derivative contract once it has been cancelled, though rollovers at maturity are allowed. The limit for hedging without documented exposure falls from $100 million to $5 million, both with banks and on stock exchanges.

Banks must also keep a new Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve with the RBI in cash. It equals 20% of the notional value of contracts above $2 million where a user buys foreign currency against rupees to hedge current account exposures. The RBI said the measures aim "to ensure orderly functioning of the foreign exchange market".

## The oil link

Business Standard has tied the rupee's slide this year to oil prices and foreign investor selling. On April 30, when Brent touched $126 a barrel, its highest since June 2022, the rupee hit a then-record low of 95.32. Foreign investors had pulled more than $20 billion out of Indian markets in 2026 by then.

Higher oil prices raise India's import bill, widen the current account deficit and add to importers' demand for dollars. The rupee has lost 1.62% against the dollar since early September. Foreign institutional investors sold equities worth ₹12,943.58 crore on October 8 alone.

> The Indian rupee appreciated after three days of weakness, strengthening against the US dollar

Parmar said he expects resistance near 97 and support at 95.30 in the near term.

## Reserves are falling

India's foreign exchange reserves fell $12.95 billion to $734.6 billion in the week ended October 2, the fourth weekly decline in a row. They are $51.1 billion below the record $785.7 billion of the week ended September 4. The week ended September 25 saw a record weekly fall of $18.3 billion.

Market participants estimate the RBI sold about $30 billion, net, in September. Gaura Sen Gupta, chief economist at IDFC FIRST Bank, put the RBI's sales at about $7.5 billion in the latest reported week, with valuation changes accounting for most of the rest of the fall.

Reserves had been rebuilt over the summer through a concessional swap window for FCNR(B) deposits, which opened on June 8 and closed on August 31. It brought in $143.6 billion by September 18, of which $132.98 billion came from FCNR(B) deposits.

The RBI raised its repo rate to 5.50% on October 7, citing the West Asia conflict and volatile crude prices among the risks to inflation.
