India's investment in clean power will **exceed the $220 billion** it needs to reach its 2030 target, Union Minister for New and Renewable Energy Pralhad Joshi said in New Delhi on Thursday, November 5, 2026.

Speaking at a press conference that closed the four-day **Bharat Renewable Energy Summit and Expo 2026**, Joshi said the money is needed to add the remaining 190 GW of non-fossil capacity. India has about **310 GW installed against a 500 GW goal**, and the minister said the country is "confident of achieving the 500 GW milestone, perhaps even ahead of schedule."

He pointed to how fast the build-out has sped up. "To achieve first 100 GW, it took 7 decades," he said, adding that "the next 100 GW was added in 2 years." About **₹7 lakh crore** has come into the sector over the past two years through domestic funds and foreign direct investment, and renewables met more than a quarter of the country's electricity use last financial year.

## Batteries and waste

Joshi said Prime Minister Narendra Modi "has asked us to bring a good policy, including for batteries," and that India had learned from past supply chain shocks. The government is also preparing plans to **add another 300 GW beyond 2030**.

Ministry Secretary Santosh Kumar Sarangi said the ministry is drafting **guidelines for recycling solar modules**. "We are trying to formulate the guidelines as quickly as possible," he said. Under current rules, waste solar modules must be stored until 2035, when there should be enough volume to recycle them.

The summit drew participants from more than 135 countries, including 32 foreign ministers and six chief ministers, the ministry said.
