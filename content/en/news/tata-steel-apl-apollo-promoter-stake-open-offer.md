Tata Steel will buy the entire **26.6% promoter stake in APL Apollo Tubes for about ₹15,510 crore** and then make an open offer for a further 26%, the company said in Mumbai on November 23. The promoters are being paid ₹2,100 a share, and the open offer price is ₹2,301.40. The two together could cost Tata Steel up to ₹32,266 crore and take its holding in APL Apollo, the tubes maker, to about 52.6%.

The purchase is one half of a larger swap. In the same announcement Tata Steel agreed to sell its steel tubes business and its wholly owned subsidiary Tata Steel Colors to APL Apollo for **a combined ₹28,000 crore**. APL Apollo will become the single vehicle for the Tata group's downstream tubes and colour-coated products, with Tata Steel as its controlling shareholder if the open offer is taken up in full.

## What the company has announced

Tata Steel's release lists four linked transactions. The first is the sale of the Steel Tubes business, with about 1.7 million tonnes a year (MTPA) of capacity, and Tata Steel Colors, with about 800,000 tonnes a year of surface-coated coil capacity, to APL Apollo. The second is the purchase of the promoters' 26.6% stake in APL Apollo. The third is the open offer. The fourth is a memorandum of understanding with SG Tech Engineering (SG Spectra), a company linked to APL Apollo's promoter group, for exclusive contract manufacturing of 5 MTPA of colour-coated capacity across India.

The stake is being bought in cash and, according to reports, **in three tranches**, with the first 9.91% bought on November 23. The open offer follows the stake purchase. It is a mandatory offer under the takeover rules of the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) for up to 72,806,387 shares, or 26% of the expanded voting capital, according to a summary of the exchange filing.

The money moves in both directions. Set against the ₹28,000 crore Tata Steel receives for its own businesses, the net outlay if every share in the open offer is tendered comes to about ₹4,266 crore, before other costs and financing. APL Apollo will fund its ₹28,000 crore purchase first through acquisition financing and then replace that debt with long-term capital.

## Why Tata Steel wants it

The release frames the deal as a push into value-added steel. APL Apollo has about 5 MTPA of tubes capacity, and the combined business would start at about 7.5 MTPA of tubes and colour-coated products. Tata Steel plans to add 3 MTPA of tubes and 5 MTPA of coated capacity "in the coming few years" and **targets about 15.5 MTPA by 2035**, made up of roughly 9 MTPA of tubes and 6.5 MTPA of coated products. SG Spectra is expected to build the first 1 million tonnes of coated capacity within 24 months, in modular plants spread across the country.

Tata Steel says the combination should bring higher capacity use, removal of bottlenecks, joint buying of equipment and materials, and shared distribution. It expects APL Apollo's operating margins to improve further, with a focus on returns on capital and a mix of light-asset and conventional growth.

T V Narendran, Tata Steel's chief executive and managing director, said in the release: "We are very pleased to have entered into a comprehensive agreement with APL Apollo and the Promoters." Speaking about the strategy, he said "we believe that there is a lot of value to be delivered by investing in downstream assets". Koushik Chatterjee, the executive director and chief financial officer, said: "The intention is to ensure that the APL Apollo balance-sheet is strong and has the ability to grow in the future."

Sanjay Gupta, APL Apollo's chairman and managing director, described the arrangement as passing the baton of leadership to an institution of Tata Steel's stature. The shares being sold are held through the promoter group entity S Gupta Holding Private Limited, according to reports.

## How the shares reacted

APL Apollo Tubes shares **fell 21.39% to ₹1,668.15** on the BSE on November 23, and Tata Steel shares fell 8.16% to ₹163.25, according to closing prices reported that day. On those prices the ₹2,100 promoter price is about 26% above where APL Apollo closed, and the ₹2,301.40 open offer price is about 38% above it, by our calculation. The exchanges are shut on Tuesday for Guru Nanak Jayanti, so the next trading session is Wednesday, November 25.

The fall in APL Apollo came on a day when its shareholders were offered a premium, and the sources read for this report do not say why. Investors will have the financing and the ₹28,000 crore purchase of Tata Steel's businesses to weigh when trading resumes.

## How we got here

Tata Steel reported a consolidated net profit of ₹3,666 crore for the September quarter on November 7, with its India operations earning ₹5,055 crore after tax, as reported in our [earlier story](/en/news/tata-steel-q2-profit-up-18-per-cent-3666-crore-noel-tata-retires). That India business is the base from which the downstream push is being made. Tata Steel already ran its own tubes and coated-steel units; the deal moves them into a listed company with an established distribution network instead of building a second one.

## Approvals and what happens next

The release says all the transactions are **subject to regulatory approvals** and to the close of Tata Steel's open offer. It does not name the regulators. Neither SEBI nor the Competition Commission of India is named in the release or in the reports read for this story, so the exact approvals are not confirmed. Tata Steel expects them "in the coming months" and aims to complete the composite transactions within FY2027, that is by March 31, 2027.

The dates for shareholders to tender in the open offer have not been announced. The next developments to watch are the second and third tranches of the stake purchase and the first trading session after the holiday. For small shareholders of APL Apollo, the open offer at ₹2,301.40 is the figure that sets their choice: tender at that price, or stay in a company that will have Tata Steel as its largest and controlling shareholder.
