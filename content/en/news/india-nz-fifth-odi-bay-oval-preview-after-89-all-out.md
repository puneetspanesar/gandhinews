India lead New Zealand 2-1 with one match to play, so **they cannot lose the series**, but the fifth one-day international at Bay Oval, Mount Maunganui, on Sunday, November 15, 2026 (7:30 am IST) comes straight after the worst batting day of Shreyas Iyer's captaincy. India were **bowled out for 89 in 26.4 overs** chasing 334 on Friday and lost by 244 runs. A New Zealand win would level the series at 2-2.

The defeat is India's **second-biggest ODI loss by runs**, behind only the 245-run defeat to Sri Lanka at Sharjah in 2000. It is also India's sixth-lowest ODI total. Earlier heavy losses were 214 runs against South Africa in Mumbai in 2015 and 208 runs against Australia in Sydney in 2004.

## How Friday went wrong

Iyer won the toss and chose to field. New Zealand made 333 in 50 overs, built on **Will Young's 125 off 108 balls**, his highest ODI score and fifth ODI hundred. He and Tim Seifert (58 off 48) put on 85 in 12 overs for the first wicket, and Finn Allen hit 41 off 30. Young was dropped off consecutive balls in the second over, first by Rohit Sharma and then by Ishan Kishan, and Kishan later took a diving catch to dismiss him. Axar Patel, Gurnoor Brar and Auqib Nabi took two wickets each, and India gave away 68 runs in the last ten overs.

The chase was over almost before it began. **Matt Henry took three wickets in the first over**: Rohit Sharma and Iyer both went first ball, Iyer lbw after a successful review, and Virat Kohli was out for a duck as well. India were 0 for 3 after six balls, which reports say had never happened to them in an ODI. Ruturaj Gaikwad fell for 5 to Kyle Jamieson and it was 9 for 4. Kishan made 27, Axar 21 and Naman Dhir 21, with two sixes. Henry finished with **6 for 27 in 9.4 overs**, his best ODI figures, including two maidens, and took the last wicket, Arshdeep Singh. Matthew Fisher took three.

## What the captains said

Iyer pointed to the pitch and to Henry. "We saw it, there was a huge difference in the first innings and the second innings in terms of wicket," he said, and called Henry's opening spell "sensational". He added: "It's important to forget about this one, obviously, analyse what went wrong in terms of bowling and batting." He also reminded the dressing room that "at the back of the mind it's important that we have also won two games."

New Zealand captain Mitchell Santner was pleased not to have had to bowl again, and called Henry, on a surface that offers some help with the new ball, "the world's best". Looking ahead, he said Sunday offers the hosts a chance "from a pitch point of view, from a conditions point of view" to go again. Henry, the player of the match, said it was "one game and we've got another one coming up soon that hopefully we can level the series."

Head coach Gautam Gambhir was blunt about the batting. "There's no benefit to sweeping it under the carpet," he said, adding that India must be ready "not only mentally but from the skill point of view" for bowling in these conditions.

## The squads for Sunday

India's squad for the last two ODIs is Iyer (captain), Rohit, Kohli, Tilak Varma, Kishan (vice-captain and wicketkeeper), Axar, Dhir, Nabi, Brar, Gaikwad, Arshdeep, Kumar Kushagra, Mohammed Siraj, Yash Thakur and Kuldeep Yadav. **Shubman Gill and KL Rahul** missed Friday and will miss Sunday too, as they prepare for the Test series. Iyer's side has not named its eleven for Sunday, and **the batting order is the question** the management has to answer.

New Zealand announced changes on Thursday. **Brett Randell, 31, has a first international call-up** after 145 domestic matches, and Josh Clarkson, who has played six ODIs and 13 T20Is, is in the squad for the fifth game only. Lockie Ferguson is out for the rest of the series. Coach Rob Walter said the changes reflect "a few movements throughout the series". Reports say Henry will be rested on Sunday to manage his workload, which would take away the bowler who ended the chase on Friday.

## Why Sunday matters

The series result is already safe in one sense: India can finish 3-1 or 2-2, but not behind. The match matters more for what comes after it. **The two-Test series begins in Wellington on November 19**, with the second Test in Christchurch from November 27, and one report says India need to win it to stay in contention for next June's World Test Championship final. The ODI World Cup is less than a year away, and India also lost a three-match ODI series 2-1 to England in July 2026. Friday's collapse has put the one-day batting against seam and bounce under the same scrutiny.
