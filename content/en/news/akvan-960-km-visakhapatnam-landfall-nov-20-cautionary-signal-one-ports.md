Andhra Pradesh is now planning for the Bay of Bengal depression to cross its coast **around November 20**, with winds of 110 to 120 km/h at landfall. In the early hours of Monday, November 16, the system lay about **960 km from Visakhapatnam**, according to the State Disaster Management Authority (APSDMA), and it is expected to become Cyclone Akvan later today. The Visakhapatnam Cyclone Warning Centre issued the landfall projection; it is a day later than the 19th that the national forecast pointed to on Sunday.

The distance is the first fresh position since our [report on Sunday evening's bulletin](/en/news/akvan-very-severe-nov-19-120-130-kmph-imd-sunday-evening), which put the system about 1,010 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam at 8:30 am on Sunday.

## Where the system is

APSDMA managing director Prakhar Jain said the depression moved **north-northwest at 15 km/h** over the previous six hours. Besides the 960 km to Visakhapatnam, it was about 800 km from Chennai, 620 km from Trincomalee in Sri Lanka and 680 km from Sri Vijaya Puram in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. That is about 50 km nearer than on Sunday morning.

## The stages, and where the forecasts differ

The authority expects the depression to become **a cyclonic storm today** over the southern and adjoining west-central Bay, a severe cyclonic storm on Tuesday, November 17, and a very severe cyclonic storm by Wednesday, November 18. It then expects the storm to hold that strength as it moves towards the coast.

That is faster than the India Meteorological Department's (IMD) Sunday table, which placed the very severe stage in the early hours of November 19, with winds of 120 to 130 km/h gusting to 145 km/h. The state's landfall figure of 110 to 120 km/h is slightly lower. The two sets of numbers will be tested once the IMD issues its Monday bulletins, the first at which a landfall coast could be named.

## Warnings already in force

An **orange alert** is in force for heavy to extremely heavy rain, especially on November 19 and 20. Squally winds of 40 to 60 km/h are expected along the coast until Tuesday and will rise after that. The IMD's Sunday forecast adds that isolated extremely heavy rain of 21 cm or more is possible over the north coast.

The IMD put the Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts under a **pre-cyclone watch** at 1:30 pm on Sunday, the first of its four warning stages. At sea, **Local Cautionary Signal No. 1** is hoisted at every Andhra port except Kalingapatnam. Fishermen have been told not to go to sea, and farmers and residents in vulnerable areas have been advised to take precautions. Fishermen were already asked on Sunday to be back on the coast, as our [Andhra report](/en/news/akvan-andhra-apsdma-north-andhra-nov-19-eight-districts-fishermen-return) records.

## A coast with little time to recover

Akvan is the third cyclone in about three weeks. Arnab crossed the coast in the Godavari delta on October 28 as a very severe storm, and Murasu crossed near Ongole on November 3 as a severe storm with winds of 100 to 110 km/h. Andhra's first estimate of the damage from Murasu was [about ₹1,919 crore](/en/news/murasu-andhra-damage-1919-crore-naidu-bapatla-guntur). A storm at 110 to 120 km/h would be stronger than Murasu was at landfall.

Our [explainer](/en/explainer/akvan-very-severe-cyclone-explained-storm-surge-landfall-warnings) sets out what a very severe storm means for surge and warnings.

## Why it matters

For fishing families, the instruction is simple: no trips to sea. For people in coastal villages, **a landfall around November 20** means about four days to move boats, secure roofs and stock food and water. The heavy rain of November 19 and 20 is forecast for a coast that has already taken two storms in three weeks. A shift of a day in the forecast changes when shelters have to open, which is why the authority says it will update the track as the storm forms.

## What happens next

The next marker is **today**, when the depression is expected to be named Akvan as a cyclonic storm. The IMD's Monday bulletins should show whether the landfall date moves back to November 19 or settles on the 20th, and whether a coast between Visakhapatnam and Gopalpur is named. The severe stage is due on Tuesday, November 17, and orange alerts for the north Andhra districts cover November 19 and 20.
