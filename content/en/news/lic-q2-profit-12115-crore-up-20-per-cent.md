Life Insurance Corporation of India, the country's largest insurer, reported a **20% rise in consolidated net profit to ₹12,115 crore** for the July-September quarter, up from ₹10,098 crore a year earlier. The state-run company announced the results in a stock exchange filing on Thursday evening, November 5, 2026.

Premiums drove the gain. Net premium income **grew 16.2% to ₹1.47 lakh crore**, from ₹1.27 lakh crore in the same quarter of 2025. Higher income from LIC's huge investment book added to the profit.

## New policies earn more

The sharper change was in how much LIC makes on what it sells. The value of new business, a measure of the profit the insurer expects over the life of policies sold in the quarter, **jumped 61% to ₹3,136 crore**. Its margin on that business rose 750 basis points to 22.9%, from 15.4% a year ago.

In other words, each rupee of new premium is now worth a good deal more to LIC than it was a year ago. Better profitability on new business, together with premium growth and higher investment income, is what the insurer's numbers point to as the **main drivers of the quarter**.

## Cleaner books

Bad loans in LIC's investment portfolio eased too. The **gross NPA ratio fell to 1.16%**, from 1.21% at the end of June.

The profit is lower than the ₹13,492 crore LIC earned in the April-June quarter, when profit had risen 22.8%. LIC's shares will trade on the results for the first time on Friday, in a market already under pressure from a sell-off across Asia.
