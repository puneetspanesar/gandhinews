India's unemployment rate eased to **4.9% in October**, the lowest in eight months, according to the monthly bulletin of the Periodic Labour Force Survey released by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation on Monday, November 16. The rate, measured on the current weekly status for people aged 15 and above, was 5.0% in September and 5.2% in October last year.

Labour force participation rose to **55.7% from 55.5%**, and the worker population ratio, the share of all people in the age group who are in work, went up to 53.0% from 52.7%. The survey covered 370,475 people, of whom 211,474 were in rural areas and 159,001 in cities and towns.

## Eight months of the series

October's reading is the lowest since February, when the rate was 4.9%. It is well below the **5.5% recorded in May and June**, the highest of the past year, and it has eased from June's 5.5% to 5.0% in August, holding there in September. The low of the period was 4.7% in November 2025.

## Villages and towns

The improvement was concentrated in cities. **Urban unemployment fell to 6.7%** from 6.9% in September, while the rural rate stayed at 4.2%. Rural labour force participation rose to 58.4% from 58.0% and the rural worker population ratio to 56.0% from 55.5%. In urban areas participation slipped to 50.5% from 50.7%, so the fall in joblessness there came partly from fewer people looking for work, not only from more people finding it. The urban worker population ratio was 47.1%.

## Women and men

Women made the month's clearest gain. **Female labour force participation rose to 35.0%** from 34.6% in September and 34.2% a year earlier. In rural areas it reached 39.5%, while among urban women it stayed at 25.9%. The female worker population ratio rose to 33.3% from 32.8%, against 73.7% for men.

Female unemployment dropped to 5.0% from 5.2%, and male unemployment stayed at 4.9%. The gap inside the female figure is wide: **urban women faced 8.7% unemployment**, down from 9.2%, while rural women faced 3.8%, down from 3.9%. Among rural men the rate was 4.3% and among urban men 6.0%.

## What it means

For households, a lower rate means a few more people in work, but the bulletin measures anyone who worked at least one hour in the week before the survey, so it does not show the quality or pay of the jobs. The rate has stayed in a narrow band of 4.7% to 5.5% across the past 13 months. The rural share of women in work, much of it on family farms, is why the female figures look healthier in villages than in cities, where the 8.7% rate shows how hard it is for educated urban women to find a job they want.

The reading comes in the week after retail inflation reached 6.09% in October, as covered in our [report on prices](/en/news/retail-inflation-october-2026-6-09-two-year-high), and ahead of the Reserve Bank of India's December policy review. Our [economy dashboard](/en/tracker/economy-dashboard) carries the monthly series.

## What comes next

The bulletin for November is expected in mid-December, on the pattern of earlier releases, when the August bulletin came on September 15 and October's on November 16. Whether the dip to 4.9% holds will show then; the rate has moved within 0.1 to 0.4 percentage points from month to month this year, and one month's change of 0.1 point is within that range.
