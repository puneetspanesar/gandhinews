Haryana will set up **special courts for SC/ST cases in five districts**, Chief Minister Nayab Singh Saini announced after chairing the State Level Vigilance and Monitoring Committee in Chandigarh on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. The courts are to come up in Hisar, Faridabad, Palwal, Rohtak and Bhiwani, and Saini told officials to **file chargesheets in 100% of cases within 60 days**.

Social Justice and Empowerment Minister Krishan Kumar Bedi attended the meeting, which reviewed how the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989, and the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955, are being enforced in the state.

## What the meeting decided

Saini told police and administrative officers that "no negligence or laxity in delivering justice to victims would be tolerated". He said ensuring justice and preventing a repeat of such incidents mattered as much as bringing down the number of crimes, and asked that the setting up of the special courts be speeded up.

Senior officers have been named **nodal officers at district and sub-divisional level** to track each investigation and see that cases are disposed of on time. Districts with the most cases will get special campaigns and detailed reviews to find out why, and police stations with high case counts will be placed under the watch of senior officers, with more police presence at sensitive locations. Long-pending cases are to be taken up first.

The government also plans to reward good records. The Panchayat Department has been asked to send in the names of panchayats where **no SC/ST case has been registered in the last two years**, so that they can be felicitated.

## The numbers the state gave

Director General of Police Ajay Singhal told the meeting that cases under the Act have **fallen by nearly 28%**. He said the 60-day chargesheet requirement is being applied, and that police are working to reach full compliance, which implies it has not yet been reached in every case. No report of the meeting gave the number of cases behind the 28% fall, the period it covers, or how many cases are pending in court.

Action has been taken against **10 police personnel for negligence** in handling victims' cases, the meeting was told. On the support side, **₹13.49 crore has gone to 1,046 victims** of atrocities for relief and rehabilitation in 2026-27 so far. Separately, 418 beneficiaries have received ₹10.45 crore under the state's Mukhyamantri Samajik Samrasta Antarjatiya Vivah Shagun Yojana, which supports inter-caste marriages.

## Why it matters

A chargesheet is the step that sends a case to trial, and delay at that stage is one of the ways cases stall. For a victim of caste violence, a special court closer to home and a fixed 60-day clock for the police are the practical changes on offer. Whether they bite depends on how quickly the courts are notified and staffed, and the government gave no date for that.

The review comes while the Supreme Court is being asked to look at other SC/ST questions: Karnataka has moved the court against a High Court verdict that [quashed its quota increase for the communities](/news/karnataka-moves-supreme-court-hc-quashes-sc-st-quota-hike). Those are separate matters from Haryana's enforcement push.

## What happens next

The meeting set no deadline for the five courts, and the state has not said how many judges or prosecutors they will have. The next steps to watch are the formal notification of the courts, the Panchayat Department's list of panchayats with no cases, and whether the district campaigns lift the 60-day chargesheet rate toward the 100% the chief minister has asked for.
