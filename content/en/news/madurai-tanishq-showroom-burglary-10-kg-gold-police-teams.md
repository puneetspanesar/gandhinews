Madurai police have cut the estimated loss in the burglary at a newly opened Tanishq showroom to **10 kg of gold and 1 kg of diamond ornaments**, down from the first reports of jewellery worth more than ₹21 crore. The revision came on November 14, 2026, two days after staff found the premises on Madurai Bypass Road broken into. No arrest has been made.

City Police Commissioner S. Rajendran said some jewellery was found undamaged, which brought the figure down after a fuller count. The showroom held about 42 kg of jewellery on display, and the first police assessment was that roughly 30% of the stock had gone.

## What police say happened

The burglary was discovered on the morning of Thursday, November 12, **15 days after the showroom was inaugurated**. At the scene that day, Commissioner Rajendran said three persons had broken the wall and door to get in. Deputy Commissioner of Police (South) A.G. Inigo Divyan said the intruders went only for items on display, and that jewellery kept in the lockers was untouched.

Divyan cautioned against guesswork. "We cannot state anything based on assumptions right now," he said on November 12. Rajendran, Divyan and Deputy Commissioner of Police (Traffic) S. Vanitha inspected the scene, and sniffer dogs were brought in.

Later reports describe the route differently. According to reports, the thief or thieves may have come in through the lift shaft and carried only what could be taken by hand. Reports also differ on whether one person or three were involved. Police have not settled the point in any statement read for this story.

## Security lapses

Rajendran has said the **anti-burglary alarm was not switched on** and that security personnel were negligent, which may have helped whoever broke in. Reports add that cameras were not being monitored round the clock.

CCTV footage shows a masked man at the showroom on three earlier occasions, according to reports. The same footage shows someone climbing the building on scaffolding on the night of November 5, which police believe was a failed first attempt. That would mean the planning began well before the night of the theft.

## The investigation

Special teams have been formed under **three Assistant Commissioners of Police**, each with three inspectors. They are examining CCTV footage from the surrounding roads to trace the route taken after the burglary.

Police are also looking at an inside job. Teams are questioning showroom employees and people who worked on the construction of the building, its interiors and the lift installation, since a thief who knew the layout would need little time inside.

On Friday, November 13, police searched the home of a 25-year-old man reported to be a suspect. The search lasted nearly three hours, but he was not at home and no jewellery was found, according to reports. He has not been charged, and police have made no public statement naming anyone.

## Why the figure changed

The first complaint spoke of 15 kg of gold and 3 kg of diamond jewellery, and early reports put the value above ₹21 crore. The showroom management revised its complaint after counting the valuables still on display. Management had not filed its full complaint when police first spoke on November 12, and police had then said the exact loss would be known only after it did.

The gap between the early and later figures matters for the investigation, because the weight and type of jewellery help police track buyers if the pieces are melted or sold. A smaller, better counted list also helps officers check what could be carried out of a building through a lift shaft or a broken wall.

## What happens next

Police have not announced when the teams expect results. The next steps visible so far are the review of CCTV from the area, the questioning of staff and contractors, and the management's final complaint with the verified inventory. The tracing of the missing pieces will depend on how quickly the thieves try to sell or melt them, so jewellers in Madurai and nearby districts are likely to be asked to report suspicious offers.

For customers, the question is whether the showroom stays open. Tanishq, part of Titan Company, has made no public statement that could be read for this report, and the showroom's own estimate remains the basis for the police count.
