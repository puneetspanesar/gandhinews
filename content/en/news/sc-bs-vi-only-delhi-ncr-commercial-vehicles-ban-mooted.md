The Supreme Court said on Monday, November 23, 2026, that it was clear it would **allow only BS-VI vehicles in Delhi**, "if not NCR". A bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana made the remark while hearing the vehicular emissions strand of its pollution case. It passed no direction: any binding rule will come only in a written order, after the states and authorities have filed their responses.

"We are clear we will allow only BS-VI vehicles in Delhi, if not NCR," the bench remarked orally, according to reports of the hearing. Bharat Stage VI is the current emission standard for cars, trucks, buses and two-wheelers sold in India. A BS-VI-only rule would go well beyond the seasonal curbs already applied to older vehicles registered outside the capital, which we reported in our earlier story.

## The commercial vehicle idea

The Chief Justice also asked how to ensure that commercial vehicles do not enter Delhi at all. The bench floated, without issuing any direction, an arrangement in which trucks would **unload at warehouses on the city's borders**, and goods would be carried in by battery-operated or other non-polluting vehicles. Justice Kant put the horizon at about five years: "This kind of arrangement may not be possible this year, may not be possible next year," he said, according to the reports. The court acknowledged that an immediate ban could have serious consequences and said the idea would need a phased approach.

The bench said it would examine further short-term and long-term measures. It also urged the neighbouring states to take part actively and directed Haryana, Punjab and Uttar Pradesh, according to reports, to file status reports on what they have done to reduce particulate pollution across the NCR.

## What the November 6 order asked for

The hearing followed an order of November 6, which we read on the court's record. It was passed in Suo Motu Writ Petition (Civil) No. 11 of 2026, *In re: Curbing Air Pollution in the National Capital Region*, which the court registered after its judgment of March 12, 2026, split the long-running pollution litigation into **five separate suo motu petitions**, one for each aspect of the problem. The court said this was a reshaping of the older case, not its closure.

On that day the amici curiae, Aparajita Singh, Uttara Babbar and Shibani Ghose, told the bench that compliance with its orders of January 21 and February 23, 2026, had not been properly reported. They presented data from the Graded Response Action Plan for October 21 to November 3, 2026, which showed **a substantial fall in stubble burning** in Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana and Rajasthan, but a sharp rise in problems linked to vehicular emissions.

The order noted that Delhi and the NCR carry traffic that originates not only within their own boundaries but also in Uttar Pradesh, Haryana and Rajasthan, including a large volume of commercial and non-commercial vehicles. It directed the Municipal Corporation of Delhi, the Delhi government, Delhi Police (Traffic) and the governments of Haryana, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh to file detailed responses on six points:

- the rules and enforcement for commercial and non-commercial vehicles entering or moving in Delhi-NCR, especially from neighbouring states;
- how non-conforming vehicles are identified and regulated, including enforcement of pollution-under-control certificates;
- steps to stop overage, end-of-life or non-compliant vehicles from entering or operating;
- enforcement figures: vehicles inspected, challaned, denied entry or otherwise proceeded against;
- coordination between Delhi and neighbouring states on inter-state movement and emission compliance;
- short-term and long-term measures, with a time-bound roadmap.

The states were given two weeks to file submissions on the steps taken, their responses to suggestions from the Commission for Air Quality Management and the amici, and any action they propose on their own.

## Why it matters

Vehicle emissions are the strand the court's own data flagged on November 6. Stubble fires fell, but the amici reported a sharp rise in issues tied to vehicles. For a city that sits under GRAP restrictions each winter, a BS-VI-only rule would decide which cars and trucks can enter, whether they are registered in Delhi or outside it, and how goods reach shops and markets.

No reaction from the Centre, the Delhi government or transport bodies was found at the time of writing.

## What happens next

The bench has not set out a date for the next hearing on vehicles in the reports read. The court's calendar for the connected petitions is fixed: solid waste, crop residue and firecracker burning on November 25, construction, power plants and industry on November 26, and green cover on November 30. Any BS-VI-only direction would appear in a written order, which readers can check on the Supreme Court's website.
