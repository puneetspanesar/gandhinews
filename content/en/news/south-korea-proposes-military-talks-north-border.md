South Korea on Friday proposed military talks with North Korea to pin down exactly where their land border runs and to reopen military communication lines, as tension builds along the frontier.

Kim Hong-cheol, the deputy defence minister for policy, told reporters the talks would aim to "mutually confirm" the precise location of the Military Demarcation Line on land, and "discuss ways to restore military communication lines and prevent accidental clashes in border areas".

No date has been set. "Specific dates and locations could be discussed through Panmunjom," Kim said, referring to the truce village. Seoul was also open to three-way talks that include the US-led United Nations Command, he said, and hoped for "a positive response from North Korea".

The Ministry of National Defence said it was hard to predict "what further incidents could occur if this situation is allowed to continue unaddressed". Three South Korean soldiers were hurt last month by blasts near the western stretch of the Demilitarised Zone. Seoul blamed mines laid by the North and demanded an apology; Pyongyang denies it.

North Korea has been fortifying its side with mines, barbed wire, tactical roads and anti-tank walls. On Wednesday, Kim Yo Jong, the sister of leader Kim Jong Un, said new military units had been moved into "firing positions" along the border.

The odds of a reply are slim. Seoul made a similar offer last year and heard nothing back. When President Lee Jae Myung called for a return to dialogue, Kim Yo Jong dismissed the idea of bilateral talks as "the climax of the farce".
