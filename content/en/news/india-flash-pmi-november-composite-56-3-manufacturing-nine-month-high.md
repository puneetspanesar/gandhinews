India's private sector kept growing at a steady pace in November, with **factories doing the work**. The HSBC Flash India Composite PMI Output Index, compiled by S&P Global, stood at **56.3, against 56.2 in October**, the survey showed on Monday, November 23, 2026. The manufacturing PMI, which blends orders, output, jobs, delivery times and stocks, rose to **56.0 from 54.7, its highest in nine months**.

Services told a softer story. The services business activity index slipped to 55.3 from 55.8, and service providers cut staff for the first time in almost a year, though only marginally. Any reading above 50 means growth from the month before.

## What the survey found

Factory output moved faster than the headline. The manufacturing output index rose to **58.3 from 57.0**. Total new business grew at its fastest pace since June, and the gain came from manufacturers, where demand picked up. Services demand was unchanged from October.

Firms pointed to favourable market demand and new customer wins, and some to marketing, discounts and better customer service. Demand was reported stronger in pharmaceuticals and healthcare, chemicals, electronic components, metals, power transmission equipment, logistics, financial services, IT and infrastructure.

Export orders rose at their fastest pace in four months. Goods producers still led, though their growth slowed, while service providers saw a sharp rise in new foreign business. Employment across the private sector rose only marginally: **manufacturers hired at their fastest rate in seven months**, which offset the cut in services.

HSBC's chief India economist, Pranjul Bhandari, said: "Manufacturing activity gathered momentum, with output and new orders accelerating." She added: "The increase in new orders was driven by stronger domestic demand, while new export orders moderated a bit."

## Stockpiling and prices

Manufacturers bought inputs at their sharpest rate since May, and stocks of purchases grew at the quickest pace in nine months. Stocks of **finished goods rose by the most since the survey began in March 2005**, a sign that producers are building inventory ahead of expected sales, though it also means unsold goods are piling up if demand fails to follow. Supplier delivery times shortened again, but the improvement was the smallest in five months.

Costs stayed in check. Input-cost inflation was subdued in both sectors, with operating expenses rising only slightly. Selling prices rose faster than in October but still modestly, and at the same pace in manufacturing and services. Bhandari said input-cost inflation stayed subdued while output-price inflation rose modestly, which supports margins.

Business confidence for the year ahead was the **best since May**, in both goods and services, but remained below its long-run average.

## How we got here

The composite index was 58.1 in the flash reading for May, when HSBC's Bhandari warned that "cost pressures intensified, with input prices rising at the sharpest rate since July 2022". November's 56.3 is the highest since June, yet still below the first half of the year, which points, according to reports, to a modest recovery rather than a strong rebound.

The manufacturing PMI had slipped to 54.7 in October, as [we reported on November 2](/en/news/india-manufacturing-pmi-october-final-54-7-export-orders), when the final reading came in under both September's 55.1 and the flash estimate of 54.9. November's flash reverses that fall. It also fits the October core-sector numbers, in which [core industries grew 10.9%](/en/news/core-sector-growth-10-9-per-cent-october-iron-ore-coal-electricity), led by iron ore, coal and electricity.

## Why the RBI will read it closely

The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% on October 7, its first increase since February 2023. In the [minutes](/en/news/rbi-mpc-minutes-rate-cuts-off-table-two-members-oppose-tightening), Governor Sanjay Malhotra said the next move could only be a hike or a pause, and cited "increasing signs of generalisation of inflation".

Official price data are already hot. [Retail inflation was 6.09% in October](/en/news/retail-inflation-october-2026-6-09-two-year-high), a two-year high, and [wholesale inflation 12.29%](/en/news/wpi-inflation-october-2026). The PMI adds two points the committee can use. Demand is strong in factories, which argues against easing. But firms report only slight increases in input costs and modest selling-price rises, so the survey does not show a fresh acceleration in prices. Services, the larger part of the economy, are growing more slowly and shedding jobs at the margin.

For households, the practical link is the cost of loans. A second increase in December would raise the interest on floating-rate home and car loans, while a pause would leave it unchanged.

## What happens next

These are flash figures, built from about 85% to 90% of survey responses collected from November 11 to 18, and they can be revised when the final November manufacturing and services readings come out in early December, around the time the MPC meets from December 2 to 4. November's retail inflation is due only on December 14, after the meeting, so the PMI is one of the few fresh data points the committee will have before it decides.
