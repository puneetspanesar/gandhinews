**Update, November 23, 2:20 pm IST:** The Tamil Nadu government's review petition is also reported to ask for an **interim stay of the Supreme Court's November 20 directions** and for a hearing in open court before the ceremony. According to reports published on Monday, the state's case is that lighting the lamp before the main dispute is decided could have irreversible consequences: once it is lit, a later order cannot undo it, and the existing arrangement should stay undisturbed until the competing claims are examined. The petition is also reported to question the permission being given without a prima facie finding on the claimed custom, and to say the review would become infructuous once the ceremony is held, which is why urgent consideration is sought. No report read by this desk gives a hearing date or a bench for the stay plea, and the court's own record of the petition was not available.

The Tamil Nadu government has asked the Supreme Court to **review its order on the Thiruparankundram lamp**. The Commissioner of the state's Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments (HR&CE) Department filed the review petition on Saturday, November 21, a day after a bench of Justices Aravind Kumar and Vipul M Pancholi allowed the Karthigai Deepam to be lit at the Deepathoon, a stone pillar above Madurai, as a one-time measure on November 24. The BJP attacked the state on Sunday, November 22.

The case is titled Commissioner HR & CE v Rama Ravikumar and connected matters. No date has been given for hearing it. The main appeals are set for the second week of January 2027, and the lighting is still due on Tuesday, November 24.

## The BJP's attack

BJP spokesperson and Rajya Sabha member Sudhanshu Trivedi told reporters on Sunday that the state's move reflected an **"anti-Hindu and anti-Sanatan Dharma" mindset** of the Congress and its allies. He called the conduct "very unfortunate", said the government was "hell-bent on not implementing" the Supreme Court's judgment, and demanded that it be implemented "immediately". The Congress is a partner in the state government led by the Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK) under Chief Minister C Joseph Vijay.

The state government's own account of the petition was not on record on Sunday evening. Law Minister R Nirmal Kumar was reported to have said the government was consulting legal experts, and Social Justice Minister Vanni Arasu was reported to have called the verdict "unacceptable", saying it could cause "unnecessary confusion". Both statements rest on a single report and are given with that caution.

## What the order says

The Supreme Court on Friday **stayed the Madras High Court's findings** on the Thiruparankundram hill dispute and allowed the Karthigai Deepam lamp to be lit at the Deepathoon, a stone pillar near a dargah above Madurai, as a **one-time measure on November 24**. A bench of Justices Aravind Kumar and Vipul M Pancholi said the lighting cannot be cited as proof of custom, and the main appeals will be heard in the second week of January 2027.

The court gave the "peculiar nature of the occasion" and the nearness of the festival as its reasons. In its words, as reported, the opportunity to perform the ceremony on that day, if denied, "cannot be restored by any subsequent adjudication".

The court stayed **paragraphs 1 to 140** of the High Court Division Bench's judgment of January 6, 2026, in so far as they are challenged, until further orders. It then set out a narrow arrangement.

A restricted team organised by the temple's Devasthanam will light the lamp. The Madurai District Collector, as nodal officer, fixes the size and make-up of the team after consulting the temple's Executive Officer, the Archaeological Survey of India and the Madurai Police Commissioner. The district administration decides the time, the route and how long the team stays, and the team must leave straight after the lighting and leave nothing behind.

Only authorised Devasthanam representatives and government officials may go to the spot. "Members of the public would not be permitted to go to the location," the bench said. **Processions, public gatherings, demonstrations and slogans are barred**, as are excavation, construction and any structural change. Worship at the customary sites and lawful access to the dargah and mosque must continue undisturbed.

Police must provide security, with central paramilitary help if needed. The Collector must report any obstruction to the court at once and file a compliance report within three days after the festival.

The bench was equally firm on what it did not decide. The permission is not an endorsement of any claimed custom, cannot be used as evidence of practice or to claim the same right in later years, and leaves open who owns the pillar, whether lighting there is an essential religious practice and the questions under Articles 25 and 26 of the Constitution. Those articles are already before a nine-judge Constitution Bench in the Sabarimala reference.

## How the dispute reached the Supreme Court

The hill carries the Arulmigu Subramaniya Swamy temple and the Sikandar Badusha dargah. For generations the Karthigai Deepam has been lit at the Deepa Mandapam near the Uchipillaiyar temple. A devotee, Rama Ravikumar, asked the temple to light it also at the Deepathoon, about 50 metres from the dargah. The temple's Executive Officer refused, and he went to the High Court.

On December 1, 2025, Justice G R Swaminathan held the pillar to be a Deepathoon. When the temple did not comply, he allowed the petitioners to light it themselves on December 3. Police stopped them citing a prohibitory order, and the lamp was lit only at the customary site, amid protests and about 50 detentions. A Division Bench upheld that order the next day, and on **January 6, 2026** it also upheld the main direction, calling the State's law-and-order fears an "imaginary ghost created by state authorities for their convenience".

The State's petition came on June 11, after the new Tamil Nadu government took office. On August 3 the same bench issued notice but declined to stay the High Court order, and asked why a petition against a January order was filed only in June.

## What each side argued

For the State, Senior Advocate Jaideep Gupta said there is no proof of a custom of lighting at the pillar and that the Agamas prescribe the lamp near the Uchipillaiyar temple. Gopal Sankaranarayanan, for the Madurai Collector, said: "This pillar came into existence for a completely different purpose. Nothing to do with religious beliefs." He called it a survey pillar, and warned that treating such markers as religious structures would spread claims. Shekhar Naphade, for the HR&CE Commissioner, asked: "If some practice has been going on for 100 years, why change it?"

For the dargah, Senior Advocate Huzefa Ahmadi said the single judge inspected the site without notice to the dargah and that a new custom needs a civil suit, not a writ petition. Amit Tiwari said a 1923 decree gives the dargah the whole hillock. Appearing for the lead petitioner, Guru Krishnakumar showed photographs he said prove there are two separate peaks and that the Deepathoon is not on the dargah's.

## Reactions

Tamil Nadu BJP president Nainar Nagenthran called the order a "hammer blow" to communal politics. Hindu Munnani state president Kadeswara C Subramaniam called it a "historic turning point" and urged devotees to visit Thiruparankundram on November 24. K Annamalai thanked the court, asked it to consider lasting relief from next year, and said he trusted the state government "will follow the court directives".

Mohan G, for petitioner Hindu Dharma Parishad, welcomed the ruling but called it "a temporary judgment", with documents due in January. For the dargah, advocate M Bazeerdeen said: "We do not accept this order." He said the side will file a review petition.

The state's stand before the order is on record from its counsel in court; its position on the review petition is described above.

## What happens on November 24, and next

A review petition does not by itself stay the order, and nothing reported so far says the court will hear the review or the reported stay plea before the festival. Unless the court says otherwise, the arrangement for November 24 stands. Because the state itself argues that the lighting cannot be undone, the court's response on Monday or Tuesday decides whether the review is heard at all.

The lamp is to be lit on **Tuesday, November 24**, by the team the Collector names, at a time the administration fixes. The Hindu Munnani's call to devotees to gather is at odds with the order, which bars the public from the spot, so the district administration's arrangements will matter. The Collector's report is due within three days of the festival.

The appeals will be heard in the second week of January 2027, when the court takes up ownership of the pillar and the custom claim.
