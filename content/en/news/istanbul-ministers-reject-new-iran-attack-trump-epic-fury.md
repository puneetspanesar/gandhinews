Foreign ministers from Britain, France, Germany and Italy and from Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Egypt closed a day of talks in Istanbul on Friday with a plain message for Washington and Tehran: no new war.

Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, who hosted the meeting, said none of the participants approved of another military intervention or attack on Iran, and that diplomacy and dialogue were the only way to end the conflict. He described the new grouping as having "several advantages, particularly because it is informal and flexible and focuses specifically on the issues we face."

The eight countries did not issue a joint text. Their ministers spoke instead.

## A warning about oil

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said the group would first call on both sides to "exercise the utmost restraint, because we cannot afford another escalation in the region". A new round of fighting, he said, "would create nothing but losers and would undoubtedly have very serious consequences for hydrocarbon prices".

Italy's Antonio Tajani, who joined the European trio of the UK, France and Germany, said the aim was "de-escalation to guarantee freedom of navigation". He added: "I do not believe the problem can be solved from a purely military standpoint."

Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty said he had pressed for lower tensions, a US-Iran dialogue and a just peace in Palestine. Before the talks, the office of British Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said he would push for a diplomatic deal that restores navigation rights in the Strait of Hormuz, and warn that Iranian tactics were spreading to the Bab el-Mandeb and the Suez Canal.

Gaza, the war in Yemen and the prospects for ending the war in Ukraine were also on the table.

## Washington signals more

The appeal came as President Donald Trump kept the door open to new strikes. He told reporters on Friday that the US "may do it a little bit further" against Iran, and that a move would come "fairly soon", without naming a target or a timetable. Later, he suggested Washington "may do another little Epic Fury".

The US blockade of Iranian shipping has now run for 100 days, and US Central Command says it has kept at least **$25 billion** of Iranian crude off the market. Brent crude touched $115 a barrel on Friday evening and $117.30 overnight.

## The Saudi question

The meeting also turned on the defence of Saudi Arabia, which faces near-daily Houthi missile and drone attacks. Turkey said the Mecca defence pact it has signed with Pakistan and Saudi Arabia is not aimed at any country. Egypt has not decided whether to join; President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi has said Cairo would defend Saudi territory but would not take part in attacks on Yemen.

## What it means for India

India was not part of the talks, but the war is keeping thousands of Indians on the move. The Ministry of External Affairs said on Friday that about 3,000 stranded Umrah pilgrims had returned from Saudi Arabia over the past week, and more than 10,000 others had come home on scheduled flights. IndiGo has extended its Riyadh suspension to October 31, and Akasa Air has stopped Riyadh flights until November 30.
