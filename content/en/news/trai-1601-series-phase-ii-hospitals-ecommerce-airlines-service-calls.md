Calls from big hospitals, airlines, e-commerce sites, food and ride-hailing apps and car dealers about your order, booking or bill are to come from **numbers starting with 1601**. The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) on Friday extended the 1601 series, set aside for genuine service and transactional voice calls, to 13 more sectors and to TV distribution platforms.

The direction tells telecom operators to **finish onboarding and moving every eligible company within 180 days**. Counted from November 27, 2026, that runs to May 26, 2027.

## What the 1601 series is

India now has two "trusted" calling series. The **1600 series is for banks, other financial firms, insurers and government**: the Department of Telecommunications (DoT) set it aside in December 2024, and TRAI ordered banks, NBFCs and SEBI- and PFRDA-regulated firms to adopt it in phases from January 1, 2026.

The 1601 series is the same idea for everyone else. DoT allocated it on June 30, 2026, and TRAI's first direction, on August 10, opened it to utilities, logistics and courier companies. The numbers go only to verified companies, so a call from a 1601 number should be a business you deal with, not a fraudster using an ordinary ten-digit mobile number and claiming to be one.

> "Being a trusted channel, the numbers from this series are allocated only to the verified entities, and thereby helping consumers to easily identify legitimate service and transactional calls."
>
> Source: TRAI, through the Press Information Bureau, November 27, 2026

The bar is high. A company in these sectors qualifies only if it had **annual turnover of ₹500 crore or more** in at least one of the last three financial years, and it must show proof to its operator. DTH, HITS, IPTV and cable operators qualify if they had **at least one million subscribers** in any month of the past year. In every case the company must be registered in India, verified by the operator and registered on the operators' DLT platform for commercial communications.

## No sales calls allowed

Every company given a 1601 number must sign an undertaking with set conditions. It may call only customers it has a relationship with, and **never for promotional calls**. A customer's inquiry allows calls for **at most seven days**; a customer's application for a product or service allows calls for at most three months, or until the relationship ends, whichever is earlier. If a complaint alleges a 1601 number was used for selling, the company must produce proof of its relationship with that customer, and the operator must act against it under TRAI's rules.

Companies that got 1601 numbers in Phase I must now sign the same undertaking.

## What the direction does not say

The direction's title speaks of the **"mandatory adoption" of the 1601 series**, and operators must move "all eligible Principal Entities" in 180 days. But it puts the duty on operators. Unlike TRAI's 2025 order for banks, which barred them from making service calls from any number outside the 1600 series after their deadlines, the 1601 direction does not say what happens to an eligible company that keeps calling from ordinary mobile numbers after May 2027.

## What happens next

Allocation to the new sectors **starts at once**. Operators must publicise the series, file a status report with TRAI every 15 days naming each company and its 1601 number, and certify completion within 15 days after the 180 days end. TRAI says it will review how the series is used, including consumer complaints, and may add more sectors later.

For now, a smaller hospital or a regional retailer below the ₹500 crore line will keep calling from ordinary numbers. The test for consumers is simple only for the companies that clear it.
