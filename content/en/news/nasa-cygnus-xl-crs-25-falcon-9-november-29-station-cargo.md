NASA is aiming to launch a fresh load of science and supplies to the International Space Station on **Sunday, November 29, at 10:49 am EST**, or later if plans change. The Cygnus XL cargo spacecraft will fly on a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida.

It is the **25th cargo spacecraft Northrop Grumman has built for NASA** under contract, and the mission is named CRS-25.

## What is on board

Besides food, supplies and equipment for the crew, Cygnus carries several experiments. One is **hardware for making tiny, DNA-inspired particles** that could improve how drugs are delivered for cancer and osteoarthritis. Another is a study that may help guide cancer treatment by predicting whether a patient's cancer will spread.

Researchers will also test **human muscle cells and medications** aimed at muscle loss, a known problem for astronauts in long spaceflight. A compact radiation sensor is meant to improve crew safety.

## Arrival and stay

After launch, astronauts will use the station's Canadarm2 robotic arm to capture Cygnus and attach it to the **Earth-facing port of the Unity module**. The spacecraft is planned to stay until May 2027.

US media must apply for accreditation by 11:59 pm EST on Thursday, November 12. The date is a target and can move with weather and range schedules.
