Prime Minister Narendra Modi **inaugurated 173 projects and laid foundation stones for 73 more** on Sunday, November 15, 2026, in a tribal-welfare package worth about ₹3,000 crore, joining the Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas programme at Gadchiroli in Maharashtra by video link on the 151st birth anniversary of Bhagwan Birsa Munda. The event closed the Janjatiya Gaurav Pakhwada, a fortnight of tribal outreach held from November 1 to 15.

In his address Modi said **tribals now have full rights over bamboo**, and that earlier governments had neither understood tribal communities nor cared about them. We had reported on Saturday the [list of projects the Prime Minister's Office announced](/en/news/modi-janjatiya-gaurav-diwas-gadchiroli-246-tribal-projects-3000-crore); the event itself added the Prime Minister's own words and the accounts of three beneficiaries he spoke to.

## What was opened

The 173 inaugurations include **38 Eklavya Model Residential Schools** for tribal students and **100 Multi-Purpose Centres** under PM-JANMAN, along with other works under PM-JANMAN and the Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan. He also inaugurated the Tribal Research Institute building in Itanagar, Arunachal Pradesh, and the Ropuiliani Tribal Freedom Fighter Museum in Aizawl, Mizoram, which we covered [when the museum project was announced](/en/news/modi-ropuiliani-museum-kelsih-mizoram-25-59-crore-tribal-freedom-fighters).

The 73 foundation stones are for **19 new Eklavya schools**, 12 Tribal Multi-purpose Marketing Centres and 42 other projects under the two schemes. The projects span education, health, livelihoods and community infrastructure.

## The bamboo remark

The most pointed passage of the speech was on forests. Modi said the British had classified bamboo as a "tree", so that **cutting it could land a tribal in jail**, and that the law should have been scrapped after Independence. He said his government ended it and "made sure that the tribals have full rights over bamboo". He also said that "some individuals in the government neither understood the tribal communities nor cared about them", according to a report of the speech.

The legal change he referred to was made in his government's first term. The Indian Forest Act of 1927 defined a tree to include bamboos. On November 23, 2017, the Environment Ministry announced an ordinance that **removed bamboo grown outside forests from the definition of a tree** and ended the need for felling and transit permits for it. Then Environment Minister Harsh Vardhan said the aim was to "promote cultivation of bamboo in non-forest areas". Bamboo growing inside forest areas, the ministry said, would continue to be governed by the 1927 Act. A Bill to replace the ordinance was introduced in the Lok Sabha on December 18, 2017.

On the scope of "full rights", the 2017 amendment freed bamboo on private and other non-forest land, which is where cultivation and trade happen. It did not change the status of bamboo in forest areas, and the reports of Sunday's speech did not say which provision he meant beyond the old tree classification.

## Three voices from the scheme

Modi interacted with beneficiaries linked to the event. **Krishna Nagorao Teka of Yavatmal**, Maharashtra, said his family once struggled with rain and pests in their old home and now has a permanent house, a toilet and other facilities under PM-JANMAN, adding "We are very grateful to you." Modi replied that "nothing gives me greater satisfaction than this" and said all facilities had reached his village.

**Vajir, from Hathakundi village in Netrang tehsil of Bharuch district**, Gujarat, said he was trained in bamboo work in 2004 under the New Gujarat Pattern Yojana and has since also benefited from PM-JANMAN. He thanked the state and central governments. **Sabavat Anvesh of Hyderabad** thanked the Prime Minister for the PM SHRI schools, saying they help people complete higher education.

## How we got here

The Prime Minister has used Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas to open tribal projects away from Delhi in recent years. Last year, on the 150th anniversary at Dediapada in Gujarat, he inaugurated and laid foundation stones for works worth over ₹9,700 crore, according to the Press Information Bureau. This year's package is **about a third of that in rupee terms**, with 38 Eklavya schools inaugurated against 42 then, 100 centres against 228, and 19 new Eklavya foundation stones against 50.

The Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan, behind many of the listed works, was launched on October 2, 2024, at Hazaribagh in Jharkhand with an outlay of ₹79,156 crore over five years, covering about 63,843 tribal-majority villages.

## Why it matters

For tribal families the practical items are residential schools, service centres and housing. The Eklavya schools and multi-purpose centres are meant to bring education and government services into the habitation. Whether each new school has teachers and students in place was not part of Sunday's announcements. The bamboo remark bears on income, because permit rules decide whether tribal families can legally cut and sell it.

## What happens next

The Pakhwada ends today. Maharashtra's own Birsa Munda Gaurav Week runs to November 18, with document-help camps on November 16 and health and nutrition programmes on November 17. The Prime Minister's next engagement is the [ASEAN-India summit in Manila](/en/news/modi-asean-india-summit-manila-november-16-18) from November 16 to 18.
