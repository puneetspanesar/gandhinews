India used its opening statement at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya to tell developed countries that **they must fulfil their climate finance obligations** under the Paris Agreement, a day after negotiators settled a bruising agenda fight in which a stand-alone Climate Finance Work Programme survived but two other African and developing-country demands did not.

Narendra Bhooshan, Secretary in the Union Environment Ministry, delivered India's statement, which the government released on November 10. He pointed to **Article 9.1 of the Paris Agreement**, which obliges developed countries to provide financial resources to developing ones. Public finance, India said, remained central to supporting climate action in developing nations, and equity "has become particularly urgent" given the growing risk of crossing 1.5°C of warming. India welcomed the Turkish presidency's idea of a "COP of Implementation" and aligned itself with the G77 and China, the Like-Minded Developing Countries and the BASIC group.

## How the agenda was settled

The conference opened on Monday, November 9, and the inauguration was delayed by more than an hour while delegations argued over what would be discussed. Developing countries wanted a separate item for the Climate Finance Work Programme, which grew out of the COP30 decision at Belém. It is a **two-year programme under Article 9.1**.

When the agenda was adopted at the opening plenary, **the work programme was in it as its own item**. Funding for the Green Climate Fund will be taken up under an existing item. Consultations on mountains and climate change were added, feeding a research dialogue next to the 66th session of the scientific advisory body.

Two things the African Group had sought did not make it. The presidency proposed dropping **a separate item on tripling adaptation finance** and another on climate-related trade measures, and both went.

## What each side said

The European Union objected to the standalone work programme. It argued that COP30 did not create a separate agenda item, that the programme already has an agreed scope and mandate, and that it should wrap up within two years. It said it remains committed to climate finance and the $300 billion a year goal agreed at Baku. On trade, it said the existing three-year platform on trade and climate change should stay the forum.

India disagreed. It maintained that COP30 had agreed to a standalone item, and it **expressed regret that the adaptation finance proposal was excluded**. India has also raised concerns about unilateral trade measures such as the EU's carbon border tax.

Before the conference, EU Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra called any new climate finance demands at COP31 "dead in the water". An EU official, citing OECD data, said the bloc supplied a third of the $136.7 billion in public climate finance given to developing countries last year, above the $100 billion a year target set in 2009.

Avantika Goswami of the Centre for Science and Environment said the work programme should provide a space within the COP political process to scrutinise the quality and predictability of climate finance.

## Why the numbers matter

The gap between money and need is the backdrop. The UN Environment Programme's Adaptation Gap Report 2026 puts international public adaptation finance at **$28.2 billion to $31.7 billion in 2024**, only about $1 billion more than a year earlier. Developing countries' yearly adaptation needs are estimated at **$305 billion by 2035**, or about $420 billion with inflation. Even tripling today's flows would cover only 20% to 29% of that need.

For India, the stakes are in farms and coasts: its pre-conference position links adaptation to agriculture, food security and the livelihoods of small and marginal farmers, and wants adaptation indicators to stay voluntary so they cannot become conditions for getting money.

## Leaders arrive, with gaps

The World Leaders' Summit runs on Wednesday, November 11 and Thursday, November 12. A preview published at 10:38 am IST today said Turkey's Recep Tayyip Erdogan, Brazil's Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and the European Commission's Ursula von der Leyen are due to speak, while the leaders of France, Germany, Italy, Canada and Japan are not expected and China is sending its Vice President. Prime Minister Narendra Modi is not attending; Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav, who is to chair a panel on climate mitigation, leads the Indian delegation.

## What happens next

The conference runs to **November 20**, when the cover decision is due. The finance work programme will now run for two years, the EU is still contesting its standalone status, and the seventh review of the financial mechanism has been deferred to COP32. Subsidiary-body chairs will invite submissions for the next trade dialogue at the Bonn session.
