The Reserve Bank of India's latest study of village governments finds that gram panchayats **depend on transfers from other governments for about 90% of their receipts**, with own-source revenue a modest 10%. The RBI released the second edition of its biennial report, on the theme "Local Finances and Capacity Building in Rural India", on November 19, 2026 ([press release 2026-2027/1524 on the RBI site](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=64002)).

It uses a **balanced panel of 2.25 lakh gram panchayats** for 2022-23 to 2024-25. Reports on the study put that at 85.4% of all gram panchayats, across 25 States.

## What the RBI says

The release says gram panchayats "remain dependent on intergovernmental transfers", which account for about 90% of total receipts. Own-source revenue is about 10%, **predominantly non-tax receipts** such as interest, Panchayati Raj programme funds and user charges, so property taxes and licence fees are a small part of it. The RBI says panchayat finances strengthened during 2022-23 and 2023-24.

On spending, **water supply and sanitation is the largest item, about 40% of the total**. The RBI says spending mainly reflected the mandates of the Fifteenth Finance Commission and the convergence of panchayat funds with national schemes and missions.

The release also notes that notable progress has been made in rural connectivity, electrification, water supply, sanitation and community assets, but that **inter-State disparities persist** in access, quality and functionality.

## What reports on the study add

Reports on the full study give rupee figures. They put the panel's receipts at ₹52,014 crore in 2023-24 and ₹43,537 crore in 2024-25 on provisional data, a fall of 16.3%, while spending rose to ₹50,457 crore. They also report that water supply and sanitation took 44.5% of spending in 2024-25, and that the largest quarter of panchayats hold about 52% of receipts and spending. These figures are not in the RBI's release and are reported, not confirmed by us.

## Growing villages, growing bills

The RBI looks at large gram panchayats that are turning into **Town Panchayats**, small urban local bodies. As they do, it says, "functional responsibilities may expand faster than fiscal and administrative preparedness". For a rural household the stakes are the taps, drains and village roads that panchayats pay for, and the quality of those services depends on how much panchayats can raise and decide themselves.

## What the RBI recommends

The release calls for **transitional fiscal support and administrative capacity building** for panchayats making that shift. It also asks for "a calibrated strengthening of OSR through improved collection efficiency and rationalised property taxes and license fees". Reports on the study add calls for updated asset registers, more transparent and standardised financial data, and more predictable transfers.

## Background and what next

The Finance Ministry's bank camps, which [opened in 2.7 lakh panchayats and towns on October 21](/en/news/bank-camps-2-7-lakh-panchayats-financial-inclusion-drive) and run until December 31, show the same bodies carrying new duties for the banking system. The RBI publishes this report every two years, so the next edition is due in about two years. Until then, the 90% figure is the baseline against which any push for panchayats to raise more of their own money will be judged.
