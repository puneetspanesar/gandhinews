The India Meteorological Department (IMD) raised its outlook for the Bay of Bengal system on **Saturday, November 14**, saying it could grow into **a severe cyclonic storm around November 18**, with winds of 85 to 95 km/h and gusts up to 105 km/h. The Bhubaneswar Meteorological Centre followed with a run of district warnings for Odisha, including **orange alerts for very heavy rain from November 19 to 21**. The IMD has still not said which coast the storm will cross.

This is a step up from the forecast reported earlier on Saturday, which stopped at a cyclonic storm by Monday evening. That forecast is in our [morning report](/en/news/akvan-track-uncertain-andhra-odisha-imd-saturday).

## What the IMD update says

A well-marked low-pressure area was observed over the southeast Bay of Bengal at 8:30 am on Saturday. The IMD expects it to become a depression within about 24 hours, then **a cyclonic storm over the central Bay on the evening of November 16**, when it would be named Akvan, a name contributed by Iran. It is moving north-westwards and is expected to turn north-north-westwards.

Rain follows the track. The IMD forecasts heavy rain of 7 to 11 cm at isolated places on November 18 and **very heavy rain of 12 to 20 cm** in some districts on November 19 and 20 across Andhra Pradesh, Odisha and West Bengal. Over the sea, a separate warning speaks of squalls of about 60 km/h, gusting to 80 km/h, from November 17 for two days.

## Odisha's warnings district by district

The Bhubaneswar centre's warnings run in three bands.

On **November 18 and 19** a yellow alert for heavy rain covers Gajapati, Ganjam, Puri and Khordha, with thunderstorm gusts of 40 to 50 km/h in many districts. On **November 19 and 20** the alert for Gajapati, Ganjam and Puri turns orange, for heavy to very heavy rain, with gusts of 50 to 60 km/h. Yellow alerts cover Kendrapara, Jagatsinghpur, Cuttack, Kandhamal, Rayagada, Koraput, Malkangiri, Khordha and Nayagarh.

On **November 20 and 21** orange alerts widen to Jagatsinghpur, Cuttack, Kandhamal, Gajapati, Ganjam, Puri, Khordha and Nayagarh. Yellow alerts then reach Balasore, Bhadrak, Jajpur, Kendrapara, Keonjhar, Mayurbhanj, Boudh, Kalahandi, Nabarangpur, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri. Along and off the south Odisha coast, winds of 50 to 60 km/h, gusting to 70 km/h, are forecast from November 18 to the morning of November 20, and the sea is expected to be rough to very rough from November 17 to 21.

Fishermen at sea have been advised to return to the coast by November 16 and not to venture along or off the south Odisha coast from the morning of November 17 to November 20. The Special Relief Commissioner's office is reported to be staying operational for two days to monitor the system.

## Where it may land

No landfall has been announced. Weather models lean towards the Andhra Pradesh coast, and one agricultural meteorologist, Surendranath Pashupalak, said the system could approach the coast **between Gopalpur and Visakhapatnam around November 17 and 18**. A landfall near Machilipatnam, he indicated, would limit the impact on Odisha, while one near Visakhapatnam could bring heavier rain there.

VR Durai, director of the Area Cyclone Warning Centre in Chennai, said weather models pointed to north Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha. "As of now, the weather models indicate the cyclone would not come towards north Tamil Nadu coast," he said, adding that the exact track would be known only after the cyclone forms on the evening of November 16. Tamil Nadu is forecast only light to moderate showers, mainly in the Western Ghats and southern districts.

Forecasters differ on timing. Some models show formation on November 16 and others on November 18, according to Sarat Chandra Sahu of SOA University, and meteorologist Biswajit Sahu said the track and intensity should be clearer after November 15.

## Why it matters

Paddy in coastal Odisha is close to harvest. Farmers quoted in reports fear that continuous rain or strong wind could flatten standing crops and shed mature grain, and that vegetables could suffer from waterlogging. Ganjam, named in every Odisha alert, is the district where Cyclone Arnab's rain set off a landslide that killed seven people; Odisha has asked the Centre for ₹2,526 crore for that damage, as our [earlier report](/en/news/ganjam-landslide-toll-seven-odisha-seeks-2526-crore) records. Andhra's government under Chandrababu Naidu has been through two storm reviews this month, including the [Arnab review](/en/news/naidu-cyclone-arnab-review-zero-casualties-fishermen), and Akvan would be the third storm in about a month, after Arnab and Murasu.

Odisha cancelled leave and alerted its collectors on Friday, as our [report on the state's preparations](/en/news/odisha-bay-of-bengal-low-pressure-cyclone-akvan-ganjam-leave-cancelled) records.

## What happens next

The next marker is the **depression expected on Sunday, November 15**, after which forecasters say direction should become clearer. The cyclonic storm is due on **the evening of November 16**. Orange rain warnings for south Odisha begin on November 19. The IMD's next bulletins will show whether the severe-storm forecast for November 18 holds and which coast is named.
