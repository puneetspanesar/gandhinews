India's core industries **grew 10.9% in October**, up from 8.2% in September, the Office of the Economic Adviser in the Ministry of Commerce and Industry said on November 20, 2026. The reading is a 13-month high and the strongest since the index was rebuilt on a 2022-23 base in July. The overall index stood at 123.1, against 111.0 in October 2025.

The October figure is provisional. September's growth was revised up from 7.9% to **8.2%** as the final index came in at 118.6, against a provisional 118.3.

One correction to the way the index is often described: it no longer covers eight industries. Since the first release of the new series on July 20, 2026, it has covered **nine**, because iron ore was added.

## What drove the jump

Six of the nine industries grew in October. **Iron ore rose 26.4%**, coal 23.1% and electricity 21.6%, followed by cement at 15.4%, steel at 7.3% and refinery products at 3.4%. Three industries shrank: crude oil fell 4.1%, natural gas 7.6% and fertilisers 12.2%.

Compared with September, coal more than doubled its pace, from 10.7% to 23.1%, and electricity went from 12.6% to 21.6%. Iron ore quickened from 18.4%, cement from 10.1% and steel from 5.6%. Refinery products slowed, from 8.7% to 3.4%, and crude oil and gas deepened their falls from 1.4% and 4.6%. Fertilisers were down 11.7% in September.

Steel is the one place where the story told in headlines and the data part ways. At **7.3%** it grew more slowly than coal, iron ore, electricity or cement. Its weight is large, 17.584 of every 100 points of the index, so it matters for the total, but it did not drive the jump.

## Weights and the cumulative picture

The index is a weighted average, and the weights tell which numbers count. Electricity carries **30.932%**, refinery products 22.572%, steel 17.584%, crude oil 7.430%, coal 5.596%, iron ore 4.905%, cement 4.410%, natural gas 3.841% and fertilisers 2.731%. Electricity's 21.6% therefore does far more work than coal's 23.1% or iron ore's 26.4%, and the shrinking crude, gas and fertiliser lines are small in the total. The Office of the Economic Adviser had already said in July that iron ore and electricity were the main drivers of growth in recent months.

For April to October, the index is **up 6.0%, against 2.3%** in the same seven months a year earlier. By sector, iron ore is up 22.1%, electricity 11.8%, cement 10.9%, steel 4.8%, coal 2.5% and refinery products 1.0%, while crude oil is down 3.7%, natural gas 4.8% and fertilisers 8.3%. Coal's October burst, in other words, is a recent change: the seven-month figure is far lower. Coal India's record October output of 67.2 million tonnes, which [we reported on November 2](/en/news/coal-india-shares-4-per-cent-record-october-output-67-mt), is the supply-side view of the same surge.

## A low base flatters October

The year-ago comparison helps. Core growth in October 2025 was only **0.8%**, so a modest absolute rise in output produces a large percentage. ICRA's Aditi Nayar was quoted as saying that the low base and a shift in the festive calendar inflated the October figure, and that she expects growth to ease to mid-single digits in November. The new series also reads differently from the old one: the 2011-12 base is discontinued, and a linking factor of 1.47 joins the two, so comparisons with figures quoted before July are not like for like.

## What it means for the IIP

The core group is a large share of what mines, refineries and power stations produce. Reports put the core group at **about 40% of the weight of the Index of Industrial Production**, a figure of 40.27% that was the old series' share. The July release says only that the new core weights are taken from the IIP weights for 2022-23 and scaled to 100, so the exact share under the new base is not stated in the material we read.

That is why the core number is read as a forecast. The Ministry of Statistics' IIP for October is the next release, and it is normally published on the 28th of the month; the August reading, released on September 28, showed factory output up 8.0%. With electricity, coal and iron ore all growing by more than 20%, the mining and power parts of October's IIP are likely to be firm. Manufacturing, which is most of the IIP, is only partly captured here, and cement and steel, the manufacturing lines in the core group, both grew less than the headline.

## How we got here

The October print follows a year of uneven industrial data. GDP [grew 7.8% in April to June](/en/news/gdp-grows-7-8-in-april-june-quarter), and the first core release on the new base, on July 20, showed June growth of 5.0%, with iron ore at 43.9% and coal at 1.4%. On oil, India's October crude imports [hit a record 23.3 million tonnes](/en/news/india-crude-imports-october-record-23-3-mt-bill-19-5-billion) in the same month that domestic crude output fell 4.1%, so the shortfall was met from abroad. Steelmakers are planning for a longer horizon; the [draft national steel policy](/en/news/draft-steel-policy-targets-604-mt-capacity-by-2047) aims for 604 million tonnes of capacity by 2047, and [JSW Steel's September-quarter results](/en/news/jsw-steel-q2-profit-5359-crore-utkal-jfe-expansion) came with an expansion plan.

## What to watch next

The next core-industries print, for November, is normally published on the 20th of the month. If Nayar is right, the headline will fall back sharply from 10.9% as the base normalises. The October IIP will show first how much of this strength reached factories. Until then, the safest reading is that mining, power and cement are running hot, steel is growing steadily, and the oil and gas lines, which depend on fields that are not growing, continue to drag.
