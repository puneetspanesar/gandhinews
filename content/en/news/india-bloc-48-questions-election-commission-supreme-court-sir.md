Rahul Gandhi and other INDIA bloc leaders have asked the Supreme Court to let them put **48 written questions to the Election Commission** about how the nationwide Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls was decided. The application was filed on Monday, November 16, 2026, in the opposition's pending petition against the revision. The court has not yet ruled on it, and the SIR petitions are listed for hearing on Tuesday, November 17.

The questions go to one issue: whether the Commission's claim that every major SIR decision was **taken unanimously** by its three members can be tested. The petitioners want Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi to answer on oath whether they consented, and the Director General (IT) to answer the technical questions on the Commission's digital systems.

## What the application asks

The application was filed through advocate-on-record Prasanna S and rests on **Order V Rule 1, Order XXXVIII Rule 11(9) and Order LV Rule 6** of the Supreme Court Rules, 2013. It also leans on the Constitution Bench ruling in the Puttaswamy case, arguing that where the facts lie within the exclusive knowledge of an authority that runs a technological system, the court can require its officials to answer specific questions.

According to reports, the 48 questions fall into about ten groups. Among them:

- **How the affidavit was approved.** Whether Kumar, Sandhu and Joshi approved the Commission's counter-affidavit, whether at a meeting, on a circulated file or electronically, and on what dates.
- **Objections.** How many written objections, dissents or reservations the two other commissioners recorded between February 19, 2025 and October 26, 2026 on SIR, ECINET, Form 6 and the filing of appeals, and whether decisions were unanimous or by majority.
- **The orders themselves.** File numbers, dates and the manner of approval for the June 24, 2025 order that said the Commission had decided to begin SIR "in the entire country".
- **Form 6.** Who introduced a new declaration on the online Form 6 page, and how many applications were rejected, kept pending or left unprocessed because of it.
- **ECINET and ERONET.** Whether anyone could change an electoral-roll entry other than on an Electoral Registration Officer's order, and, in the petitioners' words, "whether private contractors or vendors had write access to the electoral-roll database".
- **Approvals by phone.** "Whether urgent ECI approvals were obtained via WhatsApp or personal email."

The petitioners also point to an apparent conflict between the Commission's affidavit and an order of its own First Appellate Authority under the RTI Act, which reportedly said no decision to begin a nationwide SIR had been taken. They ask whether a decision file existed when the Commission's public information officer said on August 27, 2025 that no such information was available.

The Deputy Election Commissioner, Bhanu Prakash Yeturu, who filed the Commission's affidavits, would give factual answers to all 48 questions. If the Commission claims privilege over any record, the petitioners ask that it give specific grounds for each one.

## What the Commission has said

In its counter-affidavit of October 26, the Commission said its three SIR orders, dated **June 24, 2025, October 27, 2025 and May 14, 2026**, were approved unanimously by all three members, and that the Chief Election Commissioner did not act alone. It said a commissioner's note on a file is not itself a Commission decision, that urgent matters are approved electronically and recorded on file later, and that the extra declaration used during SIR is a separate document, not an amendment to Form 6. A supplementary affidavit followed on November 14 (see our report on [how excluded voters can return](/en/news/ec-supplementary-affidavit-sc-excluded-voters-restore-sir)). The Commission has not yet responded to the application, and the court has expressed no view on the merits.

## How we got here

The opposition's case grew out of a September newspaper report that Sandhu and Joshi had recorded objections at least 14 times in 10 months to SIR-related decisions. The Commission replied that written notes and differing views are routine and its decisions were unanimous; we reported it in [the dissent report and the push to remove the CEC](/en/news/election-commission-dissent-report-move-to-remove-cec). The affidavits say such a report cannot be treated as evidence of the facts it narrates.

After a march towards the court on October 12, Gandhi, Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge, Akhilesh Yadav, Tejashwi Yadav, Saugata Roy and other leaders filed the petition. It asks for the Commission's files and minutes, an audit of deletions by a committee headed by a retired Supreme Court judge, and booth-wise publication of the voters deleted, with reasons. On October 15 the Chief Justice's bench issued notice to the Commission and all three commissioners and tagged the plea with the pending SIR cases, as we reported in [the court taking up the petition](/en/news/supreme-court-notice-cec-rahul-gandhi-sir-plea-conduct). VCK president Thol Thirumavalavan and a National Conference MP later joined as petitioners.

The opposition's complaint is that the counter-affidavit speaks of "unanimous decisions and approvals" without giving dates, file notings or records. Congress also said on social media that the Commission's affidavit had not answered its questions on what it calls "vote theft".

## Why it matters

The application is a bid to move the case from argument to evidence. The Commission's affidavits say every major SIR order had the assent of all three members; the petitioners want that assent shown in dated files. The answer bears on a revision that has already struck names off the rolls in several states, and the questions on Form 6 and deletions concern the test a voter faces: whether a dropped name can be restored.

The bench will have to decide whether the facts lie in the Commission's sole knowledge, as the application argues, and whether sworn answers are the right route, or whether the disclosure of records the petition already seeks is enough.

## What happens next

The Supreme Court has listed the SIR petitions for **November 17**. The reports do not say whether the application will be taken up that day, or which bench will hear it. If the court allows it, the Commission would have to file answers on oath from the commissioners and the Director General (IT); if it refuses, the petitioners are left with their original prayer for the Commission's records.
