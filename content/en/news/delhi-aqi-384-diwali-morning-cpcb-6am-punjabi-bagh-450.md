Delhi woke to **toxic haze on Diwali**, with the Central Pollution Control Board's 6 am update putting the city's average Air Quality Index at **384**, in the very poor band and only 17 points short of the 401 mark where air turns severe.

The average hides a worse picture at the stations. **Eleven of the 23 stations** in the 6 am update were already in the severe band, led by **Punjabi Bagh at 450** and RK Puram at 449. IIT Delhi read 446, Dwarka 444, and CRRI Mathura Road 425. Siri Fort, Chandni Chowk and Delhi University's North Campus were each at 423. Pusa was at 415, Burari Crossing 411 and NSUT Jaffarpur 409.

The other stations stayed in the very poor range, among them Narela at 399, ITO at 396 and Mandir Marg at 393. Anand Vihar, often the capital's worst spot, read 328.

A later PTI count at about 9:45 am, drawn from the Sameer app across 54 stations, found **24 of them in the severe band**, with Jahangirpuri at 450 and RK Puram at 448. That count has no measurement hour, so it is not set against the CPCB figure.

## Why the air will not clear

PM10 was the main pollutant in the update. Wind speed stayed under 10 km/h, and low ventilation and vehicle emissions kept the smog near the ground. The reading is a little higher than **Saturday's 369** and Friday's 382.

The Early Warning System expects **severe air on November 8 and 9**, and levels are forecast to swing between severe and very poor over the following six days. The fireworks that Diwali night brings are yet to be counted in any of these figures.
