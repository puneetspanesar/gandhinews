DMK chief M K Stalin wrote to Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Vijay on Saturday, November 14, asking him to **convene an all-party meeting at once** on the Mekedatu dam, hours after Karnataka Chief Minister D K Shivakumar said in Bengaluru that the dam will be built. The state's Law Minister said the Vijay government "will never allow" it, and the Tamil Nadu Congress chief, who belongs to Shivakumar's own party, said the same.

Shivakumar had told Prime Minister Narendra Modi in Mysuru on Friday that "no one can stop" the project. On Saturday he repeated it at the Vidhana Soudha, called it a state right, said Tamil Nadu's entitlement of 177 tmcft of Cauvery water would be honoured, and offered to take a Tamil Nadu delegation to the dam site.

## What Shivakumar said

On Friday, with Modi present at a government event in Mysuru, Shivakumar asked the Centre to speed up the statutory clearances for the proposed balancing reservoir on the Cauvery. "The Supreme Court has delivered a clear verdict to all of us that Mekedatu has to be built in Karnataka, and no one can stop it," he said, according to reports of the event. He added: "We want you to help us legally."

On Saturday he went further. The project, he said, would be carried out **"in accordance with the Supreme Court's directive"**, and Karnataka "will not back down" in protecting its interests. He described the opposition from Tamil Nadu as political and said the dam would in fact ensure that Tamil Nadu receives its share. "Anyone is free to oppose it," he said, inviting Tamil Nadu to send a delegation so he could explain the benefits.

He said the only outstanding requirement from the Centre is **compensatory land for the forest area that would be submerged**, and that Karnataka will prepare a revised Detailed Project Report. He told reporters he would gather complete details and speak again in the coming days. Asked about Tagore, he said the Tamil Nadu Congress chief "doesn't know the actual facts" and offered to take him to the site too.

## Tamil Nadu answers on three fronts

Tamil Nadu Congress Committee president Manickam Tagore, who belongs to Shivakumar's own party, responded on X on Saturday. He called the stance "stubborn" and "utterly unacceptable", said it disregarded the Supreme Court ruling and the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal's final award, and argued that Karnataka, as the upper riparian state, cannot take up a project that affects downstream water rights on its own. Clearance without Tamil Nadu's consent would be "not only illegal" but would hurt the delta districts, he said. **"On behalf of the Tamil Nadu Congress, we will never allow this project,"** he said, adding that he would raise the issue in Thanjavur the same day.

Tagore also said the matter is before the Supreme Court, where the Tamil Nadu government has already gone, and urged Chief Minister Vijay to pursue every legal measure. He asked the Prime Minister to ensure the Centre does not let Karnataka "lay even a single brick".

The state government took the same line. Law Minister C T R Nirmal Kumar said the Vijay government "will never allow" the dam, that Tamil Nadu has filed petitions in court and is taking stronger legal steps than earlier governments, and that Karnataka lacks forest and environmental clearance and "cannot even lay a single brick". He also accused Shivakumar of making provocative statements before every election.

DMK chief M K Stalin, a former Chief Minister, wrote to Vijay on Saturday asking him to **convene an all-party meeting at once** and to lead a joint delegation of party leaders and MPs to the Prime Minister. He called the dam "a matter of life and death for Tamil Nadu" and said the delegation should carry both the meeting's resolutions and the Assembly's unanimous resolution against the project passed in June. Stalin also advised Vijay against going to Karnataka for talks, saying: "there is no room for any negotiations with Karnataka regarding Cauvery water rights". Tamil Nadu, he said, should pursue its rights through legal means only.

## Two readings of the same court

The dispute turns on what the Supreme Court has actually decided. Shivakumar says it has delivered a clear verdict in Karnataka's favour and that the Tamil Nadu share is fixed at 177 tmcft. Tamil Nadu says Mekedatu was never part of the tribunal's final award or the court's judgment. None of the reports on Saturday gave the date, case or terms of the order Shivakumar cites, and we could not open it. Tagore puts Tamil Nadu's legal entitlement at 177.25 tmcft.

The proposed reservoir would sit on the Cauvery near the Karnataka and Tamil Nadu border, below the Krishnaraja Sagar and Kabini dams, and is meant mainly to supply drinking water to Bengaluru and generate power. Tamil Nadu fears it would hold back monsoon flows that reach the state, especially in drought years, and delta farmers depend on timely releases for the Kuruvai crop. The site lies inside the Cauvery Wildlife Sanctuary, and critics say thousands of acres of forest would be submerged. Tamil Nadu also argues that Karnataka already has reservoirs enough to meet Bengaluru's needs.

## How we got here

Mekedatu was on the list Shivakumar sent the Prime Minister on November 5, when he sought early clearances for it along with Kalasa-Banduri, Upper Bhadra, Upper Krishna and Yettinahole, as we reported before the visit. Modi opened the Pink Line stretch in Bengaluru and the Cosmos-Mysuru planetarium on November 13; the Mekedatu remarks came at the Mysuru event.

## Why it matters

Cauvery water decides the Kuruvai crop across Tamil Nadu's delta districts, and Bengaluru's growing demand for drinking water is Karnataka's case for the dam. A project that needs central forest and environmental clearance, in the face of a pending Supreme Court case and a united Tamil Nadu front, is unlikely to move without the Centre taking a position. The reports of Saturday's exchange carried no response from the Centre.

## What happens next

Shivakumar has promised more details in the coming days and a revised project report. In Tamil Nadu, the next test is whether Vijay accepts Stalin's request for an all-party meeting, and how the state presses its case before the Supreme Court. Tagore has said he will take the issue to Thanjavur on Saturday.
