The United States says it is **working to formalise a Quad leaders' summit** in Manila, even as Prime Minister Narendra Modi leaves on Monday for the ASEAN meetings that would host it. Dylan Johnson, a US Assistant Secretary of State, said on X that Washington is "working to formalise a Quad leaders' summit at ASEAN next week". The Ministry of External Affairs has not confirmed any such meeting.

The Quad brings together India, the United States, Japan and Australia. A leaders' meeting in Manila would bring Modi together with President Donald Trump, Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi and Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, and it would be the first gathering of the four at the top for some time.

## What the US official said

Mr Johnson went further than earlier hints. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, he noted, has met the Quad foreign ministers **five times in two years, three of them in the past six months**, and the grouping's senior officials, the sherpas, now meet every month. The message was that the groundwork for a leaders' meeting is being laid at a steady pace, and that the Manila gathering offers a ready venue.

New Delhi has been more careful. At a briefing on November 12, Secretary (East) Rudrendra Tandon said the Ministry had no information on a Quad meeting and described it as unconfirmed, as we reported in [our preview of the trip](/en/news/modi-asean-india-summit-manila-november-16-18). Nothing in the official announcement of the visit mentions it either.

## Modi's two days in Manila

The Prime Minister's visit, at the invitation of President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., is listed by the government for **November 16 and 17**. On arrival he attends a dinner Mr Marcos is hosting for visiting leaders. On Tuesday he takes part in the **23rd ASEAN-India Summit and the 21st East Asia Summit**, which the Philippines hosts as this year's ASEAN chair.

The ASEAN-India Summit will review the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership, including the ASEAN-India Plan of Action 2026 to 2030. India has marked 2026 as the ASEAN-India Year of Maritime Cooperation, and maritime security, trade and investment lead its agenda. The government describes ties with ASEAN as central to its Act East Policy and its Indo-Pacific vision.

## A tight calendar

The timing is the difficulty. Mr Trump is reported to be due in Manila at about **5:30 pm on Tuesday**, and may miss the East Asia Summit altogether. He is expected to join the ASEAN-US engagement and hold talks with Mr Marcos before flying on to China for the APEC meetings. The Philippine foreign ministry has said Mr Marcos himself leaves for APEC on November 18, and that his bilateral meetings in Manila include Australia, Brazil, Japan and South Korea, with talks with India still being finalised.

Mr Albanese has been in Manila since Saturday, and Ms Takaichi is due on Monday. Only Mr Trump's and Mr Modi's diaries need to line up, and a Quad meeting would have to fit around the Prime Minister's summit commitments earlier in the day.

## Why it matters to India

The Quad was due to meet in India last year, but the leaders' summit did not take place because of uncertainty over Mr Trump's travel. A meeting in Manila would restart the format at the top and give New Delhi a place to raise maritime security and supply chains with three partners at once.

It would also arrive while India and the United States are still working through their trade differences, covered in [our report on the trade talks](/en/news/india-us-trade-talks-plateau-russia-oil-tariff-law). Reports so far suggest a formal bilateral between Modi and Mr Trump in Manila is unlikely, so a Quad table may be the only occasion for the two to sit together.

## What to watch

The first sign will be an announcement from the Ministry of External Affairs or the White House on whether a Quad meeting is on the schedule. Beyond that, the outcome documents of the ASEAN-India Summit on Tuesday, and any bilateral Modi holds with Mr Marcos or other leaders, will show what India takes home from Manila.
