The White House has confirmed that President Donald Trump will meet the leaders of India, Japan and Australia in Manila on Tuesday evening, **the first Quad leaders' meeting in two years**. Prime Minister Narendra Modi landed in the Philippine capital on Monday for the ASEAN summits, where the meeting will be held on the sidelines. This morning the United States said only that it was working to arrange it.

In a statement carried on Monday, White House Assistant Press Secretary Olivia Wales said: "On Tuesday evening in Manila... the president will participate in a multilateral meeting with Quad leaders." She said the leaders would discuss shared goals for peace, prosperity and security in the Indo-Pacific, and credited Mr Trump's leadership with making the Quad "a partnership of action". The meeting would bring Mr Modi together with Mr Trump, Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi.

## What the schedule says

According to the itinerary reported from Washington, Mr Trump flies out on Monday and reaches Manila on Tuesday. On landing he is to welcome the Quad leaders for an official photograph, and the multilateral meeting follows at once. He is then expected to sign a trade deal with Thailand, attend a special US-ASEAN summit and join the dinner for ASEAN and regional leaders.

The morning's reports had Mr Trump arriving at about 5:30 pm and possibly missing the East Asia Summit. The confirmed plan puts the Quad meeting after his arrival on Tuesday evening, so it does not clash with Mr Modi's daytime summits. The reports give no clock time for the meeting itself.

## Why it is a first

The last meeting of the four leaders was hosted by then President Joe Biden in Delaware on **September 21, 2024**. Mr Trump has not attended a leaders' meeting in his current term, which began on January 20, 2025, and none was held in his first term, after he revived the grouping in 2017. Quad foreign ministers have met five times since January 2025.

India was due to host the leaders' summit last year, but it did not take place because of uncertainty over Mr Trump's travel. As [our preview of the trip](/en/news/modi-asean-india-summit-manila-november-16-18) recorded, the Ministry of External Affairs had called the meeting unconfirmed on November 12. Secretary of State Marco Rubio had said last month that the leaders could meet on the ASEAN sidelines.

## The agenda the US describes

The reports point to supply chains and technology as the working content. They cite a San Francisco Declaration on protecting AI and critical-technology supply chains, which India joined, and earlier Quad foreign-minister commitments of more than $85 million for trusted AI infrastructure and $10 million for critical mineral recycling. Dylan Johnson, a US Assistant Secretary of State, had said this morning that the Quad's senior officials now meet monthly and that Mr Rubio has met the foreign ministers five times in two years, three of them in the past six months.

For New Delhi the table matters for another reason. India and the United States are still working through their trade differences, covered in [our report on the trade talks](/en/news/india-us-trade-talks-plateau-russia-oil-tariff-law), and reports so far suggest a separate Modi-Trump bilateral in Manila is unlikely. A seat at the Quad meeting may be the only occasion for the two leaders to talk face to face this week.

## Modi's first evening in Manila

Mr Modi arrived at Manila's international airport on Monday to a cultural welcome, met members of the Indian community and watched a Philippine staging of the Ramayana, *Sita: The Ramayana Revisited*, by a performing arts group from Iligan City. He then attended the **gala dinner at the Philippine International Convention Centre** hosted by President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. under the summit theme "Navigating the Future", wearing a Barong Tagalog. The Philippine government says at least 19 leaders are in Manila, among them China's Premier Li Qiang, Brazil's President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, Canada's Prime Minister Mark Carney and UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres.

In his departure statement, Mr Modi called ASEAN central to India's Act East Policy and to its MAHASAGAR vision, and said the summits would strengthen cooperation on connectivity, maritime security and shared prosperity. India wants an early conclusion to the review of the ASEAN-India Trade in Goods Agreement, which covers about $122 billion of annual trade, as noted in the same preview.

## What comes next

Mr Modi attends the **23rd ASEAN-India Summit and the 21st East Asia Summit on Tuesday**, with bilateral meetings, including talks with Mr Marcos, expected around them. The Quad meeting follows in the evening. The government's own announcement of the visit does not mention it, and no statement from the Ministry of External Affairs on the meeting had appeared by 6 pm. Any joint statement or readout after the meeting will show whether the four governments agree on more than a photograph.
