The **Reserve Bank of India has proposed a "stop switch"** that would let any bank customer block every future outgoing debit from an account in one step, along with a voluntary check in which a person the customer names must approve large transfers. The draft amendment directions, titled "Preventive Safeguards for Electronic Banking Transactions", were put out on Monday, November 23, and **comments are invited until December 14, 2026**.

The draft would apply to seven kinds of lender: commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks, urban cooperative banks and rural cooperative banks. It is a set of proposals, and the final text can change after the consultation.

## What the draft says

The central idea is that customers, not only banks, decide how far digital channels can be used on their accounts. According to reports on the draft, banks would have to let customers **switch individual payment channels off and on**, and set limits per transaction, per beneficiary and as a daily total for each channel, plus an overall daily cap across channels. A customer could also fix a time window in which a channel stays off. These limits would override any higher limit set by the bank or the payment system.

**The stop switch** goes further. Once triggered, it blocks customer-initiated debits, new payment mandates and standing instructions, and the registering of the account on new third-party UPI apps. Incoming credits are not affected. Existing recurring mandates, liens and bank debits to recover dues such as loan instalments are left out. To turn it off, the customer must authenticate at least as strongly as when it was switched on, and the bank may add a cooling-off period.

Cards would get separate switches for domestic and international use and for ATM, point-of-sale, online and contactless transactions. Gift and transit prepaid cards are excluded.

## Default-off channels and a 24-hour cap

For new customers, **digital payment channels would be off by default** and switched on only with explicit consent. New physical and virtual cards would work only for contact-based ATM and point-of-sale use in India when issued, with forex prepaid cards allowed contact and contactless use abroad. After a channel is switched on, the bank must cap total transfers through it for the first 24 hours, at a level set by its own risk assessment. No report gives a rupee figure for this cap.

## The trusted-person check

The **Trusted Person Mechanism** is voluntary and needs the consent of both sides. The customer picks an adult, whom the bank verifies with an officially valid ID, a mobile number different from the account's and an email address. For any electronic transfer above a threshold the customer sets, the trusted person is sent the amount, the beneficiary and any narration, and has **one hour to approve**. A rejection, or silence, means the transfer fails.

The trusted person cannot operate the account, see balances or hold any financial liability. Registration, changes and opt-out each carry a 24-hour cooling-off period, and banks may ask more vulnerable customers to visit a branch or confirm by phone. UPI transfers, merchant payments and recurring mandates are outside the check.

## How we got here

The draft builds on the RBI's June 24, 2026, directions, which dealt with **who pays when a customer is defrauded**. The regulator says that framework was largely about liability after the event and that "a need is felt to strengthen the same from a preventive perspective", with customers getting "greater control over the manner and extent to which digital payment channels may be used". Reports say two ideas in the April 9 discussion paper, a one-hour delay on some high-value transfers and an annual credit ceiling, have been dropped.

The draft also proposes to widen the definition of "negligence by a bank" in the June directions, so that practices are uniform across lenders. One report said the wider definition would cover a bank's own system failure or security breach that enables fraud, and collusion by a bank employee or a third party the bank has engaged.

The Supreme Court has been pressing the same issue. On [November 10 it told the RBI](/en/news/sc-digital-arrest-whatsapp-intermediaries-measures-november-23) that rules must make banks repay at least the amount lost where due diligence failed, in the "digital arrest" scam case it is hearing on its own motion.

## What happens next

Banks, customers and other stakeholders can send comments through the "Connect 2 Regulate" section of the RBI website or by email until December 14. One report said the negligence definition would take effect from January 1, 2027, and most other directions from July 1, 2027, but the dates should be read against the final text. Banks would need to offer the controls through branches, ATMs, internet and mobile banking and phone banking, alert customers at once to any change, and keep tamper-proof logs.
