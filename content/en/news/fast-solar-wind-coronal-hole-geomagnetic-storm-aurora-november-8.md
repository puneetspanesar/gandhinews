Fast solar wind from a **large, L-shaped coronal hole** on the Sun is streaming towards Earth, and forecasters expect the planet's magnetic field to stay unsettled through Sunday, November 8. A coronal hole is a region where the Sun's magnetic field opens into space and lets charged particles escape at high speed.

The forecast, as last updated on November 7, expected solar wind speeds to climb from the evening of November 6. For November 6 the Kp index, the scale of geomagnetic disturbance, was expected to reach up to 4, with a slight chance of a minor G1 storm late in the day.

## What was forecast for the weekend

November 7 was the day of the biggest expected disturbance: **active conditions, with G1 to G2 storm levels possible**, meaning minor to moderate. A glancing blow from a slow coronal mass ejection launched on November 1 might combine with the fast wind from the coronal hole.

For Sunday, the forecast said active conditions "may continue through the day as fast solar wind from the large coronal hole keeps streaming in". No storm level was given for the day, and these are forecasts, not measurements.

## Who may see aurora

Aurora watchers at high latitudes, such as **northern Scotland and Scandinavia**, were told they could get a show. Storms of this strength do not normally push the aurora far enough south to be seen from India.

Space weather forecasts can change within hours as the solar wind arrives, and the actual storm levels reached on November 7 and 8 will be confirmed by magnetic field measurements.
