The British science fiction writer **M John Harrison won the 2026 Booker Prize** on Monday night for *The End of Everything*, a short, strange satire set on the Kent coast a few decades from now. At 81, he is **the oldest writer to take the prize**. The award is worth £50,000 and was announced at a ceremony at Old Billingsgate in London on November 9, 2026.

The five judges, chaired by the broadcaster and classicist Mary Beard, picked the book **unanimously after a full day of debate** over the six novels on the shortlist. It was chosen from 163 submissions.

## What the judges said

Beard announced the decision. "In the view of the judges, it was a unanimous decision," she said. "This was the book that stood out above all the other amazing, wonderful, and mind-blowing books we were looking at."

She described the novel as one that **"turns you upside down"** and, despite its subject, "leaves you with a twinge of optimism". Beard added: "This is the politics of now seen through the lens of the future."

Gaby Wood, chief executive of the Booker Prize Foundation, called Harrison "a genuinely original thinker, a genre-defying writer who takes ideas seriously". She said: "This is a historic win, for a writer whose decades of work are lightly worn on every page." Wood also pointed out that he is **the first Booker winner to have come from the science fiction world**.

The panel also included the poet Raymond Antrobus, the Pulp frontman Jarvis Cocker, the writer Rebecca Liu and the novelist Patricia Lockwood. Harrison himself sat on the Booker jury in 2022.

## The book

*The End of Everything* is Harrison's 13th novel and, at **198 pages, the shortest book on the shortlist**. It is published by Serpent's Tail. The story follows Phillip Tennent, who collects artefacts washed up from the Channel along the Kent coast, and his elderly aunt Marnie. Something has gone wrong with the world, and the cause is a slow invasion by shape-shifting beings the book calls the "iGhetti", a word that hints at something digital.

The Booker Foundation's own description of the book calls it "shimmering and oblique" and a novel about the present moment. Harrison has said he started from current affairs and from the question of what happens when our explanations of the world become "an ongoing disaster".

## A late honour

Harrison has written for about 60 years, mostly science fiction, and has worked as a critic too. His 2006 novel *Nova Swing* won both the Arthur C. Clarke Award and the Philip K. Dick Award.

In a radio interview, he said he was "very excited that a fiction of this type should be shortlisted, and that it should have been mine". He said he had been **"working for 40 to 50 years to shift the balance"**, as a novelist and as a critic. "Why shouldn't a well-written (work of science fiction) be considered to be literary fiction?" he asked.

His reaction on the night was more rueful. "I feel like a proper writer at last, totally lacking in confidence," Harrison said. He also said prizes such as the Booker matter a great deal in what he called a landscape of industrialised fiction.

## The record he broke

The previous oldest winner was **Margaret Atwood, who was 79** when she won in 2019. Harrison takes the record by two years. The prize was presented to him by last year's winner, David Szalay, whose novel *Flesh* won in 2025.

## The shortlist

The other five books on the list were *John of John* by Douglas Stuart, *The Disappearers* by Marlon James, *The Things We Never Say* by Elizabeth Strout, *Black Bag* by Luke Kennard and *May We Feed the King* by Rebecca Perry. Stuart and James have both won the Booker before. UK-based writers dominated the shortlist.

Indian readers will remember that Kiran Desai, who won in 2006 for *The Inheritance of Loss*, was shortlisted last year for *The Loneliness of Sonia and Sunny* and lost to Szalay.
