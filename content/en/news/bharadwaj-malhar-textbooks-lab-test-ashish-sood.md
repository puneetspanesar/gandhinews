Aam Aadmi Party Delhi president Saurabh Bharadwaj on Saturday demanded laboratory tests on the Malhar textbooks handed out in Delhi government schools, accusing the BJP government of accepting books printed on thinner paper than it had ordered.

"The tender clearly states that the paper used for the books must be 80 GSM," Bharadwaj said. "However, the books distributed by the government are made of paper measuring approximately 60 GSM," he added, saying the printing was poor too.

He said Education Minister Ashish Sood, responding to AAP's allegations that commissions were taken in printing the books, had at least conceded that the Malhar books are smaller than standard NCERT textbooks. Bharadwaj said the smaller size would make things harder for children.

"Get the paper quality, printing quality and GSM of these books tested at any laboratory," he said. "Why is Ashish Sood afraid of this? Let the Rekha Gupta government conduct these tests and bring the truth before the public."

Bharadwaj also pointed to delays this year. Books and stationery, he said, were "significantly delayed at the beginning of the 2026–27 academic session", which began on 1 April, and "lakhs of children were forced to attend classes without essential study materials". The tender, he said, required delivery within 45 days of the work order, with a 5% penalty for each week of delay and blacklisting if the delay ran 15 days beyond that.

The supply delays had reached the Delhi High Court, where the NGO Social Jurist filed a contempt plea. Justice Sachin Datta reprimanded the government, and its standing counsel assured the court that the remaining books would be supplied before the summer vacation.

"Tell me the time and venue for a public discussion on this issue," Bharadwaj said, addressing Sood. There was no reply from the minister by Saturday night.
