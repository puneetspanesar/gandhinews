West Bengal Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari on Sunday promised that **about 2.5 lakh people** who have applied for citizenship under the Citizenship (Amendment) Act would have their hearings completed and certificates issued by the end of the year.

Speaking at the International Matua Gosai Convention 2026 in Thakurnagar, the heart of the Matua community in North 24 Parganas district, Adhikari said the process had moved far too slowly. "Only around 25,000 people have received citizenship certificates under the CAA in West Bengal so far," he said. "We will complete the hearings by December 31 and issue citizenship certificates to them."

The Matuas, a largely Namasudra community whose families came from what is now Bangladesh, have long campaigned for citizenship. Adhikari prayed at the Shwet Mandir in Thakurnagar earlier in the day before addressing the gathering.

## Certificates and the voters' list

The Chief Minister said that Scheduled Caste members among those granted citizenship, particularly Namasudras, would get their **SC certificates without delay**. The state government would also help new citizens get their names on the electoral rolls during the special revision due in January.

He said people who had entered from Bangladesh by 31 December 2024 would not be harassed by the police. He also promised to examine the cases of Matua refugees held without trial in jails in Bongaon, Barasat and Ranaghat and to work for their release, with land and housing for them.

"We will not allow ourselves to become refugees again," he said.

## Money for the Matuas

Adhikari announced **₹50 crore for Matua development** in the current financial year and said the Matua Development Board would be restructured. His list of promises included a 100-bed hospital at Chandpara named after Maa Binapani within six months, support for the Baruni and Sraboni melas, a new temple to be funded by the pilgrimage site's trust, and **municipality status for Thakurnagar** in the next state Budget session.

A dedicated officer in the Chief Minister's Office will look after Matua beneficiaries, he said, and the government would help them use the Annapurna Yojana and Ayushman Bharat schemes.

## A swipe at Mamata

Adhikari used the platform to attack his predecessor. He accused the Mamata Banerjee government of neglecting the Matuas and opposing their demand for citizenship. "God removed her democratically because she had committed those wrongs," he said.

The Trinamool Congress had not responded to his remarks by Sunday night. If the target holds, the new citizens would be in time to seek a place on the rolls when the special revision opens in January.
