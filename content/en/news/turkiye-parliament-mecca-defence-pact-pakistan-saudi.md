Turkiye has submitted a three-way defence treaty with Pakistan and Saudi Arabia to its parliament for ratification, moving the alliance a step closer to law as Houthi attacks on the Saudi kingdom continue.

The Mecca Joint Defence Agreement was signed on August 7 and has 16 articles. Ratification needs only a simple majority, which the ruling coalition holds, but the process is expected to take some time and could bring heated debate in the chamber.

## What the treaty says

Under Article 1, the signatories commit to use "all necessary means" to remove threats to one another's territorial integrity.

Article 2 is the heart of the pact. If one party faces an armed attack, the others "shall immediately provide assistance to the party under attack", according to the draft. The clause resembles NATO's Article 5, though each country "will decide on the scope and nature of the support to be provided" after assessing what is needed, drawing on its available resources, including military ones.

Articles 3 to 6 cover defence industry cooperation, intelligence sharing, logistics, joint training and fighting "all means of terrorism". The pact also provides for joint research in advanced technology and a defence committee made up of the three countries' foreign and defence ministers. Other states may join, but only if all three founding members agree. It runs for 10 years, after which any member may withdraw.

## Next steps

Parliament's speaker is due to refer the treaty to the foreign affairs commission, which could take it up as early as Friday. Its full text is expected to become public once the commission receives it. Ratification alone would not send Turkish troops anywhere: a deployment would need separate legislation.

Burhanettin Duran, the Turkish presidency's communications director, said on X that the partnership aims to "strengthen collective deterrence, enhance joint capabilities in the defence sector". He described it as a step towards the "institutionalisation" of cooperation between the three.

President Recep Tayyip Erdogan said on Monday that Turkiye was "surrounded by a ring of fire", referring to the Houthi strikes on Riyadh's airport.

## Why it matters now

Earlier this month the three countries agreed to activate "deterrence measures" under the pact and to send forces to Saudi Arabia to counter Yemen's Houthis, who have fired missiles and drones at the kingdom's oil facilities and its main airport. A Saudi-led coalition has answered with air strikes on Houthi positions.

For Pakistan, it is the second defence pact with Riyadh, after the Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement of September 2025. Islamabad is also the main mediator between the United States and Iran, and its army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, was in Tehran on Tuesday for talks with Iran's leadership.
