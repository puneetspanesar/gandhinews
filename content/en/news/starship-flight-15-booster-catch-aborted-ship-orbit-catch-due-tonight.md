SpaceX's fifteenth Starship test flight reached orbit on Saturday morning India time and released **26 Starlink V3 satellites**, but the company called off the planned tower catch of the Super Heavy booster and let it splash down in the Gulf of Mexico. The ship, Ship 42, is now circling Earth and is due back at Starbase, Texas, at about **2:47 am IST on Sunday**, when SpaceX hopes to catch it with the launch tower's arms for the first time.

## What happened on Flight 15

The Starship stack lifted off from Starbase at 5:47 pm Eastern time on Friday, **4:17 am IST on Saturday**, with the booster's 33 Raptor 3 engines producing more than 18 million pounds of thrust. The ship separated from the booster about two and a half minutes later in a hot-staging sequence, in which the ship's engines light while the two stages are still joined.

The booster did not return to the tower. SpaceX cancelled the catch and sent Super Heavy to a planned area offshore, where it reached the water about **6.5 minutes after liftoff**. It would have been the first recovery of a Version 3 booster, the design that began flying earlier this year. SpaceX said in a statement quoted in reports that "safety is always the number one priority, and we're still testing out things that we haven't done before on these vehicles."

The company has not given a cause. According to reports, one of the booster's 33 engines went out after liftoff, while another account cites an onboard anomaly that triggered an automatic abort. Neither has been confirmed by SpaceX.

The ship carried on. After a burn of just over eight minutes and a second burn of about 20 seconds, it was in a low orbit about **275 km above Earth**. SpaceX commentator Dan Huot told the webcast: "We've got a nominal orbit insertion, so Starship now in orbit around Earth for the second time ever."

## The satellites and the test

Deployment began about 35 minutes after launch and lasted about 42 minutes. By the later reports, SpaceX had confirmed contact with all 26 satellites. Together they add roughly **a terabit of data capacity**, which reports compare to about ten Starlink V2 launches on a Falcon 9. A few of the V3 satellites carry cameras that photograph the ship's heat-shield tiles and other hardware, giving engineers a look at the surface before it faces re-entry heating.

The same ship design flew for the first time to orbit on Flight 14 on September 28, when it also carried 26 Starlink V3 satellites. That flight cut its orbits short because of engine problems, and no such problem has been reported so far on Flight 15. Before Flight 14, SpaceX had caught three V1 boosters at the tower and reused two of them, the last catch coming in March 2025.

## What the ship has to do next

The plan is for Ship 42 to spend about 22.5 hours in flight and complete roughly **14 orbits**. SpaceX has set checkpoints at which it can change course: 10 minutes after the satellite release, at about 7 hours 15 minutes, and at about 20 hours 41 minutes. A 10-second deorbit burn is planned at about 21 hours 36 minutes, with atmospheric entry about 35 minutes later.

The catch itself is scheduled for 4:17 pm Eastern time on Saturday, **2:47 am IST on Sunday**, which is 3:17 pm local time in Texas. SpaceX will attempt it only if system checks show the ship is healthy enough to return. If not, a contingency splashdown zone in the Gulf of Mexico is available, and early deorbit options remain open.

## Why it matters

No upper stage has ever been recovered. A ship catch would show that SpaceX can bring back both halves of the rocket for reuse, the basis of its plan to fly Starlink V3 satellites in larger batches: the 26 released on this flight add about as much capacity as **ten Starlink V2 launches** on a Falcon 9. Starship is also the vehicle at the centre of the timetable in our report on [NASA's Moon base plan](/en/news/nasa-isaacman-moon-base-artemis-iii-summer-2027-landing-2028).

The skipped booster catch means one of the two recovery goals was not tested on this flight. Separately, the FAA is holding public scoping meetings on increasing Starship launches at Starbase on **November 17**, in virtual sessions and in Brownsville.

## What happens next

The ship's return is the next event. Viewers in India can follow it overnight into Sunday, with the catch window about 2:47 am IST. SpaceX has not said when it will explain the booster abort. The company's earlier flight in September ended its orbits early, so a decision at either of the later checkpoints could still send Ship 42 to a splashdown instead. This report reflects the latest information available at 4:20 pm IST on Saturday.
