Punjab Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann on Saturday, November 14, launched **"Punjab Da Crorepati", a statewide quiz for young people** aged 15 to 30, with ₹1 crore for the winner. The contest, announced in Chandigarh, is modelled on the television game show Kaun Banega Crorepati and opens through a dedicated portal and mobile app.

"The Punjab government is launching a unique programme for youths," Mann said. Describing how it will work, he added: "There will be questions like Kaun Banega Crorepati, and there will be four options."

## How the contest works

The quiz has **11 rounds**. The first nine are played online, so a contestant can take part from anywhere in Punjab. The last two are held in person for those who qualify. Every question is multiple choice with four options and one correct answer, and each round uses a different set of questions. Participants see the correct answers straight after each attempt, which the government presents as a way to learn while playing.

About **50,000 questions** have been uploaded to the portal. They cover Punjab's history, culture, language and geography, mathematics and the work of the state government. Many follow the pattern of state recruitment tests, such as those for patwari, police constable and the Punjab Civil Services, and sub-divisional officer posts. Mann said the questions resemble those in recruitment exams and that "the competition will get tougher" as players move up the rounds.

One report says the tenth round will be held in all 23 districts, with the toppers going on to a grand finale. The dates of the rounds and the finale, and the venue of the in-person stages, have not been announced.

## The prizes

The winner receives **₹1 crore**, and four runners-up get ₹10 lakh each. In all, **10,000 participants will receive prizes**, the government says. One report lists a further eight prizes of ₹5 lakh each, while another says the full distribution of the remaining cash awards is yet to be announced. Treat the lower tiers as unconfirmed until the rules are published.

Registration is free, according to the government's announcement as reported, and no entry deadline has been given. A government notification dated October 30 is reported to allow students from other states who study at institutions in Punjab to take part too, subject to verification. One account describes two age groups, a school group of 15 to 18 and a youth group of 18 to 30; the ranges overlap at 18, and the rules do not yet say where an 18-year-old would play.

## What Mann said it is for

Mann said the quiz lets students **earn money while preparing for competitive exams**, and increases their knowledge of Punjab. He asked parents and teachers in schools and colleges to encourage students to register in large numbers, and said the competition would be transparent. In a post on X he framed education as central to Punjab's progress.

The Aam Aadmi Party's Punjab unit promoted the launch with the line "Celebrate Punjab, Punjabi and Punjabiyat while winning big!"

## The election backdrop

The launch comes months before the **2027 Punjab Assembly election**, and young voters, including first-time voters, are a group every party is courting. Our [explainer on the 2027 election dates](/en/explainer/assembly-elections-2027-dates-up-punjab-uttarakhand-goa-manipur) sets out the calendar, and the quiz is the latest of several state announcements in a week that has also brought a [one-time settlement for unpaid household power bills](/en/news/punjab-ots-power-bills-penalty-waiver-24-lakh-mann), worth ₹518 crore in waived penalties, and a [second list of 31 AAP candidates](/en/news/aap-second-list-31-punjab-mlas-dropped-two-mps). The government has also run a separate scheme for [women's payments](/en/explainer/punjab-1000-women-scheme-mawan-dheeyan-satkar-yojana-explained).

None of the reports of the launch carries a reaction from the opposition parties, and the government has not said what the contest will cost or which department will pay the prize money. Those are the two figures to watch once the rules are published.

## Why it matters to readers

For a young Punjabi, the practical points are simple: it is free to enter, it is open from age 15, and the question bank is built from the same material as government recruitment tests, so the practice has a use beyond the prize. For the state, a ₹1 crore top prize and cash for 10,000 players is a sizeable commitment of public money to a competition, and the transparency Mann promised will depend on how scoring, ties and the in-person rounds are run.

## What happens next

The government is expected to publish the detailed rules, the round schedule and the prize table through the portal, and registration details are to appear in newspapers. Until then, the only fixed points are the 11-round format, the ₹1 crore top prize and the age band of 15 to 30.
