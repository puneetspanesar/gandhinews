Pakistan were **151 for 5 at lunch** on the fifth and final day of the first Test against Sri Lanka in Rawalpindi on Friday, November 13, 2026, a lead of 139 runs. Saud Shakeel was on 23 and wicketkeeper Ghazi Ghori on 7, with 50 overs left in the day at the break.

Sri Lanka's seamers took four wickets in the morning, **including captain Babar Azam for 34**, but Pakistan still lead by 139 and the weather and light have repeatedly cost play. Sri Lanka, who need a win to go 1-0 up in the two-match series, now have to bowl the hosts out cheaply and then bat for the runs.

## Four wickets before lunch

Pakistan resumed on 57 for 1, 45 ahead, after rain and bad light had ended day four early. Azan Awais, on 22, fell to **Lahiru Kumara with the first ball of the day**. Mohammad Taha, who had made 24 overnight and did not add to it, was caught behind off Asitha Fernando, and Pakistan were 57 for 3.

Babar and Shakeel then added **50 for the fourth wicket**. Babar had a life on 22 when Kamindu Mendis dropped him off Fernando, and he hit six fours in his 48-ball innings. Sri Lanka set a leg-side trap for him and Milan Rathnayake had him caught at short square leg for 34. Salman Ali Agha fell in the same way to Rathnayake for 13, leaving Pakistan on 125 for 5.

Rathnayake finished the session with 2 for 32 in 15 overs and Fernando with 2 for 20 in 10. Kumara took 1 for 42, and Dilshan Madushanka bowled 12 overs without a wicket. There was a 20-minute break for bad light as lunch approached.

## A long wait for a hundred

Babar's dismissal was his **42nd Test innings without a century**. The short-leg trap ended his 50-run stand with Shakeel. Pakistan still have Shakeel, who made 23 off 85 balls before lunch, and Ghori to build on the lead.

## How the match got here

Pakistan declared at 434 for 9 on day two. Sri Lanka answered through a fourth-wicket stand of 249 between Dhananjaya de Silva and Kusal Mendis, both of whom made centuries, as we reported in our [day three account](/en/news/de-silva-kusal-mendis-unbeaten-sri-lanka-404-4-rawalpindi-day-three). Debutant Saqib Khan then took 7 for 130 on day four and Sri Lanka were all out for 446, a lead of 12, in a collapse we described in our [report on Saqib's debut](/en/news/saqib-khan-seven-wickets-debut-sri-lanka-446-rawalpindi-test).

Pakistan's second innings was 57 for 1 when rain and bad light stopped play, as our [day four report](/en/news/pakistan-57-for-1-lead-45-rain-bad-light-end-day-four-rawalpindi) noted. Tayyab Arif was out lbw to Fernando for 11.

## What the result means

A draw is worth **4 points** in the World Test Championship and a win 12. Pakistan were ninth on 4.63% in the ICC's October 31 review, and a win would lift them to about 14.17% on one calculation, against 7.50% after a draw. Sri Lanka would move to 33.33% with a draw on the same calculation. Those figures do not allow for any over-rate penalty.

With 50 overs left after lunch, Sri Lanka's bowlers need five more wickets quickly and their batters would then need to chase a target on the same pitch in the time that remains.

## What next

The second and final Test starts at the **Gaddafi Stadium in Lahore on Tuesday, November 17**, and runs to November 21, with play beginning at 9:30 am local time.
