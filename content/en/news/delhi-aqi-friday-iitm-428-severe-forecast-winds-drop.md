Delhi woke to very poor air on Thursday and the forecast for **Friday morning is worse**: the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM) projects the city's AQI at about **428 by 9 am on November 13, 2026**, which would be in the severe band, above the 400 mark. The hourly AQI was 314 at 3 am and **321 at 7 am on Thursday**, 22 points higher than at the same hour on Wednesday, and it touched 323 at 8 am.

## What the forecast says

The projection is a PM2.5-driven figure from IITM's air quality early warning system, and it rests on the weather rather than on a sudden jump in smoke. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) expects the **mixing depth to fall from 2,000 metres on Thursday to 1,400 metres on Friday**. The mixing depth is the height up to which pollutants can spread through the air, so a lower ceiling packs the same smoke and dust into a thinner layer.

Winds are forecast to turn weak as well. On Thursday they were north-westerly at up to 15 km/h. On Friday they are expected to be variable south-westerly and westerly at only **5 to 10 km/h**, too slow to carry pollution out of the city. The numbers above are forecasts; the actual Friday readings will show whether the projection holds, and the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) bulletin at 4 pm gives the day's 24-hour average.

## Where Thursday stood

On Thursday the PM2.5 particle was the main pollutant at 48 of the 57 CPCB stations reporting data. **IIT Delhi read above 410 in the severe band**, with the CPCB data quoted as 414 in one report and 416 in another, and NSUT Jaffarpur was at 404. DTU read 372 and Anand Vihar 319. Most other stations sat in the very poor range, while the cantonment station at 128 was in the moderate band.

Satellites recorded **128 farm fires in Punjab and Haryana on November 11**, and upper-level winds were carrying some of that smoke towards the capital. IITM projects stubble burning at about 20% of Delhi's PM2.5 on Thursday, falling to about 11% in the days that follow.

## The Diwali spike has faded, the base has not

Firecrackers contributed **7.4% of PM2.5 on Wednesday**, down from 29.8% on Tuesday morning and a peak of 71.1% between 1 am and 9 am on November 9, the morning after Diwali. Even so, the three largest sources on Wednesday were secondary organic matter at 22.7%, domestic solid biofuel at 22.3% and vehicles at 19.3%, which are the everyday sources that do not go away when the crackers do.

The Centre for Science and Environment (CSE) found that Delhi's average PM2.5 in the week before Diwali was **236 micrograms per cubic metre, a three-year high**. Anumita Roychowdhury of CSE said "baseline pollution is higher than in previous years."

## Curbs in force

The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) put **GRAP Stage III in force across Delhi-NCR on November 6**. It includes curbs on non-essential construction and demolition and on road dust, hybrid classes for schools up to Class 5, and advice to offices to allow work from home. We reported the order in our story on [GRAP Stage III](/en/news/delhi-grap-stage-three-aqi-382-caqm) and Thursday's readings in [Delhi's air back at 319](/en/news/delhi-aqi-319-thursday-iit-delhi-413-severe-36-stations-above-300).

## Why it matters

For residents, another stretch of mornings with a health warning comes on top of a week of curbs. The restrictions fall on construction workers, goods transporters and parents of young children first, and they stay until CAQM reviews them. CAQM invoked Stage III early on November 6 precisely to act before the index crossed the severe line of 400 and to avoid a move to Stage IV, so a reading of 428 would test that approach.

## What happens next

The IMD expects **fog or mist on the mornings of November 13 to 15**, with daytime highs of 27 to 30 degrees Celsius and no rain. **From Monday, November 16**, maximum temperatures may fall to 26 to 28 degrees and minimums to 13 to 15, with mainly clear skies and shallow morning fog that can keep pollution close to the ground. The CPCB bulletin at 4 pm on Friday will show how the day's average compares with Thursday's, and CAQM will decide whether Stage III continues.
