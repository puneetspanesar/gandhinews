Indian shares **ended the Diwali Muhurat session slightly lower** on Sunday. The Sensex closed at 80,999.40, down 28.62 points or 0.04%, and the Nifty 50 finished 43.95 points, or 0.18%, lower at 24,986.40, slipping back under 25,000 on the first day of Samvat 2083.

The special one-hour session, which ran from 2:20 pm to 3:30 pm, **stopped a rally that had lasted seven straight regular sessions**. The Sensex traded between 80,932 and 81,442 and the Nifty touched a high of 25,131.85, but both benchmarks gave up their early gains in the last hour. They had closed Friday at 81,028.02 and 25,030.35.

## Pharma up, banks down

**Nifty Pharma and Nifty Healthcare rose the most**, with Sun Pharma and Tata Steel the leading gainers on the Sensex. Banking shares were the drag: Nifty Bank and Nifty Private Bank fell the most. SBI Life Insurance, InterGlobe Aviation, which runs IndiGo, and Bharti Airtel were among the biggest losers on the Nifty, and Mahindra & Mahindra also slipped on the Sensex.

Broader markets did better than the benchmarks. The Nifty Midcap 100 rose 0.13% and the Nifty Smallcap index gained nearly 1%. Divi's Laboratories, Finolex Cables, HDFC Bank and Reliance Industries were the most actively traded stocks by value on the NSE.

## What analysts said

Raamdeo Agrawal of Motilal Oswal was upbeat about the year ahead. "After two years of stagnation, market has the potential to give investors bumper returns by next Diwali," he said, pointing to low valuations and strong earnings growth.

Gaurav Garg of Lemonn was more measured. "Overall, the session reflected cautious optimism rather than aggressive buying," he said, adding that direction from here depends on earnings, foreign flows and crude oil.

Regular trading resumes on Monday, November 9.
