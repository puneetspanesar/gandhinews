Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi said on Monday, November 9, that **his challenge to Prime Minister Narendra Modi to a public debate still stands**, a day after Modi told security personnel in Bastar that "intellectual Naxals" had entered political parties. In a post on X addressed to the CRPF, Gandhi offered to debate "who is anti-national and who is controlled by foreign forces", "any time, any place".

## What Rahul Gandhi said

The post was written as a message to the force Modi had addressed on Diwali. "Dear CRPF, your faces said it all: you didn't buy the PM's lies. He called us Naxals," Gandhi wrote. He went on: "My challenge stands. Let him debate me on who is anti-national and who is controlled by foreign forces. Any time, any place."

He added that, in such a debate, Modi would not have the courage to face a jawan afterwards. Gandhi gave no evidence for his reading of the personnel's reaction at the camp, and the post names no new charge against the Prime Minister.

## The remark he is answering

Modi spoke on Sunday, November 8, at the CoBRA operations base at Karanpur in Bastar, Chhattisgarh. Our [full report on the address](/en/news/modi-bastar-pib-speech-no-crpf-death-47-years-dimagi-naxals-ramesh) records that he said armed Naxalites had left the forests but that "intellectual" Naxals, rendered in other accounts as "dimagi Naxals", had taken over many organisations and **had entered political parties**. He asked people to identify and defeat them, and did not name a party or a person.

Congress general secretary Jairam Ramesh answered the same evening, saying Modi had turned a non-political festival into an attack on rivals and that the Prime Minister was "distraught, disturbed and desperate". Gandhi's post on Monday is his own reply to the same remark.

## How the challenge built up

The offer is not new. According to a report published on November 4, Gandhi first posted on X on Monday, November 2, asking Modi to "choose any stage, any venue and debate me: who is anti-national? Who has connections with foreign forces?" On Tuesday, November 3, he repeated it, saying Modi had filed about 45 defamation cases against him but none over his claims of foreign links: "Go ahead, file one."

In a video the same day he said the youth of the country would watch and that Modi could **make anyone the judge**. The topics he listed were the financial system, how the India-US trade deal came about, why the Adani case was closed, and Epstein. He also tagged Rajnath Singh, Nitin Gadkari, Shivraj Singh Chouhan and Uttar Pradesh Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath, asking them to persuade the Prime Minister to attend.

## The government's side

No reply from Modi or the BJP to Monday's post was available when this report was written. The government's earlier answer to the challenge has been to point to Parliament. On November 2, Home Minister Amit Shah said at Hyderabad that the Centre was **ready to convene a special session of Parliament** to discuss the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls, EVMs and infiltrators, and dared Gandhi to take it up. Our [report on his offer](/en/news/amit-shah-special-session-sir-dares-rahul-mock-parliament) has the details. The INDIA bloc had held its own mock Parliament at Ramlila Maidan on November 1.

On October 31, at a rally marking Vallabhbhai Patel's birth anniversary, Modi had said that "obstructionist forces" were trying to divide India and had asked "who is secretly meeting whom abroad?", remarks that the opposition took as aimed at Gandhi. Modi did not name him.

## Why it matters

The two sides are now arguing over the forum, not only the charge. Gandhi wants a direct face-off outside Parliament, with a judge of the Prime Minister's choosing. The government says the House is the place. Neither side has set a date, a venue or a format, so the exchange has so far been conducted entirely through posts and speeches. For readers, the practical stake is whether a sitting Prime Minister and the Leader of the Opposition will answer each other's specific charges, on the economy, the trade deal and the closing of a corporate case, in one place and on the record.

## What happens next

There is no scheduled debate. Whether Modi or the BJP replies to Monday's post is the next thing to watch, along with whether the government names a date for the special Parliament session Shah said it was ready to call.
