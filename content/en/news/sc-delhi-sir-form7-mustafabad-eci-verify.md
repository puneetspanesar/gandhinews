The Supreme Court on Thursday asked the Election Commission of India to **verify a complaint that false Form 7 applications** were filed to get genuine voters deleted from the roll in Delhi's Mustafabad assembly constituency, and to take remedial steps if the claim holds. According to reports, a bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana disposed of the writ petition without expressing any view on the merits.

The direction went to the Commission and to Delhi's Chief Electoral Officer. They are to verify the allegation and take "necessary remedial measures in accordance with law", the reports say. The order is the Court's response to a petition filed during the Special Intensive Revision, or SIR, of Delhi's electoral rolls.

## What the petition said

The petitioner, reported to be Congress leader Ali Mehdi, argued that Form 7 objections filed in Mustafabad were **fraudulent and aimed at deleting real voters**. Form 7 is the form used to object to someone's name on a roll or to seek its deletion.

The petition leaned on recent newspaper reports. These said Form 7 applications for the constituency were filed in the names of some booth level agents, or BLAs, who later **denied having filed them**. They also said some people marked for deletion as dead or shifted were alive and living at the same address.

Counsel told the bench that **36 of the 37 Form 7 applications** filed were false, according to one report. The petition pointed to 28 electors facing deletion applications who, it said, were living at their recorded addresses. These are claims by the petitioner; the Court did not examine them.

The petitioner said he had already complained to the police, the Election Commission and the Delhi Chief Electoral Officer. He added that the police declined to register an FIR, and Justice Bagchi told him to approach the Magistrate. He also pointed to Uttar Pradesh, where, according to his submission, the Court had asked the Commission to act against bulk fake Form 7 filings during that state's SIR.

## What the bench said

The bench noted that the petitioner had already made a representation and that it should be examined by the authorities. "Since the petitioner has already submitted a representation, we dispose of the writ petition," the Court said, as quoted in reports.

Justice Bagchi drew a limit on what the Commission can do. BLAs are appointed by political parties, he said. "The Election Commission can blacklist him. Beyond that, they have no control," he said of an agent who files a fraudulent application. In practice, that means the Commission can bar an agent it finds at fault but cannot otherwise discipline party workers.

## How we got here

Delhi's draft roll was published on August 31. About **47 lakh names were left out** of a pre-revision electorate of roughly 1.45 crore. Since then the roll has been in court and on the ground. The Delhi High Court ruled on October 14 on voters' fears over Form 6, and the Supreme Court made suggestions on October 28, as we [reported on November 4](/en/news/supreme-court-delhi-sir-form-6-ec-clarification-cji-mention). Our earlier story on [the re-verification of 47 lakh deleted voters](/en/news/delhi-sir-reverification-47-lakh-deleted-voters) has the numbers.

The Mustafabad petition is separate from the plea by petitioners Anjali Bhardwaj and Amrita Johri over Form 6, which was listed for November 19. Reports of Thursday's proceedings do not say what happened in that matter.

## The INDIA bloc's questions

Separately, reports on November 16 said the INDIA bloc had asked the Supreme Court for permission to put **48 questions to the Election Commission** through an interrogatory. The questions are directed at the Chief Election Commissioner, the two other Election Commissioners and the Director General of Information Technology. They ask, among other things, how often the other two commissioners disagreed with the Chief Election Commissioner, who decided on a nationwide SIR, who decided to change Form 6, and who decided to centralise ERONET and ECINET and control voter rolls centrally. Reports of the November 19 order on Mustafabad do not mention that request, and no hearing date for it has been reported.

## Why it matters to voters

For a voter in Mustafabad, the practical question is whether a name has been targeted. Anyone listed for deletion has until **November 25** to file a claim or objection under the revised schedule, and the final roll is due on December 22. A person whose name is removed on a Form 7 they never knew about has a short window to be restored.

The order also leaves a gap. It does not fix a time by which the Commission must report, and it names no consequence beyond remedial measures under the law. With the petition disposed of, no further hearing is listed, so the complaint now rests with the Commission and the Delhi Chief Electoral Officer, and with the Magistrate if the petitioner pursues his FIR.

The Commission has not, in the reports seen, commented on the Mustafabad allegation. We will update this story when it responds or when the Court lists the connected Delhi SIR matters again.
