The Odisha government has given administrative approval to **five in-stream storage projects costing ₹625.47 crore**, officials announced in Bhubaneswar on Friday, November 13. The structures, on rivers in Rayagada, Kalahandi, Ganjam, Balangir and Subarnapur districts, are meant to hold monsoon flow in the river channel for the dry months, and the state says they will serve **about 72,000 people in 74 villages**.

The announcement says the approvals came within a month, and credits Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi's priority on water conservation. It does not name a committee or give a cost for each project.

## What has been cleared

The five projects named in the official statement are:

- **Dhuanpadar** on the Vamsadhara river, Rayagada district
- **Rebatiguda** on the Nagavali river, Rayagada district
- **Bhimpur** on the Bahuda river, Ganjam district
- **Muralijore**, near Nijharidara village in Bhawanipatna block, Kalahandi district
- **Kutasingha barrage** on the Ong river, a tributary of the Mahanadi, serving Balangir and Subarnapur districts

An in-stream storage structure is built across a river to hold monsoon flow in the channel itself. The statement says the stored water is meant to **improve availability in the lean season**, recharge groundwater, and support drinking water, irrigation, livestock and rural livelihoods. Dhuanpadar, Muralijore and Rebatiguda are also expected to open up fisheries.

The statement gives no construction schedule and no completion dates. It also does not say how many hectares will be irrigated.

## A programme running since 2024

The five approvals are part of the in-stream storage structure scheme, which the Water Resources Department has run **in mission mode since 2024**. A progress update released on November 9 described the current phase, called ISS 2.0, as a programme of **89 projects in four phases**.

According to that update, 10 structures are complete: Julko in Kalahandi, Chhatamakhana in Balangir, Jakamarapalli, Dholpita and Dhanei in Ganjam, Mahulpali in Baragada, Saptamatruka in Jajpur, and Gochara, Kirasasan and Budharaja in Sambalpur. Together they have a designed storage capacity of **about 1.10 crore cubic metres**, and are expected to serve about 1.82 lakh people in more than 122 villages.

A further 22 projects are under construction across 13 districts, with five in Sundargarh and three in Khordha. Another 35 are in the pipeline, 12 of which are expected to start construction shortly. The update's categories add up to 67 of the 89 projects, and it does not say where the remaining 22 stand.

## Why it matters to the districts

The point of the scheme is the gap between monsoons, when river flow drops and villages lose their main source of water. For the **72,000 people covered by this batch**, the practical test is whether the water is stored and available when the dry months begin, which now depends on how fast construction starts. The ten finished structures give the state a first measure: they were designed to hold about 1.10 crore cubic metres between them.

The approval comes in a month when Odisha has also dealt with the other side of the water problem. Cyclone Arnab brought floods and landslides, and the state has asked the Centre for ₹2,526 crore in assistance, as we reported in our [Ganjam landslide story](/en/news/ganjam-landslide-toll-seven-odisha-seeks-2526-crore). Bhimpur, one of the five new sites, is in Ganjam.

## What happens next

The statement gives no dates for tendering or for the start of work, so the next markers are the work orders for the five structures and the completion of the 22 projects already under construction. Per-project costs and the number of hectares each structure will irrigate have not been published.
