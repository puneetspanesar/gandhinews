India's governments spent **about ₹4.14 lakh crore on health in 2023-24**, roughly three times the ₹1.30 lakh crore of 2013-14, and the share of health costs that households pay from their own pockets fell to **42.7%**, the Union Health Ministry said in the National Health Accounts (NHA) estimates for 2023-24 released in New Delhi on Sunday, November 15. A decade earlier that share was 64.2%.

The figures are the eleventh round of national health accounts. They were prepared by the National Health Accounts Technical Secretariat at the National Health Systems Resource Centre, which sits under the ministry, using the System of Health Accounts 2011 framework. Reports of the release appeared from about 2:40 pm IST on Sunday.

## What the accounts show

The headline numbers cover the ten years from 2013-14 to 2023-24. According to the release as reported:

- **Government health spending**, counting the Centre and states, went from ₹1,29,778 crore to ₹4,14,196 crore.
- **Per head of population**, it rose from ₹1,042 to **₹2,959**, about 2.8 times.
- **Out-of-pocket expenditure**, what patients pay for consultations, medicines and tests at the time of care, fell from 64.2% of total health expenditure to 42.7%, a drop of **21.5 percentage points**.
- **The government's share** of total health spending rose by 14.9 percentage points, which the ministry describes as an increase of nearly 52%.
- **Primary healthcare**, the clinics and sub-centres that are a patient's first stop, drew about ₹1.53 lakh crore of government money in 2023-24.

Two other lines moved up as well. **Social security spending on health**, which covers government-financed insurance, state insurance schemes, social health insurance and medical reimbursement for government employees, went from 6% of the total to 11%. **Private health insurance** went from 3.4% to 10.2%.

## How we got here

The 2013-14 figures are the starting point the ministry uses in every comparison. The previous round, for 2022-23, put government health spending at ₹3.85 lakh crore and the out-of-pocket share at 43.4%, with per-head spending at ₹2,786. Set against that, the new round adds about ₹29,000 crore of government spending in a year, and takes **0.7 percentage points** off the out-of-pocket share.

That is a much slower fall than the one over the decade. Of the 21.5-point drop since 2013-14, only 0.7 points came in the last year covered. The ministry's own numbers therefore show the steepest gains came earlier in the period and the latest year moved the share only slightly.

## What the ministry says it reflects

The release credits a set of central schemes. It names **Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY**, which gives eligible families cashless hospital treatment, the Ayushman Arogya Mandirs that run primary care, the free drugs and diagnostics initiative, the PM National Dialysis Programme, the PM Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana that sells low-priced generic medicines, the AMRIT pharmacies and price regulation by the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority. Our explainer on the [Ayushman Vay Vandana card](/en/explainer/ayushman-vay-vandana-card-70-plus-5-lakh-cover-how-to-apply) sets out how the scheme's cover for people over 70 works.

The ministry said the trends "reflect India's progress towards a more accessible, affordable and financially protective healthcare system", and that the decline in household spending "reflects the expanding role of government-supported healthcare services". The release presents the shift towards public funding as a step towards universal health coverage.

The accounts come from the ministry headed by Union Health Minister J. P. Nadda; our earlier report on him is [here](/en/news/nadda-colombo-bimstec-health-ministers-jamnagar-centre).

## Why it matters to readers

Out-of-pocket spending is the part of the health bill that falls on households directly, with no insurance or government facility to share it. At 42.7%, it still means that of every ₹100 spent on health in India in 2023-24, about **₹43 was paid by patients and families themselves**. The fall from 64.2% is real, but the share remains high, and the accounts do not say how much of it is spent on medicines, hospital stays or outpatient care.

The figures also show who now carries more of the cost. Government money pays for more of the total, social security schemes cover 11%, and private insurance covers 10.2%, three times its share of ten years ago. For a family, that mix decides whether a hospital stay can be paid through a scheme card or an insurance policy, or through savings and borrowing.

## What the reports do not say

Neither the ministry's statement nor the reports on it include comment from independent economists, state governments or health campaigners, and the release itself credits the Modi government for the progress. The accounts report spending, not outcomes: they do not measure whether treatment is reaching more people, how long they wait, or what patients pay for care that is not covered by a scheme.

The accounts also count spending by both the Centre and states, so the ₹4.14 lakh crore is not a Union Budget figure. The government's share of total health spending in 2023-24 is not given in the reports as a percentage; they give only the 14.9 point rise.

## What happens next

No date for the next round of estimates, covering 2024-25, was given in the reports. Until then, the 2023-24 accounts are the latest official measure of how India pays for its health care, and the Union Budget for 2027-28 will show how much the Centre sets aside for health in the coming year.
