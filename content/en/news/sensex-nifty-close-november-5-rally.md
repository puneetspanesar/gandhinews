Dalal Street had its best day in a long while on Thursday. The **Sensex closed up 2,008.61 points, or 2.6%, at 79,310.01**, and the Nifty 50 gained 576 points, or 2.4%, to finish at 24,536.60, back above the 24,500 mark.

It was the **sixth straight session of gains** for the benchmarks. The Sensex had touched 79,372.57 around 2 pm and gave up only a little of that in the last hour and a half of trade. Investors were richer by **about ₹7 lakh crore** by the close.

## Why the market rose

Two things far from Mumbai set the mood. Crude oil kept sliding: Brent, which fell 8.7% on Wednesday, slipped further to around $93.50 a barrel on Thursday, **roughly 20% below its recent highs**. For India, which imports most of its oil, that means a smaller import bill, less pressure on prices and lower costs for industry.

The second was the result of the US midterm elections. The Democrats' **win in both houses of Congress** is expected to push the Trump administration towards diplomacy over the Iran conflict, though no peace agreement has been reached. Wall Street set the lead overnight, with the Dow rising 3.03% to 53,356.12.

A firmer rupee, at a two-week high against the dollar, and a strong rally in government bonds added to the cheer.

## Banks do the heavy lifting

Financial shares carried the rally. **ICICI Bank alone added 119.19 points** to the Nifty, followed by HDFC Bank with 78.31 points and Bajaj Finance with 45.14. Bank Nifty rose more than 3% and the Nifty Financial Services index 3.99%.

Nifty Auto was the best-performing sector, **up 4.14%**, while realty and IT also gained. InterGlobe Aviation, which runs IndiGo and gains most when jet fuel gets cheaper, rose 5.43%, and Asian Paints 4.36%. Shriram Finance was also among the leading gainers.

The few drags were Bharti Airtel, which cost the Nifty 7.30 points ahead of its September-quarter results, NTPC and Apollo Hospitals. Pharma, healthcare, media and energy lagged the market.

## Broad buying

The rally reached beyond the biggest names. The **Nifty Midcap 100 rose 1.87% to 60,620.90**, while the Nifty Smallcap 100 added a more modest 0.56% to 19,132.45. Of 3,723 stocks traded, 2,547 rose and 1,084 fell, and 99 hit 52-week highs.

Foreign investors have been back in force. They **bought a net ₹7,284.03 crore on Wednesday**, their biggest single-day purchase in three years, and about ₹20,834 crore over four sessions, while domestic institutions sold ₹3,712.23 crore. Thursday's figures are due later in the evening.

Analysts had said a close above 24,780 on the Nifty would signal a clearer turn in the trend. The index now stands about 240 points short of that, with crude and the outcome of US-Iran diplomacy likely to decide the next leg.
