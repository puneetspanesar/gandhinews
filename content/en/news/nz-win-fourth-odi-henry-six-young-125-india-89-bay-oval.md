Matt Henry took **6 for 27, a career best**, and New Zealand bowled India out for 89 in 26.4 overs to win the fourth one-day international at Bay Oval, Mount Maunganui, by 244 runs on Friday, November 13, 2026. Will Young's 125 had earlier carried New Zealand to 333, and the chase of 334 was over almost as soon as it began. India still lead the five-match series 2-1, with the fifth and last ODI at the same ground on Sunday.

It was New Zealand's first win of a series that India had led 2-0 after rain shaped the first two games and washed out the third.

## A first over that settled it

India, led for the first time in this series by Shreyas Iyer after Shubman Gill and KL Rahul left for the Test camp, lost **Rohit Sharma, Virat Kohli and Iyer without scoring**. At 0 for 3 the chase was already in trouble, and it was 9 for 4 in six overs when Ruturaj Gaikwad went for 5, caught at second slip off Kyle Jamieson.

Henry's spell then cleaned out the rest. He bowled Ishan Kishan for 27, the top score of the innings, and later bowled Kuldeep Yadav and Gurnoor Brar, finishing with six wickets in 9.4 overs. Matthew Fisher took 3 for 31, including Axar Patel caught at slip for 21 and Auqib Nabi caught behind for 5, and Jamieson had 1 for 12 from six overs. Naman Dhir made 21 before he was caught at extra cover.

Kishan and Axar gave the innings some shape with a fifth-wicket stand of 44, the only partnership of note. The defeat is described in match reports as the **second-heaviest by runs** in India's ODI history.

One report points out that India's 9 for 4 was the same position as in their 1983 World Cup game against Zimbabwe, which Kapil Dev turned around with 175 not out. No one at Bay Oval was able to do the same.


## Young leads New Zealand to 333

Asked to bat, New Zealand got away to a start that India never recovered from. Young and Tim Seifert put on **85 for the first wicket**, and the hosts were 72 for 0 after the powerplay. Seifert made 58 with three sixes before he fell.

Young went on to **125 off 108 balls**, with 14 fours and four sixes, his fifth ODI hundred and his first against India. He was dropped twice on 5, by Rohit at slip and by Kishan behind the stumps, and India paid heavily for both chances. Finn Allen then hit 41 off 30 balls, and he and Young added 90 in 65 balls.

New Zealand were 258 for 6 when Michael Bracewell was out, and Mitchell Santner (24), Kristian Clarke (22, lbw to Nabi) and Jamieson (19 not out) kept the total moving. The innings ended on 333 in the last over.

For India, Axar Patel took 2 for 39 from his 10 overs and Nabi 2 for 59, and Brar took two wickets for 69. Arshdeep Singh and Dhir took one each, and Kuldeep Yadav went wicketless for 73 in eight overs.

## How the series got here

India won the first ODI at Eden Park by five wickets on DLS and the second at Wellington by four runs on DLS, both after rain, as we reported in [the Wellington match report](/en/news/india-beat-new-zealand-dls-four-runs-brar-wellington-second-odi). The third, at Hamilton, was [abandoned without a ball bowled](/en/news/hamilton-third-odi-abandoned-rain-india-lead-2-0-iyer-to-captain), which left India 2-0 up.

Before this game India had split the squad, as we reported in [our preview](/en/news/iyer-first-odi-captaincy-mount-maunganui-fourth-odi-preview): Gill and Rahul went to Wellington to prepare for the Tests, while Rohit, Kohli, Kishan, Gaikwad and a group of fringe players stayed at Bay Oval. New Zealand were also short, without Lockie Ferguson, Blair Tickner and five Test-bound players. Friday's pitch, described in previews as the flattest of the three grounds, offered the first full 50 overs a side of the series, and New Zealand made the most of it.

## What it means for Sunday

The result leaves the series open. India need one more win to take it, and a washout would also leave them 2-1 ahead. New Zealand must win on Sunday to level it at 2-2. The Test series starts on November 19, 2026, so the Sunday game also decides how much work the white-ball group gets before the squads merge.

For India the immediate questions are batting depth and fielding. Two catches went down in Young's first five runs, and Rohit, Kohli and Iyer, who all fell for 0, will want a better outing. 

## What happens next

The fifth and final ODI is on Sunday, November 15, 2026, at Bay Oval, Mount Maunganui. India lead 2-1, and the Test series in New Zealand begins on November 19, 2026.
