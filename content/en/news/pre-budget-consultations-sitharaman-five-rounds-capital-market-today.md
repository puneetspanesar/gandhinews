Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman is due to meet representatives of the **capital market, financial services, banking and insurance sectors** in New Delhi on Wednesday, November 18, the latest step in the pre-Budget consultations for the Union Budget 2027-28. It follows **four rounds held on Monday and Tuesday**, with economists, agriculture experts, manufacturers and small businesses.

The Budget is expected to be presented on **February 1, 2027**, with the Economic Survey likely on January 29 or January 31. It will be the fourth Budget of the Modi government's third term and Sitharaman's tenth in a row.

## What has been held so far

The exercise opened on Monday, November 16, when Sitharaman chaired the **first meeting with leading economists**. The Finance Ministry said on X that it was attended by Anuradha Thakur, Secretary of the Department of Economic Affairs, and Chief Economic Adviser V Anantha Nageswaran, along with senior officials of the department.

Later the same day she chaired the **second round, with experts from agriculture and rural development**. The ministry said the secretaries of Economic Affairs and Fisheries, and senior officials of the Department of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare, were present. Discussion covered agriculture, rural growth and ways to strengthen the sector's contribution to the economy.

On Tuesday, November 17, she chaired the **third consultation with manufacturing sector leaders**, joined by the secretaries of Economic Affairs, the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade, Mines and Corporate Affairs, and the Chief Economic Adviser. The **fourth round, with MSMEs and startups**, followed the same day, with the MSME secretary also present.

The Finance Ministry has not disclosed what was said in any of the meetings, or the recommendations made. The ministry's posts name only the sector and the officials present.

## What the markets side is expected to raise

For the Wednesday meeting, capital market stakeholders are expected to press for changes on **long-term capital gains tax, short-term capital gains tax and the Securities Transaction Tax**. The industry has in the past asked for long-term capital gains tax on equities to be abolished, the short-term rate to be cut to 10%, and the Securities Transaction Tax to be rationalised.

The industry chamber PHDCCI made that ask in a pre-Budget submission on November 10, urging the government to **scrap long-term capital gains tax on equity investments** and cut the short-term rate to 10% from 20%. In a separate recommendation it called for customs duty reforms, including rationalising duties on critical industrial raw materials and energy inputs.

No attendee list for Wednesday's meeting has been published, and the ministry has not said which tax proposals it will take up.

## The growth backdrop

Economists at the first meeting were reported to have projected growth of around **7.5%**. Bank of Baroda's Sonal Badhan estimated 7.4% to 7.5% for July to September, against 8.1% in the same quarter of 2025-26, while Morgan Stanley expects 7.6% for 2026-27. The official July to September growth figure is due on November 30, and the Finance Ministry will have it before the Budget is finalised.

Earlier this year the economy grew 7.8% in April to June, as we reported in [our story on the first-quarter GDP data](/en/news/gdp-grows-7-8-in-april-june-quarter).

## What happens next

The consultations are the first step of a wider process in which the government gathers inputs from industry, trade bodies, agriculture experts, labour organisations, states and the social sector before it finalises Budget proposals. Sitharaman is expected to meet **young people next week**, the first time youth have been formally included in the pre-Budget process.

For taxpayers and investors, nothing changes until the Budget speech. Tax proposals, if any, are announced only when the finance minister presents the Budget in Parliament on the expected date of February 1, 2027.
