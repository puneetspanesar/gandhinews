Tata Trusts chairman Noel Tata has said that listing Tata Sons on the stock market **"will change the Tata way"**, and that the Trusts will work with the Reserve Bank of India and other regulators to find a way for the group's holding company to stay unlisted. He spoke at a leadership summit in New Delhi on Saturday, November 14, 2026.

"My worry is that listing, which is what some people are proposing, will change the Tata way," he said. The Trusts, he added, "will play a constructive role with regulators and others".

## What Noel Tata said

His main argument was about who owns the group. Tata Sons is held mostly by charitable trusts, and Noel Tata said that structure has protected the group's values for generations. "The owner cannot die, the owner cannot be bought and the owner cannot retire," he said.

A listing would bring in a different kind of owner. He warned that **with 30,000 to 40,000 shareholders**, "profit may take precedence over philanthropy". The group, he said, has been a partner in the development of India, pointing to its semiconductor project and its electric vehicle battery plant.

He did not argue against the group's strategy. "I do not question the direction of what we have done," he said. His concern, as he put it, is **execution and capital allocation** on large projects that take years to pay back. He also spoke of the retail businesses, noting that Westside runs about 320 stores and aims for more than 500, and that Zudio, launched in 2016, starts at ₹199.

## Why a listing is on the table

Tata Sons holds the shares of the roughly $165 billion Tata Group. According to reports, the Reserve Bank classified it as an upper-layer non-banking finance company in September 2022, a category that is expected to list. The company reportedly repaid about ₹21,813 crore of debt and in March 2024 applied to give up its registration as a core investment company, a route that would have kept it outside that rule.

In September 2026 **the RBI rejected that application**. That left the board with fewer options, and at a meeting on September 17 it decided to begin steps towards a listing in line with RBI regulations. The same meeting approved a fresh five-year term for chairman N Chandrasekaran, reversing his August decision not to seek reappointment after February 2027. The board approved it by a 4:1 vote, and Noel Tata dissented on both decisions.

## The two camps

The Trusts hold about 66% of Tata Sons. The Shapoorji Pallonji Group, the second-largest shareholder with 18.37%, backs a listing. Its chairman, Shapoor Mistry, has argued that a listing would improve transparency and accountability.

Tata Sons' management has said it must follow the law and will list as the RBI instructs. Within the Trusts, trustees Venu Srinivasan and Vijay Singh wrote on November 11 to fellow trustee Darius Khambata arguing that charity-owned companies can be public. They cited Novo Nordisk, Hershey, Lundbeck and Demant, and pointed to listed Tata companies such as TCS, Tata Steel, Tata Power and Tata Motors. Their case is that **a listing would bring a market valuation**, better governance and less uncertainty over succession.

Noel Tata, who is 70, is the half-brother of Ratan Tata and took over the Trusts after his death.

## How we got here

The dispute became public in September. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said on November 2 that the quarrel [cannot be spilling out in public](/en/news/sitharaman-tata-critical-component-settle-amicably-investors/) and must be settled amicably among the two sides. Noel Tata had by then challenged the board resolution that reappointed Chandrasekaran, and the Trusts have since questioned its legality.

## Why it matters

Tata Sons is the holding company of the roughly $165 billion Tata Group, and its ownership decides how the group's companies are run. For investors, a listing would put a market value on the holding company itself, and the trustees who favour it say it would bring more transparency and less uncertainty over succession. For the Trusts, the question is whether a wider shareholder base would change the group's priorities, as Noel Tata fears.

## What happens next

No date has been announced for a listing or for any RBI decision on an alternative. The Trusts have said they will talk to regulators, and Tata Sons' board has said it will proceed as the RBI requires. Neither side has said when the matter will return to the board.
