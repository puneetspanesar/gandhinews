The Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS) has warned hospitals across the country against reusing medical devices and surgical items that are made to be used only once, saying the practice puts patients at risk and must stop in both public and private hospitals.

In a letter to the health departments of states and union territories, the directorate said some private and corporate hospitals were sterilising and reusing single-use items in specialties including cardiology, nephrology, oncology and neurosurgery.

The DGHS, part of the Union Health Ministry, set out four areas of concern: patient safety, infection control, compliance with the regulations on medical devices, and the cost of care. Reprocessing items that were never designed to be cleaned and used again can spread infection and lead to the equipment failing during a procedure, it said.

The letter asks states and union territories to enforce the existing rules strictly in both government and private hospitals.

The directorate has not publicly named any hospital, and the letter as reported does not set out new penalties. Action against hospitals that keep reprocessing such items now rests with the state and union territory authorities it has written to.
