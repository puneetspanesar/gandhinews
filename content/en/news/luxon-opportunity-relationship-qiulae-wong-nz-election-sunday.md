New Zealand Prime Minister Christopher Luxon said on Sunday that it is **'entirely appropriate to build a relationship'** with the Opportunity Party, a change of tone from a campaign in which he repeatedly ruled out working with it and called it a party of "too much tax".

"I think it's entirely appropriate to build a relationship with Qiulae [Wong] and Opportunity," Luxon said, naming artificial intelligence, infrastructure and social media bans as areas where parties could work together. He said the result showed a need for more cooperation across Parliament.

## A one-seat majority

Luxon was careful to say that a deal with Opportunity was not on the table. "We believe we can actually form a government with New Zealand First and Act," he said, calling it **"highly unlikely" that National would need Opportunity's support**. He declined to say whether he would be comfortable governing with a majority of one seat.

The preliminary count from the Electoral Commission gives National 42 seats, ACT 13 and New Zealand First 12, a total of **67 in a Parliament of 131**, one more than the 66 needed. Labour has 36, the Greens 16, Opportunity 7 and Te Pāti Māori 5, which comes to 64. Parliament has 11 more seats than the usual 120 because of overhang, which arises when a party wins more electorate seats than its share of the party vote entitles it to.

## Why the numbers can still move

National and Labour each won just under 30% of the party vote, and Luxon rejected the idea that this leaves National without a mandate. "There's no doubt about it. We've got a mandate," he said. "New Zealanders gave us 42 seats. That's the biggest number of seats in Parliament."

Opportunity won 5.96% of the party vote, enough to enter Parliament for the first time. Wong has said her party will open talks with the major parties, and Opportunity sits in the left bloc, which Labour and the Greens see as their natural partner.

About **399,365 special votes, roughly 14% of the total**, are still to be counted. They have tended to help the left, and the Commission will release the official result on **Friday, November 27**. Until then the coalition stays on as a caretaker government, and there is no legal deadline for forming a new one.
