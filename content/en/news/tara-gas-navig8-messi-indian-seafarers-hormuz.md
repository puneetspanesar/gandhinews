An Indian seafarer is still missing after the Panama-flagged LPG tanker **Tara Gas was hit by missiles on two successive days** near the Strait of Hormuz, and on Wednesday evening a second ship carrying Indians was struck. The Liberia-flagged crude tanker Navig8 Messi was **targeted in a projectile attack** in the Persian Gulf as it approached the Strait, Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal said in a post on X. All three Indians aboard are safe, though one officer was taken to hospital for first aid and medical evaluation.

The two incidents have left the shipping ministry and the Ministry of External Affairs tracking crews on both vessels from New Delhi, while the Indian Embassy in Oman works with the Omani authorities on the search for the missing man.

## What the minister and the ministry said

On Wednesday Sonowal said the Navig8 Messi, a crude oil tanker, carried three Indian nationals along with the rest of its crew. He said all were "safe and accounted for". **No serious injuries were reported**, but one Indian officer was taken to hospital. The government, he said, is in close contact with the ship's management company and is tracking developments. The reports do not give the time or position of the strike, the damage to the vessel, or the nationalities of the other crew.

The earlier case is graver. Tara Gas, an LPG tanker, had 25 crew, 24 of them Indian. Sonowal said on Monday, November 16, that a missile had hit the ship and that "all 24 Indian nationals, along with the entire 25-member crew, are completely safe with no injuries". He added that the government was "continuously monitoring the situation".

That account did not hold. A second attack the next day forced the whole crew to abandon the ship. On Tuesday, November 17, the Ministry of External Affairs said **21 Indians had been brought ashore at Khasab**, in Oman's Musandam province, two injured Indians were admitted to hospital and one remained unaccounted for. Sonowal called the situation distressing and said his ministry was urgently coordinating with the ship management company and regional rescue authorities to locate the missing seafarer and support the rescued crew. The missing man has not been named in any report.

## India's line

The MEA said the continuing attacks on shipping were "deeply worrisome and pose a serious threat to the safety of civilian seafarers". It said **the targeting of commercial shipping and civilian infrastructure must end** and that free and unimpeded navigation through international waterways should be restored, in line with international law. It thanked the Government of Oman for its help in the rescue. The ministry did not say who fired on the ship.

No report on either incident names the attacker, and none gives a claim of responsibility. Iran's Revolutionary Guards have previously claimed strikes on tankers in these waters, as our earlier reporting shows, but nothing published so far ties them to these two ships.

## A fortnight of strikes on ships with Indian crews

The attacks fit a pattern that has run through November. On November 10 a projectile hit the Liberia-flagged supertanker Fujairah Energy in the Persian Gulf, injuring four crew, one of them Indian; our report on [that attack and the Sonowal statement](/en/news/indian-seafarer-hurt-fujairah-energy-tanker-hit-persian-gulf-sonowal) has the detail. Days later Iran said three more tankers had hit mines in and east of the Strait, which we covered in [the Hormuz mines and drone report](/en/news/hormuz-three-tankers-mines-predator-qeshm-brent-india). Those were Iran's claims and were not independently confirmed.

Indian crews have been caught before. In October we reported [two Indian seafarers missing after tanker attacks off Oman](/en/news/two-indian-seafarers-missing-after-tanker-attacks-off-oman), when 22 Indians were aboard two tankers hit on a single Sunday. The MEA's last count, given in the week of November 11, was that **23 Indians, 15 of them seafarers, have died** in the West Asia conflict, with a missing Tara Gas crew member not yet counted among them.

## Why it matters to readers

Indian seafarers crew a large share of the world's merchant fleet, so an attack on almost any tanker in the Gulf is likely to put Indian families at risk whatever flag the ship flies. The Tara Gas carried liquefied petroleum gas, the fuel in India's household cylinders, and the Navig8 Messi was a crude carrier; both are cargoes India buys from the Gulf. Neither report says where the two ships were bound, and neither says what cargo was aboard at the time of the attacks.

For the families, the practical questions are narrower. They want the name and condition of the missing man, news of the two hospitalised crew members and a date for the rescued men to fly home. The reports give none of these.

## What happens next

The first test is the search at sea. The Indian Embassy in Oman is monitoring the search and rescue operation with Omani authorities, and Sonowal has said his ministry will keep coordinating with the ship management company until the missing seafarer is found. A fuller statement on the Navig8 Messi, on the condition of the officer in hospital and on the damage to the ship, is awaited from the ministry and the vessel's managers. Whether the 21 Indians landed at Khasab are flown home, and when, has not been announced.

Diplomacy has so far not slowed the strikes. The MEA has repeatedly called for free and safe navigation through Hormuz and for an end to hostilities in West Asia, and it has urged a settlement through dialogue. So far each new incident has brought a fresh round of statements from the ministry.
