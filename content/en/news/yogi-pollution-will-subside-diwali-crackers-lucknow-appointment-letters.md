Uttar Pradesh Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath on Wednesday brushed aside concerns about the smog that followed Diwali, telling a Lucknow audience: "**So what if it has increased? It will subside as well.** Why are you worried?"

He was speaking at a government programme in the state capital where appointment letters were handed to 1,146 newly recruited junior assistants and ₹215 crore in accident aid was released to 4,300 farmers' families. Diwali was on Sunday, November 8, and Delhi's air was very poor to severe through Tuesday, and very poor again by 8:40 am on Wednesday.

## What he said

Adityanath said people had celebrated the festival with enthusiasm. "It is good that people celebrated Diwali with 'dhoom-dhadaka' and burst crackers," he said, according to the reported translation of his Hindi remarks. "If the public burst crackers one day, it created pollution!" he added, and told critics not to worry because the pollution "would be mitigated as well".

**He said his government had "decided not to impose any prohibitions"** on crackers. He turned the argument on those who raise the issue: "When someone celebrates a festival, people speak up about pollution, but not when someone does anything wrong." And: "Nobody says anything to those who polluted the entire system with their corrupt misdeeds." He also said that before 2017 riots would break out ahead of festivals, a claim he made without figures.

The remark came in the middle of a speech that was largely about jobs. The chief minister accused earlier governments of recruitment fraud, including impersonation in exams, and said talented young people had left Uttar Pradesh because they could not find honest work at home.

## Where Delhi's air stands

On Wednesday the capital's overall index read **299 at 7 am**, in the "poor" band, against 307 ("very poor") at the same hour on Tuesday. By 8:40 am the reading was 308, with 29 of 59 reporting stations in the very poor range and none severe. Chhawla was highest at 398, followed by Chandni Chowk at 383 and ITO at 369. Falling temperatures and moderate fog were holding pollutants close to the ground, and the Delhi government was running water sprinklers.

The relief is recent. On Monday, the morning after Diwali, 15 stations were severe, with Burari Crossing and Sonia Vihar both at 441, as we reported [here](/en/news/delhi-aqi-368-7am-monday-15-stations-severe-burari-sonia-vihar-441/). Forecasters expect wind speeds to rise over the next few days, which should improve air further. The India Meteorological Department expects fog or mist in the early mornings from November 11 to 16.

## How we got here

The Supreme Court had allowed only certified green crackers in Delhi-NCR on November 7 and 8, with the full ban returning on November 9, as we reported [here](/en/news/supreme-court-green-crackers-delhi-ncr-diwali-november-7-8/). Bursting went on well past the permitted windows. On Diwali night 20 of 59 Delhi stations were severe, and hourly data that went missing from the central board's records later showed PM2.5 at **3,744 micrograms at Mundka**, far above the 500 ceiling of the index.

Punjab recorded 1,152 farm fires on Diwali day, the highest single day of the season, and that set off a row between Delhi's BJP government and the Aam Aadmi Party, which alleged the monitoring data was unreliable ([details](/en/news/delhi-diwali-night-pm25-data-released-aap-fraud-bjp-punjab-stubble-plot/)). Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge asked on Tuesday why India was becoming a "Vishwaguru" in air pollution and demanded a "Hawa Ka Hisaab" from the Prime Minister ([story](/en/news/kharge-vishwaguru-air-pollution-hawa-ka-hisaab-modi/)).

## The other view

Traders have made a related case. The Chamber of Trade and Industry, chaired by Brijesh Goyal, wrote to political parties saying Diwali fireworks should not be blamed as the main cause of Delhi's pollution and urging joint action with the Centre on stubble burning in neighbouring states. It said Delhi's index was already between 350 and 400 on November 5, 6 and 7, before the festival.

## Why it matters

The National Capital Region towns on the Uttar Pradesh side were also in the poor band on Wednesday morning, with Noida at 278 and Greater Noida at 240, while Gurugram read 292. The Supreme Court's cracker order covered Delhi-NCR, which includes Noida and Greater Noida; the chief minister's decision not to impose prohibitions applies to the rest of the state.

## What happens next

Forecasters expect stronger winds to push readings down over the coming days, though the India Meteorological Department expects fog or mist each morning until November 16. The Supreme Court's temporary cracker relaxation for Delhi-NCR ended on November 8, and the full ban has been in force since November 9.
