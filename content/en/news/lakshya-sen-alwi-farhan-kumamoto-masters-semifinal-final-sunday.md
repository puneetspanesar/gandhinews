**Lakshya Sen is into the final of the Kumamoto Masters.** The eighth seed beat Indonesia's sixth seed Alwi Farhan **21-12, 15-21, 21-11** in the men's singles semi-final on Saturday, November 14, at the Kumamoto Prefectural Gymnasium in Japan. The match, on Court 1, began at about 6:50 am IST and lasted about 69 minutes, according to reports from the venue.

It is the first time Sen, ranked 13th in the world, has beaten the 10th-ranked Farhan. **He had lost both of their earlier meetings**, and the win sends him into a Super 500 final for the second time in a fortnight.

## How the semi-final went

Sen started with a 3-0 lead and pulled away at the interval to take the first game 21-12. Farhan answered in the second game, **won it 21-15** and levelled the match.

The decider was one-sided. Sen **won it 21-11**. With the defeat, Indonesia has no player left in the tournament.

## The record against Farhan

Farhan had won both earlier meetings. He won the semi-final at the Macau Open on **August 1, 2025, by 21-16, 21-9**, and beat Sen again in the round of 32 at the Indonesia Open on June 2, 2026, by 21-19, 21-16. The head-to-head now stands at 2-1 in Farhan's favour.

## The road to the semi-final

In the round of 16 on Thursday, November 12, he beat China's Dong Tian Yao 21-8, 21-14 in 35 minutes. On Friday, November 13, he **beat Japan's Yushi Tanaka 21-16, 21-15** in about 40 minutes. Against Tanaka he led 11-8 at the first interval and 11-9 at the second, then pulled clear. He had also beaten Chi Yu Jen of Chinese Taipei earlier in the week.

In the first game Tanaka fought back with cross-court smashes, but Sen held his nerve to close it out, and in the second, level at 9-9, he raised the attacking pressure and built a comfortable lead. He is the last Indian standing. Kidambi Srikanth, who had beaten Japan's Kodai Naraoka on Thursday, lost his quarter-final to Koki Watanabe 24-22, 21-15 after leading 12-5 in the second game, as we [reported on Friday](/en/news/srikanth-watanabe-kumamoto-masters-quarter-final-exit-after-naraoka-win). The Indian doubles pairs had already gone, Hariharan Amsakarunan and M R Arjun losing 21-14, 21-14 to Japan's Takuro Hoki and Yugo Kobayashi, and the World Championships bronze medallists Treesa Jolly and Gayatri Gopichand going down 21-14, 21-14 to third seeds Rin Iwanaga and Kie Nakanishi.

## Back-to-back titles on offer

The Hylo Open in Saarbrücken was also the tournament where **PV Sindhu won the women's title** hours before Sen's final, an Indian double at the same Super 500 event. Sen's win over Christie ended a wait for a World Tour title in 2026. Sen arrived in Kumamoto as the **Hylo Open champion**. He beat Jonatan Christie 21-17, 21-10 in Saarbrücken on November 1, his first BWF World Tour title since the Australian Open a year earlier, as we [reported at the time](/en/news/lakshya-sen-hylo-open-title-indian-double-saarbrucken). A win on Sunday would give him a second Super 500 title in two weeks.

## What it means for Hangzhou

The final also matters for the season-ending event. Before the semi-final **Sen was 12th in the BWF World Tour Finals race with 64,980 points**, and only the top eight qualify for the Finals in Hangzhou, China.

## What next

The final is on Sunday, November 15. Sen's opponent is the winner of the other semi-final, between Japan's Koki Watanabe, who beat Srikanth, and Thailand's Kunlavut Vitidsarn, the second seed. That match had not finished when this report was filed.
