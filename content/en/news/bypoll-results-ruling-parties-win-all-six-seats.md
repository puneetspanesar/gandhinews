All six seats that went to bypolls on October 6 were won by the party that governs the state concerned. The BJP took the Nagaon Lok Sabha seat in Assam and two assembly seats in West Bengal. Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK) won both Tamil Nadu seats, and the All India N.R. Congress (AINRC) held **Thattanchavady in Puducherry**. Votes were counted on October 9.

## Assam: BJP takes Nagaon from the Congress

Devasish Sharma of the BJP beat AIUDF chief Badruddin Ajmal by **1,42,224 votes**, according to Election Commission figures (7,14,349 votes to 5,72,125). The Congress's Sibamoni Bora was third. The seat fell vacant when Pradyut Bordoloi, who had held it for the Congress since 2019, resigned; he had joined the BJP and won the Dispur assembly seat in this year's state election. Turnout was 76.19%.

Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma wrote on X: "Under the leadership of Prime Minister Narendra Modi ji, BJP has secured a decisive mandate".

## West Bengal: two large BJP wins

In Nandigram, which Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari gave up after winning both Nandigram and Bhabanipur in 2026, the BJP's Hasirani Rath polled **1,64,427 votes** to Congress candidate Milan Pradhan's 41,674, a margin of 1,22,753. The Trinamool Congress candidate, Sanchita Pradhan Dey, withdrew her nomination at the last moment, and Trinamool chief Mamata Banerjee then backed the Congress candidate.

Rejinagar was vacated by Humayun Kabir, who won two seats in 2026 and kept Naoda. The BJP's Shankharavh Sarkar won with 1,29,914 votes, about **63.14%** of the total, against 54,098 for Golam Nabi Azad of the Aam Janata Unnayan Party (AJUP). The Congress candidate polled 6,168 votes and the Trinamool candidate 3,847. The Trinamool and its breakaway, the Democratic Trinamool Congress, fielded separate candidates.

> Nationalist forces are all set to win both the seats. Bengali Muslim voters have supported the BJP in both the seats.
>
> Source: Suvendu Adhikari, Chief Minister of West Bengal, October 9, 2026

The results alone do not establish how any community voted.

## Tamil Nadu: TVK keeps both seats it took from the AIADMK

Both Tamil Nadu seats, reserved for Scheduled Castes, fell vacant after their AIADMK MLAs resigned and joined the ruling TVK, which then fielded the same two people. In Madurantakam, K. Maragatham Kumaravel won 72,149 votes to DMK candidate I. Paranthamen's 52,068, a margin of 20,081. In Dharapuram, P. Sathyabama polled 66,841 against 57,370 for the DMK's S. Suganya, a margin of 9,471. The AIADMK finished third in both.

TVK won 108 of the 234 seats in this year's assembly election. Its strength had fallen to 107 after Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay vacated Tiruchirappalli East, and that the two wins take it to 109, still short of the 118 needed for a majority. The government is supported by the Congress, the VCK, the Left parties and the IUML, though the Left parties contested these bypolls separately and campaigned against TVK. AIADMK general secretary Edappadi K. Palaniswami accused TVK of trying to build a majority of its own by inducing opposition MLAs to join it.

## Puducherry: AINRC holds the Chief Minister's old seat

Thattanchavady was vacated by Chief Minister N. Rangasamy. AINRC's Ashok Anand won 13,358 votes against 4,876 for the Congress's E. Vinayagam, a margin of 8,482, according to Election Commission figures. The DMK's K. Sethu polled 2,664. Turnout was **80.81%**, down from 87.20% in April.

## What comes next

The next contests on the calendar are the assembly elections due in Manipur, Goa, Punjab, Uttarakhand and Uttar Pradesh, which reports expect in February and March 2027. Rajya Sabha seats from Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and West Bengal that were due to be filled on October 16 were all decided unopposed on October 9.
