India on Wednesday condemned a strike on a tower in Riyadh's financial district that **injured two Indian nationals**, and called for an end to attacks on civilian and energy infrastructure in Saudi Arabia.

In a statement issued on November 25, the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) said the Indian Embassy in Riyadh is giving "all possible assistance" to the two injured Indians and their families. Their names and the extent of their injuries have not been made public.

## What the ministry said

The ministry condemned "continued attacks on civilian, economic and energy infrastructure" in Saudi Arabia. "These attacks seriously endanger civilian lives," it said, adding that they violate the kingdom's sovereignty and weaken regional security.

It asked for an "immediate cessation of such attacks" and repeated India's **solidarity with the Saudi government and people**, and its support for the kingdom's sovereignty and territorial integrity. The ministry said it hoped peace and normalcy would return to the region soon. The statement did not name who carried out the attack.

## What happened in Riyadh

The strike came on Tuesday, November 24, in the King Abdullah Financial District. Saudi Civil Defense called it a "terrorist attack" on one of the district's towers and blamed the Houthi movement of Yemen. It said **11 people were injured**: nine foreign residents (four Egyptians, three Nepalese and two Indians) and two Saudi citizens.

Officials did not name the building. Witnesses said the target was the Public Investment Fund tower, the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund, and a photograph released by officials showed that tower's facade. The Saudi-led coalition's spokesman called the strike a "cowardly terrorist attack" and said an on-site investigation was under way. A witness said a vehicle with a loudspeaker warned pedestrians to take cover, and that, unlike in recent strikes, no alert reached phones warning of a threat to the capital.

On the same day the coalition said it shot down a Houthi cruise missile fired towards the Riyadh region and destroyed two drones aimed at Riyadh and the kingdom's southern regions. No claim of responsibility for the tower strike was reported in the accounts available when this story was written.

## The second hit in five days

The same tower was struck on **November 19**, when windows shattered across seven or eight floors of the 80-storey building, sections of its metal facade were damaged and neighbouring buildings were hit. No one was injured then. Saudi Civil Defense described that attack too as a Houthi terrorist attack, and said it showed the group's continued hostility to civilian facilities. Reports said it followed a strike on a Saudi Aramco building in the district on November 8.

## How India has responded before

The Indian community in Saudi Arabia has been caught in the attacks for weeks. On October 10, **Mohammed Yusuf Ali of Hyderabad was killed** when a missile struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, and 11 Indians were hurt. Our [report on his funeral](/en/news/hyderabad-man-killed-riyadh-airport-strike-envoy-attends-funeral) records that India's ambassador attended and that three injured Indians were in intensive care at the time.

On October 21, **one Indian was killed and three were injured** in strikes on Dammam airport and the Shaybah oil field. The ministry then called the attacks "completely unacceptable" ([our report](/en/news/india-condemns-dammam-shaybah-attacks-indian-killed)). Its first statement on the Riyadh airport strikes, on October 11, used the same words ([our report](/en/news/india-condemns-houthi-attacks-saudi-airports-riyadh)).

The embassy's advice since mid-October has been to shelter indoors during alerts, stay away from windows and open areas, and not film incidents ([embassy advisory](/en/news/indian-embassy-riyadh-advisory-shelter-indoors-drones)).

## Why it matters to Indians

The government told Parliament that 27,47,551 Indian nationals lived in Saudi Arabia as of February 24, 2025, one of the largest Indian communities abroad. Companies in the King Abdullah Financial District had already sent staff home during earlier attacks. Saudi Arabia is also among India's biggest suppliers of crude oil, which is why strikes on energy sites are watched in Delhi.

Families in India can reach the embassy's 24-hour Pravasi Bharatiya Sahayata Kendra helpline on 8002471234, the number the embassy has published since the October attacks.

## What happens next

The ministry has not announced any change to its advice for Indians in the kingdom. The coalition's investigation into the November 24 strike is continuing, and the embassy has not yet said when the two injured Indians are expected to be discharged.
