The Indian Navy is due to commission Samarthak, the first of four multi-purpose vessels built in India for the force, at Naval Base Karwar in Karnataka on Friday.

Larsen and Toubro designed and built the ship at its Kattupalli shipyard near Chennai, with more than 75% indigenous content, and handed it over to the Navy on 27 August. The company is building a second vessel of the class.

The Defence Ministry has called the ship an "important component of the Navy's future" and an ideal "utility" ship for strategically important operations.

## What the ship will do

Samarthak's main job is maritime surveillance and patrol, along with low-intensity maritime operations. It is also meant to deploy targets and serve as a trials platform for new weapons and systems, and to act as a mothership for unmanned and autonomous craft.

The ship can carry out humanitarian assistance and disaster relief, be converted into a limited hospital ship and support salvage operations. Its integrated combat suite combines Indian-made sensors, weapons and electronic systems built for difficult sea conditions.

Its crest shows an Indian elephant rising from the waves, meant to stand for strength, intelligence and agility. The motto, "Vijayarthama Eknishtatha", translates as "Victory is our sole aim".

## A busy month for induction

The commissioning comes four days after the Navy inducted INS Mangrol, the third of its Mahe-class anti-submarine shallow water craft, at Mumbai.
