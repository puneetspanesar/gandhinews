Prime Minister Narendra Modi opened the **Bharat Renewable Energy Summit and Expo** at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi on Monday, and used his speech to tally how far solar power has spread across government offices, farms and homes.

"More than 44,000 government buildings across India are now connected to solar energy," Modi said. The panels save **about ₹700 crore a year**, he said, and the government plans to bring every government building, school, college, hospital and street-lighting system under solar power.

## Farms and rooftops

Modi pointed to farms as the other big gain. More than **30 lakh farmers** now run solar-powered irrigation under the PM-KUSUM scheme. "Estimates suggest that each farmer can save around Rs 1 lakh annually by using solar pumps," he said.

On homes, he said more than **65 lakh households have joined** the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana in the 24 to 25 months since it was launched in February 2024. The scheme aims to put rooftop solar on one crore homes.

He also returned to India's climate pledge. "More than 50 per cent of India's installed power capacity comes from non-fossil fuel sources," Modi said, a target the country **met in 2025, five years ahead** of its Paris Agreement schedule.

The summit, organised by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, runs until November 5.
