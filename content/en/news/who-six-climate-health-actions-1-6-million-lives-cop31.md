The World Health Organization says **six climate and health measures could prevent 1.6 million deaths a year**, about one in every 40 deaths, if governments scaled them up worldwide. The figure comes from a report released from Geneva on November 4, 2026, ahead of the COP31 climate talks.

The report, *Global benefits and costs of prioritized actions on climate change and health*, is described by WHO as the first full analysis of the economic and fiscal returns from the measures. The numbers are model projections, not counted deaths.

"The climate crisis is a health crisis, but much of the harm it causes can be prevented with tools we already have," said Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, WHO Director-General.

## The six measures

The package covers heat early-warning systems linked to health response, drinking-water services that withstand climate impacts, better sanitation, hygiene facilities including handwashing, clean fuels and technologies for cooking and heating at home, and **efficient fossil fuel pricing**, meaning an end to inefficient subsidies and the use of appropriate taxes.

## What it would deliver

Fossil fuel pricing does most of the work: **1.2 million of the 1.6 million lives saved**. The measures together would avoid **16.7 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide** a year, about 31% of global greenhouse gas emissions excluding land use, and bring US$3.9 trillion a year in welfare benefits and US$5.4 trillion in fiscal benefits, all of the latter from fuel pricing.

On returns, WHO estimates that across five of the actions **each US$1 invested yields about US$24**. Heat-health warning systems return an estimated US$835 for every dollar. Benefits are expected to be larger in low- and middle-income countries.

## Limits of the model

WHO modelled 2024 to 2075 and compared scale-up with no scale-up. It lists its own caveats: the scale-up assumed is aspirational, some measures are modelled as if they do not reinforce one another, evidence is thin in places, and long-range projections are uncertain.

"Protecting health must be at the centre of climate action," Tedros said. WHO will run a Health Pavilion at COP31 from November 9 to 20, 2026.
