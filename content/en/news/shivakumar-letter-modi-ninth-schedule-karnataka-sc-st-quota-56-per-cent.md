Karnataka Chief Minister **D K Shivakumar has written to Prime Minister Narendra Modi** asking him to intervene personally and have the state's 2022 reservation law for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes placed in the Ninth Schedule of the Constitution. The letter was reported on Saturday, November 14, 2026.

Shivakumar asked the Prime Minister to treat the matter "as one of national constitutional importance". The law raised SC reservation from 15% to 17% and ST reservation from 3% to 7%, taking **total reservation in the state to 56%**, above the 50% ceiling that the courts have applied.

## What the letter asks for

The Chief Minister has made four requests. He wants the Prime Minister to **start the process for a Constitutional Amendment Bill**. He wants the Centre to consult the National Commissions for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. He asks the Ministries of Law and Justice, Social Justice and Empowerment, Tribal Affairs and Home Affairs to carry out a coordinated review within a fixed time. And he has offered the state's own data and full cooperation.

The Ninth Schedule is the list of laws that are shielded from most judicial review. Shivakumar pointed to Tamil Nadu, whose 69% reservation was placed there in 1994 through the 76th Amendment. Karnataka's request, he wrote, "deserves no less constitutional attention".

## The case Karnataka makes

The letter leans on the population data. According to the 2011 Census, Scheduled Castes were 17.15% and Scheduled Tribes 6.95% of Karnataka's people, set against the new quotas of 17% and 7%. The earlier percentages, 15% and 3%, had **not changed for nearly seven decades**, since 1958.

The increase followed the recommendation of a commission headed by retired High Court judge Justice H N Nagamohan Das, which also recommended Ninth Schedule protection. The state first sent its proposal to the Union government on March 23, 2023.

## How the demand reached this point

The request has been repeated several times and has so far not been taken up.

The Centre's reply in the Lok Sabha on July 25 acknowledged the request but said that adding the law to the Ninth Schedule was **not considered appropriate at that stage** because the High Court was still hearing the matter. That ground has now changed, since the High Court has decided the case.

## The High Court ruling

On October 12, the Karnataka High Court struck down the enhanced provisions. It held that the state had crossed the 50% ceiling without the exceptional justification the law requires, and it also examined whether the state had consulted the National Commissions for SCs and STs, as Articles 338(9) and 338A(9) require. Shivakumar's request for consultation with those commissions speaks to that second point.

The state is also fighting the verdict in court. As we reported on November 11, it has filed an appeal in the Supreme Court, and the government has said it will pursue both routes together: the legal appeal and the constitutional amendment.

## Why it matters

The quota decides how seats in colleges and posts in government service are shared. Karnataka has said it approved filling 56,432 vacancies in February, and the court's interim arrangement had required appointments under the higher quota to be marked as subject to the final decision. Until either the Supreme Court or Parliament acts, **the old 15% and 3% quotas stand** in practice.

An amendment placing a state law in the Ninth Schedule needs Parliament's approval by a special majority. The Union government has not said whether it will act on the letter, and no response from the Centre or from the opposition parties was available on Saturday evening.

The question also touches other states. Courts have lately struck down quotas above the 50% mark elsewhere, including Madhya Pradesh's 27% OBC quota.

## What happens next

The Supreme Court has to admit Karnataka's petition and decide whether to stay the High Court order; no hearing date has been reported. On the political side, the next step is the Prime Minister's reply, and whether the Centre is willing to bring a Constitutional Amendment Bill in a coming session of Parliament. The state's Legislative Assembly passed a resolution in favour of inclusion on December 18, 2025.
