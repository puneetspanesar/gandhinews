Indian shares slid on Monday afternoon even as the rupee posted its biggest one-day gain in six months, as rising bond yields and steady foreign selling outweighed relief from the Reserve Bank of India's weekend steps to defend the currency.

The Sensex was down 566.64 points, or 0.78%, at 71,905.69 in afternoon trade, and the Nifty 50 was 142.70 points lower at 22,377.75. The day began on a firm note: the Sensex opened nearly 300 points higher, and touched 72,884 before sellers took over, dragging it to a low of 71,845.

## The rupee bounces

The rupee opened at 95.61 against the dollar, from Friday's close of 96.71, and traded around 95.8 through the morning. It gained about 1%, its biggest jump since April, after nearing a record low of 97 last week.

The RBI announced on Saturday that it would meet the full daily dollar needs of Indian Oil, HPCL and BPCL through a special window, taking a large chunk of demand out of the market. It also tightened rules on rupee derivatives. Banks must now keep 20% of the rupee value of certain foreign exchange contracts above $2 million with the RBI in cash, and the limit for contracts without an underlying exposure has been cut from $100 million to $5 million.

Madhavi Arora, lead economist at Emkay Global Financial Services, called the measures "tactical fixes, not structural solutions", saying they should curb speculation and offer the rupee some near-term support.

Defending the currency has been costly. Foreign exchange reserves fell by $12.95 billion to $734.61 billion in the week to 2 October.

## Why stocks fell anyway

Bond markets told a different story. India's 10-year government bond yield rose 8 basis points to 7.38%, its fourth straight daily rise, after the RBI raised the repo rate to 5.50% last week and said banks must hold 99% of their required cash reserve every day from 16 October.

Banking and FMCG stocks led the losses. ITC, Dabur and Hindustan Unilever fell, with Hindustan Unilever touching a 52-week low, and State Bank of India, Tech Mahindra, Coal India and Bharti Airtel were among the heaviest drags. Bajaj Auto, Dr Reddy's and Mahindra & Mahindra gained, and realty stocks rose. The India VIX, a gauge of expected volatility, rose as much as 8.1%. Foreign investors sold shares worth a net ₹3,568.90 crore on Friday, while domestic institutions bought ₹4,743.26 crore.

Anand Shah, chief investment officer for PMS and AIF at ICICI Prudential, warned in a monthly note that "companies with excessive leverage may face heightened balance sheet risks as funding costs rise."

## What comes next

September's retail inflation figure is due at 4 pm on Monday. Ashish Rajodiya, head of commodities at PL Capital, expects it around 5.4% and said "softer data and cooler crude would give the rupee room to test the 94.90 support." US inflation data follows on Wednesday.

HCLTech is also due to report its July to September results on Monday. Brent crude, which eased slightly, remained above $100 a barrel.
