People who abandon or abuse senior citizens in Keralam could go to jail for up to three years and pay a fine of ₹50,000 under a bill Chief Minister V D Satheesan has placed before the state Assembly.

Satheesan wants the Keralam Senior Citizens' (Care and Protection of Rights) Bill, 2026 passed quickly. "I hope we can pass the legislation this month itself," he said last week, before the Assembly session opened on 12 October. "We are preparing legislation to provide a legal guarantee to senior citizens."

## Doctors must report abuse

The bill puts a duty on doctors and registered care centres to report suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of an elderly person to a district-level protection unit. Where a medical examination shows that food, water or medicine has been withheld, or finds injuries that do not match the explanation given, the report must be made within 48 hours.

It also guards residents' money. Any voluntary gift of money or property by a resident of a care centre will have to go through a formal inquiry and be finalised in the presence of the District Magistrate.

Living wills, in which a person sets out in advance what treatment they want at the end of life, get legal recognition. The bill promises hospice care and specialised facilities for people with Alzheimer's, dementia and Parkinson's disease.

## Caregivers, clinics and cybercrime

The state plans six-month and one-year certificate courses for caregivers in public and private institutions, starting next academic year. Regional geriatric centres, dedicated outpatient departments and mobile medical units, including units for tribal settlements, are also planned. New residential buildings will have to include features that make them easier for older people to use.

The bill also sets up fast-track mechanisms against cybercrime aimed at senior citizens and names AI-generated fraud specifically. Widows, survivors of violence and destitute women will get priority for places in government and local-body care centres.

## Why Keralam is moving first

Satheesan has said Keralam "had to prepare early for the challenges of an ageing population". Speaking earlier this month of elderly couples left living alone, he said: "A brain drain is happening." "We want to protect them."

His government has also talked of daily checks by local volunteers on whether old people have eaten and taken their medicines, and of a "silver economy" of care jobs. The cabinet cleared the bill before the session began. It now needs the Assembly's approval.
