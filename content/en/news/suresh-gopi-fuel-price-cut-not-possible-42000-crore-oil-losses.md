Union minister **Suresh Gopi said on Monday that petrol and diesel prices cannot be cut immediately**, pointing to ₹42,000 crore in losses that oil companies have run up during the West Asia conflict and to what he called "chaos" in the supply of petroleum products.

Speaking to reporters at Chennai's Meenambakkam airport, Gopi was asked whether pump prices would come down as crude oil eases. "We may wish for prices to come down the next day. But technically it is not possible right away," he said. He added that the oil companies' finances have to be considered first: "Oil companies have already incurred losses of Rs 42,000 crore. All this needs to be compensated."

## What he said on supply and taxes

Gopi did not hide the strain on the system. "There is chaos in the supply of petroleum products. We are doing whatever needs to be done to fix it," he said. The reports do not say where the disruption is worst or how long it would last.

He also **put part of the answer with the states**. Individual state governments, he said, can reduce the taxes they levy on petrol. He acknowledged that prices have risen in Kerala and Karnataka, and said he did not know how they had moved in Tamil Nadu. Reports of the remarks give no figures for current pump prices and no ministry statement accompanied them.

## How we got here

The oil shock has been building since the war began. Brent crude was about **$72 a barrel in late February** and touched $120 at the peak, according to the Prime Minister. Speaking at the Bharat Agriculture Conference on Thursday, November 12, Narendra Modi said that when crude hit $120 the pressure was for a **₹50 a litre rise in diesel**, that the Centre raised the price by ₹7 and absorbed the other ₹43, at a monthly burden the PMO release puts at about ₹14,000 crore. Our report on that speech is [here](/en/news/modi-bharat-agriculture-conference-natural-farming-mother-earth-fertiliser).

The pressure shows in company books. Bharat Petroleum's profit fell 21.5% in the September quarter, and the state-run refiner said its LPG under-recovery had reached **₹17,334 crore** by September 30. It had lost ₹1,873 crore in April to June, when prices were raised four times in May, as we reported in [the BPCL results story](/en/news/bpcl-q2-profit-falls-21-per-cent-marketing-margins-lpg). The petroleum ministry has approved ₹7,594 crore in compensation for the LPG gap, to be paid in 12 monthly instalments. Gopi's ₹42,000 crore figure is his own and is not broken down by company or product in the reports.

Crude has since eased. Brent settled at **$95.89 a barrel on Friday, November 13**, after trading near $97.5 on November 9, though Iran's claims of tankers hit by mines in the Strait of Hormuz keep the route risky, as we reported [here](/en/news/hormuz-three-tankers-mines-predator-qeshm-brent-india).

## The ethanol dispute

The supply remarks come in the middle of a separate argument over what is in the petrol being sold. Karnataka said on November 6 that samples from seven districts had **up to 31.2% ethanol** against a 20% limit; Indian Oil, HPCL and BPCL rejected the finding, citing 893 E20 samples across 27 states and Union Territories that fell between 19% and 21%. On November 8, Karnataka Industries Minister MB Patil said the Centre's own lapses had caused the crisis in the ethanol sector and asked for an inquiry into alleged irregularities in blending. Petroleum Minister Hardeep Singh Puri has earlier called concerns about E20 a "contrived narrative". Our account is in the [ethanol story](/en/news/karnataka-31-2-per-cent-ethanol-claim-oil-companies-reject-e20-bis).

## Why it matters

Fuel is the one input that touches every price. Wholesale fuel and power prices were **25.85% higher in September** than a year earlier, and retail inflation reached 6.09% in October, above the RBI's 6% upper limit. Diesel moves trucks, tractors and pumps, so a pump price that stays high keeps freight and food costs high, while a cut would ease them. The Centre has so far held back most of the diesel rise, which is the cost Gopi says oil companies are still carrying. A fall in state taxes is the quicker route for motorists, but it would cost state budgets revenue.

## What happens next

October's wholesale price figures are due from the commerce ministry around noon on Monday and will show how much fuel and power added to the index; our [preview](/en/news/wpi-inflation-october-data-due-monday-rbi-december) lists what to watch. The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee meets from December 2 to 4 with inflation above its limit. Whether prices at the pump come down will depend on where Brent goes after Hormuz, and on whether the Centre or the oil companies are compensated first for the losses Gopi described.
