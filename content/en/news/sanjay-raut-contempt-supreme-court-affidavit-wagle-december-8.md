The Supreme Court on Tuesday told Shiv Sena (UBT) MP **Sanjay Raut that an apology "must come from the heart and not the mind"**, and directed him to file an additional affidavit explaining social media posts he made after he had first apologised for his remarks about Chief Justice of India Surya Kant and the judiciary.

A bench of Justices Aravind Kumar and Vipul M Pancholi heard Raut and the journalist Nikhil Wagle in the criminal contempt petition filed by the Supreme Court Bar Association (SCBA). Raut appeared in the court in response to the show-cause notice issued on October 16. Wagle appeared virtually. According to reports, the matter comes up next on **December 8**.

## What was said in court

SCBA president Pradeep Rai told the bench that Raut had apologised for his comments but had then gone on posting against the Supreme Court. One post, he said, showed **the Supreme Court of Albania on fire**, which in his submission carried an indirect message about the Indian court. Another alleged that the BJP had brought democracy, the judiciary, central investigating agencies and the Election Commission under its control. Rai said the later posts were not mentioned in Raut's affidavit, called him a habitual offender and said his conduct called for strict action.

Justice Kumar told Raut: "You have expressed apology. But in a matter of this magnitude, apology must come from the heart and not the mind." He added that a politician of Raut's experience "should know what restraint in language means", and noted that his years in politics, Parliament and journalism should have made him careful when commenting on the judiciary.

Raut's counsel did not defend the remarks. He asked the bench to bear in mind that **Raut is undergoing treatment for cancer and heart-related ailments**. The court ordered an additional affidavit on the later posts and said it would examine his response before deciding how to proceed.


## Wagle seeks discharge

Wagle, who is accused of posting an abusive remark about the CJI on X, had already deleted it, his senior counsel Indira Jaising told the court. She said he had **offered an unconditional apology** to the CJI and the Supreme Court and asked that he be discharged from the proceedings.

Justice Kumar did not end the case against him on the spot. He observed that journalists, as members of the fourth estate, also carry a responsibility when they exercise free expression. According to reports, Wagle too has been asked to file an additional affidavit.

## How the case began

The contempt case grew out of a decision by the CJI to **decline a personal meeting** requested by a delegation of opposition parties, who wanted to raise the functioning of the Election Commission. The CJI suggested that they bring the matter to the court by petition. Our earlier reports on the opposition's petitions are [the INDIA bloc's questions to the Election Commission](/en/news/india-bloc-48-questions-election-commission-supreme-court-sir) and [the Court's notice over the Congress march](/en/news/supreme-court-notice-congress-rahul-march-browbeat-judiciary).

Wagle and Raut criticised the CJI's refusal in public. The SCBA filed its criminal contempt petition on October 15, and Attorney General R Venkataramani gave his consent the same day under **Section 15(1)(b) of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971**. Such consent is required before a body such as a bar association can bring a contempt petition. On October 16 the Supreme Court issued show-cause notices to both men in the case titled Supreme Court Bar Association v Nikhil Wagle and another.

Neither the notice reports nor the hearing reports quote Raut's original remarks in full. What the court has before it, for now, are the posts the SCBA has put on record and the apologies that followed.

## Why it matters

Criminal contempt is the Supreme Court's power to punish speech that, in its view, scandalises or lowers the authority of the court. A case against a sitting Rajya Sabha MP and a senior journalist tests where the line falls between sharp political criticism of a judge's decision and contempt. Raut is a serving Rajya Sabha member and Wagle a working journalist, so the way the bench treats their apologies will be read well beyond this case.

The bench's remark on the quality of an apology also shows what the court is weighing. An apology offered on paper, followed by fresh posts, may not be accepted as sincere, which is why the later posts are now the centre of the case. The punishment for criminal contempt under the 1971 Act can include imprisonment, though the court has not suggested what, if anything, it will impose and has so far only sought explanations.

## What happens next

Raut must file his additional affidavit on the later posts before the next hearing, which according to reports falls on **December 8**. The court will then decide whether his explanation is enough, and what to do about Wagle's plea for discharge. No statement from Raut or the Shiv Sena (UBT) on the hearing had been reported when this story was written on Wednesday morning.
