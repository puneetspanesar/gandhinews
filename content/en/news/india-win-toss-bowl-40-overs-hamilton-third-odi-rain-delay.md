India captain Shubman Gill **won the toss and chose to bowl first** in the third one-day international against New Zealand at Seddon Park, Hamilton, on Tuesday, November 10, after rain pushed the toss back by well over an hour. The match has been cut to **40 overs a side**.

The toss came a little after 9 am IST, against a scheduled start of 7:30 am IST. A wet outfield and then a returning drizzle kept the covers on through the morning, and a thunderstorm alert was in force for Hamilton at one stage. The five-match series is poised: India lead 2-0 after two rain-decided wins, and **one more win would settle the series** with two matches still to play.

## What the captains said

Gill, who had also won the toss in each of the first two games, said India would try to repeat their performance but needed to be better with the bat. "We will bowl first. We will try to repeat our performance, but we need to be better with the bat," he said, adding: "Hopefully the weather clears and we will repeat the good things we have done."

His counterpart Mitchell Santner, who has lost most of the tosses on this tour, pointed to the surface. "The pitch has been under covers for a while so it will do a bit," he said, and he reckoned that **180 might be a good score** in the conditions.

The pitch is the main question. Ground staff had only about half a day to prepare it before the rain, and the covers have been on for long spells since. India's batting coach Sitanshu Kotak was reported earlier in the tour to have spoken of wickets with a lot of grass, and both earlier games were low-scoring: New Zealand were bowled out for 134 in Wellington.

## The shortened match

The revised playing conditions allow each bowler a **maximum of 8 overs**. The first powerplay covers overs 1 to 8, the second overs 9 to 32 and the third overs 33 to 40. Gill chose to field under overcast skies, with a pitch that had spent long spells under covers.

## The teams

India have kept the same eleven that played in Wellington: Rohit Sharma, Gill, Virat Kohli, Shreyas Iyer, Axar Patel, KL Rahul (wicketkeeper), Naman Dhir, Anshul Kamboj, Auqib Nabi, Gurnoor Brar and Arshdeep Singh. Brar is the man in form, having taken a career-best **4 for 30** in the second ODI.

New Zealand have made one change. Matthew Fisher comes in for Blair Tickner, who has been struggling with the effects of a blow to the back of the head. The home side are Will Young, Tim Seifert, Henry Nicholls, Daryl Mitchell, Tom Latham (wicketkeeper), Glenn Phillips, Santner, Nathan Smith, Kyle Jamieson, Jacob Duffy and Fisher. Matt Henry remains unavailable through injury.

## How the series got here

India won the first ODI at Eden Park by five wickets on DLS, in [our report of that match](/en/news/india-beat-new-zealand-five-wickets-dls-eden-park-first-odi), and the second by four runs on DLS in Wellington, as [we reported](/en/news/india-beat-new-zealand-dls-four-runs-brar-wellington-second-odi). Both results came from rain rules rather than full contests, and this match has already been shortened too.

New Zealand's record at home is the other side of the story. They have not lost a bilateral ODI series at home since India's 4-1 win in 2019, and they had not lost an ODI at Seddon Park since February 2017. India have won only one of their eight ODIs against New Zealand at this ground.

## What happens next

If play goes ahead without further interruptions, New Zealand will bat first, with a target to set for India's chase. Further showers were forecast through the afternoon and evening in Hamilton, so the DLS method may again have a say in the result. Our [pre-match report](/en/news/hamilton-third-odi-india-nz-rain-seam-pitch-gill-last-match) has the forecast and the team news. This is expected to be **Gill's last ODI of the tour**, as he is due to leave the squad to prepare for the Test series. The fourth ODI follows this match in the series.
