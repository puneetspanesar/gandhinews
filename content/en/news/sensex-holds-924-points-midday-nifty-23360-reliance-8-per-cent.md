Indian shares **held most of their big morning gains** into midday on Monday, with Reliance Industries still doing the heavy lifting and the Nifty 50 pushing to a new high for the day.

At 11:22 am the Sensex stood at 75,383.98, **up about 924 points, or 1.24%**, and the Nifty 50 at 23,319.55, up about 266 points, or 1.16%. The Sensex had been as much as 1,030 points higher in the first hour, so the rally has cooled only slightly, while the Nifty climbed to **a fresh intraday high of 23,360.50**, about 30 points above its mid-morning peak.

## Reliance leads, coal and steel follow

Reliance Industries traded **nearly 8% higher**, near ₹1,254, as investors kept buying after its September-quarter profit rose about 41% from a year earlier. It remained the single biggest contributor to the index gain.

Behind it, the morning's leaders were a mix of commodity and financial names. **Shriram Finance rose about 5%**, Coal India and Max Healthcare about 4.6% each and Tata Steel about 4.4%. On the other side, Adani Ports, Infosys, ICICI Bank and Sun Pharma were the main Nifty laggards, each down between about 0.7% and 1.3%.

## A nervous undertone

Not every signal was as cheerful. The India VIX, which measures expected swings in the Nifty, **rose about 2.4% to 15.66** by 10:05 am, an unusual move on a day when the index itself was up more than 1%. A rising VIX alongside a rally usually means traders are paying more for protection against a sharp move either way.

The currency market was also cautious. The rupee **slipped 21 paise to 94.97 per dollar** in early trade, from 94.76 on Friday, as the dollar firmed, even though Brent crude fell about 3% to just under $110 a barrel.

"USDINR is likely to trade within 94.40-95.60 after ending October near 94.95," said Anindya Banerjee, head of commodity and currency research at Kotak Neo. He said softer crude would help the rupee, while a fresh rise in oil would push the dollar towards the top of that range.

## What to watch this afternoon

Cheaper oil and the return of foreign buyers, who **bought about ₹3,600 crore of shares on Friday**, remain the main props for the market. Traders will watch whether the Nifty can hold above 23,300 into the close and how oil moves as talks to end the US-Iran conflict continue.
