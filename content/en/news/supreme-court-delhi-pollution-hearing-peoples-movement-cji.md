Chief Justice of India Surya Kant said on Friday that court orders alone will not clean Delhi's air, as the Supreme Court took up its own case on pollution in the capital. "The more orders the Court passes, the more violations there may be," he said, adding that the effort "should become a **people's movement**."

The bench of Chief Justice Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana was hearing the suo motu case on air quality governance in the National Capital Region. The Chief Justice opened with a dig at the conditions outside: "I hope you all are enjoying the weather in Delhi."

Delhi's overall air quality index stood at **389, in the very poor band**, on Friday, according to Central Pollution Control Board data, with IIT Delhi and Rohini touching 474, in the severe band.

## Customs and festivals

The Chief Justice questioned how festivals had changed. "Why don't we revert to our original customs?" he asked. Justice Bagchi linked the problem to the commercialisation of festivals. "Poor people do not generate any pollution. They are the sufferers," the Chief Justice said.

Amicus curiae Aparajita Singh agreed that enforcement has limits, saying the State "cannot have one police officer in every house."

Additional Solicitor General Aishwarya Bhati placed status reports and charts before the court. The bench said it would seek compliance updates from the authorities and **list the case after the Diwali vacation**.

A separate bench has allowed green firecrackers in Delhi-NCR on November 7 and 8, from 6 am to 7 am and 8 pm to 10 pm.
