Prime Minister Narendra Modi said on Diwali morning that he is **heading to Bastar** in Chhattisgarh to celebrate the festival with the country's security forces, continuing a tradition he began in 2014.

"I am going to Bastar on the sacred occasion of Diwali," he wrote on X in Hindi. "Where Naxalites spread terror until yesterday, **development is in bloom today**." He said he was going to pay tribute to the brave jawans, their sacrifice, austerity and dedication.

The announcement came a few hours after he wished the nation a happy Diwali. The exact venue within Bastar, the units he will meet and the time of the programme had not been announced when this report was filed.

## A tradition since 2014

Modi has spent Diwali with troops every year since he became Prime Minister. He began at the **Siachen Base Camp in 2014** and has since been to the Punjab border near Amritsar, Sumdoh in Himachal Pradesh, Gurez, Harsil, Rajouri, Longewala, Nowshera, Kargil and Lepcha. In 2024 he was at Sir Creek in Kutch, and last year he spent the festival aboard **INS Vikrant** off the Goa-Karwar coast.

In a video message on Saturday he said spending Diwali among the country's jawans had become his regular routine every year, and described the festival as "a reflection of our resolve against darkness".

## Why Bastar

This year's choice carries a message of its own. Bastar was for decades the heart of the Maoist insurgency, and the Centre has made ending Naxalism a stated priority under Home Minister Amit Shah. The CRPF has reported **12 consecutive months without an operational fatality**, the last on October 30, 2025. Modi called the record "a ZERO that speaks volumes".

CRPF Director General GP Singh credited new equipment, better training, planning and faster evacuation of the injured, and said the force remained committed to keeping the country peaceful and safe.

Further details of the visit will follow as the programme begins.
