**Life Insurance Corporation of India** reported a **20% rise in standalone net profit** for the July-September quarter, to ₹12,076 crore from ₹10,053 crore a year earlier, as it sold more group business and earned better margins on new policies.

The state-run insurer's board approved the results on November 5, 2026. Net premium income for the quarter grew 16.2% to about ₹1.47 lakh crore, and total income rose to ₹2,62,739 crore from ₹2,39,614 crore.

## Better margins, weaker stock

The more closely watched number for insurers is the value of new business, the profit expected from policies sold in the period. For the first half of the year, LIC's **value of new business rose 33.95% to ₹6,846 crore**, and its net margin widened to 22.3% from 17.6%.

Its solvency ratio improved to 2.35 from 2.13, and assets under management stood at about ₹59.61 lakh crore. One weak spot was persistency: the share of premiums still being paid in the 61st month **slipped to 61.03%** from 63.81%.

Investors were not convinced. On Friday the shares were **down 4.01% at ₹391.65** on the National Stock Exchange at 12:13 pm. The brokerage Jefferies was reported to have cut its price target to ₹510 from ₹530, saying margins on new business may come under pressure.

LIC's chief executive and managing director, R Doraiswamy, sounded upbeat on the insurance regulator's planned changes to commission rules.

> "We at LIC are very optimistic about the positive impact of the Proposed commission Regulations changes."
> Source: R Doraiswamy, CEO and MD, LIC, November 5, 2026

He said LIC plans to keep non-par products at about **one third of individual sales**, while it works on cutting costs across the business.
