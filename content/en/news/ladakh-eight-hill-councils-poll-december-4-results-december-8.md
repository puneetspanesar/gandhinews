The Ladakh administration has set **December 4 as polling day** for the first elections to the Union Territory's eight Autonomous Hill Development Councils, with results expected by December 8. Chief Secretary Ashish Kundra announced the schedule, and a notification issued on November 10 started the process.

The notification was issued by Lieutenant Governor Vinai Kumar Saxena, acting as Administrator, under the Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils Act, 1997, on the recommendation of the State Election Commission. It asks the constituencies of each of the eight councils, one for every district, to elect their representatives. The full election order, with nomination, scrutiny and withdrawal dates, is issued as a separate document that was not available to us at the time of writing.

## What the notification covers

An administration notification dated October 21 (S.O. 290) fixed the number of seats for each council. Together the councils have **100 elected constituencies**, against 52 elected seats under the earlier arrangement of two councils, Leh and Kargil. Leh has 22, Kargil 21, Sankoo 13, Changthang 10, and Nubra, Sham and Zanskar 9 each, while Drass has 7. The seats were built from panchayat halqas and municipal wards, adjusted for population and area.

The new law behind the numbers is the Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils (Amendment) Regulation, 2026, which **took effect on October 19**. It replaced the fixed 26 seats per council with a range of 7 to 24 elected seats, allows up to four nominated members from women and unrepresented communities, and gives councils powers equivalent to those of district-level panchayats. The State Election Commission supervises the polls. Our earlier report on the regulation is [here](/en/news/ladakh-hill-council-regulation-amended-7-24-seats).

## How Ladakh got here

Ladakh had only two districts until this autumn. Three more, Drass, Zanskar and Sankoo, were carved out of Kargil, and Sankoo was notified on October 16. The Leh council's five-year term **ended on October 30, 2025** without an election, and the administration later cited new districts and women's reservation for the delay. Powers of the Kargil council, the last elected body in the Union Territory, passed to the district's Deputy Commissioner after its councillors resigned on October 20 and the Lieutenant Governor dissolved it on October 21, when the Model Code of Conduct came into force.

At that point officials said council elections could not be held before April or May because of winter, and that panchayat polls, the first since 2018, would come first. Those polls were scheduled for November 21, and the administration says **55 sarpanches**, 1,186 panches and four councillors have already been elected unopposed in the municipal and panchayat contests. The new councils will have three months to prepare their budgets.

## Reaction and the wider dispute

The date has met opposition in Kargil, where **a bandh was reported** over holding council elections in winter. Separately, talks between Ladakh's representative groups, the Leh Apex Body and the Kargil Democratic Alliance, and the Ministry of Home Affairs on constitutional safeguards remain stalled; the Chief Secretary has said the round originally planned for October 22 will be held later. Activist Sonam Wangchuk has said the talks were [called off](/en/news/wangchuk-ladakh-talks-called-off-centre-promises) and accused the Centre of walking back promises. Whether the groups will contest the council polls, or press for the Sixth Schedule protections they have sought, has not been announced.

## Why it matters

For the first time since the Union Territory was formed in 2019, **every district of Ladakh will have its own elected council**, and Leh will have an elected body again after more than a year of administrator rule. The councils get powers equivalent to district-level panchayats, so the vote decides who runs local development decisions across 100 constituencies at once.

## What happens next

Polling is on **December 4** and results are due by December 8. The nomination, scrutiny and withdrawal dates will be in the State Election Commission's schedule. The Kargil bandh and the stalled talks with the Home Ministry are the two things to watch before polling day.
