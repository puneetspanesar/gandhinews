Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath marked Diwali on Sunday with the Nishad community in Ayodhya, after offering prayers to Ramlala and posting a festival message on X.

In the Nishad settlement he spoke to residents and children, took selfies with them and **handed out sweets and Diwali gifts**. The community is traditionally linked to Kevat, the boatman in the Ramayana who ferried Rama, Sita and Lakshmana across the Ganga.

## "Bullets will no longer be fired"

Addressing the gathering, Adityanath urged a firm resolve to protect the city. "No malevolent gaze, no 'Manthra' or 'Maricha', ever falls upon our Ayodhya," he said. He added: "From now on, **bullets will no longer be fired at the devotees of Lord Ram** in Ayodhya." He went on to say: "No one will be able to wield sticks or batons against those who chant 'Jai Shri Ram' in Ayodhya."

His X post called Diwali "a grand celebration of the eternal consciousness of Sanatan culture."

## After Deepotsav

The visit came a day after the 10th Deepotsav on the Saryu, where more than 30 lakh diyas were lit across 56 ghats and Adityanath performed the Maha Aarti with Vice-President C P Radhakrishnan. On Saturday he also took aim at political rivals from the stage.

Delhi Minister Ashish Sood, in a separate remark, described Kevat's story as an example of devotion and surrender in Indian culture, in a nod to the Nishad link.
