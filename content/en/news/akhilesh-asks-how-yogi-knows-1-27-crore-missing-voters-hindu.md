Samajwadi Party chief Akhilesh Yadav on Friday asked how Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath could know that the voters missing from Uttar Pradesh's rolls are Hindus, and accused the BJP and the Election Commission of working together.

Speaking at a party programme in Lucknow, Akhilesh said: "**How do they know that the names of Hindu voters are missing?**" Adityanath had claimed last week that the names of 1.27 crore Hindu voters had been deleted from the voter list.

Akhilesh went further. "It appears that the Election Commission has shared the entire data with the chief minister or the chief minister's office," he said, alleging that the data reaches the public only after it has gone to the chief minister first.

## The BJP's plan, as Akhilesh tells it

The SP chief said that during Union Home Minister Amit Shah's visit to Lucknow for the BJP's state working committee meeting, party workers were urged to help **add 2 crore names** to the rolls. He argued that the BJP had first used the "infiltrators" issue to back the special intensive revision, and had now switched to a Hindu-voter argument.

He did the arithmetic for his audience. Losing 1.27 crore voters, he said, would mean about **31,000 votes per assembly constituency**, and the BJP "has effectively lost the election even before the voting has taken place."

## Form 7 and the Sultanpur case

The Samajwadi Party also alleges that **Form 7, the objection form used to seek deletion of a name**, was misused with forged signatures. Akhilesh cited Nand Lal Nishad, a Sultanpur resident, whose name was allegedly used on applications seeking the deletion of about 26 voters, and who was reportedly given ₹1 lakh. He said SP workers caught the practice and the fake filings stopped, and demanded an Election Commission inquiry and action against those responsible. According to the party, the Commission has taken cognisance and an FIR has been registered. That account has not been independently confirmed.

Akhilesh also suggested that electoral rolls be linked to identity cards, pointing to the biometric and retinal data already collected from citizens, and warned that names missing after the revision could feed fears about citizenship.

## What comes next

The Election Commission's revision of Uttar Pradesh's rolls for 2027 begins on **November 12**, with the final roll due on January 4. The reports available carry no response from the Chief Minister's office or the BJP to Akhilesh's remarks. With the 2027 assembly elections ahead, the rolls look set to be a main line of attack between the two parties.
