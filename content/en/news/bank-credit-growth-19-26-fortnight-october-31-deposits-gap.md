Bank lending in India **grew 19.26% from a year earlier** in the fortnight ended October 31, up from 17.94% in the fortnight before, the Reserve Bank of India's data released on Friday, November 13, showed. Credit outstanding stood at ₹237.03 lakh crore, against ₹198.76 lakh crore a year ago. The figures capture the run-up to Diwali, when families borrow for homes, vehicles and consumer goods.

Deposits did not keep pace. They grew **16.76% year on year**, a shade above the 16.6% of the fortnight ended October 15. Credit therefore grew faster than deposits by 2.5 percentage points, compared with 1.34 points two weeks earlier, so the gap between what banks lend and what they take in almost doubled in a fortnight.

## A second reading of the month-end

A separate set of RBI figures, reported the same day for the month-end, put credit growth at **19.54%**, the **fourth straight month-end above 19%**. In that series outstanding credit was ₹231.82 lakh crore, up ₹37.89 lakh crore over the year, while deposits were ₹282.51 lakh crore, up ₹40.75 lakh crore, a growth of 16.86%, slower than the 17.83% of September. The two sets differ in level, and we use the fortnight series for the headline. Both point the same way: lending is growing about 2.5 to 2.7 points faster than deposits.

## What is driving the borrowing

Reports on the data attribute the pick-up to **pre-festive demand**, especially ahead of Diwali, for loans to buy consumer durables, homes and vehicles. CareEdge Ratings said part of the growth also came from corporate borrowers turning to banks because high bond yields make raising money in the market expensive.

CareEdge expects credit growth to slow in the second half of FY27 as borrowing costs rise and last year's base becomes less favourable. It forecasts growth of about 15% to 16% for the full year.

## How we got here

The Monetary Policy Committee [raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%](/news/rbi-raises-repo-rate-to-5-50) on October 7, its first increase since February 2023. Since then the RBI has tightened the daily cash reserve ratio floor, sold government bonds and run reverse repo auctions to pull out surplus cash. On November 9 it [lent banks ₹50,003 crore for four days](/news/rbi-4-day-repo-50000-crore-liquidity-flip-november-9) at 5.51%, days after absorbing money, a sign of how finely it is managing short-term funds.

On November 12 it met bank chiefs on the **expected credit loss rules due on April 1, 2027**, under which lenders must provision for losses earlier than they do now. State Bank of India has put its one-time hit at ₹19,500 crore, as we [reported](/news/rbi-ecl-meeting-bank-ceos-november-12-sbi-19500-crore).

The same day, new inflation data showed [retail inflation at 6.09% in October](/news/retail-inflation-october-2026-6-09-two-year-high), above the RBI's 6% ceiling for the first time in two years. Economists quoted in reports expect another rate hike of 50 to 75 basis points.

## Why it matters

When credit grows faster than deposits, banks have to fund the difference by raising deposit rates, selling investments or borrowing in the market. That tends to push up the cost of both deposits and loans. For a borrower with a home or vehicle loan linked to the repo rate, the 25 basis point rise has already been passed on by several state-run banks. A savings account holder may see better deposit rates as lenders compete for money.

For the RBI the numbers cut two ways. Strong lending shows demand is healthy, but credit growing near 19% while inflation is above 6% is the kind of mix that supports further tightening.

## What happens next

The RBI publishes the next fortnight's figures, for the period ending November 14, in its regular release in the coming weeks. The Monetary Policy Committee meets from **December 2 to 4**, when it will weigh these credit numbers against the October inflation figure.
