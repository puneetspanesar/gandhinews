Uttar Pradesh begins the **Special Summary Revision 2027** of its electoral rolls on **November 12**, and the state's top election official has told districts to hunt for the voters the lists have missed.

Chief Electoral Officer Navdeep Rinwa has directed officials to make special efforts to **find missing women and young voters**. He noted that the share of women in the current Assembly rolls is "unusually low". The qualifying date is January 1, 2027, and the final roll is due on January 4.

## The gap behind the drive

The push follows a **difference of nearly 2.4 crore** between the panchayat electoral rolls and the rural voter count found while Assembly rolls were being prepared. The figure is a reported gap between two lists, not a confirmed count of missing voters. The two lists have different owners: the State Election Commission prepares panchayat rolls, while the Election Commission of India manages Assembly and Lok Sabha rolls.

The Chief Electoral Officer's office has told district election officers to **cross-check Assembly lists** against panchayat and urban local body rolls so that no eligible citizen is left out.

## Door-to-door help

ASHA workers, auxiliary nurse midwives, Anganwadi workers and women's self-help group members will visit homes to identify eligible women and help them register.

The revision comes as the Samajwadi Party and the BJP argue over what the state's rolls have lost. Akhilesh Yadav has questioned Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath's claim that 1.27 crore Hindu voters have gone missing, a dispute that will follow the drive through the winter.
