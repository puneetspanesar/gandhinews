The first Test between Pakistan and Sri Lanka at Rawalpindi goes into its final day on Friday, November 13, with **Pakistan 45 runs ahead and nine wickets in hand**. Play is due to start at 9:30 am local time, 10 am IST, after rain and bad light cut short the fourth day with Pakistan on 57 for 1 in their second innings.

Most reports expect a draw. Even so, the arithmetic of the World Test Championship gives Pakistan a reason to try for more: **a win is worth 12 points and a draw 4**, and Pakistan were ninth in the table at the end of October.

## Where the match stands

Sri Lanka, resuming on 404 for 4, were bowled out for 446 on Thursday, a first-innings lead of 12 over Pakistan's 434 for 9 declared. Debutant **Saqib Khan finished with 7 for 130** from 34 overs, taking five of the six wickets that fell for 42 runs. Dhananjaya de Silva made 167 and Kusal Mendis 119, and their stand of 249 for the fifth wicket is what took Sri Lanka past Pakistan's total. Our [day four report](/en/news/pakistan-57-for-1-lead-45-rain-bad-light-end-day-four-rawalpindi) has the full innings.

In the second innings, opener Tayyab Arif was lbw to Asitha Fernando for 11. **Azan Awais (22) and Mohammad Taha (24)** are unbeaten after adding 42 for the second wicket in 21.2 overs. Babar Azam, Saud Shakeel and captain Salman Agha are still to bat, which is the middle order Pakistan will lean on if they want to set a target quickly.

## What a result is worth

The championship scoring is simple. A win earns 12 points, a draw 4 and a defeat nothing. Pakistan were ninth with 4.63 per cent of the available points as of October 31. One calculation circulating in Pakistani reports, which assumes no over-rate penalty, puts the change at stake like this.

That is a gap of about **seven percentage points between winning and drawing**, which is why the pace of Pakistan's batting on the final morning matters as much as the weather.

## How a win could be built

A lead of around 200 is the figure one report suggests could be defended, which means roughly 155 more runs from the overnight position. Pakistan would then need to take **all ten Sri Lankan wickets in the time left**. That is a tall order on a surface where de Silva and Mendis batted together for most of day three and the first part of day four, and one report notes their stand showed batters can settle on it.

Time is the enemy. Rain and bad light ended day four early, and reports say a draw is the likelier result with more showers about. Any shortening of the day on Friday pushes the match further towards four points each.

## What happens next

The second Test, from **November 17 to 21**, has been moved from Lahore to Rawalpindi because of smog, so the same ground will host the decider. A draw on Friday would leave the two-match series level going into it, with the 12 points still to be won.

For Sri Lanka, a result in their favour is the least likely outcome, but the scoring leaves little to play for apart from a draw: a Sri Lankan win would be worth 12 points to them and only 5 of 120 to Pakistan on the same calculation. Whether the weather or the bowlers decide the day will be clear by the evening.
