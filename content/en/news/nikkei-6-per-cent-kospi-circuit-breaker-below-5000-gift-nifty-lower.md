Asian markets **crashed on Friday morning** as the sell-off in artificial intelligence stocks that battered Wall Street overnight reached Tokyo and Seoul. Japan's benchmark lost more than 6% and South Korea's exchange had to halt trading minutes after the opening bell.

Indian shares now face a test after six straight days of gains. GIFT Nifty futures were **indicated about 0.7% lower** shortly before 7 am IST, pointing to a weak start when trading opens at 9:15 am.

## Seoul stops trading

The Kospi opened 2.24% lower at 5,263.26 and kept falling. At 9:03 am in Seoul, 5:33 am IST, it was down 460.33 points, or 8.55%, at 4,923.47, and the Korea Exchange **triggered a circuit breaker that stopped trading for 20 minutes**. The rule kicks in when the benchmark stays more than 8% below the previous close for a full minute.

The index slipped **below 5,000 for the first time in 10 months**. It had already lost 5.28% on Thursday, when a softer circuit breaker suspended programme selling.

Memory chipmakers, which have ridden the AI boom, took the worst of it. By 10:30 am in Seoul, Samsung Electronics was down 7.99% and **SK hynix had lost 10.28%**. When the halt began, the two had been down nearly 10% and nearly 12%.

## Tokyo's worst morning

Japan's Nikkei 225 **fell about 6.2% to 57,271.19** in morning trade, down from 61,077.90 at Thursday's close. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 got off more lightly, down 0.7% at 9,003.40. The dollar rose to 157.83 yen.

The trigger was Thursday's rout in New York. The Nasdaq Composite closed **down 5.52% and in a correction**, and the S&P 500 fell 4.36%, its worst day since the tariff crash of April 2025. Wall Street's fear gauge, the VIX, was reported to have jumped nearly 70%, though US stock futures recovered some ground in Asian hours.

## Why AI stocks are falling

Investors fear the costly race to build AI data centres will slow. Borrowing costs have risen, and critics have been saying for months that AI share prices look like a bubble. The Democrats' sweep of the US Congress has added a new worry: that tighter rules on the industry could follow, and that local governments could pause new data centres. US voters have complained about rising power bills.

## What it means for Dalal Street

Foreign money has powered India's recent run. On Thursday the Sensex **jumped 2,008.61 points to 79,310.01** and the Nifty 50 closed at 24,536.60, while foreign institutions bought a net ₹14,975 crore of shares.

Oil is the bigger concern for India. Brent crude **rose 3.88% to $98.29 a barrel** on Thursday and was moving back towards $100, which would raise the import bill and squeeze company margins. Traders will watch whether foreign buyers keep coming once the opening bell rings.
