Indian shares look set for a **gap-up start on Monday**, as the gloom that hung over Asia at dawn faded through the early morning. GIFT Nifty, the dollar-denominated Nifty futures traded at GIFT City in Gandhinagar, was **near 23,291, about 205 points or 0.89% higher**, ahead of the 9:15 am open. The Nifty 50 closed Friday at 23,053.10 and the Sensex at 74,459.53.

The change in mood comes mostly from oil. Brent crude was **down 2.7% at $110.4 a barrel** by about 8:45 am IST, and US crude was 2.9% lower at $101.1, as Iranian and Qatari officials kept talking about a way out of the conflict in the Gulf. Brent rose 13.6% in October, and every dollar off the price eases the import bill of a country that buys most of its crude abroad. Attacks on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz have not stopped, however, which keeps a floor under prices.


## Asia steadies after a rough start

Asian markets opened under the shadow of Friday's sell-off in US technology shares. The Nasdaq 100 lost 3.5% that day and the Philadelphia semiconductor index **plunged 7.2%**, as Intel fell close to 29%, its steepest one-day drop in more than four decades. In early Asian trade the Kospi fell 1.06%, the Nikkei 225 lost 0.39% and the Topix 0.51%.

By late morning the picture had changed. Tokyo had **cut its loss to about 0.3%**, Seoul's Kospi had swung to a gain of 1.4%, and the MSCI index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan was down only 0.2%. US stock futures rose, with the S&P 500 contract up 0.4% and the Nasdaq contract up 0.8%, and European futures pointed slightly higher. The yen was weaker at 157.80 to the dollar.

Investors are still uneasy about how much American technology companies are spending on artificial intelligence, a combined **$750 billion this year**, after results from Alphabet and Amazon. The Nasdaq fell nearly 7% in October, its worst month since April 2025. Traders also price a 72% chance that the US Federal Reserve will **raise rates by a quarter point** in December, and the US votes in its midterm elections on Tuesday.

## Results stocks in focus

At home, several large companies are in focus after their September-quarter numbers. Indian Oil Corporation posted a **record standalone profit of ₹21,630 crore**, against a loss of ₹2,661 crore in the previous quarter. Reliance Industries reported that profit attributable to shareholders rose 41% to ₹25,548 crore, and Jio Platforms, in its first results since listing last week, posted a 14% rise to ₹8,389 crore.

Sun Pharmaceutical Industries went the other way, with consolidated profit **down 36% to ₹1,989 crore**. Bharat Electronics reported a 19.8% rise in standalone profit to ₹1,541 crore, ahead of market estimates.
