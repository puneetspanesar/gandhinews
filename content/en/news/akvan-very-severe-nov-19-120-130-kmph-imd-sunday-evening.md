The India Meteorological Department (IMD) now says the Bay of Bengal depression that was named a threat on Saturday could become **a very severe cyclonic storm by early November 19**, the early hours of Thursday, with sustained winds of 120 to 130 km/h and gusts up to 145 km/h. The latest bulletin, issued on Sunday, November 15, adds that third and highest stage to a forecast that until now stopped at a severe storm. The system is expected to move north-north-westwards and then north-westwards towards the **north Andhra Pradesh coast**, but the IMD has not named a landfall point or time.

This is a step up from our [report on the depression](/en/news/akvan-depression-forms-severe-cyclone-nov-17-imd-sunday), which carried the expected severe stage on November 17 and wind figures of about 60 to 80 km/h. The bulletin's own table is higher than those figures, and it now looks past November 17 to the peak.

## What the bulletin says

On Sunday the depression lay over the southeast and adjoining southwest Bay of Bengal, **about 840 km east-southeast of Chennai** and about 1,010 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam, according to the day's bulletin. It was 1,130 km south-southeast of Gopalpur in Odisha and 690 km west-south-west of Sri Vijaya Puram in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. At 5:30 am it had been about 1,040 km from Visakhapatnam, so it is closing on the Andhra coast slowly: reports put its movement at 10 to 15 km/h.

The IMD expects it to intensify in four steps, with winds rising from **120 to 130 km/h** in four days, from 40 to 50 km/h now.

Manorama Mohanty, director of the IMD's Regional Meteorological Centre in Bhubaneswar, said: "By November 17, it is likely to intensify into a severe cyclonic storm." The Regional Meteorological Centre in Chennai is the source for the very severe stage by November 19. The IMD itself cautions that its forecast does not establish a confirmed landfall location or time.

## Sea and rain warnings

The sea is expected to turn **rough to very rough on Monday, November 16**, high to very high on November 17 and 18, and **very high to phenomenal from November 19** over the central Bay. Fishermen already at sea were told to be back on the coast by November 15, and fishing is restricted in several zones through November 20. Shipping, port and offshore operators have been asked to review their preparedness.

Rain follows the track. Coastal Andhra Pradesh can expect light to moderate rain with isolated heavy falls on November 18, and **heavy to very heavy rain at isolated places in the north on November 19**. Tamil Nadu is forecast only light to moderate rain and no direct impact, although an upper-air circulation persists over the south of the state. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands have heavy rain through November 16.

In Odisha, the dry spell ends on November 18. The Bhubaneswar centre's warnings run to November 22.

On **November 19 and 20**, Gajapati, Ganjam and Puri are under orange alert for heavy to very heavy rain, with yellow alerts for 14 more districts from Baleshwar to Nayagarh. On **November 20 and 21** the orange alert shifts to Rayagada, Koraput, Malkangiri, Gajapati and Ganjam. On **November 21 and 22** it reaches the western districts of Sundargarh, Jharsuguda and Bargarh, with yellow alerts for Sambalpur, Kendujhar, Mayurbhanj, Subarnapur and Balangir. Saturday's warnings had stopped at November 21 and had covered coastal districts only.

## How the forecast has moved

The IMD first flagged the system on Friday, November 13, as a low that would become a cyclonic storm by Monday evening, as our [first report](/en/news/akvan-track-uncertain-andhra-odisha-imd-saturday) set out. On Saturday it raised the outlook to a [severe storm around November 18](/en/news/akvan-severe-cyclone-forecast-nov-18-odisha-orange-warnings-nov-19-21) with orange warnings for south Odisha. On Sunday morning Andhra's State Disaster Management Authority said the system could near the north Andhra coast by November 19, as our [Andhra report](/en/news/akvan-andhra-apsdma-north-andhra-nov-19-eight-districts-fishermen-return) records. Each update has moved the peak earlier and made it stronger.

## A coast already hit twice

This coast has had no time to recover. Cyclone Arnab crossed near Yanam on October 28, and Cyclone Murasu crossed near Ongole on November 3 as a severe storm. Andhra's first damage estimate for Murasu was about ₹1,919 crore, as our [damage report](/en/news/murasu-andhra-damage-1919-crore-naidu-bapatla-guntur) records. A very severe storm would be stronger than Murasu, which had winds of 100 to 110 km/h at landfall.

## Why it matters

For people on the coast the practical change is time. A storm that reaches 120 to 130 km/h will bring down trees, power lines and kutcha houses, and a **very high to phenomenal sea** makes any boat trip unsafe. Fishing families in north Andhra and south Odisha have been told to stay ashore, and harvest-ready paddy in Odisha is at risk from the rain on November 19 to 22. Coastal administrations have limited room to shift people if the IMD later narrows the track, which is why forecasters keep stressing that the landfall point is open.

## What happens next

The next marker is the **cyclonic storm on the evening of Monday, November 16**, when the system takes the name Akvan, a name contributed by Iran, and becomes the third cyclone in about three weeks. The severe stage is due on the evening of November 17 and the very severe stage early on November 19. The IMD has said the track will be clearer once the storm forms, and its Monday bulletins are the first at which a landfall coast could be named.
