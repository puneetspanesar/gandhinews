Wall Street had its best session in days on Monday, **the S&P 500 rising 2.35% to 7,549.94** as a slide in oil prices and hopes of a US-Iran deal pulled stocks back from last week's rout. The Nasdaq Composite gained 2.74% to 25,253.14 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 1,104 points, or 2.15%, to close at 52,389.75. The Russell 2000 index of smaller companies rose 3.15% to 2,840.85.

The rally recovered most of the ground lost in the first week of November. The S&P 500 is now about **3.4% below its record high** of last month, and the Nasdaq about 8.5% below its own peak.

## Oil leads the move

The trigger was crude. **Brent fell about 5.5% to a little below $93 a barrel**, down from more than $112 at the start of the month, while US crude (WTI) dropped around 4% to roughly $87. President Donald Trump said in an interview on November 8 that a resumption of strikes on Iran was "highly unlikely" if negotiations progress, while Qatar has said its mediation between Washington and Tehran continues. Our reports on [Trump's remarks](/en/news/trump-renewed-iran-strikes-highly-unlikely-38-bridges-oil-falls) and on [the Qatari mediation](/en/news/qatar-says-us-iran-mediation-continues-trump-minor-strikes-qeshm) give the detail.

Cheaper fuel eased the pressure on bond markets too. The yield on the 10-year US Treasury note was about 4.87%, well below last month's peak above 5.30%. The dollar index slipped 0.61% to 101.67, and the VIX gauge of expected volatility **fell nearly 22% to 20.02**, from 25.64 on Friday.

## Chips rebound, Nvidia slips

Technology and AI-infrastructure shares led the gains after a rout last week, when worries that Democratic wins in the midterm elections could slow the building of AI data centres hit the sector. We reported then that the [Nasdaq closed in a correction](/en/news/wall-street-close-dow-1833-nasdaq-correction-chip-rout) as AMD and Micron fell more than 17% each in one session.

On Monday **AMD gained more than 9%**, Marvell Technology gained about 9%, Arista Networks about 8.9%, and The Trade Desk jumped 14.6%. Power producers that supply data centres also climbed: Constellation Energy rose 10.5% after strong earnings.

Nvidia moved the other way, **falling more than 4%**. The company said its board is investigating chief executive Jensen Huang after he acknowledged an intimate relationship with a business executive. The board said it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised. China's Foreign Ministry called the allegations "nothing but smears against China". A congressional briefing is scheduled for November 18, as we reported in [our story on the inquiry](/en/news/nvidia-board-investigation-jensen-huang-megaspeed-congress-briefing-november-18).

## The rest of the world

Markets elsewhere were mostly higher. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 2.3% and France's CAC 40 gained 1.2%, while South Korea's Kospi fell 0.8%. Gold edged up 0.11% to $4,138.65 an ounce, copper rose 2.2% and Bitcoin was little changed near $74,800.

## Why it matters in India

Oil is the number that links Wall Street to Dalal Street. India imports most of its crude, so a Brent price of about $93 against $112 at the start of the month lowers the import bill and the pressure on the rupee and on retail prices. Monday's lower oil price in New York came after Indian markets had closed, and the GIFT Nifty contract in Gujarat, a pointer to the Indian open, was at 25,058, about 13 points above the Nifty's close. Indian exchanges are shut on Tuesday for Diwali-Balipratipada and reopen on Wednesday, when the overnight rally in US stocks and the fall in oil can first be priced in.

## What happens next

Applied Materials and Cisco report earnings on November 12, and PDD Holdings on November 16. Traders will also watch the October US consumer price data and any word from Washington or Doha on whether the Iran talks produce a signed agreement. Monday's rise still left the S&P 500 below its high, after a fortnight that took in the post-election gain, Thursday's 4.36% fall and now this recovery.
