The Reserve Bank of India has **fined One Mobikwik Systems Limited ₹9 lakh** after a statutory inspection found that the wallet and payments company let some prepaid payment instrument holders move money to unregistered beneficiaries beyond the permitted limit, and that it did not report certain frauds on time. The penalty was announced in an RBI release on Friday, November 13, 2026.

The order itself is dated **November 6, 2026**, according to reports on the release, and Mobikwik informed the stock exchanges of it on November 13. The action was taken under Section 30(1), read with Section 26(6), of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007.

## What the RBI found

The inspection looked at the company's operations **from June 2025 to March 2026**. It found two lapses. First, certain holders of prepaid payment instruments, the category that covers mobile wallets, were allowed to transfer funds to beneficiaries who had not been pre-registered, above the limit the rules prescribe. Second, certain frauds were **not reported within the required timeline**.

The process was the usual one for such penalties. After the inspection the RBI issued a show-cause notice. It then considered the company's reply, its additional submissions and the oral arguments made at a personal hearing, and concluded that the charges were established and that a **monetary penalty was warranted**.

The central bank added two standard clarifications. The action is based on deficiencies in regulatory compliance and is **not meant to pronounce on the validity of any transaction or agreement** between the company and its customers. And the penalty does not stop the RBI from taking other action against the company.

## Why the rules exist

The RBI's prepaid instrument rules cap what a wallet holder can send and tie transfers to beneficiaries added in advance, and they set deadlines for reporting frauds so that regulators and banks can act on them quickly. These are the two areas in which the inspection found lapses.

For customers, nothing changes in their wallets because of the order. The release does not direct the company to stop onboarding customers or restrict any service, and it does not suggest any customer loss.

## The company's position

Mobikwik had a strong quarter just days before the penalty. On November 2 it reported **a consolidated net profit of ₹11.8 crore for July to September 2026**, against a loss of ₹28.6 crore a year earlier and a profit of ₹7.6 crore in the April to June quarter. Revenue from operations rose 9 per cent to ₹295.7 crore, and platform gross merchandise value reached a record ₹59,677 crore, up 34 per cent. UPI transactions on the platform rose 52 per cent to 210 million.

Against those numbers, a ₹9 lakh fine is **about 0.8 per cent of one quarter's profit**, a small financial hit. Its weight is in what it says about compliance: the lapses were in the very areas the RBI watches closely in digital payments.

## How we got here

This is not the first time the RBI has fined the company. On December 24, 2021, it penalised One Mobikwik Systems Private Limited and Spice Money ₹1 crore each for not meeting net worth requirements for Bharat Bill Payment Operating Units. The two cases concern different rules, but both are findings of non-compliance by a payments operator.

The central bank has been tightening the surrounding rulebook as well. Earlier this week it [folded its domestic money transfer instructions into a single set of directions](/en/news/rbi-consolidates-domestic-money-transfer-rules-cash-neft-50000-limit), keeping the cash limits and adding authentication and monitoring requirements for banks and payment operators.

## What happens next

The penalty is payable by the company, and the RBI has kept open the option of further action. The company's quarterly filings and any statement to the exchanges are where a change in its business or risk would show up first. The RBI's full release, with the order details, is on its website.
