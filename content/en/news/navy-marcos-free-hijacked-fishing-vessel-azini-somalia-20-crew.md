The Indian Navy has freed a fishing vessel hijacked by armed pirates off Somalia, **rescuing all 20 crew members** with no casualties or injuries, after an operation that ran for 48 hours.

The vessel, the Azini, had been seized **off Somalia's north-eastern coast** last month. The Navy said the rescue was completed on Monday evening, when marine commandos boarded and secured the ship.

The hunt was led by the guided-missile destroyer **INS Kolkata**, backed by a P-8I long-range maritime reconnaissance aircraft and a Sea Guardian drone. Through constant surveillance the three located the Azini, kept track of it and used non-kinetic measures to **stop it from slipping away**.

The final step came from the air. Navy commandos, the MARCOS, were **airdropped from an Indian Air Force C-17** close to the vessel and took control of it.

The Navy said the Azini could have been turned into **a "mother ship" for pirates**, a base from which smaller skiffs can strike merchant ships far out at sea. Somali pirate gangs have used captured fishing boats this way before, to reach shipping lanes well beyond the coast.

The Navy called the operation a "testament to the Indian Navy's high operational preparedness" and said it showed its commitment to being the **"First Responder and Preferred Security Partner"** in the Indian Ocean Region.

The rescue comes at a tense time for shipping across the wider region, with the war involving Iran keeping merchant traffic around the Strait of Hormuz under threat. The Navy has not yet said when or where the 20 crew will be brought ashore.
