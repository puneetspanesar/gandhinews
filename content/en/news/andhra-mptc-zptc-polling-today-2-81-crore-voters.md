Rural Andhra Pradesh goes to the polls on Thursday to elect **members of its mandal and zilla parishads**, two days later than first planned. Voting in the Mandal Parishad Territorial Constituency (MPTC) and Zilla Parishad Territorial Constituency (ZPTC) elections runs from 7 am to 5 pm.

When State Election Commissioner Anil Chandra Punetha announced the schedule in Vijayawada on October 19, **659 of the state's 663 ZPTC seats** and 9,829 of its 9,858 MPTC seats were listed for election. About 2.61 crore voters are eligible, and the commission set up 35,333 polling stations. It classed 9,966 of them as sensitive and 9,186 as very sensitive, with webcasting at mandal and district level. Some seats were settled before polling day, when returning officers declared unopposed winners on October 27.

After scrutiny, **50,815 nominations were found valid**, 46,045 of them for MPTC seats and 4,770 for ZPTC seats.

## Why the date moved

Polling was originally fixed for November 3. After the Supreme Court's directions on October 21, the commission gave candidates in four urban local bodies two more days to file nominations, which **pushed municipal counting to November 3**. To avoid stretching police and election staff over two big exercises on the same day, it moved the rural vote to Thursday.

At 88 polling stations in Alluri Sitarama Raju and Polavaram districts, voting closes early, at 3 pm. Any repoll will be held on Friday, and **counting begins at 8 am on Saturday**, November 7.

The run-up has been bitter. The YSR Congress Party has said its candidates were attacked when nominations opened, and filing closed after clashes in Palnadu, Anantapur and Tirupati, and in Odisha, Naveen Patnaik wrote to Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi on Wednesday asking him to take up **Andhra's decision to hold polls in Kotia**, a disputed cluster of villages on the border.
