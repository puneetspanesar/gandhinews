Prime Minister Narendra Modi used his **Constitution Day article on Thursday** to blame the largest Opposition party for the defeat of the women's reservation amendment Bill in April, while Congress marked the 77th anniversary of the Constitution's adoption with Samvidhan Raksha marches in states, aimed at the electoral rolls and the Election Commission. The government and the main Opposition spent the day making opposite claims about who is defending the document.

## What the Prime Minister wrote

According to reports of the article, published on the morning of November 26, 2026, Modi paid tribute to B R Ambedkar, Rajendra Prasad and the members of the Constituent Assembly and set out what he called the Constitution's unfinished work. His sharpest passage concerned the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, which would have raised the Lok Sabha's strength to as many as 850 seats and tied a 33% quota for women to delimitation.

The Bill fell short in the Lok Sabha on April 17, 2026: **298 members voted for it and 230 against**, below the two-thirds majority a constitutional amendment needs. Modi wrote that what happened that day showed an Opposition party's "disdain for constitutional values" and should fill the country's hearts with shame. "I assure the 'Nari Shakti' of India that no force can prevent us from ensuring that justice is done to them," he wrote.

He went on to say the biggest Opposition party had become an "Avrodh Paksh" (an obstructing party) rather than a "Virodh Paksh" (an opposing one), adding that "disrupting democratic processes is no substitute for discharging constitutional responsibilities." The reports do not say whether the article names Congress.

## OBC quota and the Emergency era

The article also carried an allegation on reservations. "It is most appalling that the biggest party in the Opposition wants to reduce OBC reservation and bring Muslim reservations," Modi wrote, adding that the framers, Ambedkar above all, would not have accepted it. The reports give no detail of any proposal behind the charge, and no party has yet answered it on the record.

Modi was also reported to have criticised the First Amendment of 1951 and the 42nd Amendment passed during the Emergency, and the repeated use of Article 356 to dismiss state governments. He credited the **elimination of Naxalism this year** with letting the poorest use their rights without fear, saying only "Dimagi Naxals" remain, and noted that an updated Constitution is now available in all 22 languages of the Eighth Schedule. He also said the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita puts citizens' rights and justice ahead of punishment.

## Congress takes the Constitution to the streets

Congress had announced the marches days earlier. In a statement shared by the party, general secretary (organisation) K C Venugopal said that on Constitution Day every Pradesh Congress Committee "will organise a massive Samvidhan Raksha March in its state as the centrepiece of our campaign to safeguard the Constitution."

Reports say the marches were to start at statues of Mahatma Gandhi or Ambedkar, with participants reading the Preamble and taking a pledge to protect the Constitution. Congress president **Mallikarjun Kharge was scheduled to walk in Lucknow**. The marches are the centrepiece of the party's "Vote Rakshak" campaign, which has accused the Election Commission of failing to protect the right to vote during voter-list revisions, and carry its demands on Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. Congress units have run the same campaign at booth level elsewhere, as in Assam, where the party said it would post "Vote Rakshaks" at every polling booth.

No Congress response to the Prime Minister's article had been reported by the early afternoon.

## The government's own programme

The official observance is run by the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs. Its **release of November 25** says the day has been marked as Constitution Day since 2015 and that the ministry is running an online reading of the Preamble on the MyGov portal in 23 languages, the 22 Scheduled Languages and English, where readers get a certificate after reading. A Constitution Quiz ran on the MyGov and MyBharat portals from November 18 to 26, and a **Viksit Bharat Yuva Samvad was held in 786 districts** across all 36 States and Union Territories from November 24 to 26. Ministries, state governments and schools were asked to hold collective Preamble readings, special morning assemblies, debates and quizzes on the making of the Constitution.

## The BJP's own programme

The BJP has launched a five-day Constitution awareness campaign aimed at students and young people, according to reports, and Defence Minister Rajnath Singh accused Congress of undermining democratic institutions. The party's attack on Congress over the Emergency is not new: BJP president J P Nadda made the same case in Bhopal in November, when he said Rahul Gandhi was "pretending" to protect democracy.

## How the argument got here

The women's quota law of 2023 gives women 33% of seats in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies, but it takes effect only after a census and delimitation. The government's amendment Bill was meant to speed that up by expanding the House. The Opposition said it backed the quota but not the delimitation link, and the Bill failed on April 17, 2026. The government is reported to have later withdrawn the delimitation Bill and a Union Territories Bill that went with it.

The subject returned in November. In mid-month the Centre was reported to be planning a special session to pass a delimitation Bill, and Congress called the government's claim of having the votes bluster, while Priyanka Gandhi Vadra said the Opposition had not been told of any session.

## Why it matters

For readers the dispute has two practical edges. One is seats: any renewed push for the quota Bill would change the size of the Lok Sabha and the share each state holds in it. The other is the voter list: Congress's marches are about who stays on the rolls in states where revisions are under way, which decides who can vote in the next elections.

## What happens next

The Constitution Day exchange is likely to carry into the next Parliament session. The Centre has not announced a new date for the quota and delimitation Bills, and Congress has not said whether its marches will be followed by a larger programme. The Chief Justice of India's own Constitution Day address could not be confirmed from any source at the time of writing and is not covered here.
