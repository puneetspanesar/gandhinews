The Indian Navy will hold this year's Indo-Pacific Regional Dialogue in New Delhi from 3 to 5 November, with ocean science and research cooperation at its centre, the Defence Ministry said on Friday.

The annual meeting, the Navy's main strategic-level international outreach, is organised with the National Maritime Foundation. The 2026 theme is "Science, Technology and Academic Cooperation in the Indo-Pacific". Scientists, academics, policymakers and maritime practitioners from 18 countries are expected.

Defence Minister Rajnath Singh is scheduled to give the commemorative address on the first day. The opening day will also have a session called "Perspectives from the Flag Bridge" with former naval chiefs of India and Sri Lanka, and an interaction among ambassadors titled "Sahyog Ka Samvaad".

## Five sessions

Over three days, five sessions will take up academic networks and building human capital; maritime awareness and the protection of undersea and other critical infrastructure; oceanography and climate resilience; ocean governance; and new maritime technologies with both civil and military uses.

The ministry said the oceans are connected but the science, technology and institutions needed to manage them are unevenly spread, while climate change, supply-chain weaknesses and threats to infrastructure cross borders. For that reason the dialogue will reach beyond engineering and science to the social sciences, life sciences and humanities.

Past editions have each taken up parts of the Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative, which Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced at the East Asia Summit in Bangkok in November 2019. The Navy said the aim is to come away with specific, practical proposals that countries can take forward through existing bilateral and regional arrangements.
