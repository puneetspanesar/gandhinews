The Supreme Court on Thursday **stayed criminal contempt proceedings** against Punjab Chief Secretary KAP Sinha and pressed the state to come back with a plan to pay the dearness allowance arrears it owes its employees and pensioners.

A bench of Justices B V Nagarathna and R Mahadevan halted the Punjab and Haryana High Court's case against Sinha and other officials "until further orders". It also directed that **no coercive steps** be taken in a separate civil contempt case over the same dues. The state then has to tell the court **how much it can pay by March 31, 2027**.

"You have got a breather. Please come up with a positive response," the bench told the state. The matter will be heard again **on December 1**.

## A bill of ₹22,818 crore

The unpaid dearness allowance and dearness relief arrears stand at **₹22,818 crore**. Appearing for the state, senior advocate Vikas Singh said Punjab had already paid about ₹5,000 crore under a plan to clear the dues. The state also told the court that the present government inherited about ₹13,000 crore of arrears from its predecessor.

The bench was sharp with the contempt petitioner. "Why are you interested in sending the chief secretary to jail? We will stay the contempt proceedings," it said, adding: "We don't appreciate this arm twisting." It also asked how the petitioner could rely on an internal note said to have been sent by the Chief Secretary to state departments.

## How the case reached here

The dispute goes back to the High Court's **August 3 order**, which dismissed appeals by the state and its power utility PSPCL and directed Punjab to clear pending dearness allowance and relief within a fortnight, at the rates paid to All India Services officers. It also barred spending such as large advertising campaigns until the dues were paid.

A retired PSPCL chief engineer then moved a contempt petition. On October 12, Justice Harsh Bunger asked Sinha to show cause why he should not face criminal contempt, over what the court saw as a misstatement in a state affidavit about the status of Punjab's appeal in the Supreme Court. The High Court had told him to file a compliance affidavit by November 13 and to appear in person on November 17.

Punjab argued that criminal contempt could not be used to get around the normal procedure for enforcing payment, and that the proceedings arose from applications in cases already disposed of. No contempt finding has been recorded against any officer, and the state's appeal against the August 3 judgment is still pending.
