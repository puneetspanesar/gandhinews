The Supreme Court on Thursday **stayed criminal contempt proceedings** against Punjab Chief Secretary KAP Sinha, pausing a High Court case that grew out of the state's long delay in paying dearness allowance arrears to its employees and pensioners.

A bench of Justices B V Nagarathna and R Mahadevan stayed the Punjab and Haryana High Court's October 12 order that asked Sinha to show cause why he should not face criminal contempt, and halted all further proceedings in that case "till further orders". "We have perused the impugned orders," the bench said. Earlier in the day a bench led by Chief Justice Surya Kant had agreed to take the state's plea up at once.

The dispute goes back to the High Court's **August 3 order** directing Punjab to clear pending dearness allowance and dearness relief arrears by August 18. A retired chief engineer of the state power utility PSPCL then moved a contempt petition, saying the order had not been obeyed. The criminal contempt notice from Justice Harsh Bunger arose from what the court saw as a misstatement in a state affidavit about the status of Punjab's appeal in the Supreme Court.

Appearing for the state, senior advocate Vikas Singh said Punjab had **already paid about ₹5,000 crore** under a plan to clear the dues. The state argued that criminal contempt could not be used to bypass the procedure for enforcing payment. A separate civil contempt petition over the arrears is still pending in the High Court.


The High Court had told Sinha to file a compliance affidavit by November 13 and to appear in person on November 17.
