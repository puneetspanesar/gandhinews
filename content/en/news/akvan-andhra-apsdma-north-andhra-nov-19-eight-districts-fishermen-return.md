Andhra Pradesh's disaster managers are preparing for the Bay of Bengal system to come ashore on the **north Andhra coast around November 19**, as the low-pressure area over the southeast Bay moves towards depression strength on **Sunday, November 15**. The State Disaster Management Authority (APSDMA), whose managing director is Prakhar Jain, has told coastal residents to stay alert, and fishermen to be back on shore by Sunday evening. The system has not yet been named; it becomes Cyclone Akvan if it reaches cyclonic storm strength, which is now forecast for Monday evening.

## What the state authority expects

The authority's outlook follows the stages the India Meteorological Department (IMD) set out on Saturday. The low is due to become a depression on Sunday and **a deep depression by Monday morning, November 16**, and a cyclonic storm by that evening. By **Wednesday, November 18**, it is expected to be a severe cyclonic storm over the central and west-central Bay, before it moves towards the north Andhra coast by November 19.

Jain's authority has not fixed a landfall point. It describes the system as following an unusual track, and a firmer estimate is expected within about 24 hours. Winds of 40 to 50 km/h, gusting to 60 km/h, are forecast from Monday. The authority's outlook puts wind over the sea at about **120 km/h**, a figure higher than the IMD's own forecast of 85 to 95 km/h, gusting to 105 km/h, for November 18, so the two will need to be read against each other as bulletins arrive.

## Rain and the districts in line

Heavy to extremely heavy rain is forecast for **November 18 and 19**, with isolated extreme rainfall possible, and the IMD has issued an orange alert for vulnerable areas. The districts likely to get heavy rain are Parvathipuram Manyam, Alluri Sitarama Raju, Kakinada, Dr B R Ambedkar Konaseema, East Godavari, West Godavari and Eluru, along with Yanam. The whole north Andhra coastal belt may see heavy to very heavy rain.

Fishermen have been told to **return to the coast by Sunday evening** and not to go fishing until the storm has passed and the weather is back to normal. Reports based on the IMD's Saturday update carry the same advice for the Bay.

## Where else it could go

The track is the open question. On Saturday the IMD named no coast. VR Durai, director of the Area Cyclone Warning Centre in Chennai, said weather models pointed to north Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha, and that "as of now, the weather models indicate the cyclone would not come towards north Tamil Nadu coast". He said the exact path would be known only after the system forms on Monday evening. An agricultural meteorologist, Surendranath Pashupalak, has said it could approach the coast between Visakhapatnam and Gopalpur around November 17 and 18. A private forecaster, Skymet, said on Friday that the chances of the system sparing Andhra and Tamil Nadu outnumbered other outcomes, with West Bengal and Bangladesh facing a higher risk. A senior IMD official said at the time that it was too early to predict a cyclone at all.

Odisha is on the other side of the same forecast. The Bhubaneswar Meteorological Centre has orange warnings for Gajapati, Ganjam and Puri on November 19 and 20, as our [report on Saturday's IMD update](/en/news/akvan-severe-cyclone-forecast-nov-18-odisha-orange-warnings-nov-19-21) sets out.

## A coast still recovering

The warning lands on a coast that has not recovered from two storms in three weeks. Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu ordered boats back and repair crews into position when Arnab approached in October, as our [report on that review](/en/news/naidu-cyclone-arnab-review-zero-casualties-fishermen) records. Murasu then crossed the south coast in early November and caused damage of **about ₹1,919 crore** in Andhra, with eight deaths and crops hit on 1.4 lakh hectares, according to the state's first estimate in our [earlier report](/en/news/murasu-andhra-damage-1919-crore-naidu-bapatla-guntur).

## Why it matters

For fishing families the first effect is lost days at sea in a season when boats were already damaged by Murasu, which damaged 346 boats and 220 nets according to the state's count. For farmers, rain of this kind on **standing and drying crops** threatens grain loss and waterlogging, the worry farmers voiced in Odisha on Saturday. For the administration, a storm that arrives on a track nobody can yet fix means shelters, power crews and relief stocks have to be placed along a long stretch of coast, from south Odisha to the Godavari delta.

## What happens next

The next marker is the **depression expected today**, after which the authority says a clearer landfall estimate should come within a day. The cyclonic storm is due on **the evening of November 16**, when it takes the name Akvan, and the severe stage is forecast for November 18. The IMD's bulletins on Monday will show whether the north Andhra track holds or the system turns towards Odisha or Bangladesh.
