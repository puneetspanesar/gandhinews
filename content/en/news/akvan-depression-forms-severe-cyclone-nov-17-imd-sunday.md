The Bay of Bengal system that has been under watch since Friday **became a depression at 5:30 am on Sunday, November 15**, the India Meteorological Department (IMD) said, and the forecaster now expects it to reach **severe cyclonic storm strength on November 17**, a day earlier than the Saturday outlook. The depression was centred near 9.0°N, 86.9°E over the southeast and adjoining southwest Bay, **about 1,040 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam** and 1,160 km south-southeast of Gopalpur in Odisha. The IMD has named no landfall point.

## What the bulletin says

The 5:30 am bulletin, as relayed by Odisha news reports, places the depression about 700 km west-southwest of Port Blair and about 860 km east-southeast of Chennai. It says the system is **very likely to keep moving north-northwestwards** and to strengthen further.

Winds are forecast at **about 60 km/h from November 16**, rising to about 80 km/h from November 17 and staying near that level for two days. Strong winds and rough seas are warned for the Bay of Bengal, the Andaman Sea and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. Saturday's warnings from the Bhubaneswar Meteorological Centre put the peak higher, at 85 to 95 km/h gusting to 105 km/h, so the figure may change as the next bulletins come in.

If the depression reaches cyclonic storm strength on Monday it takes the name Akvan, a name contributed by Iran, and becomes the third cyclone in a month.

## How the forecast has moved

On Friday, November 13, the IMD said a low formed over the southeast Bay would become a depression on Sunday and a cyclonic storm by Monday evening, as set out in our [first report](/en/news/akvan-track-uncertain-andhra-odisha-imd-saturday). On Saturday it raised the outlook to a severe storm around November 18, with orange warnings for south Odisha on November 19 to 21, as our [report on that update](/en/news/akvan-severe-cyclone-forecast-nov-18-odisha-orange-warnings-nov-19-21) records. Andhra's State Disaster Management Authority said on Sunday morning that the system could near the north Andhra coast by November 19, as our [Andhra report](/en/news/akvan-andhra-apsdma-north-andhra-nov-19-eight-districts-fishermen-return) sets out.

The depression has formed on schedule. The change in the new bulletin is the speed: the severe stage is now expected on November 17 rather than November 18, which gives coastal districts a day less to prepare.

## Reactions and preparations

In the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Lieutenant Governor Dinesh Sharma said the administration was prepared, that the disaster management force was fully ready and that the Navy, Air Force and Army were on high alert. He asked residents not to panic. The Directorate of Shipping Services has warned that harbour and vehicle ferries linking Chatham, Bambooflat, Phoenix Bay, Dundas Point and Panighat may be delayed, suspended or cancelled at short notice. Heavy rain is forecast over the islands through November 16.

On the mainland, coastal administrations in Odisha and Andhra Pradesh have begun precautions: fishermen are being recalled from deep-sea areas and cyclone shelters are being inspected, according to reports from Odisha. The advice to fishermen is to be back on shore by November 15 and to stay off the sea until conditions ease. Odisha's Revenue and Disaster Management Minister Suresh Pujari chaired a meeting with collectors on Friday, and the state was not waiting for an IMD track, as we reported then.

## A coast already hit twice

This coast has had little time to recover. Cyclone Arnab crossed near Narasapur on October 28 as a very severe cyclonic storm with winds of 125 to 135 km/h, and at least six people died. Cyclone Murasu crossed near Ongole on November 3 as a severe cyclonic storm with winds of 100 to 110 km/h. Andhra's first damage estimate for Murasu was **about ₹1,919 crore**, as our [report on the damage](/en/news/murasu-andhra-damage-1919-crore-naidu-bapatla-guntur) records.

For residents the practical effect of a faster forecast is that boats, power crews and relief stocks have to be in place a day earlier along a long stretch of coast from south Odisha to the Godavari delta, while the track is still open.

## What happens next

The IMD's next bulletins will show whether the depression has deepened. A **cyclonic storm is due on Monday, November 16**, when it takes the name Akvan, and the severe stage on November 17. Heavy rain is forecast for Odisha and coastal Andhra Pradesh on November 18 and 19. The IMD has said the track will be clearer once the storm forms, and a landfall forecast is expected only then.
