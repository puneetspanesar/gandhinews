The Union Home Ministry has **reportedly sought a report from Tamil Nadu Governor Rajendra Vishwanath Arlekar** on the state's law and order situation, following a run of killings across the state over Deepavali on November 8 and in the early hours of November 9. The request, reported on November 10 and 11 and attributed to sources in New Delhi, is said to cover recent violent crime and the steps the state administration has taken to keep the peace. Neither the Ministry nor Raj Bhavan has published the communication.

Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay, who also holds the Home portfolio, leads a Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK) government that has come under attack from the DMK, the AIADMK and the BJP over the killings.

## What happened over Deepavali

Reports on November 9 said that **ten unconnected murders** took place across Tamil Nadu from Sunday, November 8, into the early hours of Monday. Police said most were set off by drinking, rowdy behaviour and sudden anger, with minor quarrels over firecrackers turning fatal, and that in two cases of old rivalry the attackers used the noise of the festival to settle scores.

The victim who drew the most attention was **Special Sub-Inspector Senthil Kumar, 56**, of Theni district. He was returning home from work in plain clothes when he saw youths setting off high-decibel crackers in the middle of a road at Samadharmapuram, blocking traffic while they filmed social media reels. He asked them to move aside and began recording a video of the violations to send to the police control room. The group surrounded and attacked him, and he died at Theni Government Medical College Hospital on November 9. Police said a pre-existing heart condition probably made the assault worse. Police described him as having died in the line of duty even after his shift had ended. Chief Minister Vijay ordered **₹40 lakh in relief** for his family.

Other killings reported that night included two 17-year-old daily wage labourers hacked to death near the Pullambadi canal at Samayapuram in Tiruchirappalli district, a 20-year-old IT employee stabbed after an argument over where crackers were being burst, an auto-rickshaw driver, Dhanush, killed in Vyasarpadi in Chennai, and a history-sheeter stabbed to death in Cuddalore. Greater Chennai Police had deployed more than 18,000 personnel for the festival, and said the Vyasarpadi killing was a targeted, local dispute and not a failure of crowd control.

On the evening of November 9, PMK functionary Gnanavel was shot dead near Gudiyatham in Vellore district, a case covered in [our earlier report](/en/news/pmk-functionary-shot-dead-vellore-gudiyatham).

## Two counts of the dead

The numbers are contested. The DMK and AIADMK say **more than 10 people were killed on Deepavali day alone**. The state government and police say **eight murders** were recorded that day. PMK president Anbumani Ramadoss said 10 people, including the Special Sub-Inspector, were murdered across the state during Deepavali.

## Reactions

DMK leader M. K. Stalin said: "Law and order is in the intensive care unit. The administration is in a coma." Leader of the Opposition Udhayanidhi Stalin said there is no safety for people or for police under the present government. AIADMK general secretary Edappadi K. Palaniswami said law and order had deteriorated and demanded ₹1 crore for the Special Sub-Inspector's family. A Tamil Nadu BJP leader, Narayanan Thirupathy, said the situation had reached a dangerous level and pointed to liquor and drug peddling.

The government rejects the charge that order has broken down. Vijay met senior police officers to review the situation, and police have run operations against alleged criminal elements across the state over the past two days.

## How we got here

Arlekar was moved from Keralam to Tamil Nadu in the President's appointments of October, covered in [our report on the new Governors](/en/news/president-appoints-governors-six-states-keralam-tamil-nadu). The Deepavali killings are the latest round in a running argument over law and order in the state. In September, Udhayanidhi Stalin accused the Chief Minister of running a "reels government" after what he said were nine murders in two days.

Law and order is a state subject under the Constitution, so a report from a Governor is a request for information and an assessment, not an order. The Governor's reply goes to the Centre, and it is for the Union Home Ministry to decide what, if anything, follows.

## What happens next

Governor Arlekar is scheduled to travel to New Delhi on **November 13**. What his report will say, and how the Home Ministry will respond, has not been reported, and no deadline for the report has been given. The TVK government has not issued a statement on the Centre's request in any of the reports read.
