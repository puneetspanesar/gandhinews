The Election Commission has told the Supreme Court how voters struck off during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) can be put back, and the answer is simpler than the process that removed them. In a **supplementary affidavit filed on Saturday, November 14, 2026**, the Commission said electors excluded as "absent, shifted, dead or duplicate" can submit forms afresh, and that those whose forms are accepted will **get back the same EPIC numbers** they held before.

The affidavit was filed in the proceedings over the Delhi SIR, brought by Anjali Bhardwaj and Shailendra Mani Tripathi, and it spells out the steps the Commission set in motion on November 6.

## What the affidavit says

Voters who were excluded because they **never returned an enumeration form** can submit it now, signed, together with an **Annexure B statement** explaining why it was not filed earlier. They can do this in person with the electoral registration officer (ERO), the assistant ERO or the booth-level officer (BLO), either themselves or through a family member. They can also file online on the Commission's ECINET platform with an electronic signature.

Voters who **moved within the same state** no longer need Form 8, the address-change form. They file the enumeration form with an **Annexure C statement** giving the new address and Assembly constituency, along with proof of residence, to the ERO or assistant ERO of the constituency where they now live.

The Commission said forms will be handled under **Rule 21(1) of the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960**, the provision for adding names that were left out by mistake. Once a form is accepted, the voter's original EPIC number is assigned again.

Booth-wise lists of the excluded electors, each with a probable reason for exclusion, are to be displayed at polling stations and public offices and posted as PDFs on the websites of the chief electoral officers. Partly filled forms will be regenerated on the ERO dashboard on ECINET, and BLOs will visit homes, phone electors and coordinate with the booth-level agents of political parties. Where contact details exist they may also use WhatsApp, email or SMS.

On the question that has drawn the sharpest criticism, the Commission repeated that **voters who already filed Form 6 will not face prosecution**. Form 6 is meant for new voters, and the Commission said it will not start proceedings merely because an applicant had been enrolled in an earlier roll. Such electors, provided they are not enrolled in another constituency, can apply under Section 31 of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 without fear, and those who have already filed Form 6 need not submit enumeration forms again.

The Commission said the mechanism is meant so that "no elector is deprived of the opportunity to get his/her name included in the final electoral roll", while ensuring nobody is enrolled twice.

## How we got here

The Supreme Court suggested on **October 28**, during the Delhi SIR hearing, that the Commission find ways to reach voters left off the draft rolls. On **November 3** the Commission clarified that electors already on earlier rolls could apply through Form 6 without being prosecuted, a point we reported when the matter was [mentioned before the Chief Justice](/en/news/supreme-court-delhi-sir-form-6-ec-clarification-cji-mention). On **November 6** it directed the chief electoral officers of Delhi, Maharashtra, Haryana, Andhra Pradesh, Jharkhand, Telangana and Nagaland to open [a fresh window for excluded electors](/en/news/ec-fresh-window-electors-missing-sir-draft-rolls-seven-states). The affidavit now puts those directions on the court's record.

The scale is large. About **13 crore names were deleted** in the SIR exercise, according to reports, and opposition parties have accused the Commission of disenfranchising voters, a charge it rejects.

## Reactions and the Form 6 dispute

The use of Form 6 to bring back deleted voters has been contested. Former Chief Election Commissioner S.Y. Quraishi has said Form 6 is only for new voters, that people who filed it as deleted voters may have committed an offence, and that officials who required it were abetting one. The Commission's waiver of Form 8 for movers and its assurance on prosecution answer part of that objection, though the affidavit does not withdraw Form 6 as a route.

The petitioners, represented by Prashant Bhushan, Neha Rathi and Chand Qureshi, had earlier pointed to the alleged misuse of Form 6. No response to the new affidavit has been reported yet.

## A dispute inside the Commission

Separate from the court case, Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi have written to the Cabinet Secretary about the handling of files in the Commission's IT wing. According to their letters, a Deputy Election Commissioner who routed files from the Director General (IT) was removed, and the files were then overseen by a Senior Deputy Election Commissioner. A new Deputy Election Commissioner joined on November 2, and a work allocation order of November 4 placed the division's oversight with a senior deputy. The Commission says a new workflow now routes the Director General's files through a senior Deputy Election Commissioner.

## Why it matters

For a voter, the practical change is the paperwork. A person who moved from one part of Delhi or Hyderabad to another no longer has to file Form 8 first and can instead file one form with proof of the new address. A person who never saw the enumeration form can file it with a short explanation. Families can file for those who cannot go in person. The lists of the excluded will be public at the booth, so a voter can check the reason given for the exclusion.

The affidavit gives **no deadline** for filing. Electors in the seven states will need to act before the final rolls are published, and the Commission has not said when that will be. The affidavit also does not say how many of the excluded have so far been restored.

## What happens next

No hearing date has been reported for the supplementary affidavit. Booth-wise lists are to appear at polling stations and on chief electoral officers' websites, and BLOs have been asked to start home visits and calls to the excluded.
