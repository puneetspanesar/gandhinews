On Monday Shiv Sena leader Ramdas Kadam said he wondered whether NCP state president Sunil Tatkare had wanted to become deputy chief minister after Ajit Pawar died, and that the crash probe "must" be looked at "from that angle". He produced no evidence. Tatkare called the charge baseless. The NCP's spokesperson asked Kadam to come with proof and said the two men have been rivals in the Konkan for years.

That last point matters. An allegation of complicity in a death, made in the middle of a local feud, is the kind of claim that cannot be left standing without a document behind it.

## What the record shows

The Learjet 45 crashed near Baramati on January 28, 2026, and **five people died**, Ajit Pawar among them. The Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau published a preliminary report on February 28. It states no cause. It says all aspects will be examined to establish the facts, the root cause and contributing factors.

What it does describe is a set of **weaknesses at the airfield**. Visibility at landing was 3 km and was conveyed to the pilots, against the 5 km that visual flight rules require. The jet failed its first landing attempt, turned round and tried again, and about a minute after clearance struck a tree and rough ground beyond the runway's edge. Baramati has no certified instrument landing system, no meteorological facility, and the tower that day was a temporary one staffed by a ground instructor from a flying training organisation. The AAIB's interim recommendations asked the DGCA to make operators follow procedure for visual flights into uncontrolled airfields and to allow flying only when weather meets the DGCA's criteria.

Nothing in that report points to a person. It points to a runway, a weather estimate and a set of rules.

## The fair objection

Kadam's defenders would say that a serious allegation deserves examination, and that Congress's Vijay Wadettiwar has said an inquiry should be started given its gravity. There is something in that. A family or a party that doubts an accident can ask for every angle to be closed. The State CID took over the accident case on January 30, and no one can say a crime was ruled out if no one has published why.

But the answer to a doubt is the report, not a name. An inquiry opened because a rival says "I wonder" would be a template for any leader to use against any other, and it would drag a grieving family into a quarrel over seats in Dapoli and Guhagar.

## What should happen

First, **Kadam should either give the investigators what he has or withdraw the charge**. Doubt that cannot be put in writing is not a lead.

Second, the **Civil Aviation Ministry should publish a dated timeline** for the final report. On March 6 the minister of state said work had begun and that he would not comment until it was complete. Eight months later the public has no date. Rumour fills a silence of that length.

Third, the **DGCA should say what it has done** on the AAIB's two interim recommendations, and whether the airfields in Maharashtra that run visual flights without a certified tower, weather facility or instrument approach have been checked.

Fourth, if the CID has found nothing criminal, it should say so in a line. That would end the speculation Kadam is feeding.

Parties in the ruling alliance should also settle their Konkan feud where it belongs, at the ballot box and in the party meeting, not over the memory of a man who cannot answer.
