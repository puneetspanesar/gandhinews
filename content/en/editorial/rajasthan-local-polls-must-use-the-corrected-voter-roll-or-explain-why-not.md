Two official lists of voters now exist for the same state, and they do not agree. The Election Commission's final SIR roll for Rajasthan, published on February 21, has **5.15 crore electors**. The State Election Commission's rolls for the municipal and panchayat elections carry **about 5.45 crore voters**. The difference is close to 30 lakh names.

The activist Nikhil Dey of the Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan said on Sunday that 26.63 lakh voters deleted in the SIR are still on the local lists. A study by the PUCL, cited in an earlier report, reaches a similar figure. Of those, it says, 17.57 lakh were removed from the Election Commission's roll as permanently migrated, yet they remain at Rajasthan addresses on the state commission's lists. Another 3.68 lakh were deleted as untraceable or absent.

## What the state commission says

The commission does not dispute the difference. Its secretary, Rajesh Verma, says it used the **pre-SIR roll, last updated on January 1, 2025**, because it has traditionally built local lists from the annual summary revision and because preparations for these elections began early. Chief Electoral Officer Naveen Mahajan said that no objection to the final SIR roll had been raised in Rajasthan. A senior commission official has said no formal complaint on the discrepancy was received and that corrections are not possible while polling is under way. The panchayat polls are scheduled from October 25 to November 16.

That is a fair account of how the lists were made. It is not an account of whether they are right.

## The argument for the older roll

There is a case for the state commission. Local elections have their own calendar. A commission that waits for another body's exercise may find itself unable to hold polls on time. The SIR itself is contested, and critics in several states say eligible people were struck off. Leaving a name on a local list is, in that light, kinder than deleting it in haste. Nobody wants a living voter turned away at the booth.

But that argument cuts both ways. The same study says about **15.52 lakh people on the final SIR roll are missing** from the local rolls, and more than half of them are aged 18 to 25. A roll that keeps names the Election Commission removed and drops young voters it added is not a more generous roll. It is simply a different one, and nobody can say what it is based on.

The gap is not even across the state. The study says voters on local rolls but not on the final roll make up 8.5% of municipal rolls and 3.6% of panchayat rolls. In Bhilwara the share is 20.5%. In those places the question is no longer about method. It is about **whether the list is clean**.

## What should happen

First, the State Election Commission should publish a name-by-name reconciliation of its rolls against the final SIR roll, district by district, so that any voter or party can check it. The commission has said that no complaint was received. A published list lets people complain.

Second, the 15.52 lakh people missing from the local rolls should be given a clear window to claim a place. Young first-time voters should not lose their vote because of a date chosen for convenience.

Third, a deleted name that stays on a local roll should be checked on the ground, by booth-level staff, with the reason for the SIR deletion on record. If a person was marked dead or shifted in one list, someone must say why the same person is a voter in another.

Fourth, the state should settle by law or rule which roll local elections use. The commission should adopt the **latest Election Commission roll** by default and explain in writing any decision to use an older one. Rules decided before an election are fairer than explanations offered during it.

Elections held on a roll nobody can reconcile will be questioned whoever wins. Rajasthan's voters, and its candidates, deserve a list they can trust.
