Candidates of the 2025 civil services examination who belong to the OBC are still waiting to learn which service they will get. They have waited because the Centre treated some of them as creamy layer on one ground: **their parents earned salaries in public sector undertakings**. On Friday, November 6, the Supreme Court told the government, for the second time this year, that this ground does not work.

The bench of Justices P S Narasimha and R Mahadevan **refused to modify the March 11 judgment**. The salary income of parents cannot be mechanically counted, it said, and the court's reading of the 1993 Office Memorandum and the 2004 clarificatory letter binds everyone under Article 141. Service allocation for the 2025 batch will follow that reading. Completed selections and the seniority of other employees are left alone.

## What the Centre asked for, and why it was reasonable

The Centre told the court that the difficulty lies in the rules themselves. It wanted **two years** to work out equivalence between posts in PSUs, banks and other bodies and posts in government, and to build a uniform creamy layer mechanism.

That is a real difficulty. Posts in different institutions carry different pay scales, titles and responsibilities, and any table will have to be argued over. A hasty table could wrongly exclude families who need reservation, or let in families who do not.

## Why two years was too long

Reservation is a right with a date attached to it. A candidate who is denied the OBC benefit in the 2025 allocation does not get that service back later. The 2004 clarificatory letter is more than twenty years old, and the March judgment gave the Centre **six months**. Those months are now spent.

The court has granted a further two months and refused to name a timeline for the full mechanism, calling it a matter for the executive and the legislature. That is the right division of work, but it also means no outside deadline will force the Department of Personnel and Training to produce a table. The department must set one for itself.


## What we recommend

**First,** the department should publish a draft table of equivalence between PSU, bank and government posts for public comment within the two months, not at the end of them. Families, state backward classes commissions and candidates' groups know where the lines bite and can say so before the rule is final.

**Second,** the draft should state in plain terms that income from salary is judged by the rank of the parent and not counted mechanically, in line with the court's reading, so that the next batch is not allocated under uncertainty.

**Third,** the Centre should tell Parliament and the court when the final mechanism will be in force. A date the government sets for itself is easier to hold to than silence.

The court has now made its position clear twice. What remains is work for the executive, and candidates who have waited since the examination should not wait through another year for it.
