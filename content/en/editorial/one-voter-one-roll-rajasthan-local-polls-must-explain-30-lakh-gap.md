Rajasthan's local body voter lists carry about **5.45 crore names**. The Election Commission's final roll from the Special Intensive Revision (SIR), published in February 2026, has 5.15 crore. The activist Nikhil Dey said in Jaipur on Sunday that **26.63 lakh voters deleted in the SIR** are still on the State Election Commission's urban and panchayat lists. The panchayat elections are already under way.

The State Election Commission's explanation is simple. Secretary Rajesh Verma said the lists were built on the **pre-SIR roll updated on January 1, 2025**, as local polls traditionally use rolls from the annual summary revision, and preparations began early. Chief Electoral Officer Naveen Mahajan said no objections had been raised in the state to the final SIR roll.

None of that is irregular on its face. It still leaves a problem that no citizen should have to solve.

## Two answers, both uncomfortable

The SIR deleted 44.28 lakh names and added 12.91 lakh. If the 26.63 lakh names Dey identifies were removed because the voters had died, moved or been counted twice, then **dead and departed people remain on lists** that will elect sarpanches and zila parishad members. That is a standing invitation to impersonation.

If instead some of those names were wrongly struck off, then the SIR roll has locked out real voters from the assembly and Lok Sabha polls, while the local lists, by accident of timing, still include them. That would be a far graver finding about the SIR itself.

Either way, the numbers cannot sit side by side without explanation. Dey's question, how a booth-level officer can mark a voter deceased on one roll and leave that voter on another, has not yet been answered.

## The fair objection

The two commissions are separate bodies, and the state commission is entitled to choose its base roll within the law. Changing the roll after a poll schedule is announced could disenfranchise people mid-process, and the older roll has the advantage of being the one every local candidate has already worked from. A rushed switch to the SIR roll might lose real voters as readily as it removes dead ones.

That is a reason to move carefully. It is not a reason for silence.

## What should happen

First, the Election Commission and the State Election Commission should **match the two rolls name by name** and publish the list of the 26.63 lakh, by constituency, with the SIR's recorded reason for each deletion.

Second, any person who is alive and eligible but was deleted in the SIR should be able to claim a place through a **single, simple form**, valid for both rolls, with the claim decided before the next local poll.

Third, for the phases still to vote, the state commission should say plainly how it will stop a voter marked deceased on the SIR roll from casting a ballot, or why it will not need to.

Fourth, the two bodies should agree before the next cycle on **one roll for every election in a state**, so that a voter's status does not depend on which poll it is.

Elections are trusted when the roll is trusted. A citizen who is dead to one commission and alive to another has every reason to wonder which one is right.
