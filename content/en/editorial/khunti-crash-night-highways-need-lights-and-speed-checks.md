They had done what decent neighbours do. A little before 10 pm on Thursday, a scooter crashed near a culvert at the Jone-Ludru turn on the Karra-Torpa road in Khunti, and its rider was thrown into the bushes. People from the nearby homes, many of them women and children, came out to pull him to safety. Minutes later an 18-wheeler trailer came down from the Torpa side at speed and **drove straight into them**.

Seven people died: four women and three children, among them a 12-year-old boy who died in Khunti Sadar Hospital. A young girl is still being treated. The driver and his helper ran away in the dark, and **no arrest had been reported** by Friday afternoon.

## A known danger, not a freak

What makes this hard to bear is that the villagers saw it coming. At the roadblock near the Karra railway crossing on Friday they said the turn is **badly lit and dangerous**, and that heavy vehicles race through it at night. Their demands were modest: the driver's arrest, jobs and compensation for the bereaved families, speed breakers and regular police checks.

Officials who came to talk promised help "in line with government rules", without naming a sum. That phrase has ended too many such conversations in rural India. The families need a figure and a date, and the road needs work.

Khunti is not an exception. The Road Transport Ministry's own count shows **1,72,890 people killed on India's roads in 2023**, about 20 every hour. Where deaths were linked to traffic rules being broken, overspeeding accounted for 68.1% of them. More than 40% of all deaths happened on roads other than national and state highways, the kind of road that runs past Jone-Ludru.

## The fair objection

Speed breakers on a road used by goods traffic are not a cure-all. Badly built humps cause their own crashes, and a district cannot post a constable on every bend. Nor can the state stop people from rushing to help someone lying hurt on the road, and it should never want to.

That is the point. A road that carries trailers at night past village homes has to be built and run on the assumption that **people will be standing on it**.

## What Jharkhand should do

First, the Jone-Ludru turn and the stretch around it should get street lights, reflective signs, rumble strips and properly designed speed tables **within a month**, with the district publishing the work order so residents can hold it to account.

Second, heavy vehicles need speed control at night. Speed cameras or interceptor patrols on the Karra-Torpa road, and others like it that carry freight through villages, would do more than any number of condolence messages.

Third, the police should **trace the trailer's owner and driver** quickly; a trailer of that size is not hard to identify. Driving away from people you have run over is a crime of its own, and a prompt arrest is the least the families are owed.

Fourth, compensation should be announced in rupees, not in rules, and paid within weeks, along with free treatment for the injured girl.

Finally, people who help at a crash scene need a safer way to do it. Good Samaritans are already protected by law from harassment. The next step is simple training through panchayats and schools: switch on lights, set up a warning well behind the scene, and **keep the crowd off the carriageway**.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Chief Minister Hemant Soren have both said they are pained. Pain is not a policy. The test of Friday's promises is whether the Jone-Ludru turn looks any different by Christmas.
