On Friday, Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann announced a one-time settlement for unpaid household electricity bills in Punjab. Penalties and interest on dues up to March 31, 2026, are waived; a family pays **only the principal**, a quarter of it upfront and the rest in instalments over up to a year. About **24.86 lakh domestic consumers** are eligible, and the state puts the waiver at **₹518 crore**. The window opened Saturday and stays open for 45 days.

For a household that stopped paying because the penalty had grown bigger than the bill, this is real relief. By our arithmetic from the government's figures, the average family owes about ₹10,100 and the waiver removes roughly ₹2,080 of it. A connection cut years ago can be restored once the principal is paid. Few would argue that a family should carry a penalty forever.

## The questions the announcement skips

The first is who pays. The state government is said to bear the ₹518 crore, but the reports of the announcement do not say whether it will be paid to the Punjab State Power Corporation from the budget, from the subsidy account, or left as a gap in the utility's books. That matters because a utility that writes off money it was owed, with no one paying for it, borrows more or raises tariffs elsewhere. Honest relief is relief someone has funded.

The second is how many of those families are on disconnected connections. The state has not said, and the answer shows whether this is a scheme for people who could not pay or for people who would not. The third is why the dues built up at all. If lakhs of families fell behind, the arrears may say something about billing and tariffs, not only about defaulters.

## The fair objection

A government may reasonably say that old penalties on poor households are uncollectable, that disconnected connections earn the utility nothing, and that taking the principal now is better than chasing the whole sum in vain. That is a sound argument, and the principal of about ₹1,998 crore is a sum the corporation badly needs. An earlier Punjab government ran a similar waiver for domestic dues up to the end of 2021.

But the earlier waiver is exactly the trouble. Each amnesty teaches the honest customer who paid on time that paying was the mistake, and teaches the next defaulter to wait. And the timing, with Punjab due to elect a new Assembly early in 2027, invites the suspicion that this is arithmetic written for a campaign.

## What Punjab should do

First, **publish the notification and the instalment schedule** now, since reports of the announcement differ on whether it is six monthly payments or two six-monthly ones. Families cannot plan on a rumour.

Second, state in writing how the ₹518 crore will reach the power corporation, and in which year's accounts it appears.

Third, publish after 45 days how many families applied, how much principal was collected, and how many connections were restored.

Fourth, say plainly that this is the last such scheme for this category, and back it with firm action on new arrears, such as timely billing and a path for hardship cases that does not need an amnesty.

Relief for people who cannot pay is a fair use of public money. A waiver announced with no payer, no figures and an election ahead is only a favour, and the people who paid on time will notice the difference.
