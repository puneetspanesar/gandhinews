**Anyone in India aged 70 or older can now get free hospital cover of up to ₹5 lakh a year**, regardless of income. That is the promise of the Ayushman Vay Vandana card, launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on October 29, 2024, as an extension of Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY). Two years on, it has paid for **23.46 lakh hospital admissions**, but many eligible older people have yet to sign up.

## Who is eligible?

**Every Indian aged 70 or above, with no income, job or asset test.** The scheme is aimed at about 6 crore senior citizens in roughly 4.5 crore families. It gives cashless cover of up to ₹5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospital treatment.

If your family is already covered under PM-JAY, members aged 70 and above get an **additional top-up of up to ₹5 lakh a year** for themselves, which they do not have to share with younger family members.

## How do I get the card?

**On the Ayushman app or the PM-JAY beneficiary portal, with Aadhaar.**

1. Download the **Ayushman app** or open the PM-JAY beneficiary portal.
2. Enter the details of the person aged 70 or above.
3. Complete **Aadhaar-based e-KYC**, which is mandatory.
4. Download the **Ayushman Vay Vandana card** once it is generated.

## How does the cashless treatment work?

**Show the card at an empanelled hospital and the hospital deals with the payment.** At a hospital that is part of PM-JAY, an Ayushman Mitra at the help desk checks the patient's identity and eligibility. The hospital seeks pre-authorisation for the treatment and then treats the patient **without any payment at the counter**, settling the bill later with the state health agency or insurer. More than 33,000 hospitals were empanelled under PM-JAY by the end of 2025, though coverage is thin in many hill and tribal districts.

## What has the scheme paid for so far?

**Cataract operations, dialysis and heart procedures, above all.** Up to October 20, 2026, 23.46 lakh admissions worth ₹5,668.80 crore had been authorised, an average of a little over ₹24,000 each. A year earlier, at the end of October 2025, the count was 7.89 lakh admissions worth ₹1,741 crore, so about 15.6 lakh were added in the second year.

**Cataract surgery** is the most common treatment, with 4.71 lakh procedures, about a fifth of the total, followed by chronic haemodialysis, with about 2.90 lakh. By value, angioplasty (PTCA), a procedure to open blocked heart arteries, leads at about ₹649 crore. Knee replacements, COPD flare-ups, fevers and kidney failure are among the other major categories.

## What does it not cover?

**Outpatient visits and long-term medicines.** The cover is for hospitalisation. Consultations without admission and the cost of regular medicines that many older people take for years largely remain out of pocket.

## How many people have signed up?

**Far fewer than are eligible.** As of December 5, 2025, a little over 93 lakh Vay Vandana cards had been created, about one for every six eligible senior citizens. If you have a parent or grandparent over 70, enrolling them before they need hospital care avoids delays at admission.
