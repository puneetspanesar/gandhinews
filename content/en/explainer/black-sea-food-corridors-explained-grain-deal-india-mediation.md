## What is a food corridor?

**It is a sea lane agreed between warring sides so that grain ships can sail without being attacked.** Ukraine is one of the world's largest grain exporters, and most of its crop leaves by sea. Russia and Ukraine together supply roughly **a quarter of the world's traded grain**, so when ships cannot sail safely, prices rise far from the battlefield.

## How did the 2022 deal work?

**The United Nations and Turkey brokered it in Istanbul in July 2022.** Russian Defence Minister Sergei Shoigu and Ukrainian Infrastructure Minister Oleksandr Kubrakov signed it. It opened a safe route from three Ukrainian ports, Odesa, Yuzhny and Chornomorsk, with Ukrainian pilots guiding ships through mined waters.

A Joint Coordination Centre in Istanbul, staffed by the UN, Ukraine, Russia and Turkey, tracked the loading and inspected inbound ships to check they carried no weapons. Under the deal, **about 32.9 million tonnes of food** moved out, with more than half going to developing countries.

## Why did it end?

**Russia said its side of the bargain was never delivered.** In July 2023 Moscow refused to extend the deal. It listed conditions: an end to sanctions on its agricultural bank, supplies of farm machinery and parts, relief on insurance and the restart of an ammonia pipeline to Odesa. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said the deal's effect had ended because the parts concerning Russia had not been carried out.

Ukraine did not stop shipping. From August 2023 it opened **its own temporary channels** along the coast, without Russia's consent, and kept some exports moving under the threat of attack.

## Why is it back in the news?

**Because ships are being sunk again.** In October 2026 at least seven cargo vessels were reported hit by drones off Romania and Bulgaria, leaving about 30 sailors dead or missing, and the cargo ship Khoryv was sunk off Constanta on October 23, 2026. Ukraine's agriculture minister said on September 29, 2026 that he saw no sign of a grain truce in the coming months. Reported estimates say about **30 million tonnes** of Ukrainian produce will go unexported this season, and wheat prices have reached a three-year high.

Zelenskyy said after a call with Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday, November 9, 2026: "We discussed Russia's blocking of grain exports via the Black Sea. **India is ready to mediate** in establishing food corridors, and we are counting on these efforts to continue."

## What has India said?

**Not that.** Modi's post called the call "productive" and spoke of India's commitment to "contributing in every possible way" to peace. Neither it nor the Prime Minister's Office statement mentioned food corridors. On October 4, 2026 External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar had said Delhi was in touch with both Moscow and Kyiv about restoring safe navigation, "starting with the Black Sea safety of shipping issue".

## Why India, and what are the limits?

**India talks to both sides.** It is among the largest buyers of Russian crude, taking about 2.6 million barrels a day in June and July, more than half its crude imports, and Modi has told Putin in person that the time has come to end the war. Ukraine's foreign minister has called India's truce proposal the most comprehensive before Kyiv. Reports say it has three parts: a halt to strikes on energy sites, uninterrupted grain and energy shipments from Black Sea ports, and an end to attacks on commercial ships. India has not published the text.

The limits are real. Russia has rejected every Black Sea grain proposal so far, and India has no seat at the talks that opened in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, which bring together the United States, Russia and Ukraine.

## What to watch

Whether Delhi accepts a formal role is the question. A working corridor would bear on shipping risk and on food prices in importing countries. The Abu Dhabi talks run for two days, and Russia's demand for large territorial concessions, which Kyiv rules out, makes a quick breakthrough unlikely.
