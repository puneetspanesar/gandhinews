When Coal India sells a slice of its Odisha subsidiary to the public, the subsidiary will not see a rupee of it. That is the central fact of the **Mahanadi Coalfields IPO**, and it comes from one phrase in the offer papers: **offer for sale**. Coal India filed the red herring prospectus, the final offer document, on November 13, 2026. This piece explains what that phrase means, how it differs from the other kind of IPO, and what the investor and the company each get.

## What is an offer for sale?

**It is a sale of shares that already exist, by the people who own them.** In an initial public offering, a company can do two things. It can issue brand-new shares and keep the money, which is called a **fresh issue**. Or its existing shareholders can sell some of their holding to the public, which is an offer for sale, or OFS. Many IPOs do a bit of both.

In a fresh issue, the number of shares in the company goes up and the company gets cash to spend. In an OFS, the number of shares stays the same. They only change hands, and **the cash goes to the seller**. For the company, an OFS is a listing: it gets a market price and a wider set of shareholders, but no new funds.

## How is the Mahanadi issue structured?

**It is entirely an offer for sale, with Coal India as the only seller.** The offer covers up to 66,18,36,300 equity shares of ₹2 face value, or about 66.18 crore, which is about 10% of Mahanadi Coalfields. Coal India, the parent, would keep about 90% after the sale. The shares are to be listed on BSE and NSE.

Reports of the final papers say that no more than half the issue is meant for large institutional investors, at least 15% for non-institutional investors and at least 35% for retail investors. Those splits come from listing-site reports that do not fully agree with one another, and some mention a separate quota for Coal India shareholders, so check the final prospectus before relying on them.

## Why would a parent sell instead of the company raising money?

**Because the aim here is to sell down the parent's holding and not to fund the subsidiary.** The numbers suggest why. Mahanadi Coalfields made a profit after tax of ₹10,698 crore on revenue of ₹30,550 crore in FY26, both slightly lower than a year earlier, according to its offer documents. It is a cash-rich business that does not obviously need outside capital, and a fresh issue would bring in money it has no stated plan for.

For Coal India, a sale at the subsidiary level raises money without selling anything in the parent. Reports put the size at about ₹10,000 crore, or $1 billion, but the company has not confirmed that figure, and it depends on the price. The draft prospectus also says what an OFS means for the company: **no fresh capital for expansion**.

## How did the offer get to this stage?

**Through three steps that every large IPO in India has to take.** Mahanadi Coalfields filed its draft red herring prospectus with the market regulator SEBI on August 31, 2026. SEBI issued its observations on **November 4, 2026**, which is the clearance a company needs before it can open an issue. The red herring prospectus, the final version, followed on **November 13**.

The red herring prospectus carries everything except the price: the business, the risks, the financials and the structure. The **price band, lot size and the dates** for opening, closing and listing are not yet announced. Until then, an investor cannot know how much one lot costs.

## What does it mean for an investor?

**An investor buys a share of a profitable coal miner but gets no say in how the proceeds are used, because they go to Coal India.** The company produced 218.31 million tonnes in FY26, about 22.4% of India's non-coking coal output, and held reserves enough for about 45 years at that rate, according to the offer papers. It had a profit after tax of ₹10,698 crore in FY26, down from ₹10,825 crore a year earlier.

The risks the prospectus names are those of any coal company: demand and prices, tighter environmental rules and a long-term shift of power generation to renewables. Because the issue is an OFS, the share price will depend on how investors value those earnings. A higher price means a larger sum for Coal India; it does not change the money available to the miner.

## What happens next?

The next step is the announcement of the price band and the dates. A report has said the issue could open next week, but the company has not confirmed any date. The exchanges are shut today, a Saturday, and reopen on Monday, November 16, 2026.
