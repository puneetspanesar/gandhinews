## Why does this visit matter?

**Balendra Shah is in Delhi on his first bilateral trip as Prime Minister**, at Prime Minister Narendra Modi's invitation. He arrived on Sunday, November 22, was received by Minister of State Jitin Prasada, and met External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar, who said "India cherishes the special friendship with Nepal." Talks with Modi at Hyderabad House are due on Monday, along with a call on President Droupadi Murmu, a wreath at Rajghat, and the exchange of agreements.

The two governments agree on tone and differ on substance. That is the pattern of a relationship that is close in daily life and tense in its politics.

## How close are the two countries?

**The border is open.** Under a 1950 treaty of peace and friendship, citizens of each country can cross freely, live and work in the other, and millions do. Nepali workers, traders and pilgrims are a fixture of Indian cities and temples, and Gorkha soldiers have served in the Indian Army since before Independence.

Trade is just as one-sided in reach. India accounts for **about 62.3% of Nepal's goods trade**. In 2025-26 India exported $7.45 billion to Nepal, while Nepal sold India $1.94 billion, leaving a deficit of about $5.5 billion that Kathmandu raises at every meeting.

## What is the border dispute?

**Nepal and India both claim about 372 sq km at Nepal's north-western corner**, in Darchula district, where the borders of Nepal, India and China meet. India has administered it for decades. The argument turns on a river: the 1816 Treaty of Sugauli, which ended the Anglo-Nepal War, fixed Nepal's western limit at the Kali. Nepal says the river rises at Limpiyadhura, which puts Kalapani and Lipulekh on its side. India says the river begins nearer Kalapani, which shrinks Nepal's claim.

The row turned sharp in 2020. On May 8 India's Defence Minister opened a 75-km road to Lipulekh; on May 20 Nepal's cabinet published a map including the three areas, and Parliament amended the constitution to put it there, with the House **voting 258 to none on June 13**. India called the map an "unjustified cartographic assertion."

The dispute flared again this year. After India announced the 2026 Kailash Mansarovar Yatra through Lipulekh, Nepal sent notes to India and China on May 3 repeating its claim. The same day Foreign Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal called the claim "untenable" and said the pilgrim route has run since 1954. On October 25 Nepal's foreign ministry told Parliament that **encroachment exists along the border** beyond Kalapani and Susta, and that joint mapping continues. Few expect any breakthrough this week.

## Where do they cooperate best?

**Electricity.** Nepal's rivers make it a power exporter in the monsoon months. At the 13th Joint Steering Committee in Pokhara on July 15, 2026, the two sides agreed to raise export capacity on the Dhalkebar lines from 1,100 MW to **1,650 MW**, and import capacity from 1,000 MW to 1,400 MW. A January 2024 agreement aims at up to 10,000 MW over ten years.

The sticking point is Bangladesh. Nepal sells 40 MW to Dhaka through Indian lines, and a planned rise to 60 MW was held up in June when India's Central Electricity Authority cited transmission limits. Nepal wants Delhi to unblock it. The Pancheshwar dam on the Mahakali has been stalled for nearly three decades over water sharing and costs.

## What else is on the table?

**Connectivity and money.** The agenda lists development cooperation, energy, railways, check posts, climate and disaster resilience, technology and sports. Reports from Kathmandu say the two leaders will inaugurate the **604-km Ayodhya-Janakpur train** and that Nepal has asked for about $2 billion in credit, grants and flood reconstruction after the August floods in the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi corridor. India has said it will consider the reconstruction proposals. A route exchange for air services, giving Nepal an entry for its Bhairahawa airport and India access from Bagdogra, is also being prepared.

## What is likely to stay open?

**Gorkha recruitment is stalled.** Fresh Nepali recruitment into the Indian Army has been on hold since August 2022, after India's Agnipath scheme kept only 25% of recruits for regular service; Nepal has not accepted the model. The boundary dispute, the trade gap and Pancheshwar are also unlikely to see more than talk of mechanisms.

The test of the visit will be less the length of the communiqué than whether India and Nepal can treat the border question as something to manage by talking. Shah, 35, comes with a large majority and a long wish list, and Modi has the neighbourhood policy to show for it.
