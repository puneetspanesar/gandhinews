## What does suspending a sentence mean?

**It puts a prison term on hold while the convict's appeal is heard.** A conviction is not the last word in Indian law: the person can appeal to a higher court, and an appeal can take years. Section 430 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, which replaced Section 389 of the old Code of Criminal Procedure, lets the appeal court suspend the sentence in the meantime and release the appellant on bail.

The power comes with conditions. The court must act **"for reasons to be recorded by it in writing"**. Where the offence is punishable with death, life imprisonment or a term of not less than ten years, the public prosecutor must first get a chance to oppose the release in writing, and can later ask for the bail to be cancelled. If the appeal fails, the time spent outside does not count towards the sentence.

## Is it the same as bail?

**It works like bail, but the starting point is very different.** Before a trial ends, an accused person is presumed innocent, and bail is the norm in many cases. Once a court convicts, that presumption is gone. The Supreme Court said in 2021 that after a conviction "there shall not be any presumption of innocence thereafter", which is why judges are slower to release a convict than an undertrial.

## How do judges decide?

**They look at the length of the sentence, the gravity of the crime and whether the conviction looks shaky on its face.** The leading rule on fixed terms comes from a 1999 judgment by Justices K T Thomas and M B Shah. Where a convict serving a fixed term appeals under a statutory right, they held, "suspension of sentence can be considered by the appellate court liberally unless there are exceptional circumstances". The reasoning is practical: if an appeal takes longer than the sentence, a wrongly convicted person would have served it all before being cleared.

Life sentences are treated more strictly. In a 2023 judgment the Supreme Court drew the line between the two, and later rulings have said that a convict serving life must point to a **"gross and apparent error"** in the verdict before the sentence is put on hold. Judges also say they will not weigh the evidence afresh at this stage, since that is the job of the final appeal hearing. A short look at the record is allowed, and the seriousness of the offence counts heavily.

## Why can Tejpal appeal to the Supreme Court at all?

**Because the High Court turned his acquittal into a conviction with a 10-year term.** Most people convicted by a High Court must ask the Supreme Court for permission to appeal. The law makes an exception when a High Court, hearing an appeal, **reverses an acquittal and sentences the accused to death, life or ten years or more**. That person has a right to appeal. The rule was Section 379 of the old code and is now Section 420 of the BNSS.

Tejpal's case fits it exactly. A Goa trial court acquitted him in 2021. On August 6, 2026, a High Court bench of Justices Neela Gokhale and Amit Jamsandekar set that aside, convicted him of rape and other sexual offences under the Indian Penal Code and sentenced him to **10 years of rigorous imprisonment**.

## How did the case reach this point?

**Through 13 years, two opposite verdicts and a surrender.** After he appealed in August, the Supreme Court turned down his plea to be excused from surrendering, and he gave himself up before a court in Mapusa on September 14. He has since been held in the Central Jail at Colvale in North Goa.

## What did the court weigh on Friday?

**Arguments that went to the heart of the evidence.** Senior advocate Kapil Sibal, for Tejpal, said technical evidence showed the lift doors had opened twice during the ride, questioned why the hotel's CCTV footage was never seized, and pointed out that Tejpal had stayed on bail for years without misusing it. Solicitor General Tushar Mehta, for Goa, relied on colleagues' accounts of the woman's distress, messages Tejpal sent and an email of apology.

A bench of Justices M M Sundresh and Prasanna B Varale said it was **"not inclined to suspend the sentence"**. The order shows the limits of the 1999 rule. A 10-year term is a fixed term, but it also sits on the ten-year line at which the law asks the court to hear the prosecution first, and the bench's first look at the material, including the emails and messages, was reported not to have persuaded it to let him out.

## What happens next?

**The appeal against the conviction itself will be heard, and that decides everything.** If the Supreme Court restores the acquittal, Tejpal walks free. If it upholds the conviction, he serves the rest of the 10 years, with his time in custody counted. The Goa government is reported to have asked, separately, for the sentence to be raised to life imprisonment. No date for the final hearing has been reported.
