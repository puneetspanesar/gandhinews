The crash on National Highway 715 at Rangajan on Friday afternoon, November 13, 2026, **killed 26 people** and left about 20 injured, after a minibus travelling from Golaghat towards Nagaon hit a container truck head-on near Kaziranga National Park. A day later the state's chief minister has put numbers on what he thinks went wrong: speed, a crowded vehicle and possibly the driver's health. This explainer sets out those claims, the rule they are measured against, and what the state says comes next. For the first report, see [our news story](/en/news/assam-golaghat-kaziranga-minibus-truck-26-dead-modi-ex-gratia).

## What is the speed limit on this road?

**Vehicles must keep to 40 kmph on the stretch through Kaziranga.** A forest department notification fixed that limit on the highway that crosses the national park, which was then numbered NH-37 and is now NH-715. The same notification added an **environmental compensation of ₹5,000 per incident** on top of the usual fine under the Motor Vehicles Act, for a vehicle challaned for overspeeding or for killing or injuring an animal.

The limit exists because the road cuts across the animals' routes. The park has nine animal corridors on this stretch, and in the monsoon floods rhinos, deer and elephants walk up to the higher ground on the other side, across the road. Six cameras with number-plate recognition and speed radar were set up to catch violators.

## What does the chief minister say happened?

**Mr Sarma says the minibus was going over 120 kmph, three times the limit.** He told reporters on Saturday that the vehicle was built for 26 people but carried about 40 or 41, which he called "a crime". He also said the vehicle's speed governor, a device that caps its speed, had been disconnected, something he said is easy to do.

He added that the vehicle's **fitness certificate was valid**, and that 10 to 12 challans had reportedly been issued against it over time. This matches the earlier official account that the bus had been fined for speed in March and had several unpaid overspeeding fines in the Kaziranga area.

## Could the driver have fallen ill?

**The chief minister has suggested a stroke or heart attack, but it is a suggestion, not a finding.** He said the driver, Abdul Mujid, who died in the crash, had a sugar problem, and a sudden drop in blood sugar could have caused him to lose control of the wheel. Officials have asked for a full post-mortem to rule out intoxicants.

A medical event would not undo the other facts. A vehicle that is far over its seating capacity and moving at three times the limit gives a driver little room to recover, and a disconnected governor removes the one device meant to stop it. The district administration's inquiry into speed and overloading is still open.

## What will the state do now?

**Assam says it will enforce traffic rules strictly, even if people object.** Mr Sarma named helmets, intoxicants, speed limits, overloading and fitness certificates as the areas for enforcement. He said the government will **reconsider a decision of the Nalbari cabinet meeting** that had eased some enforcement, and noted that road deaths in the state have risen over the past two years.

For families, the compensation is stacked: ₹5 lakh from the Chief Minister's Relief Fund and ₹2 lakh from the revenue and disaster department, plus the ₹2 lakh the Prime Minister announced from the PMNRF, a total of **₹9 lakh for each family** of the dead. Of the 20 injured at Jorhat Medical College, three have since died, according to the chief minister, and one patient is on a ventilator.

## Why is the road being rebuilt?

**Because the Union Cabinet has approved making it a four-lane road with an elevated section.** In October 2025 it cleared the Kalibor-Numaligarh stretch of NH-715, which runs largely through Kaziranga, at **₹6,957 crore**. About 34.5 km of it is to be an elevated corridor, so that animals can cross underneath, without meeting traffic, between the park and the Karbi Anglong hills.

The plan answers the animals' problem more than the passengers'. A raised road would stop vehicles from sharing the ground with rhinos, but a 40 kmph limit exists for both, and a limit that is ignored protects no one. A year after the approval, the stretch where the crash happened was still a speed-limited road, and no completion date for the elevated section has been announced in the reports available.

## What happens next?

The chief minister said he would visit the families within two or three days, and post-mortems were being finished on Saturday. The inquiry into the vehicle, its owner and its driver will decide what action follows. The harder test is enforcement: whether the cameras on the park stretch catch a vehicle at 120 kmph before it reaches a crowd, and whether the state follows through on checking seats, governors and fitness papers across Assam. Until then, **the 40 kmph limit remains a rule on paper** for many drivers on NH-715.
