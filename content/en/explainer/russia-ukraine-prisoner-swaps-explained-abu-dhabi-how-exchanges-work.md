## What is a prisoner swap?

**It is an agreed handover of detainees between the two sides, usually at a border point whose time and place are kept secret.** Ukraine and Russia have done this since the start of the full-scale war in February 2022. Both governments announce it only when it is under way or finished, and families are often told only once their relative is on Ukrainian soil.

The numbers do not have to match. In September 2022, more than 200 Ukrainian prisoners of war were returned for one man, the politician Viktor Medvedchuk.

## What did Abu Dhabi agree?

**A large exchange, with the numbers still to be settled.** After the talks ended on Wednesday, November 11, US envoy Jared Kushner and Russia's chief negotiator Vladimir Medinsky both confirmed it. Rustem Umerov, who leads Ukraine's delegation, said another swap had been agreed, with the figure to follow.

Volodymyr Zelenskyy had earlier floated **1,000 for 1,000, or 1,500 for 1,500**. Neither government has confirmed that range. The two sides were to settle the numbers on Thursday, November 12.

## Who decides the numbers and who is on the list?

**In practice, Ukraine's Coordination Headquarters and officials in Russia.** The Headquarters, set up in March 2022, is a body of Ukraine's Cabinet that coordinates the agencies involved. Russia has no single body: its side has typically included the Defence Ministry, the FSB and the prison service.

Ukraine has asked for an "all for all" exchange, which Russia has rejected. Without a political deal, the size of each swap comes out of negotiation between those bodies. Ukraine submits lists of people it believes are held, and Russia often declines to confirm who it has, which is why many families wait years for news.

## How many people have come home?

**More than 8,050 Ukrainians by March 2026**, according to the Headquarters, across more than 70 exchanges. Russian figures were not available.

The flow has not been steady. Exchanges stopped from August 2023 until January 2024, which Ukraine blamed on Russian delays. In May and June 2025 a 1,000-for-1,000 exchange was agreed at talks in Istanbul. The most recent large one before this week came on February 5, 2026, after an earlier Abu Dhabi round, when **157 detainees were returned by each side**, the first large exchange in nearly five months.

## Why do swaps happen when nothing else does?

**Because they cost neither side territory.** The Third Geneva Convention requires prisoners of war to be released and sent home once active hostilities end, but nothing in a ceasefire or border deal is needed to swap them while the fighting goes on. Each side gets its soldiers back, and the families of those held press their governments to agree.

The swaps also run on a track separate from the political one. The February swap came with no joint political or security statement, and no narrowing of differences on territory or a ceasefire. Kushner said this week that **deep distrust** between the two sides remains the main obstacle to a wider deal.

## Who is hardest to bring home?

**Civilians.** Ukraine has no Russian civilians to trade, so releasing civilians is especially difficult. Ukraine's ombudsman said in December 2024 that at least 16,000 Ukrainian civilians were held, though the true number is unknown. Ukraine also says Russia has blocked the Red Cross and the UN from reaching Ukrainian prisoners, while they inspect Russian prisoners in Ukraine about every two months. Those are Ukrainian claims as of mid-2025 and Russia's account was not found.

## What happens next?

**The numbers, then Moscow.** US envoys Steve Witkoff and Kushner are reported to be flying to Moscow on Thursday, November 12 to brief Vladimir Putin, then to meet Zelenskyy in Ireland. No date is set for another round of three-way talks.

For a reader in India, the swap changes little directly. What matters is whether the track that delivers prisoners can also reach the shipping and energy attacks that move oil and food prices, and so far it has not. As of November 12, 2026, only the exchange has been agreed.
