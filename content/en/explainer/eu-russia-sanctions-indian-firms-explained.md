## What did the EU do on Thursday?

**It added three people and nine companies to its Russia sanctions list for helping Moscow get round existing curbs.**

The Council of the EU said the targets had helped Russia evade restrictions on trade, oil and payments. Most are Russian firms and their owners, along with two trading companies in Türkiye. Four come from India, the UAE and the Seychelles. Three of those, DIS Logistics DMCC, Vigor Marine Services LLP and Shiva Shipping Ltd, were described as part of Russia's "shadow fleet", the ageing tankers that carry its oil outside Western rules. The fourth, Hurus Trade FZE, was listed as a trading intermediary supplying Russian-origin crude to India.

"Circumventing EU sanctions is akin to aiding and abetting Russia's illegal war of aggression," said Kaja Kallas, the EU's foreign policy chief.

## What does being listed actually do?

**It cuts a company off from anything the EU controls.**

Any money or property the firm holds within the EU's reach is frozen. EU citizens and companies, wherever they are, may not make funds or economic resources available to it. Listed individuals cannot enter the EU. In practice this means European banks will not handle the firm's payments, European insurers and shipping service providers will not cover its vessels, and European buyers will not take its cargoes. Non-European banks and traders often steer clear too, to avoid trouble with their own European business.

## Is an EU listing binding in India?

**No.** EU sanctions are EU law. They bind people and companies in the EU and EU nationals abroad, not Indian authorities or Indian firms acting at home. India has said plainly that it does not follow sanctions imposed by one country or bloc on its own.

When the EU listed an Indian refinery last year, the Ministry of External Affairs replied: "India does not subscribe to any unilateral sanction measures." Its spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal added: "We stress that there should be no double standards," a remark linked to European countries' own continuing purchases of Russian gas.

## Has this happened to Indian companies before?

**Yes, and on a much bigger scale.**

The Nayara listing was the one that mattered most. Kallas described it at the time as "the biggest Rosneft refinery in India". The company, formerly Essar Oil, runs more than 6,750 petrol pumps, and the listing barred it from selling petrol and diesel into Europe.

## How has India's oil trade felt it?

**Mostly through the refined fuel ban.**

Since January 21, the EU has refused fuel refined from Russian crude in other countries. Europe is an important market for Indian diesel, so refiners that export have had to choose. Reliance Industries stopped taking Russian crude into its export-oriented refinery at Jamnagar. Provisional tanker data showed Russian seaborne crude shipments to India falling from 1.03 million barrels a day in December 2025 to about 125,000 barrels a day in the first half of January, though those early figures were expected to be revised up.

## Why does Thursday's list matter, if the firms are small?

**Because it signals where Brussels is looking next.** Naming a trader for supplying Russian crude to India, and shipping firms that move it, tells banks, insurers and port operators that the EU is watching the trade route itself, not only Russian companies. Firms that deal with those named now carry a risk of being listed themselves.

## What happens now?

**India is unlikely to change course because of an EU list, but the pressure is coming from two sides.** A new US law, the Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, allows tariffs of up to 100% on countries that keep buying Russian energy, if the president chooses to use it. The MEA said on October 14 that India had "conveyed to the US side our concerns" about it. No Indian response to Thursday's EU listings had been issued by Friday evening.
