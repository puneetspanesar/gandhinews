## What are US chip export controls?

**They are Commerce Department rules that stop American companies selling the most powerful AI chips to certain buyers, above all in China.** Washington's aim is to slow China's development of military and frontier AI systems that run on those chips. The rules are enforced by licences, and by the threat of penalties for firms that sell around them.

The first big step came in **October 2022**, when the United States banned exports of Nvidia's A100 and H100 processors to China. Nvidia has since sold cut-down chips that fell under the limits, and the limits have been tightened and loosened more than once.

## What can Nvidia sell to China today?

**Its newest Blackwell chips are barred for China, but the older H200 was cleared for sale.** President Donald Trump allowed the H200 sale, a decision credited to lobbying by Jensen Huang. The H200 is roughly six times more powerful than the H20, the chip previously approved for China.

The United States has also made clear that the limits apply to Chinese-owned companies operating outside China, not only to shipments to the mainland. That matters for the Megaspeed case below.

## How does Megaspeed come into it?

**Megaspeed is a Singapore-based data-centre company whose purchases US authorities have been examining since 2024.** It provides computing infrastructure used to train and run AI systems. According to an analysis of trade data, its sister companies in Malaysia bought at least **$4.8 billion of Nvidia equipment, about 136,000 chips**, between June 2024 and February 2026.

US authorities suspect it helped China obtain advanced chips in breach of the restrictions. Megaspeed says inspections by the Commerce Department, Malaysia's trade ministry and Nvidia found no violations. Nvidia says it complies with export law.

## Why is this in the news now?

**Because of reports that Beijing recruited a Megaspeed executive to gather information about Nvidia's chief executive.** Late last month US intelligence officials were reported to believe that Alice Huang, who is not related to Jensen Huang, was working for China. Huang admitted an affair in a note to staff on Sunday, November 8, and Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation, saying it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. China's foreign ministry calls the spy narrative unsubstantiated.

Three House chairmen asked Huang last week to brief Congress behind closed doors on **November 18**, and to reply by November 11.

## Which bills are in Congress?

**Four measures are in play, and none has become law.** The House Foreign Affairs Committee advanced three earlier this year, and all are candidates to ride on the annual defence authorization bill for fiscal 2027.

- The **Chip Security Act** would make companies track their chips to deter smuggling. It passed the House committee unanimously.
- The **AI Overwatch Act** would give Congress a say over exports of advanced AI chips.
- The **Match Act** would push allies to restrict their sales of chipmaking equipment to China.
- The **Remote Access Security Act** would extend controls to cloud computing, stopping Chinese firms renting servers with advanced chips in third countries such as Malaysia. It passed the House in January and awaits the Senate.

Senators Elizabeth Warren and Jim Banks have urged Congress to pass the AI Overwatch Act through the defence bill. "It is time for Congress to take decisive action," Warren said in a joint statement.

## Who is for and against?

**Nvidia and its industry group oppose most of them; the White House has not spoken.** A former congressional staffer said Nvidia is the biggest opponent of the Chip Security and AI Overwatch acts, and the Information Technology Industry Council, whose members include Nvidia, opposes all three of the House-committee bills. Nvidia declined to comment. Chris MacKenzie of Americans for Responsible Innovation said the hurdle for the bills is "getting the White House onboard."

## Does this matter for India?

**Indirectly, through price and supply.** The reports on the bills do not say India is covered by them. Indian data-centre builders and AI companies, however, buy hardware from the same global supplier, so a tighter licensing regime, or a leadership change at the leading chipmaker, could affect supply and pricing there. As of November 10, 2026, none of the bills has passed both chambers, and the next fixed date is the House briefing on November 18.
