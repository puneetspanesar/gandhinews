**Small savings rates are unchanged again.** On September 30, 2026, the Finance Ministry's Department of Economic Affairs kept interest rates on every post office small savings scheme the same for the quarter from **October 1 to December 31, 2026**. These rates apply whether you open the account at a post office or at a bank that offers the schemes.

## What are the rates for October to December 2026?

**From 4% on a savings account to 8.2% on Sukanya Samriddhi and the senior citizens' scheme.**

## How do the main schemes work?

**Each has its own lock-in, limit and purpose.**

The Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme pays interest every quarter and the Monthly Income Scheme every month, which makes them popular with retired people who need a regular income. NSC compounds its interest every year and pays it all at maturity. Kisan Vikas Patra simply doubles your money: at 7.5%, a deposit doubles in **115 months, or nine years and seven months**.

## How are these schemes taxed?

**Some give a deduction, and PPF and Sukanya interest is tax-free.** Deposits in PPF, Sukanya Samriddhi, NSC, the Senior Citizens' scheme and the 5-year time deposit qualify for the investment deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakh a year, but only for taxpayers who choose the old tax regime. Interest on **PPF and Sukanya Samriddhi is tax-free**; interest on the others is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.

Since April 1, 2026, India's income tax has run under the new Income-tax Act, 2025, which renumbered the sections. The old-regime deduction remains available under the new law.

## How often do the rates change?

**They are reviewed every quarter.** The government announces the rates for each quarter before it begins. The rates have been held steady through 2025 and 2026. The last changes came in 2024, when the 3-year time deposit went up from 7% to 7.1% and Sukanya Samriddhi from 8% to 8.2%. The rates for **January to March 2027 are due to be announced by December 31, 2026**.

## Is the Mahila Samman Savings Certificate still available?

**No.** The two-year Mahila Samman Savings Certificate for women and girls, which accepted up to ₹2 lakh, was discontinued from April 1, 2025. Existing certificates run to maturity, but new ones cannot be bought.
