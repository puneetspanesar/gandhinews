## What is the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement?

**It is a mutual defence treaty between Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkiye, signed in Mecca on 7 August 2026.**

Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif signed it at Al-Safa Palace, at a gathering billed as the Makkah Al-Mukarramah Summit for Joint Defence. Their joint statement said an armed attack on any one of the three "shall be regarded as an attack against them all", and that the pact "provides for the enhancement of all aspects of defence cooperation".

It ties Saudi Arabia to nuclear-armed Pakistan and to Turkiye, a member of NATO. That combination is why some commentators have called it an "Islamic NATO", a label none of the three governments uses.

## What does it actually commit them to?

**Help in a war, but each country decides how much.**

According to a draft of the 16-article treaty, Article 1 commits the signatories to use "all necessary means" to remove threats to one another's territory. Article 2 says that if one is attacked, the others "shall immediately provide assistance to the party under attack". Each state, however, will judge for itself the scope and nature of that help, drawing on its own resources.

The remaining articles cover defence industry, intelligence sharing, logistics, joint training and counter-terrorism. A committee of the three countries' foreign and defence ministers will oversee the pact. New members can join only if all three founders agree. The treaty runs for 10 years, after which any member may leave. There is, so far, no public sign of a joint command, a common budget or a standing force.

## How is it different from the Saudi-Pakistan pact of 2025?

**It adds Turkiye and puts more detail on paper.**

On 17 September 2025 Mohammed bin Salman and Shehbaz Sharif signed the Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement at Al-Yamamah Palace in Riyadh. It said "any aggression against either of the two countries shall be considered as an aggression against both". That pact was short on specifics. The Mecca agreement builds on it, followed nearly a year of negotiation, and came after a regional war involving Iran that began in February 2026 exposed Gulf states to repeated missile and drone attacks.

## Why is it in the news now?

**Because it is being tested, and Turkiye is moving to make it law.**

Yemen's Houthis have been firing missiles and drones at Saudi oil sites and at Riyadh's main airport. Earlier this month the three partners agreed to activate "deterrence measures" under the pact and to send forces to the kingdom. On 19 October Pakistan's military spokesman, Lt Gen Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry, warned: "If you directly or indirectly wage this war against any of these three countries, then the response will be collective."

On 20 October Ankara sent the treaty to parliament. Ratification needs a simple majority, which Erdogan's coalition holds, though it would not by itself send Turkish troops anywhere: a deployment needs separate legislation. Burhanettin Duran, the Turkish presidency's communications director, said the partnership would "strengthen collective deterrence".

## Does it cover nuclear weapons?

**Nothing made public says so.** Saudi Arabia has said the pact is not a military axis or a sectarian bloc and has nothing to do with nuclear ambitions. Turkiye calls it "purely defensive" and not aimed at any country. Analysts who have studied the published material say it does not confirm any extension of Pakistan's nuclear deterrent to its partners. The full text is expected to become public only once Turkiye's parliamentary commission receives it.

## What does it mean for India?

**It gives Pakistan a formal military role in the security of a Gulf state India depends on.**

Saudi Arabia is home to about 2.74 million Indians, according to the Ministry of External Affairs. Two-way trade was $41.88 billion in 2024-25, and the kingdom supplied 13.58% of India's crude oil imports and 14.99% of its LPG imports that year. The two countries have had a Strategic Partnership Council since 2019, and their forces have held joint naval and land exercises.

New Delhi has kept its response measured. When the Riyadh pact was signed in 2025, MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said: "We will study the implications of this development for our national security as well as for regional and global stability." After the Mecca signing he said: "This is also a development that we are closely following, and we shall keep you updated."

## Could it pull Saudi Arabia or Turkiye into an India-Pakistan conflict?

**On paper the treaty names no enemy, and how it would work in such a crisis is untested.** Article 2 applies to an armed attack on any member, from any quarter. But the same article leaves each partner to decide what help to give, and Riyadh's commercial ties with India are deep. For now the pact's real test is in the Gulf, against the Houthis. How far Pakistan, a mediator between the US and Iran as well as Saudi Arabia's ally, can carry both roles is the question New Delhi will be watching most closely.
