**No dates have been announced yet.** As of November 10, 2026, the Election Commission of India has not issued a schedule for the five assembly elections due in early 2027: Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Goa and Manipur. Between them they elect **690 MLAs**, and one of them, Uttar Pradesh, sends more members to the Lok Sabha than any other state.

The calendar leaves little room. The assemblies of Manipur, Goa, Punjab and Uttarakhand complete their five-year terms in March 2027, and **Uttar Pradesh's term ends on May 22, 2027**. A new House has to be in place before each term runs out, which is why polling is widely expected in February and March.

## When will the Election Commission announce the dates?

**Most likely after December 15, 2026.** Senior Commission officials have been touring the five states since early October to check preparations, and the Commission plans to finish these visits by December 15. The schedule normally follows once the reviews are done.

For comparison, the last round of elections in the same five states was **announced on January 8, 2022**. Uttar Pradesh then voted in seven phases between February 10 and March 7, Punjab voted in a single phase on February 20, and all five states were counted together on March 10, 2022.

The law gives the Commission some flexibility. Under the Representation of the People Act, 1951, a general election to an assembly can be held at any time in the six months before its term ends, so the Commission can club states whose terms end weeks apart into one polling season, as it did in 2022.

## Which states vote, and how many seats does each have?

**Uttar Pradesh dwarfs the rest.** Its 403-member assembly is the largest in the country, and a party or alliance needs 202 seats to form a government. Punjab has 117 seats, Uttarakhand 70, Manipur 60 and Goa 40.

## Who governs each state now?

**The BJP runs four of the five, and the Aam Aadmi Party runs Punjab.** Yogi Adityanath has been Uttar Pradesh's Chief Minister since 2017 and won a second term in 2022, which was sworn in at Lucknow's Ekana Stadium on March 25, 2022. **Bhagwant Mann of AAP** has led Punjab since March 16, 2022, when he took oath at Khatkar Kalan in Shaheed Bhagat Singh Nagar district.

In Uttarakhand, Pushkar Singh Dhami leads a BJP government, and Pramod Sawant leads Goa's. Manipur's story is different. Its BJP government under N. Biren Singh resigned on February 9, 2025, amid ethnic violence between the Meitei and Kuki communities that has killed at least 260 people since May 2023. **President's rule was imposed on February 13, 2025**, and revoked on February 4, 2026, when **Y. Khemchand Singh** was sworn in as Chief Minister of a BJP-led NDA government.

## How did these states vote in 2022?

**Every ruling party won a clear majority last time, though some margins were thin.** In Uttar Pradesh the BJP won 255 seats on its own and 273 with its allies, while the Samajwadi Party rose to 111. In Punjab, AAP swept 92 of 117 seats, with the Congress at 18, the Shiromani Akali Dal at 3 and the BJP at 2.

Uttarakhand gave the BJP 47 of 70 seats and the Congress 19, making the BJP the first party to win back-to-back terms in the state, even though Dhami himself lost his own seat of Khatima by 6,579 votes. Goa was the tightest: **the BJP won 20 of 40 seats, one short of a majority**, and kept power with the support of independents. In Manipur, the BJP won 32 of 60 on its own for the first time.

## What is the Election Commission doing to prepare?

**Its officials are checking machines, rolls and security in every state.** Deputy election commissioners began field visits in October, starting with Goa and then Uttarakhand, rather than the full Commission travelling as it usually does before a schedule. First-level checks of electronic voting machines and VVPAT units are under way in all five states, with engineers from BEL and ECIL inspecting each machine in front of party representatives and on CCTV.

In Uttar Pradesh, Senior Deputy Election Commissioner Manish Garg chaired a review of 18 districts across four divisions at Meerut. According to a statement from the state's Chief Electoral Officer, Navdeep Rinwa, officials were told to **re-examine rejected Form 6 applications**, recheck all new entries made since the roll revision, run enrolment drives in Classes 11 and 12 and colleges, set up mobile phone deposit boxes at polling stations, and hand out voter information slips five days before polling.

## Why does the census matter for the poll dates?

**Because both have to be done in the same months.** The Registrar General of India has moved the door-to-door population count in Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Goa and the non-snowbound parts of Uttarakhand forward to **December 1, 2026, to January 4, 2027**, so that it is finished before the elections. Residents of these four states can fill in the census form themselves online from November 16 to 30, and a revisional round runs from January 5 to 9, 2027. The rest of the country is counted with a reference date of March 1, 2027.

## What are the big contests to watch?

**Each state has its own fight.** In Uttar Pradesh, the BJP seeks a third straight term for Yogi Adityanath against a Samajwadi Party that won 37 of the state's 80 Lok Sabha seats in 2024, three more than the BJP. The roll revision is itself an issue there: the electorate fell from 15.44 crore in October 2025 to 13.39 crore in the final roll of April 10, 2026, and opposition parties have protested what they call irregularities in the exercise.

In Punjab, AAP defends its 2022 landslide. Reports in late October said **talks on reviving the BJP and Akali Dal alliance**, which broke in September 2020 over the farm laws, were in their final stages, though neither party has announced a deal. In Goa, the Goa Forward Party has chosen to ally with AAP, while the Congress, the main opposition party, has held alliance talks of its own. In Uttarakhand the BJP will contest under Dhami, and in Manipur the first election since the violence will test whether normal campaigning is possible across the hills and the valley.

## Will Himachal Pradesh and Gujarat vote at the same time?

**No, they are due later in 2027.** Both states elected their current assemblies in late 2022, so their terms run to about the end of 2027 and they are expected to vote in November and December. The President's five-year term, which began on July 25, 2022, also ends in July 2027, making 2027 a crowded year for India's electoral calendar.
