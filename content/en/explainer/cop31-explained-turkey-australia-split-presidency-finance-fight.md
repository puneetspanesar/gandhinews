Once a year, almost every government in the world sits down to argue about the climate. This year's meeting, **COP31, opened on Monday, November 9, in Antalya**, and runs until November 20. The opening ceremony began an hour and a half late, and no reason was given, a fitting start to a conference that has already been arguing over its own agenda.

## What is a COP?

**It is the yearly meeting of the countries that signed the UN climate convention.** COP stands for Conference of the Parties. Decisions need consensus, so a single objecting bloc can hold up or dilute a text. What comes out is a set of decisions, plus a looser Action Agenda of voluntary pledges from companies, cities and governments.

COP31 is the 31st such meeting. UN Secretary-General António Guterres told delegates to "get serious" about cutting emissions, speeding the shift from fossil fuels and providing finance and protection for vulnerable countries. "We have entered a new age of climate extremes," he said.

## Why two countries run it

**Turkey and Australia both wanted to host.** UN rules require global consensus on a host, and without agreement the conference would have defaulted to Bonn, Germany, where the UN climate secretariat is based. Neither side withdrew during COP30 in Belém. After a week of talks in November 2025 they compromised: Turkey would host, and Australia's Chris Bowen would preside over the negotiations. Australia had planned to host in Adelaide.

The split matters for how the two weeks work. Turkey's **Murat Kurum**, its Minister of Environment, Urbanization and Climate Change, is the official president and leads the Action Agenda, which has ten themes from zero waste to resilient health systems. These run alongside the talks and produce no negotiated outcome. Bowen has what one legal briefing calls "exclusive authority" over the negotiations, including drafting the texts that countries adopt.

## What is being negotiated

**Four files carry the most weight.** The first is how to make the Belém Action Mechanism on a just transition work, with some 25 possible functions still to be narrowed. The second is whether the Mitigation Work Programme, which deals with emission cuts, continues beyond 2026. The European Union and island states want more substance, while the group of Like-Minded Developing Countries opposes sectoral targets.

The third is science: countries are split on whether the next major IPCC assessment can be ready in time for the second Global Stocktake of progress, and the IPCC suspended a plenary in October without agreement. The fourth is how to measure adaptation, and whether the indicators should be set by countries or by experts.

Turkey's own centrepiece is an electrification drive to lift electricity's share of global energy demand from about 20% to **35% by 2035**. The EU wants the word "clean" added, which could reopen the fight over fossil fuels. COP28 pledged a transition away from them, but COP30 ended without an explicit mention.

## The money fight

**Finance split the conference before it began.** Negotiators were still arguing late on Sunday over whether developing countries' finance demands would be on the official agenda. African countries want a stand-alone item on tripling adaptation finance, which pays for measures such as sea walls and drought-resistant crops. A source said a climate finance work programme would be on the agenda but tripling would be discussed under other items. A senior EU official warned last week that there would be "no political will" for new funding pledges.

The promise made at Belém was to triple adaptation finance by 2035, with no baseline, no precise target and no named source of money. One common reading, tripling an earlier goal of $40 billion, would mean about $120 billion a year, but delivery methods are disputed. The wider goal of $1.3 trillion a year by 2035 is tracked through the Baku to Belém Roadmap, and a first progress update is expected here.

## What India wants

Environment Minister **Bhupender Yadav** said on November 3 that India would work with the G77 and China and with the BASIC group. Its first priority is a decision **launching a Climate Finance Work Programme**. Yadav said finance is a developed-country obligation under Article 9.1 of the Paris framework and "must be predictable, largely public and concessional", and must not add to developing countries' debt. India also wants adaptation indicators to be voluntary and not tied to access to finance, and opposes new top-down targets.

## What to watch

World leaders arrive for a summit on **November 11 and 12**, on days three and four, later than at earlier COPs. The United States is not sending administration officials, though a congressional delegation will attend. The conference is due to close on November 20 with its cover decision.
