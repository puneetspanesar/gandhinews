When Russian, Ukrainian and US negotiators met in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11, 2026, one subject kept surfacing. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia's delegation claimed its forces already control **about half of the Donbas land Ukraine still holds**, and that his team rejected the claim. Here is why one region in the east of the country matters so much.

## What is the Donbas?

**It is Ukraine's old industrial heartland, the provinces of Donetsk and Luhansk.** The name is short for Donets Coal Basin. Before 2014 its coal mines, steel mills and chemical plants made up a large share of the Ukrainian economy, and more than six million people lived there, many of them Russian speakers. Most of the industry in the areas Russia holds has since stopped or been taken over.

## How did the fighting start?

**In 2014, when Russia-backed armed groups seized government buildings in Donetsk and Luhansk** and declared "people's republics". Ukraine sent its army. The Minsk agreements of 2014 and 2015 reduced the fighting but never ended it, and each side accused the other of breaking them. On February 21, 2022, Russia recognised the two "republics", and three days later launched its full-scale invasion. On September 30, 2022, Moscow declared Donetsk, Luhansk and two other regions to be part of Russia, a claim Ukraine and most UN members reject.

## How much does each side hold now?

**Russia holds almost all of Luhansk and about four-fifths of Donetsk**, by one estimate. Measured against the DeepState map of October 30, 2026, the figures are about 98.7% of Luhansk and 79.7% of Donetsk. Russia took Bakhmut in May 2023 and Pokrovsk in early 2026, though its advance has slowed sharply since.

The two sides give very different numbers for what remains. In mid-October, Putin said Ukraine held only about 10% of Donetsk and that Russian forces had taken some 6,500 square kilometres and 310 settlements since January. On November 10, Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha called that false and said Ukraine controls **20% of the territory**, a figure in line with the DeepState estimate. Ukraine reports it has freed 277 square kilometres and 16 settlements.

## What is left in Ukrainian hands?

**A belt of fortified cities.** It is built around Sloviansk and Kramatorsk, with Druzhkivka and Kostiantynivka nearby, where fighting was heavy in October. Since 2014 Ukraine has dug trenches, laid minefields and built layered defences there, and the terrain has forced Russia into slow, costly attacks. Ukraine's army sees the belt as the shield of its east. Giving it up, Kyiv argues, would hand Russia fortified ground and a launch pad for the next offensive.

## Why does it matter at the table?

**Because Russia's central demand is that Ukraine withdraw from land it has not captured.** Putin wants Ukraine out of the four regions Moscow claimed in 2022, Donetsk first among them. Kyiv wants the front line frozen where it is, and Zelenskyy says only a meeting of the two presidents can settle land. A US official was reported to have floated each side ceding a little ground, which a senior Ukrainian official denied.

That is why the two accounts of the front matter. **A territorial deal cannot be drawn until both sides agree who holds what.** Zelenskyy said Putin told the Americans Russia could take the whole east within one to three months, which he called untrue, and that the US had backed Ukraine's reading of the battlefield.

The land is not the only demand. Putin also wants Ukraine to drop its NATO bid, accept limits on its army and ease curbs on the Russian language and the Moscow-linked Orthodox Church, and Moscow wants a wider European security framework settled first. Kyiv and its European partners say the war must end before that. US envoy Jared Kushner said on November 10 that the sides had "narrowed the gaps", but Russia still rules out a ceasefire and Ukraine still refuses to cede territory. Three earlier rounds this year produced no major breakthrough.

## What does it mean for India?

India has no seat at the table, but a settlement would reach it through prices. It could let Russia sell more crude and reopen Black Sea trade, which feed into fuel and edible oil costs. Zelenskyy said after speaking to Prime Minister Narendra Modi on November 9 that India is ready to help with food corridors, which Delhi has not confirmed.

## What happens next

No outcome of the second day in Abu Dhabi had been announced when this was written. Details of a large prisoner exchange are promised in the coming days, Kyiv will press for a leaders' meeting, and Moscow has not answered the Donbas figures. No date is set for another round.
