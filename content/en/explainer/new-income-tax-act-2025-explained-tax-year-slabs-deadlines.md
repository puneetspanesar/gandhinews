**India's income tax now runs under a new law.** On April 1, 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961, which had governed direct taxes for more than six decades. For most taxpayers the change is about language, structure and deadlines rather than how much tax they pay: **the slabs and rates did not change**.

## What is the new Income-tax Act, and why was it needed?

**A rewrite of the 1961 law in simpler form.** Parliament passed the Income-tax Bill, 2025 on August 12, 2025, and it received the President's assent on August 21, 2025. The new Act reduces the text and the number of sections by about half, removes provisions linked to taxes that no longer exist, and cuts down the cross-references that made the old law hard to read. The government's stated aims are a clearer law and fewer disputes.

## What is a "tax year"?

**A single name for the year in which you earn and are taxed.** Under the old law, income earned in the "previous year" was assessed in the following "assessment year", so income from April 2025 to March 2026 was "financial year 2025-26, assessment year 2026-27". The new Act drops that pairing. The year from April 1, 2026, to March 31, 2027, is simply **tax year 2026-27**.

## Did my tax slabs change?

**No.** Rates and slabs are set every year by the Finance Act that accompanies the Budget, not by the Income-tax Act. The Budget presented on February 1, 2026, left them unchanged. So the new regime still works as the 2025 Budget set it:

Because of the rebate, **income up to ₹12 lakh pays no tax** under the new regime. A salaried person also gets a standard deduction of ₹75,000, so gross salary up to ₹12.75 lakh is effectively tax-free. When the slabs were introduced, the Finance Minister said a person earning ₹12 lakh would save ₹80,000 a year, someone on ₹18 lakh ₹70,000, and someone on ₹25 lakh ₹1.10 lakh. The old regime, with its deductions and exemptions, remains available for those who choose it.

## What are the new filing deadlines?

**Staggered by type of taxpayer.** From tax year 2026-27, returns are due on:

The window to **file a revised return has grown from 9 months to 12**. A revision filed after nine months attracts a fee of ₹1,000 if income is up to ₹5 lakh and ₹5,000 above that.

## What else changed on April 1, 2026?

**A set of smaller changes, several of them taxpayer-friendly.** Tax collected at source on money sent abroad for education or medical treatment above ₹10 lakh fell from 5% to 2%. Interest on motor accident compensation is now tax-free. Employer-provided travel between home and office is no longer a taxable perk. Taxpayers can claim TDS refunds in returns filed after the due date without penal charges for the claim.

On the other side, the Securities Transaction Tax went up on certain derivative trades, income from share buybacks is taxed as capital gains, and the tax benefits on Sovereign Gold Bonds apply only to bonds bought at the original issue price.

## What should taxpayers do now?

**Mostly, carry on, but learn the new labels.** Your tax for 2026-27 is calculated as before, under the regime you choose. What changes is the vocabulary on forms and notices, the numbering of sections you may see quoted, and the due dates for your return. When she presented the Budget, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said the simplified rules and forms under the new Act would be notified with enough time for taxpayers to adjust, and the first returns under the new law, for tax year 2026-27, fall due from July 31, 2027.
