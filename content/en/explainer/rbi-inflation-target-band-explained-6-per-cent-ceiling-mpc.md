India's retail inflation was **6.09% in October**, above the Reserve Bank of India's upper limit of 6% for the first time in two years. The number made headlines, but it does not mean the central bank has legally failed at its job. The law judges it on a longer run.

## What is the RBI actually asked to do?

**Keep retail inflation at 4%, give or take 2 percentage points.** Since 2016 the Reserve Bank Act has made price stability the central task of monetary policy, and the target is set by the Centre, in consultation with the RBI, once every five years. According to a report of the notification, the Centre kept the target at **4%, with a band of 2% to 6%**, for the period from April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2031.

The measure is the consumer price index, the same retail figure published by the statistics ministry every month. The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee, chaired by the governor, votes on the repo rate every two months to steer inflation back towards 4%.

## Is 6.09% a breach?

**Not by the legal test.** The rule is not about a single month. Under the 2016 notification, as the Economic Survey archive summarises it, the RBI has failed if **average inflation stays above the upper level, or below the lower level, for three consecutive quarters**. A quarter is three months, and its inflation is the average of those months.

On that test October is only the first data point. The monthly readings for July, August and September were 4.45%, 4.83% and 5.54%, which average about 4.94%, so the July to September quarter stayed inside the band. The October to December quarter is the first that could cross 6%: the credit-rating agency ICRA expects an average of about 6.2% for those three months, and the November reading is due on December 14.

## What happens after three quarters?

**The RBI must write to the government.** Section 45ZN of the Act requires the bank, when it fails to meet the target, to send the Centre a report with three things: the reasons for the failure, the remedial actions it proposes, and an estimate of the time in which the target will be achieved if those actions are taken on time.

The report carries no penalty. It is an accountability step: the central bank explains itself to the government that set its target.

## Has it happened before?

**Once, in 2022.** Average inflation was 6.3% in January to March 2022, 7.3% in April to June and 7% in July to September, according to reports, so the three quarters ran above 6% back to back. It was the first time since the framework began in 2016 that the RBI had to send such a report. In a written reply in the Lok Sabha, the government said the letter would not be made public, as the Act and the committee's regulations do not require it.

## Where does that leave the RBI now?

**It has already started tightening.** On October 7 the committee voted unanimously to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%, and it projected inflation at 6% for October to December. October's reading matched that projection. Economists quoted after the data expect another increase at the December meeting and see inflation staying close to 6% for two more months, driven mainly by food: onions cost more than twice what they did a year ago.

Three things now decide whether the legal test is ever met. Food prices, which a weak and uneven monsoon has kept high, would have to stay stubborn through the winter vegetable season. Crude oil prices, which feed into transport and fuel costs, would have to remain high. And the base effect, which ICRA calls unfavourable, would have to keep pushing the yearly comparison up.

## What it means for borrowers and savers

**Rates stay high for longer.** A higher repo rate raises the cost of floating-rate home, car and business loans as banks pass it on, and lifts what new fixed deposits pay. The cost of living meanwhile rises faster than the headline suggests for families who spend more on food, since food inflation (8.94%) is well above the overall rate.

The next dates to watch are December 14, when November's inflation is published, and the December policy meeting. If the average for October to December comes in above 6%, the RBI will have one quarter on the board. It would take two more, through the first half of 2027, before Section 45ZN is triggered.
