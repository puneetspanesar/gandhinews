When India and Japan hold their **fourth 2+2 in Tokyo today**, nothing about the format will look dramatic: two foreign ministers, two defence ministers, a long table and a joint statement at the end. The reason it matters is the table itself. The people who decide what a navy does and the people who decide what a country says are in the same room.

## What is a 2+2?

**A 2+2 is a meeting of two countries' foreign and defence ministers together.** Each side brings both of its senior ministers, so a decision to hold an exercise, share a technology or back a position on a sea lane can be taken once, rather than passed between two ministries.

India holds the format with only a few partners: **the United States, Japan, Australia and Russia**. Japan is the one with the steadiest rhythm. Here the Indian side is Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar. Japan fields Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi.

## What have the earlier rounds produced?

The first India-Japan 2+2 was held in New Delhi on **November 30, 2019**. The second was in Tokyo on **September 8, 2022**, and the third in New Delhi on August 20, 2024.

Japan's own record of the 2022 round shows what the format yields. The ministers agreed to start **Joint Service Staff Talks** between Japan's Joint Staff and India's Integrated Defence Staff. They committed to keep the Dharma Guardian, JIMEX and Malabar exercises going, welcomed progress in coast guard cooperation and cybersecurity, and opposed changing the status quo by force anywhere. The results are small steps, but each one creates a standing channel that the next round can build on.

## What did the August defence talks already settle?

The 2+2 does not start from a blank page. On **August 20, 2026**, Singh and Koizumi met in New Delhi and signed a **Memorandum of Arrangement on Maritime Security Cooperation** between their ministries. It covers sharing of maritime domain awareness information, search and rescue, disaster relief, and protection of sea lanes through ship visits, joint exercises and logistical support, including use of ports and repair facilities.

The statement also set out a list of concrete projects:

- **Equipment:** the shipborne **UNICORN** communications antenna system is the first defence equipment transfer project between the two countries. Both sides want it realised early.
- **Ships:** they will explore joint naval shipbuilding and design, joining Japanese technology to Indian production capacity, and expect a ship repair arrangement "in the near future". They also agreed to work on mine countermeasures.
- **Exercises:** Japanese Air Self-Defense Force fighters will join the air exercise Veer Guardian 26 in India for the first time. The two sides want more complex exercises, with unmanned systems and short-notice drills.
- **Structure:** a working group of director-general and joint-secretary rank to coordinate operations, intelligence, equipment and industry, and an intended Defence Industry Forum.

Singh also welcomed Japan's recent review of its rules for transferring defence equipment and technology, which is what makes projects such as UNICORN possible.

## What is different this time?

**The foreign ministers are in the room.** Jaishankar will also hold the 19th Foreign Ministers' Strategic Dialogue with Motegi, and the two sides will review understandings from the leaders' summits of August 2025 and July 2026. That lets the talks tie defence projects to economic security, energy and technology, which Jaishankar has named as priorities.

The regional backdrop is also busy. Jaishankar came to Tokyo straight from Manila, where he accompanied Prime Minister Narendra Modi to the ASEAN, East Asia and Quad meetings. The Quad leaders there agreed a joint P-8 and P-3C maritime patrol aircraft exercise for 2027, and the same maritime-awareness theme runs through the India-Japan agenda. Japan and India also held their first trilateral naval passage exercise with Indonesia in February.

## What would count as progress?

The Ministry of Defence's release speaks of "a roadmap for enhanced operational and industrial synergy". It names no project to be signed, so **dates and named programmes** are the test. Four things will show whether the roadmap has substance:

1. A firm timetable for **UNICORN** and a signed **ship repair arrangement**.
2. A named **shipbuilding or mine countermeasure** project, not just an intention to explore one.
3. Dates for larger **tri-service exercises**, including the first Japanese fighter visit.
4. The first meeting of the new **working group** and the Defence Industry Forum.

A joint statement that only repeats a "free, open, inclusive and rules-based Indo-Pacific" would be the weaker outcome. One that attaches dates to the August list would show a partnership moving from agreed principles to shared hardware.
