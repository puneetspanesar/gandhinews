## What is Naxalism?

**It is an armed Maoist insurgency, named after the village of Naxalbari in West Bengal where a peasant uprising began in the late 1960s.** The government calls it Left Wing Extremism, or LWE. Its followers want to overthrow the state through armed struggle and have drawn most support in forest and tribal belts, where roads, schools and clinics were thin.

The movement spread through the 1980s to Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha, and in 2004 its main groups merged to form the CPI (Maoist). At its widest, the area it influenced was called the **Red Corridor, covering 12 states**, in Shah's words to the Lok Sabha.

## What did the Centre say in March?

**That affected districts fell from 126 in 2014 to two.** Replying to a debate in the Lok Sabha on March 30, 2026, a day before the ministry's own March 31 deadline, Home Minister Amit Shah said Naxalism had nearly ended in Bastar and that the Maoists' top structure had been "almost completely dismantled".

He put the human cost at **more than 20,000 lives**, including about 5,000 security personnel. For the period 2024 to March 2026 he gave these figures: 706 Naxalites killed in encounters, 2,218 detained and 4,839 surrendered.

## How does a district get counted as 'affected'?

**By a classification the Home Ministry reviews and revises.** Districts are sorted into bands according to the level of Maoist violence and presence. A regional report of the ministry's latest review says that from March 27, 2026 only **Bijapur in Chhattisgarh and West Singhbhum in Jharkhand** stay in the top "LWE affected" category, Kanker in Chhattisgarh is a "district of concern", and 35 districts in nine states fall into a lower "Legacy and Thrust" group that still gets development funds and watchfulness. We have not seen the ministry's document itself.

This is why the word 'free' needs care. A district can leave the affected list because incidents have stopped, while a few armed cadres, weapons or sympathisers may remain in the same area. Other speakers in the March debate gave different district tallies for the past, such as 200 districts falling to three, and Modi on Sunday spoke of about 230 districts in 20 states, so earlier counts depend on the definition used.

## What did Modi claim on Diwali?

**That the country has won its fight and that every district is free.** Speaking to CRPF, CoBRA and police personnel at Karanpur in Bastar on November 8, the Prime Minister said no CRPF jawan had died on duty in the past year, **the first such year in 47 years**, and that more than 1,000 security personnel had died in Chhattisgarh alone. He listed more than 15,000 km of roads and over 9,500 mobile towers in the affected areas, all as his own figures.

His "every district" claim goes further than the two-district figure Shah gave seven months earlier, and no release explains the difference. The ministry's April review, as reported, puts the road network built at about 12,000 km of a planned 17,579 km, which sits below Modi's number.

## Why is it also a political argument?

**Because Modi also said that 'intellectual' or 'dimagi' Naxals now sit in political parties.** Congress general secretary Jairam Ramesh called this "a brazen assault on all institutional norms". Shah has also alleged links between Maoist-front groups and the Congress leader Rahul Gandhi, which Congress has previously denied. The security claim and the political charge are separate questions, and the first can be tested by the data on incidents and deaths while the second cannot.

## What is still unsettled?

**Whether 'no district affected' means 'no armed group left'.** Three things will show it over the coming months: whether incident and death counts stay at or near zero through a full year, whether the 35 Legacy and Thrust districts are moved out of that list, and how many of the surrendered cadres are rehabilitated. The Home Ministry has not yet published a fresh, district-by-district list that matches the Prime Minister's wording.

Until then, the safest reading is this: the government's own March figure of **two affected districts** is the documented claim, the Diwali statement is the Prime Minister's description of the same trend, and the villages of Bastar will judge the rest by whether the roads, towers and schools stay in use after the troops thin out.
