## How do New Zealanders vote?

**Every voter gets two votes: one for a local MP and one for a party.** The local vote chooses the winner of each electorate seat, seven of them reserved for Māori roll voters. The party vote is the one that counts for the size of the House. It decides how many of the **120 seats** each party is entitled to, so a party with 29% of the party vote should end up with about 29% of the seats.

Parties fill their share first with the local winners they have won, and then with MPs from their ranked party lists. This is Mixed Member Proportional, or MMP, representation, and it has been used since 1996.

## Why have National and Labour tied?

**Because the two big parties are almost level on the party vote.** On the preliminary count Labour has 710,330 party votes and National 706,680, or **29.4% against 29.2%**. On election night Prime Minister Christopher Luxon said it "looks like an MMP special" and that it was "too close to call". Labour leader Chris Hipkins said it was clear "we're not going to know the exact shape of the next government".

## What is an overhang seat?

**It is an extra seat added when a party wins more local seats than its party vote entitles it to.** The party keeps every local seat it wins, and the House grows to make room. Nobody is removed.

This time the effect is large. National won **42 electorates**, though its 29.2% of the party vote entitles it to 35 seats, an overhang of seven. Te Pāti Māori won five Māori electorates on 0.57% of the party vote, which entitles it to one seat, an overhang of four. Together that is **11 overhang seats, the most under MMP**. Overhangs have occurred in five of the ten MMP elections, usually adding one or two seats, and the previous record House was 123 MPs in 2023.

## Why does the size of Parliament matter?

**A majority is half the House plus one, so extra seats raise the bar.** In a 120-seat House it is 61. With the overhang, sources put the new House at 130 or 131 members, depending on how many seats Opportunity ends up with, and the **majority mark at 66** either way.

The overhang has also shifted the balance. On a strict reading of the party vote, the 120 seats would split 60 to 60 between the right and the rest. With the extra seats, National, ACT and NZ First have **67 seats against 64** for Labour, the Greens, Te Pāti Māori and Opportunity.

## What is the 5% threshold, and who is the kingmaker?

**A party with less than 5% of the party vote and no local seat gets nothing.** Opportunity, founded in 2017, cleared it with about 5.96% and is set to enter Parliament for the first time, on six seats on one count and seven on another. Its leader Qiulae Wong says she has "both the Chrises' numbers" and will be reaching out. Luxon has ruled out working with the party, while Hipkins is open to talks but opposes its new tax. If the right bloc holds 67 seats it does not need Opportunity, but a slip of two seats could change that.

## What are special votes, and what is still to count?

**They are votes cast outside the voter's own electorate, overseas, or by people enrolling late, and they are counted after election day.** The Electoral Commission estimates about **399,365 special votes, around 14% of the total**. They arrive last and are checked before counting, which is why the official result is not due until **November 27**.

They tend to help the left. They usually add one or two seats to left-leaning parties, and in 2023 Te Pāti Māori went from four seats on election night to six in the final count. Some local contests are also close: on the ordinary count National leads Wairarapa by 139 votes and Nelson by 1,210, while Chris Bishop trails Labour's Ginny Andersen in Hutt South by 889.

## What happens next?

**Nothing is decided until November 27, and talks can run longer.** The sitting government stays in office under caretaker conventions in the meantime. The last two rounds of coalition talks took about four weeks in 2020 and around 40 days in 2023, and this one could stretch close to Christmas. The figures above are from the preliminary count and will change when the special votes are added.
