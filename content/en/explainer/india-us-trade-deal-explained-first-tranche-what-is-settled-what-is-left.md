## What is being negotiated?

**A bilateral trade agreement, to be signed in stages.** Talks began in early 2025. India and the United States announced a framework in early February 2026, under which Washington would cut its reciprocal tariff on Indian goods from 25% to 18% and drop an extra 25% duty tied to India's purchases of Russian oil. India said it would reduce or remove tariffs on selected American industrial and farm goods, with tree nuts, fruit, soybean oil, wine and spirits among the items named in reports.

The framework never took effect as written. On February 20 the US Supreme Court struck down the emergency-powers tariffs, and Washington moved to other legal tools. The deal is now being finished in tranches.


## What does the first tranche cover?

**Trade in goods only.** Services, which matter to India's IT and professional sectors, are left for a second tranche. Talks on the first tranche and the wider agreement run side by side. The Commerce Minister, Piyush Goyal, said in October that the first tranche was finalised and in legal drafting, and would be put into operation once India gets what he called **a comparable or competitive advantage over peer countries**.

Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal has said the deal can be finalised only after the tariff structure is settled. A small US team visited India in the first week of November, and discussions reportedly cleared several issues in the customs and trade facilitation chapter. No more in-person rounds are planned for now.

## What did Jaishankar say on Saturday?

**That the two countries are in the last stretch.** External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar told a leadership summit in New Delhi on November 14 that most issues, including the tariff lines, had been resolved. He added that the remaining changes were "more at the American end than our end", and that he hoped the deal could be closed out soon. He gave no date.

## What is still holding it up?

**The final tariff rate, which depends on an American investigation.** India wants its tariff to be lower than those paid by competitors such as Vietnam and others in its neighbourhood and in South-East Asia. A senior Indian official said on Saturday that the US has assured India of a comparative tariff advantage, but the assurance depends on a second Section 301 investigation, into excess manufacturing capacity, that is not finished.

Until the second probe ends, nobody knows what other economies will pay, so no one can say whether India's rate is an advantage. Dairy access is another reported sticking point, because American producers want entry to a market India has long guarded for its farmers. The sources give no details of what is on offer.

## Why did relations sour?

**Several issues stacked up.** Washington imposed punitive tariffs of up to 50% on most Indian goods in August 2025, tied partly to India's Russian crude. Other irritants included American remarks on the May 2025 India-Pakistan clashes, a higher H-1B visa fee and changes to green-card processing that affected firms such as TCS and Wipro. The Russia sanctions law signed on September 18 allows tariffs of up to 100% on the biggest buyers of Russian oil, though President Donald Trump set aside its penalties on October 16.

## Why does it matter to India?

**The US is India's largest export market.** Indian goods exports to the US in the first seven months of 2026-27 reached $59.52 billion, up 14.48% from $51.99 billion a year earlier, so exporters are already selling more despite the uncertainty. A clear, lower tariff would give Indian exporters the certainty they need to plan, while a higher one would hand business to rivals.

## What happens next?

India aims to close the first tranche by the end of the financial year on March 31, 2027. Reports point to a possible announcement around the G20 summit in Miami on December 14 and 15, if Prime Minister Narendra Modi and President Trump meet on its sidelines, but that is speculation and the Ministry of External Affairs has not confirmed the visit.

What to watch for is the result of the excess capacity investigation. Once the American tariff structure is known, the first tranche can be signed, and Delhi and Washington can turn to services in the second.
