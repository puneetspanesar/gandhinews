**The biggest unanswered question in Punjab's 2027 election is whether the Akali Dal and the BJP will fight it together.** For nearly 24 years they were partners, with the Akali Dal as the senior ally. They split in September 2020, and by the 2022 assembly election both had been reduced to a handful of seats. Now, with the vote due by March 2027, both parties are signalling that a reunion is possible, and neither has ruled it out.

## How long were they allies, and how did it work?

**From the 1990s until 2020, with the Akali Dal contesting most seats.** The two parties fought five Punjab assembly elections together, from 1997 to 2017, and six Lok Sabha elections, from 1998 to 2019. In the assembly the arrangement was lopsided: in its last version, in 2017, **the BJP contested 23 of the 117 seats**, mostly urban ones, and left the remaining 94 to the Akali Dal.

Its best result came in 1997, when the Akali Dal won 75 seats and the BJP 18. By 2017 voters had turned against it, and the two parties won only 15 and 3 seats.

## Why did the alliance break?

**Over the Centre's three farm laws.** The Akali Dal opposed the laws, which farmers in Punjab protested against through 2020. Harsimrat Kaur Badal resigned from the Union Cabinet, and in September 2020 the party left the National Democratic Alliance. The laws were repealed in 2021, but by then the parties had gone their separate ways.

## What happened when they fought apart?

**Both were routed in 2022, then both recovered some ground in 2024.** In the 2022 assembly election the Akali Dal won 3 seats with 18.38% of the vote, its worst result, and the BJP won 2 with 6.6%. AAP swept the state with 92 seats.

The 2024 Lok Sabha election told a different story. The BJP polled 18.56% and the Akali Dal 13.41%. **Together that is 31.97%**, about 43.25 lakh votes, more than the Congress (26.31%, about 35.60 lakh votes) or AAP (26.03%, about 35.22 lakh). Lok Sabha votes do not carry over directly into an assembly election, but these are the figures both parties' strategists are working from.

## Where do the talks stand now?

**Reported to be close, but not announced.** On August 7, 2026, Akali Dal president Sukhbir Singh Badal met Prime Minister Narendra Modi at Parliament House, and the party then backed the Centre's delimitation bill. The state BJP's public line has stayed the same.

> We have already stated that we will contest all 117 Assembly seats on our own.
>
> Source: Kewal Singh Dhillon, Punjab BJP president, August 2026

On October 22, Union Home Minister Amit Shah said the NDA would form Punjab's next government. The next day Badal responded with an open door.

> Any party that agrees with the ideology and programme of the Shiromani Akali Dal is welcome. We have kept our alliance options open.
>
> Source: Sukhbir Singh Badal, president, Shiromani Akali Dal, October 23, 2026

Reports at the end of October, citing unnamed sources, said talks were in their final stages and an announcement could come after Diwali.

## What is the sticking point?

**Who gets how many seats.** Under the old deal the BJP was the junior partner with 23 seats. According to the October reports, the BJP now wants between 40 and 47 seats, pointing to its higher vote share in 2024, and a compromise of about 36 or 37 seats was seen as likely. The BJP is said to want in particular the seats where it outpolled the Akali Dal in the 2024 Lok Sabha vote, and has urged the Akali Dal to bring back breakaway groups and leaders who left.

## What would a reunion mean for AAP and the Congress?

**A three-cornered fight instead of a four-cornered one.** In 2022 AAP's 42% of the vote turned into 92 seats because the opposition vote was split between the Congress, the Akali Dal and the BJP. A joint BJP and Akali Dal ticket would consolidate part of that vote. Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann has dismissed Amit Shah's prediction of an NDA government.

If the parties fight separately again, the multi-cornered contest would split anti-incumbency and Sikh votes several ways, which analysts say could help AAP or the Congress. **Either way, the decision will shape the 2027 result** more than almost anything else that happens before polling day.
