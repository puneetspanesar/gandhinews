## What was Naxalism?

**It was an armed Maoist insurgency against the Indian state**, named after Naxalbari in West Bengal, where it began. The Home Ministry dates the armed rebellion to the early 1970s, when 1971 saw a peak of 3,620 violent incidents. In the 1980s the People's War Group spread it across nine states, and the CPI (Maoist) was formed in 2004.

Its heartland was the so-called **Red Corridor**, which Amit Shah described in the Lok Sabha on March 30 as a belt across 12 states "where the rule of law ended." The ministry said it once covered about 17% of the country's territory and 12 crore people. Shah put the death toll from left-wing violence at 20,000.

## How was it counted?

**By districts the Home Ministry called "LWE-affected".** The yardstick matters because the numbers quoted differ. Modi said on Sunday that at its height Naxalism touched **more than 230 districts in nearly 20 states**. The ministry's own list of affected districts stood at 126 in 2014, and Shah said it had fallen to 18 by September 2025, with the most-affected districts down from 36 to six.

## What changed on the ground?

**Force and roads, in that order.** The government built a net of fortified police stations, 596 of them since 2014 by Shah's count, and set up new Central Armed Police Force camps deep in forest areas, 336 in the six years to September 2025. Operations in the last two years were heavy. Between 2024 and March 2026, Shah said, **706 Naxalites were killed in encounters, 2,218 were arrested and 4,839 surrendered**.

The leadership was hit hardest. Shah told the Lok Sabha that 12 central committee members had been killed, with one absconding and talks on his surrender under way, and that no State Military Commission member remained intact anywhere. The reports of the speech do not name them.

The government paired this with a surrender offer. Shah has said those who lay down arms get a "red carpet," that there would be no ceasefire for those who do not, and that **a bullet is answered with a bullet**. On the development side, the ministry says 12,000 km of roads were built in affected states between 2014 and 2024, along with 5,000 mobile towers and more than a thousand bank branches.

## Is it really over?

**The ministry says no district is now LWE-affected, but it has not stopped watching.** Officials told reporters that a security review completed after March 31 found none left, and that West Singhbhum in Jharkhand is the only "district of concern." A separate list of "legacy and thrust" districts, including Bastar, Bijapur, Sukma and Dantewada in Chhattisgarh, stays under focus, according to the same account. That list was not available to us in the ministry's own words, so treat it as officials' description.

Shah himself spoke on March 30 of formal declaration once "final formalities" were done, and Modi on Sunday said the government declared the country free of Naxalism on March 31. Both are statements by the government, and independent counts of violence have not been published alongside them.

## What does the Prime Minister mean by "dimagi Naxals"?

**He means supporters of the ideology who carry no weapons.** At Karanpur Modi said armed Maoists have left the forests but "dimagi Naxals" have entered political parties and organisations and work against the Constitution and democracy. He gave no names. The remark turns the next phase into an argument about politics, not a security operation.

He also claimed that no CRPF jawan died in the line of duty over the past year, the first time in 47 years. For Bastar, the test now is whether roads, schools and clinics keep arriving.

## What to watch next

Three things decide whether "zero districts" lasts. The first is **surrender and rehabilitation**: thousands of former cadres need work, and a failure to provide it can push some back. The second is the **security camps** built deep in the forest, which in time must give way to civil administration. The third is the **list of former strongholds**: whether the ministry publishes the criteria for dropping a district, so that the claim can be tested, and not only announced.
