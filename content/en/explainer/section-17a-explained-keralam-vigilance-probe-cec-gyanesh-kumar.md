Keralam's Home Minister Ramesh Chennithala said on Wednesday, November 11, that the Vigilance inquiry into Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar is going on. The case rests on an allegation from about 2006, when Kumar was the state's Public Works Department secretary, and it could start only after the state government **granted sanction under Section 17A** of the Prevention of Corruption Act on November 3. This explainer sets out what that section is, and what shields a CEC.

## What does Section 17A say?

**It makes the government say yes before an inquiry into a public servant begins.** The provision was added to the 1988 anti-corruption law in 2018. It says no police officer may conduct an enquiry, inquiry or investigation into an offence under the Act if it relates to **a recommendation or decision the servant made in official duty**, unless the right authority has first approved.

Who approves depends on who employed the person when the alleged offence took place. For someone in the affairs of the Union, it is the Central government. For someone in the affairs of a state, it is the state government. For anyone else, it is the authority that could remove them. The authority must decide within three months, which it may extend by one month in writing with reasons. There is one exception: no approval is needed for an arrest on the spot for taking or attempting to take a bribe.

## Why was it Keralam's call?

**Because Kumar was a state officer in 2006.** The rule looks at his job when the alleged act took place, not the post he holds now. As PWD secretary he served in the affairs of Keralam, so the approval had to come from the state government, which is led by the Congress. Chennithala said on November 4 that the legal opinion the government took allowed an inquiry once sanction was given.

## What exactly is alleged?

**That Kumar asked for ₹2 crore to clear a contractor's bills.** The bills were for rebuilding the MC Road between Thiruvananthapuram and Angamaly. A supplier to the contractor consortium says he was told of the demand by others, so his account is second-hand. The allegation is unproven. **Neither Kumar nor the Election Commission has responded**, and some people linked to the project have said they knew of no demand. The inquiry is at a preliminary stage, where the bureau decides whether to register a case at all.

## Why has the Supreme Court not settled Section 17A?

**Because its two judges disagreed.** On January 13, 2026, in a case brought by the Centre for Public Interest Litigation, Justice B V Nagarathna held the section unconstitutional. She said it "protects the corrupt rather than seeking to protect the honest" and revives a safeguard the court struck down in earlier cases. Justice K V Viswanathan upheld it, but only if the decision on sanction is taken by the **Lokpal, or the Lokayukta in a state**, and not by the government. The matter went to the Chief Justice of India to form a larger bench. Until that bench rules, the section stands as written, which is why Keralam had to grant sanction before the inquiry could begin.

## What protects a Chief Election Commissioner?

**Two separate shields, and neither is clearly about 2006.** The first is removal. Under Article 324(5) a CEC can be removed only in the manner and on the grounds of a Supreme Court judge, which needs a notice signed by 100 Lok Sabha or 50 Rajya Sabha members, an inquiry committee and a **two-thirds majority in both Houses**. No CEC has ever been removed.

The second is Section 16 of the 2023 law on Election Commissioners. It says no court shall entertain or continue any civil or criminal proceeding against a person who is or was a CEC or Election Commissioner for acts done in discharge of official duty. It does not mention arrest or investigation, and its words are tied to official duty. In January 2026 the Supreme Court agreed to examine a challenge by the group Lok Prahari to this section but did not stay it. In another hearing, on petitions against Kumar's conduct over the special intensive revision of electoral rolls, Justice Joymalya Bagchi said the protection is "perhaps only for the President" and noted that it goes beyond what judges get.

## Does the protection reach back to 2006?

**That is the disputed point.** Chennithala's view is that the inquiry concerns Kumar's time as PWD secretary and so "he will not get the protection attached to the post of Chief Election Commissioner". Section 16 speaks of official duty, and an alleged demand for a bribe in 2006 was not an act as CEC. But no court has ruled on how the section applies to conduct before the office, and the sanction order itself has not been made public.

## What happens next

The Vigilance bureau must decide whether the preliminary inquiry shows enough to register a case. If it does, the sanction and the protections above could be tested in court. The larger bench on Section 17A, once it sits, could change who decides such sanctions in every state.
